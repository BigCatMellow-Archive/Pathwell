# Reading List

**Reference authors, craft books, and philosophical sources informing the novel.**

---

## Authors to Emulate

| Author | What to Learn |
|---|---|
| **Terry Pratchett** (primary) | Irreverent voice with philosophical weight. Ideas smuggled inside jokes. The tone Pathwell wants. |
| **Max Gladstone** | Philosophically-grounded urban fantasy magic. How to make magic systems feel like economic and political systems. |
| **China Miéville** | Original worldbuilding with thematic weight. Worlds that feel alien and earned. |

**Reading method:** Read these authors with the [architecture document](Writing-Principles.md) as a lens. Look for how they execute the principles — where they show the iceberg's tip, how they breathe between punches, where their characters want things.

---

## Technical Craft

| Book | Focus |
|---|---|
| Ursula K. Le Guin, *Steering the Craft* | Sentence-level prose, rhythm, voice |
| John Gardner, *The Art of Fiction* | Scene construction, narrative movement |
| John Yorke, *Into the Woods* | Story structure, the five-act shape |

---

## Philosophy (for Thematic Depth)

| Book | Relevance to Pathwell |
|---|---|
| Daniel Dennett, *Freedom Evolves* | Compatibilism and free will — the philosophical backbone of the Pruning system and the question of whether agency is real |
| Jean-Paul Sartre, *Existentialism is a Humanism* | Choice and freedom — Elizabeth's arc is essentially Sartrean: you are your choices, not your circumstances |
| Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons* | Personal identity and branching futures — directly relevant to Shade's existence as pruned possibilities made flesh |
