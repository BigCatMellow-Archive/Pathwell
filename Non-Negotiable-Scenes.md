# Non-Negotiable Scenes

**These ten scenes must exist in the current manuscript. They are the structural pillars of this edited version.** The Chapter 14–18 climax beats below reflect the author's November 24, 2026 direction, superseding the October blob-death staging. Earlier staging remains in decision history; the broader exploratory story review may revisit structure separately.

> **Status, 2026-11-24:** The author selected the directly fatal reintegration and the late admission that Pathwell could have stopped but did not want to (R73). The original October blob-capture death (R45–R47) is superseded in this edited version. Chapters 14–18 have been updated; a fresh independent full-context reader/voice pass is still pending. 1. **At least one clean, casual prune early.** Pathwell buys Camp's books at the Space Between with his own future, a job nobody gave him, and clears an old debt. Establishes pruning as routine before its cost becomes plot-critical. (Q107–Q109)

2. **Elizabeth cuts Pathwell out of the bar blob.** The mirror has turned the problem toward him; the blob takes him, not her, and she chooses to use Stansbury's imbued foam dagger. Stansbury made the tool magical; Elizabeth chose to use it. (Bible decisions 08-23 §6; Q106)

3. **The cookbook given to heal a stranger.** A young woman hurt in a wagon accident at Camp; Elizabeth gives the whole book, one page is spent, and the rest becomes the archive's, with a lived life there before it burns. (Q129)

4. **The midpoint crash, chosen.** Elizabeth deliberately puts the car in the ditch because the brothers keep talking around her. She chooses the crash, not its consequences. "It was the crash that woke her." recurs here, bare. (Refinements §9; the author, 2026-09-30)

5. **The diner.** Elizabeth recognizes that the man isn't Pathwell when he refers to Pathwell as someone else, then learns she was never the point. Shade doesn't hand her a confession: "He knows what he did. Ask him." (Refinements §11–15)

6. **The Ask, and the museum choice.** She makes Pathwell say plainly what he did ("But you still chose it." / "Yes."), chooses Camp because he doesn't control the room there, and tells him "I don't need to be rescued." He overrides her anyway; the discharge destroys the museum's records and hurts her. (Refinements §16–24; Q142–Q146)

7. **The forced reintegration.** Pathwell builds the frame while talking; Shade explicitly refuses and wants to live, Mama Baga says stop, and Pathwell forces the prune through his own contradiction. As the working begins to take Shade apart, Pathwell is repeatedly told he can release the *working* even though his payment is spent. He consciously keeps his hands on the loop. A pin tears free and breaks the lantern at the archive door, causing an ordinary fire that reaches the charged records. No one else causes the disaster for him. (Author's 2026-11-24 R73, superseding October death staging)

8. **The archive burning, and Elizabeth's choice.** Shade is fighting the forced absorption and calling for help while Milo cries from inside the burning archive; Elizabeth hears both, and Shade looks to her and says “Go” without surrendering his own desire to live. She chooses to physically rescue the pinned child without magic, leaving the split foam dagger unused. The diary and Nana's cookbook are within reach, but she passes both and they burn in ordinary fire. The blue road notebook and other records remain at risk.

9. **Shade's death, without consent, and Pathwell's deliberate refusal.** The forced rejoining working consumes Shade while he struggles and begs Pathwell to stop. Another Camp member attempts to hold him. Pathwell **can stop sustaining the working and save Shade**, but chooses not to; Shade is destroyed as the frame finally collapses. Unclaimed pruning waste draws a cleanup creature, but **the creature only clears spillage and does not take Shade**. The draw goes quiet because Shade is gone. Camp names Shade and mourns him. His death is not a chosen martyrdom, useful sacrifice, or forgivable mistake. The full admission of Pathwell's deliberate choice is given privately to Elizabeth later, not to Camp.

10. **The quiet refusal, later confession and invitation.** Pathwell sees another family's cookbook, wants to buy it to fill Nana's place, and silently puts it back; he chooses not to find out whether he can still prune. **Later that visit**, he admits, in the author's words: “I could tell something was wrong. I could have stopped it at any time if I wanted to. But I didn't want to.” Elizabeth relates hearing Shade while choosing to save Milo; he distinguishes their actions. The existing hour of browsing follows his confession. Elizabeth independently chooses their next destination and invites him **without absolving or excusing his choice**. Readers may still like Pathwell while being forced to live with the fact of what he did. The novel ends on “Are you ready?” / “No.” / “Perfect.” (R73; Version C's ending order retained)
