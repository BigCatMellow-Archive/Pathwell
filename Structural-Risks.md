# Structural Risks

**Known failure modes to monitor throughout revision.**

> **Status, 2026-09-29:** written around June, before the 2026-08-28 reconciliation; still a useful checklist. Checked against the chapters in [pass P1](Story/Revision/Pass-Log.md): risks 3 (Elizabeth's agency with Shade), 5 (camp loss feeling abstract) and 8 (lore lectures) are met by the current chapters; 2 and 10 (Shade and the diner speaking the theme) are mostly met, though the narration now states the theme instead ([assessment B4](Story/Revision/Assessment-2026-09-29.md#book-level-findings)); 1, 6 and 7 were superseded by later rulings (the museum's outward discharge, ruling F1; no private retry, 2026-09-18; Stansbury's smaller arc, ruling E3), which are listed on the revision's [Decisions page](Story/Revision/Decisions.md); 11 (symmetry feeling designed) is live in the doubled ending. The current findings are in the [assessment](Story/Revision/Assessment-2026-09-29.md).

---

### 1. Too Many Magic "Explosions"
The camp fire must be unique. The museum confrontation is internal overload, not an outward blast. If every magical conflict ends in a boom, none of them land.

### 2. Shade Turning into a Lecture Machine
The diner scene hinges on ONE accusation. Everything else implied. [[Shade]] works through precision, not volume. See [[Shade#the-diner-accusation]].

### 3. Elizabeth Losing Agency in Act III
She must make at least one meaningful choice while with Shade. She cannot just be transported from diner to museum. **(Currently underdeveloped.)**

### 4. Pruning Rules Getting Fuzzy
Consent must be consistent. Don't let characters force [[Pruning]] casually. The consent rule is what makes the climactic cancel/force moment work — if consent was already fuzzy, the drama evaporates.

### 5. Camp Loss Feeling Abstract
Name specific artifacts early; burn them later. If the reader hasn't connected to what's in the archive, the fire is just spectacle. Letters, journals, histories — give them names and owners before they're destroyed.

### 6. Pathwell's Shift Happening Too Fast
The pacing is: denial → quiet retry → relief → admission. He does NOT have a lightbulb moment. See [[Pathwell (Character)#arc-beats]].

### 7. Stansbury Being Too Functional
He needs the "keeps people small" flaw established *before* the fire moment, or his leadership during the crisis isn't earned. He's not just a useful ally — he's a foil with his own version of the same controlling flaw. See [[Stansbury]].

### 8. Dewey Decimal Lectures
Elizabeth's body tells the reader what section she's in. Not Pathwell pointing at shelves. This is the biggest violation of "write it like the sequel" in the discovery draft.

### 9. Comfort as Elizabeth's Trap
Every time she settles in somewhere, check: is she becoming a passenger again? Camp Cunnan is the most dangerous location for her arc — not because of blobs, but because it's *nice*.

### 10. Diner Scene Articulating Theme Too Cleanly
The diner accusation scene (not yet written) carries a risk of characters speaking the theme directly. Flagged for future attention.

### 11. Architectural Symmetry Feeling Designed
Three characters cleanly mapped to thematic positions, deliberate bookend structures — at full-draft stage, monitor that the story feels lived rather than designed.
