# Stansbury

**The pragmatist. Grounded competence with an overprotective instinct that masquerades as care.**

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Function** | Alternative model of protector. Foil to Pathwell. |
| **Core flaw** | Protects people by keeping them small. |
| **Ability** | Can imbue objects with emotional charge (Nerf weapons). |
| **Arc** | Overcomes his flaw in crisis — leads by trusting, not shielding. |

## The Flaw

The teacher who won't let students take risks because he's seen what happens when they do. His version of "protection" is control too — gentler and more socially acceptable than Pathwell's, but still control.

This makes him a genuine foil to Pathwell: two flavors of the same flaw (recklessness vs. over-caution). It creates friction with [[Elizabeth]] as she climbs the [[Agency Ladder]] and he keeps pulling her back to safety.

## The Nerf Weapons

He wants to arm people but gives them toys instead of real weapons. **That contradiction IS his character.**

Imbuing transfers emotional charge into objects. Stansbury imbues Nerf weapons — which tells you everything about who he is without a single line of exposition.

## Key Beat

Notices the archive fire first. Snaps [[Pathwell (Character)|Pathwell]] out of his spiral. Takes over leadership — not by controlling, but by trusting people to act. His flaw, overcome in crisis.

## Why This Matters for the Theme

He represents the uncomfortable middle ground: agency isn't always pretty. He does morally questionable things (harvesting children's latent magic) but does them with open eyes. He chose this. He's a foil for both Pathwell (who hides from the cost of his choices) and Elizabeth (who hasn't started choosing yet).

## ⚠️ Open Problems

- **Underdeveloped interiority and motivation** beyond his functional role
- Needs the "keeps people small" flaw established *before* the fire moment, or the fire moment isn't earned
- His leadership arc must feel earned, not stumbled into
