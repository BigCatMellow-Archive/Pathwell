## Short description

Some Pathwell scenes create real emotional or causal debts, then move to the
next beat before the reader can feel the cost on the page. The current story
does not need a plot change so much as a targeted debt-payment pass.

## Trigger

TASK-020 earnedness audit found the same pattern in several places:

- Shade's nature and sacrifice are allusive but never grounded once in concrete
  terms.
- Elizabeth destroys the diary, her core tether, without a follow-up aftermath
  beat before the Coda.
- Shade disappears after honest scenes with Elizabeth, but his absence is not
  acknowledged on page.

## The synthesis

Expansion is most useful when it pays a debt the plot has already created:
clarify one load-bearing rule, let a cost register in the body, or acknowledge a
loss before the story moves on. This is different from adding more explanation
or more plot.

## Why it might matter

This gives future Pathwell expansion work a tight filter. Add substance where
the story already made a promise, not where the manuscript is merely short.
That should make the existing story feel more earned while preserving its
current direction and tone.
