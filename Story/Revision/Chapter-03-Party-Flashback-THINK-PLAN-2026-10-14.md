**Preserve the first line of Chapter 1 unchanged:** “It was the crash that woke her.” Keep the immediate discovery of Pathwell in her apartment, the threat at the door, and the opening's momentum. **Do not add a prologue or start Chapter 1 with the party.** This agrees with the earlier Chapter 1 interview lock: the party is established first through its aftermath.

This is a **placement hypothesis to test**, not an instruction to add a flashback to published chapter text without reviewing it.

## 2. Current on-page evidence / handoff

- **Ch1:** Elizabeth's welcome party is over when the crash wakes her. Paper cups, cheese cubes, an incomplete cake inscription (“ELIZ”), and a WELCOME ELIZABETH banner remain. The party was genuinely good, better than she expected. Pathwell was a party guest who talked to everyone and laughed at her joke about the landlord. She never learned his name and assumed he had left. The guests left hours earlier; she went straight to bed. She has a work meeting at nine.
- **Ch2:** Her life has been recently repacked and relocated; she breaks down at the street-side table. Chapter 2 already lets the reader feel the vulnerability beneath her practical worries. A new flashback must *not simply repeat* this diagnosis.
- **Ch3, Space Between:** Elizabeth enters a labyrinth of other people's records and feels emotions that do not belong to her. The transition is explicit: “None of that had been hers, and she held on to that. Then something was.” Her own panic overwhelms her. The blackout boundary is after “Grey crept into the edges of her vision. The last thing she registered was the carpet against her knees.” The return is “When her eyes opened, they were heavy. She was in a chair, her feet up on an ottoman. A thin orange cat sat in front of her, green-eyed and waiting.” Pathwell offers her terrible, familiar coffee. This is the **candidate insertion point** between loss of consciousness and waking, subject to testing rhythm and clarity.
- **Ch4:** Mama Baga notices Elizabeth's guarded pain before the gift of clothes and her own gold chain. Elizabeth's gradual relief at Camp is an emotional counterpoint, not the same feeling as being distracted at the party.
- **Rung Q15–16:** Author says Elizabeth hides loss, isolation and loneliness with distractions and drinking, moving moment-to-moment; at Camp she eventually becomes caught up in something and realizes she is happy without trying. These are **character facts**; the specific Camp activity and flashback presentation remain open.

## 3. THINK — what must the added scene earn?

**Problem:** Ch1 currently reports a good welcome party and shows its aftermath. Readers do not yet see Elizabeth move through that social space: funny, engaged, competent, maybe briefly off balance when she has no task to occupy her. Later sadness is clear, but the contrast between *social distraction* and *spontaneous ease/belonging* has not been experienced.

**Proposed story function:** Put the reader *inside* a credible, enjoyable party. Allow Elizabeth to be sincerely funny and enjoy other people. Reveal her habit of staying in motion through small behavior — finding another drink, refilling a bowl, cleaning up, starting another conversation — **without** presenting her as obviously miserable, intoxicated, dishonest or clinically diagnosed. A small quiet beat when guests leave can hint at the familiar isolation, but it should not announce the theme.

**Earned connection:** In Chapter 4, ordinary kindness and later ordinary participation at Camp will feel different: Elizabeth discovers she can be present and happy without keeping herself busy to escape herself. Don't write that comparison as a speech inside either scene.

**Reader contract:** The reader should understand that the party is a *past event, Elizabeth's own remembered experience*, not a stranger's record and not the current time. A flashback should not erase or replace her actual panic from Ch3.

**Magical rule caution:** The Space Between induces experienced emotions from records, but the book has **not established an ability to project coherent cinematic memories into unconscious people**. Do not invent such a rule casually. Candidate framing is a subjective recollection or dream-like memory at the blackout boundary; if it reads as forced magical playback or requires explanation, change the framing or abandon the placement. The mechanism is **open**.

## 5. Canon and regression gates

- **Locked:** First line of Chapter 1 remains “It was the crash that woke her”; this isn't a prologue plan.
- **Locked:** The welcome party is hers; Pathwell attended but she did not get his name. Their meeting at the party does not make her the original target or special to the blob.
- **Locked:** The first attack predates the cookbook's activation; do not imply Elizabeth summoned the blob.
- **Locked:** Pathwell's reason for approaching charged material, the blob's pursuit, and Shade's knowledge remain separate from this party memory. No new lore.
- **Locked:** “Lizzy” is grounded in Nana's echo (R21); do not rewrite that origin as something a party guest said.
- **Character:** Elizabeth can truly enjoy this party and still feel lonely afterward. Distraction and drinking are established behaviors, **not** license to diagnose addiction or stage an obligatory collapse.
- **Emotion:** The Camp exhale is **relaxation**, not a literal cinematic flashback counterpart or complete cure; the first time she notices unforced happiness at Camp has not been assigned a scene.
- **Language:** Avoid an explanatory voice-over about “existing, not living,” pre-announced motifs, ornate aphorisms or an omniscient psychological speech.
- **Continuity:** Preserve Ch3's own-emotion panic, blackout and waking with the cat/coffee; ensure the flashback does not relocate Nana's cookbook or move Pathwell into a new role.
- **Review:** Use [line-editing protocol](../Sunday-Morning/Line-Editing-Protocol.md) and [reader protocol](../Sunday-Morning/Reader-Protocol.md) with an actual before/after compare. Do not claim a cold review until it happens.
