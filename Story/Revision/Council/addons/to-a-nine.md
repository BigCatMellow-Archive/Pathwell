# Brief add-on: what it would take to make this a 9

First used in council round 2 (2026-10-28), after bt 7274 asked "what it takes to make this 6 into a 9". Append it unchanged after the "Finish with your ranked top ten" line of a member's brief. It doesn't give the reader any earlier score, so each reader scores the book cold and the scores can be compared.

````text
**One more section (put it after your top ten): from where it is to a 9**

1. **Score it.** Give one overall score out of 10, measured against published books in its space. Name the two or three books you're measuring against. Then score prose, character, plot and structure, humor, emotional payoff, originality, and the ending. Say briefly what a 6, a 7, an 8 and a 9 look like for a book like this one.
2. **The gap to a 9.** What would it take for this book to be a 9 in your eyes? Name the specific changes in order of how many points each is worth. For each: where (chapters), what changes, how big a job it is (small, medium or large), and whether it's a *fix* (removing a flaw) or a *lift* (adding something the book doesn't have yet).
3. **The ceiling.** Is a 9 within reach of this book as it's conceived, or would it take a different book? What does a 9 have that a solid 7 doesn't? Many manuscripts improve quickly up to competent and then stall. Where is this one on that curve, and what kind of work moves a book past competent?
4. **What already reads like a 9.** Quote the passages that are already at that level.

Stay concrete. Describe the direction of each change; don't write the new prose.
````
