## The target isn't double

| | Words |
| --- | --- |
| Now | 45,800 |
| Low end of a normal adult debut | 70,000 |
| Comfortable | 75,000–80,000 |

**About +25,000 to +35,000 words gets the book into normal range.** That's roughly the length of five to eight new scenes of the kind the book already has (its chapters run 1,100–4,000 words), plus some room inside existing scenes. That's growing by about half, not doubling.

## The test every candidate has to pass

The author's rule: **"If I remove a scene, does it impact the story at all? If not, remove it."** A scene survives only if cutting it weakens a *later* effect in one of seven ways:
1. **causality**: a later event no longer follows;
2. **character**: a later choice is less believable;
3. **relationship**: trust or rupture loses its evidence;
4. **world attachment**: a later loss feels abstract;
5. **setup/payoff**: a later moment loses its preparation;
6. **escalation**: pressure no longer builds;
7. **contrast**: a big beat loses its "before".

The audit added two more rules:
- "We need more time here" isn't enough. A scene must have **its own immediate want, conflict and turn**.
- If two scenes create the same later effect, combine them.

**One more filter, added for this map:** each candidate should also fix something the reader panel actually complained about. A scene that earns a later beat *and* answers a reader's complaint isn't padding.

## Tier 1: the strongest candidates (about +14,000–16,000)

These pass the removal test cleanly, and each one fixes a convergent panel finding.

### 1. The night between (Ch2, new scene, about 2,500 words)

- **Where:** after the street changes and before the bookshop.
- **Engine:**
  - **Want:** Elizabeth insists on going back to her flat. This is her first real choice, the missing rung.
  - **Conflict:** the shadow in her window (Ch2, currently never paid off), and Pathwell not wanting her there.
  - **Turn:** he lets slip *why he was at her party*. Per the canon (Bible 08-22 §3), he was scavenging charged handwriting, "passed as a party guest", and her diary and cookbook drew him in. The scene also makes plain what "He still has it" means.
- **If cut:**
  - **Setup/payoff:** Ch9's "You were never the point" has no earlier belief to overturn.
  - **Character:** her following him stays "the plot needs her" rather than her choice.
- **Fixes:**
  - the panel's **top finding**, why he was there (6 readers, 3 kinds);
  - "He still has it" (3 readers);
  - the shadow in the window;
  - "Elizabeth complies too fast" (the prose reader's stopper).
- **Must not:** turn into an explanation scene. The reason should slip out, or she should catch him at it, not get a lecture.

### 2. Camp as a place, before it's a sacrifice (Ch4, rebuild the arrival, about +2,500)

- **Where:** the first Camp arrival, before the sick girl and the cookbook.
- **Engine:**
  - **Want:** Elizabeth wants her cookbook and the way home.
  - **Conflict:** Camp keeps absorbing her into its ordinary business: breakfast, a storage argument, children, someone pleased to see Pathwell and someone annoyed.
  - **Turn:** she's handed a job and does it, or Milo attaches himself to her.
  - **Also introduce here, by behaviour, not by list:** Milo with his sock, the archivist (with a name), and Olan and Hess.
- **If cut:**
  - **World attachment:** the Ch14 fire burns "a set", not a home.
  - **Character:** Milo in the fire reads as "stock child-in-peril" (3 readers), because we barely know him.
  - **Setup/payoff:** the archivist's "LIKED POCKETS" entry has less behind it.
- **Fixes:**
  - the Camp roll call that reads as a glossary (4 readers): replace the list with people;
  - Olan and Hess (a stopper for 2);
  - the archivist and the older woman being confusable;
  - the "stock peril" note.
- **Must not:** be a tour. No explaining Camp's philosophy.

### 3. A small job that goes right (between Ch5 and Ch6, new, about 3,000)

- **Engine:** someone asks Pathwell for help with a small problem.
  - **Want:** to fix it.
  - **Conflict:** it's fiddly, and Elizabeth spots the piece he misses.
  - **Turn:** he does a **small, clean prune**, *because they asked*, and it works. Then he decides, again, that the important information is for him and Stansbury, not her.
- **If cut:**
  - **Contrast:** the reader has never seen a prune, so "the prune that went wrong" has no "right" to compare with.
  - **Relationship:** the Ch8 ditch breaks a pattern we've seen only in "Not here" delays.
- **Fixes:**
  - "what is a prune?" (the reluctant reader waited until Ch10; the skimmer nearly quit at "Which prune?");
  - "one plain rule of the magic" (several readers);
  - "the brothers' 'Not here' gets thinner each time" (target-2), by replacing one dodge with a real scene.

  It also gives the September map's midpoint gate what it asked for: two times following him went well, two times she was useful, then exclusion.
- **Must not:** introduce new lore or a villain. Use the world as it already exists, ideally Stansbury's schoolwork-magic.

### 4. Shade's days (between Ch13 and Ch14, new chapter, about 3,000–3,500)

- **Engine:** the book treats Shade being a person as **old news**.
  - Milo is angry because Shade is late for cards.
  - Shade has a Camp job he considers his.
  - Someone dislikes him for an ordinary reason.
  - He makes a plan for next week.
  - **The scene the hostile reviewer asked for:** Shade alone with Elizabeth or Milo, with no Pathwell in the room, *deciding something about his own future that Pathwell can't decide for him*.
  - Possibly the anger at Pathwell the character reader missed: *"One scene of Shade being angry at Pathwell for what Pathwell is."*
- **If cut:**
  - **Character:** "Can." / "Not will." in Ch14 rests on one afternoon of wanting.
  - **Setup/payoff:** Ch15's walk into the blob reads as the plot removing him. The character reader wrote *"I can't tell what he changed"*, and two readers call his death convenient (stoppers).
- **Fixes:**
  - Shade's death feels convenient, and his reason isn't stated;
  - "more days" (the character reader);
  - the hostile reviewer's *single change that would have won it over*.
- **Must not:** be a second "Shade is a person" chapter. Ch13 already does that.

### 5. Waiting, and trying other things (between Ch13 and Ch14, combined with 4 or separate, about 3,000–4,000)

- **Engine:** the draw worsens, and competent people try alternatives before Pathwell acts:
  - distance (Pathwell leaves Camp; where was he before he turns up hurt in Ch14?);
  - observation;
  - Camp arguing about whether Shade should stay;
  - **Elizabeth proposing that the dagger cut the draw.** This is the rules reader's biggest "clever move". It's tried and fails, or is refused, for a reason on the page.

  Pathwell is also asked to handle something where the authority *is* his, and does it well. That sets up the contrast "Please handle this" against "This affects you, but it isn't yours to decide."
- **If cut:**
  - **Escalation:** the Ch14 pin reads as "I can contain it" a third time (3 readers: *"repeats the same mistake"*). With waiting first, it reads as a man who tried to tolerate uncertainty and couldn't.
  - **Causality:** the dagger plot hole stays open.
- **Fixes:**
  - "I can contain this" read as plain repetition;
  - the dagger never being tried on the draw (rules reader);
  - "a quiet scene with Shade and Pathwell before Ch14" (the September whole-book reader);
  - and possibly the climax rule. If Elizabeth's proposal is the one that *would have worked*, or is what Ch15 finally uses, her choice changes the outcome (the 09-18 §3 rule, still open).
- **Decision for the author:** whether her alternative fails, is refused, or becomes the key to Ch15. That decision is where the climax rule could finally be met.

## Tier 2: strong, smaller, or dependent on a decision (about +7,000–9,000)

### 6. The person who asked (Ch10 plus restitution, about +2,000)

- **Engine:** name the person who asked Pathwell for help in Ch10 (one line). Then, in restitution, Pathwell goes to them. **They ask for help again and turn down his preferred method**, and he does it their way. This is the September map's best restitution test.
- **If cut:**
  - **Setup/payoff:** "Did they ask you to prune?" / "No." stays abstract. Four readers say the unnamed client is *"a hole in the middle of the plot."*
  - **Character:** Ch18's invitation rests on less evidence of change.
- **Decision for the author:** the Bible (08-22) left this open "unless the story later needs it", and the panel says it does. Who asked, and what was the problem?

### 7. Restitution as different tests, not lists (Ch16–17, rebuild, net about +1,500)

- **Engine:** replace the inventory lists with three short scenes, each testing a different restraint:
  - someone **refuses** his help entirely, and he leaves it alone;
  - the person from 6;
  - Henry Vale's letters (this already exists and is loved).
- **If cut:** the restitution scenes are a convergent **stopper** ("tidy restitution scenes… that mostly repeat 'P learned to ask first'"). Varying the test is the fix; adding more lists would make it worse.
- **Note:** this is mostly a rebuild. The lists come out and the scenes go in.

### 8. Elizabeth's own life and want (Ch1–2 seeds, Ch17 scene, about +2,000)

- **Engine:**
  - **Early:** her job and the building, a sentence or two each.
  - **Ch17:** back at work, the missed nine o'clock meeting has a consequence, a neighbour knocks, and she realises *what she wants to go toward*. That gives "Because she wanted to go" an object.
- **If cut:** "Elizabeth's own want is thin" is a convergent finding (3 readers, 3 kinds), and the ending's choice has nothing to choose against.
- **Caution:** both target readers wanted "the building", but their briefs gave them that interest, so weight it lightly. The *want* finding is solid; the building is a way to show it.

### 9. Ch12: the diary given for its own reason (about +1,000)

- **Engine:** before she gives the diary, she tries to write Nana's funeral page (the reluctant reader and the hostile reviewer both asked for it). The give then comes *from* that page, not as a copy of Ch4's ritual.
- **If cut:** the diary donation is a convergent "repeat" (4 readers), and the Ch15 fire's loss of the diary is "quieter, almost a relief" (the September reader).

## What not to add (carried from September, still true)

No:
- lore excursions;
- new villains;
- new magic systems;
- travel for its own sake;
- action whose only job is excitement;
- philosophy conversations;
- new tragic backstory;
- magical powers for Elizabeth;
- romance;
- extra twists about Shade;
- more sacrifice objects for callbacks.

*"The expansion should come from living inside what is already there."*

## Decisions for the author

1. **Who asked Pathwell for help, and what was the problem?** (needed for 6)
2. **In the waiting section, does Elizabeth's alternative fail, get refused, or become the key to Ch15?** (5; this is the climax rule)
3. **How plainly does Pathwell's reason for being at the party come out in Ch2?** (1; the canon says he was scavenging, and the question is how it's revealed)
4. **Which tier to start with.** The recommendation is Tier 1, items 1 and 3 first: they fix the opening, which is where readers quit.
