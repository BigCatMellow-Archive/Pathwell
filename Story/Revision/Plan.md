### 3. First principles

What the book must do for the reader, from its opening promise and the [voice guide's](../Sunday-Morning/Sources/Voice-Guide.md) final aim: be a strange, funny story with grief under it, about a woman used to life happening to her who starts choosing, in a world where magic is made of people's handwriting and consequences stay. Stripped to that:

- **The ending** needs only two things: Elizabeth chooses where next, and Pathwell shows restraint without a demonstration. The interview locked the smaller mechanism (see, want, put back, don't find out); Ch18's full near-prune was the larger, and it answered the question the author locked open. *Done in P43: the smaller mechanism.*
- **The opening** needs the reader to know why he's there, from the aftermath and without a prologue. The interview's party-guest setup is that mechanism, and it also answers "Lizzy".
- **The climax** needs Elizabeth to act without power. It does. The interview locked her as witness in the confrontation; whether she must also change the Pathwell–Shade outcome is a question about what the book is ([Q-E](Decisions.md#open-for-the-author)), not a craft fault.
- **The prose** needs the scene to be trusted. Most of the fix is cutting.

### 4. Counterexample search

Attacking the plan itself:

- **A book-wide prose pass could flatten the voice.** The first Sunday Morning collection over-corrected into fragments ([History](../Sunday-Morning/History.md#what-went-wrong-and-where-the-lesson-lives-now)); here the danger is the reverse, into blandness. *Guard:* one test chapter first; read revised chapters against the author's pre-August Chapters 1–2; keep fragments where the voice guide wants them (danger, comic timing) and keep some commentary (the narration may have opinions, [W3](../Sunday-Morning/Decisions.md#working-decisions)).
- **Some of the commentary may be the author's own.** *Guard:* narrator commentary is a cautious category, so every cut of a narrator line that carries a judgment is listed in the change notes with its old text, and the ones that aren't plainly redundant are flagged, not made.
- **Contractions everywhere would lose insistence.** "Shade did not." can be the point. *Guard:* keep full forms where a character or the narration is insisting (Craft: "Full forms… only when someone is insisting").
- **Applying the rulings could remove what the cold reader loved** (the Ch18 ledger scene). *Guard:* the interview locked it, so it goes; the trade-off is named for the author in the [open questions](Decisions.md#open-for-the-author) so he sees what's lost.
- **Fixing Ch12's diary motive could add explanation**, the fault being fixed. *Guard:* prefer cuts; add a line only where the [one-line test](../Sunday-Morning/Craft.md#the-one-line-test) shows a person's choice is missing.
- **Choosing the correction rule (Q-E) could lead to a climax act that breaks the rules** (Writing Principles 17) or put the fire on Elizabeth (the W15 contract: "No one else causes the failure for him"). *Guard:* THINK on Ch14–15 only after the author rules, and test every option against both.

## The climax: what the story has already planted

The author: "Id love to see what the story needs to make it happen" (R13). The question is whether Elizabeth's climax choice should change what happens between Pathwell and Shade (the 2026-09-18 rule), not only whom she saves. This section grows options from what the chapters already contain rather than inventing a mechanism. Nothing changes until he picks one, or none.

**What any option has to keep** (interview locks): Elizabeth does no magic, though she may use a finished tool Stansbury made (Q106: "Stansbury made the tool magical; Elizabeth chose to use it"). Shade owns his no, and Pathwell owns crossing it and owns the failure. The recoil still ignites the archive. She still runs past the diary and the cookbook to Milo. Shade's last act is still his own, an informed risk. And a working can only do what's been set up ([Writing Principles 17](../../Writing-Principles.md#17-rules-are-sacred-once-established)).

**Seeds already in the ground:**

- **The foam dagger** (Ch5): "Cuts separation into things that don't separate easily. Adhesions. Clinging effects. Certain magical bindings." Used once, in Ch7 ("Not flesh. A seam."), and last seen at her waistband in Ch10. It's the book's biggest unpaid setup.
- **The draw is the frame's working part** (Ch14): "You are going to use the existing connection as the return line." Pathwell's own name for the draw in Ch6 is "a thread caught somewhere behind the ribs."
- **Her witness lines** (Ch14): "Let it fail." / "That's what you said at the museum." Pathwell answers "I can contain it" (Ch11's words) and holds the loop, and that's what tears the pins out into the lantern.
- **The costs Shade's locked reason needs him to see** (Ch15): Elizabeth's arm out of the sling, Milo's bare foot, Stansbury's hand, all of it carried out of the fire just before the blob arrives.

| Option | What happens | What changes if her choice is removed | Rules and locks | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

It's too late to stop the failure, but the frame collapses toward him (as it does now) instead of settling Shade into him, so Shade stays himself. | **3b. **Decided (R37, 2026-10-05; P39):** option 2, with 3b. Option 1 was chosen first and dropped after the [tenth seat](Tenth-Seat/2026-10-05_Ch14-decisions.md): Shade is already not folded in on the page (the frame collapses toward Pathwell with his hands on the loop), so her "Let it fail" changing his grip would change nothing, and his letting go would soften Ch16 ("chose not to stop") and the Ch18 restraint (since P43 a silent put-back, not a lift). Her line stays unheeded. 3b is in Ch14 (her hand goes to the dagger and stays, with the reason on the page: she doesn't know what it would cut, or whose it is to cut). Option 2 is in Ch15 (P40): Shade looks at Stansbury's hand, Milo's bare foot and her arm out of the torn sling, then gives his reason in one line. **Neither option meets the 2026-09-18 rule** (the Ch15 tenth seat: take her rescue away and everything that turns him is still in front of him; the outcome is the same), so the rule is put to the author.

**The lean before the ruling:** 1 and 2 together. Both are already half on the page, both keep every lock, and between them her choice changes both men: Pathwell lets go because of what she says, and Shade decides because of what she carried out. If you want the dagger paid, 3b pairs with either at no cost. Option 3 is the real alternative if you want her act to change the mechanism itself; it's the most satisfying on paper and the hardest to keep from turning her into the solver.

**How to test it, the gardener way:** draft the chosen option into Chapters 14–15 after Step 2, then run the cold read on Chapters 12–16 in order. If the reader says the climax is hers without being told it is, it grew. If they notice the machinery, it didn't.

## Reconsideration triggers

Evidence that would make this plan wrong. When one fires, stop that branch, record it in the [pass log](Pass-Log.md), and route it.

- **The author says the test chapter doesn't sound like him** → stop the batch. Go back to voice: re-derive the target from his pre-August Chapters 1–2 and the voice guide, or the author writes over the test chapter first. Start from the [AI detection notes](AI-Detection-Notes-2026-09-30.md#what-it-teaches-this-revision): whether the narrator's voice follows its [register guide](../Story_Files/pathwell_narrator_register.md) (present at arrivals, transitions and aftermath, clinical in action, sensation and around Shade, never after a punchline), whether the characters' humour is their own, and how clean the interiority is. The checker's surface numbers don't answer these. (THINK)
- **A revised chapter loses the voice guide's range**: no fragments left even for danger or comedy, or no narrator attitude at all → over-correction toward flatness; revisit before the next chapter. The author's own chapters have almost no fragment paragraphs and almost no commentary, but they aren't flat, so the checker's numbers can't catch this; the fresh check reads for it. (DO)
- **A replacement tic appears** (cut commentary replaced by silences, "She let that stand.", or a new stock beat) → the replacement-tic check fails; fix before continuing. (DO)
- **A line cut removes a setup the ledger needs** → update the [ledger](Promise-Ledger.md) and the paying chapter first. (PLAN)
- **The author chooses the correction rule (Q-E)** → a THINK pass on Ch14–15 with the inversion method, before any line work there. (THINK)
- **The welcome-party opening would change the sugar-cookie scene** → stop and ask. (the author)
- **The chosen climax option needs a rule the chapters haven't set up** → set it up earlier, or drop the option; never introduce it at the climax. (PLAN)
- **The ending's wording or speaker changes** → check Chapter 1's version of the exchange and [ledger R1](Promise-Ledger.md#relationships-and-running-elements) in the same pass. (PLAN)
- **A fresh check finds a new continuity error in a revised chapter** → fix it before the next chapter. (DO)
- **Two chapters' passes both want to cut the same running element** (the coffee, the sock) → decide its returns once, in the ledger, not chapter by chapter. (PLAN)

4. Update the pass log, the ledger, the Registry and Revision-Status; commit and push; send the links.
5. Stop for the author's verdict.

**Planning DONE when:** (1) major panel objections have an evidence-backed disposition; (2) each proposed change names an existing authority or an unresolved authority question; (3) viable competing remedies and counterexamples are considered for consequential changes; (4) the first revision slice is executable with concrete tests; (5) later phases remain conditional where upstream findings could change them; (6) an author can review the choices without approving a fabricated new canon. **Manuscript L4 DONE is separate**: revised text, ledger and status reconciled, fresh independent whole-book read, then author's verdict.
