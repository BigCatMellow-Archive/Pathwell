## THINK at book level

**Reasoning allocation:** a structured single path. The book makes mechanical claims a reader can catch out, and three layers of rulings disagree about its ending, opening and climax, so "act directly" isn't enough. No parallel versions: none of the findings is a failure that calls for one.

### 3. First principles

What the book must do for the reader, from its opening promise and the [voice guide's](../Sunday-Morning/Sources/Voice-Guide.md) final aim: be a strange, funny story with grief under it, about a woman used to life happening to her who starts choosing, in a world where magic is made of people's handwriting and consequences stay. Stripped to that:

- **The ending** needs only two things: Elizabeth chooses where next, and Pathwell shows restraint without a demonstration. The interview locked the smaller mechanism (see, want, put back, don't find out); Ch18's full near-prune is the larger, and it answers the question James locked open.
- **The opening** needs the reader to know why he's there, from the aftermath and without a prologue. The interview's party-guest setup is that mechanism, and it also answers "Lizzy".
- **The climax** needs Elizabeth to act without power. It does. The interview locked her as witness in the confrontation; whether she must also change the Pathwell–Shade outcome is a question about what the book is ([Q-E](Decisions.md#open-for-james)), not a craft fault.
- **The prose** needs the scene to be trusted. Most of the fix is cutting.

### 4. Counterexample search

Attacking the plan itself:

- **A book-wide prose pass could flatten the voice.** The first Sunday Morning collection over-corrected into fragments ([History](../Sunday-Morning/History.md#what-went-wrong-and-where-the-lesson-lives-now)); here the danger is the reverse, into blandness. *Guard:* one test chapter first; measure revised chapters against Chapters 1–3, not against zero; keep fragments where the voice guide wants them (danger, comic timing) and keep some commentary (the narration may have opinions, [W3](../Sunday-Morning/Decisions.md#working-decisions)).
- **Some of the commentary may be James's own.** *Guard:* narrator commentary is a cautious category, so every cut of a narrator line that carries a judgment is listed in the change notes with its old text, and the ones that aren't plainly redundant are flagged, not made.
- **Contractions everywhere would lose insistence.** "Shade did not." can be the point. *Guard:* keep full forms where a character or the narration is insisting (Craft: "Full forms… only when someone is insisting").
- **Applying the rulings could remove what the cold reader loved** (the Ch18 ledger scene). *Guard:* the interview locked it, so it goes; the trade-off is named for James in the [open questions](Decisions.md#open-for-james) so he sees what's lost.
- **Fixing Ch12's diary motive could add explanation**, the fault being fixed. *Guard:* prefer cuts; add a line only where the [one-line test](../Sunday-Morning/Craft.md#the-one-line-test) shows a person's choice is missing.
- **Choosing the correction rule (Q-E) could lead to a climax act that breaks the rules** (Writing Principles 17) or put the fire on Elizabeth (the W15 contract: "No one else causes the failure for him"). *Guard:* THINK on Ch14–15 only after James rules, and test every option against both.

## Reconsideration triggers

Evidence that would make this plan wrong. When one fires, stop that branch, record it in the [pass log](Pass-Log.md), and route it.

- **James says the test chapter doesn't sound like him** → stop the batch. Go back to voice: re-derive the target from Chapters 1–3 and the voice guide, or James writes over the test chapter first. (THINK)
- **A revised chapter's numbers fall well below the benchmark's** (fewer fragments or commentary paragraphs than Chapters 1–3 have) → over-correction; revisit before the next chapter. (DO)
- **A replacement tic appears** (cut commentary replaced by silences, "She let that stand.", or a new stock beat) → the replacement-tic check fails; fix before continuing. (DO)
- **A line cut removes a setup the ledger needs** → update the [ledger](Promise-Ledger.md) and the paying chapter first. (PLAN)
- **James chooses the correction rule (Q-E)** → a THINK pass on Ch14–15 with the inversion method, before any line work there. (THINK)
- **The welcome-party opening would change the sugar-cookie scene** → stop and ask. (James)
- **The ending's wording changes** ("Good." for "Perfect.", or who asks) → check Chapter 1's version of the exchange and [ledger R1](Promise-Ledger.md#relationships-and-running-elements) in the same pass. (PLAN)
- **A fresh check finds a new continuity error in a revised chapter** → fix it before the next chapter. (DO)
- **Two chapters' passes both want to cut the same running element** (the coffee, the sock) → decide its returns once, in the ledger, not chapter by chapter. (PLAN)

5. Stop for James's verdict.
