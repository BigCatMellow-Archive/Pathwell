### 3. First principles

What the book must do for the reader, from its opening promise and the [voice guide's](../Sunday-Morning/Sources/Voice-Guide.md) final aim: be a strange, funny story with grief under it, about a woman used to life happening to her who starts choosing, in a world where magic is made of people's handwriting and consequences stay. Stripped to that:

- **The ending** needs only two things: Elizabeth chooses where next, and Pathwell shows restraint without a demonstration. The interview locked the smaller mechanism (see, want, put back, don't find out); Ch18's full near-prune is the larger, and it answers the question James locked open.
- **The opening** needs the reader to know why he's there, from the aftermath and without a prologue. The interview's party-guest setup is that mechanism, and it also answers "Lizzy".
- **The climax** needs Elizabeth to act without power. It does. The interview locked her as witness in the confrontation; whether she must also change the Pathwell–Shade outcome is a question about what the book is ([Q-E](Decisions.md#open-for-james)), not a craft fault.
- **The prose** needs the scene to be trusted. Most of the fix is cutting.

### 4. Counterexample search

Attacking the plan itself:

- **A book-wide prose pass could flatten the voice.** The first Sunday Morning collection over-corrected into fragments ([History](../Sunday-Morning/History.md#what-went-wrong-and-where-the-lesson-lives-now)); here the danger is the reverse, into blandness. *Guard:* one test chapter first; read revised chapters against James's pre-August Chapters 1–2; keep fragments where the voice guide wants them (danger, comic timing) and keep some commentary (the narration may have opinions, [W3](../Sunday-Morning/Decisions.md#working-decisions)).
- **Some of the commentary may be James's own.** *Guard:* narrator commentary is a cautious category, so every cut of a narrator line that carries a judgment is listed in the change notes with its old text, and the ones that aren't plainly redundant are flagged, not made.
- **Contractions everywhere would lose insistence.** "Shade did not." can be the point. *Guard:* keep full forms where a character or the narration is insisting (Craft: "Full forms… only when someone is insisting").
- **Applying the rulings could remove what the cold reader loved** (the Ch18 ledger scene). *Guard:* the interview locked it, so it goes; the trade-off is named for James in the [open questions](Decisions.md#open-for-james) so he sees what's lost.
- **Fixing Ch12's diary motive could add explanation**, the fault being fixed. *Guard:* prefer cuts; add a line only where the [one-line test](../Sunday-Morning/Craft.md#the-one-line-test) shows a person's choice is missing.
- **Choosing the correction rule (Q-E) could lead to a climax act that breaks the rules** (Writing Principles 17) or put the fire on Elizabeth (the W15 contract: "No one else causes the failure for him"). *Guard:* THINK on Ch14–15 only after James rules, and test every option against both.

## The climax: what the story has already planted

James: "Id love to see what the story needs to make it happen" (R13). The question is whether Elizabeth's climax choice should change what happens between Pathwell and Shade (the 2026-09-18 rule), not only whom she saves. This section grows options from what the chapters already contain rather than inventing a mechanism. Nothing changes until he picks one, or none.

**What any option has to keep** (interview locks): Elizabeth does no magic, though she may use a finished tool Stansbury made (Q106: "Stansbury made the tool magical; Elizabeth chose to use it"). Shade owns his no, and Pathwell owns crossing it and owns the failure. The recoil still ignites the archive. She still runs past the diary and the cookbook to Milo. Shade's last act is still his own, an informed risk. And a working can only do what's been set up ([Writing Principles 17](../../Writing-Principles.md#17-rules-are-sacred-once-established)).

**Seeds already in the ground:**

- **The foam dagger** (Ch5): "Cuts separation into things that don't separate easily. Adhesions. Clinging effects. Certain magical bindings." Used once, in Ch7 ("Not flesh. A seam."), and last seen at her waistband in Ch10. It's the book's biggest unpaid setup.
- **The draw is the frame's working part** (Ch14): "You are going to use the existing connection as the return line." Pathwell's own name for the draw in Ch6 is "a thread caught somewhere behind the ribs."
- **Her witness lines** (Ch14): "Let it fail." / "That's what you said at the museum." Pathwell answers "I can hold it" and holds the loop, and that's what tears the pins out into the lantern.
- **The costs Shade's locked reason needs him to see** (Ch15): Elizabeth's arm out of the sling, Milo's bare foot, Stansbury's hand, all of it carried out of the fire just before the blob arrives.

| Option | What happens | What changes if her choice is removed | Rules and locks | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. She lets it fail** (Ch14) | After the payment has released, her "Let it fail" reaches him, and Pathwell takes his hands off the loop instead of holding it. It's too late to stop the failure, but the frame collapses toward him (as it does now) instead of settling Shade into him, so Shade stays himself. The pins still tear loose and the lantern still falls. | Pathwell holds on, and the working keeps trying to fold Shade in until it breaks; whether Shade survives that as himself is unclear. | Keeps everything: she only speaks (she "may challenge a dodge"), he decides, and the failure is still his. | His letting go is an early flicker of the restraint he learns later; it could make the ending's restraint land softer. |
| **2. Shade's reason is what she carried out** (Ch15) | Shade's locked reason (he sees his pursuit making others pay and stops) is set off by what he sees: Elizabeth coming out with Milo, her arm hanging, then Stansbury's hand. He looks at them before he looks at the blob. | Shade's decision loses its trigger; without her in the fire, there's nothing in front of him that turns his pursuit into other people's cost. | Keeps everything; no new mechanism. It's mostly making visible what the locks already say. | Her choice changes *his* choice, not the mechanism; some readers may feel that's still influence rather than outcome. |
| **3. The dagger** (Ch14) | When the frame pulls Shade's arm rigid toward Pathwell, she cuts the draw, the return line the working depends on. The frame loses what it was using and collapses; Shade is left himself, and the spill and the fire still come. | The frame keeps its return line; Shade is folded into Pathwell or broken by the attempt. | **Needs setup:** nothing on the page yet says the draw is the kind of binding the dagger can cut, so Ch5 or Ch10 would need a line (probably Stansbury being honest that he doesn't know). It must not stop the payment (the failure has to stay Pathwell's), and it mustn't read as her solving the magic. With the draw cut, Shade's death still has to follow from the cleanup, because the signature overlap remains. | The biggest change, and the one that pays the dagger. It risks making her the solver, the thing the interview guarded against. |
| **3b. The dagger, not used** (Ch14) | Her hand goes to the dagger when the frame goes rigid, and stays there: it's Shade's no to defend, not hers to cut. | Nothing changes in the outcome. | Keeps every lock, pays the dagger. | Fails the 2026-09-18 rule by design; it's the "witness" answer made visible. |

**My lean:** 1 and 2 together. Both are already half on the page, both keep every lock, and between them her choice changes both men: Pathwell lets go because of what she says, and Shade decides because of what she carried out. If you want the dagger paid, 3b pairs with either at no cost. Option 3 is the real alternative if you want her act to change the mechanism itself; it's the most satisfying on paper and the hardest to keep from turning her into the solver.

**How to test it, the gardener way:** draft the chosen option into Chapters 14–15 after Step 2, then run the cold read on Chapters 12–16 in order. If the reader says the climax is hers without being told it is, it grew. If they notice the machinery, it didn't.

## Reconsideration triggers

Evidence that would make this plan wrong. When one fires, stop that branch, record it in the [pass log](Pass-Log.md), and route it.

- **James says the test chapter doesn't sound like him** → stop the batch. Go back to voice: re-derive the target from his pre-August Chapters 1–2 and the voice guide, or James writes over the test chapter first. (THINK)
- **A revised chapter loses the voice guide's range**: no fragments left even for danger or comedy, or no narrator attitude at all → over-correction toward flatness; revisit before the next chapter. James's own chapters have almost no fragment paragraphs and almost no commentary, but they aren't flat, so the checker's numbers can't catch this; the fresh check reads for it. (DO)
- **A replacement tic appears** (cut commentary replaced by silences, "She let that stand.", or a new stock beat) → the replacement-tic check fails; fix before continuing. (DO)
- **A line cut removes a setup the ledger needs** → update the [ledger](Promise-Ledger.md) and the paying chapter first. (PLAN)
- **James chooses the correction rule (Q-E)** → a THINK pass on Ch14–15 with the inversion method, before any line work there. (THINK)
- **The welcome-party opening would change the sugar-cookie scene** → stop and ask. (James)
- **The chosen climax option needs a rule the chapters haven't set up** → set it up earlier, or drop the option; never introduce it at the climax. (PLAN)
- **The ending's wording or speaker changes** → check Chapter 1's version of the exchange and [ledger R1](Promise-Ledger.md#relationships-and-running-elements) in the same pass. (PLAN)
- **A fresh check finds a new continuity error in a revised chapter** → fix it before the next chapter. (DO)
- **Two chapters' passes both want to cut the same running element** (the coffee, the sock) → decide its returns once, in the ledger, not chapter by chapter. (PLAN)

## Step 2: the test chapter

What Step 2 does, in order:
