## THINK

### 1. The frame

**The rule (Writing-Principles):** *"Never add material because the manuscript is short. Add material only because the existing story asks the reader to feel, believe, understand, or mourn something it has not yet earned."* And: *"Before adding a new scene, first ask whether an already-necessary scene can carry the missing function."*

**The test (the author's, 09-18):** *"If I remove a scene, does it impact the story at all? If not, remove it."* A new scene must:
- fill in the blank "If this is removed, ___ becomes materially weaker", specifically;
- have its own want, conflict and turn;
- obey the [non-negotiable scenes](../../Non-Negotiable-Scenes.md), the locks, and the voice rules (no explaining, no narrator grading, Elizabeth never uses magic).

So the question isn't "how do we reach 70,000?" It's **"what does the story ask for that it hasn't earned?"** Length is whatever that answer comes to.

### 5. Counterexamples: the case that the book is the right size

- **Readers praised compression.** Target-1: the best things are *"short, specific and left alone."* Their advice was to cut the second and third time the same beat is made.
- **The skimmer never quit.** It kept going through 18 separate short sittings.
- **The protected beats all land** without extra scenes in front of them: the cookbook give, the marginalia, the fire, the ledger, "You were never the point".
- **The September necessity audit** said the removal test *"reverses the expansion bias that entered during the dwell-time pass."* The 10-13 map repeated that bias.

On this evidence, **the book mostly wants to be tighter and clearer, not longer.** The one place it may want more is Shade.

### 6. Options, and what each means for the story

**E, earned fixes.** These pass the removal test, need no new scene, and fit the locks. Each line gives what it fixes, then the test for success.
- **E1. Ch1: R38 plus one behavioural clue** to why he's there. Fixes the opening premise. *Test:* a cold reader can say why that flat, and still believe "You were never the point".
- **E2. Ch3: the price in human terms; the Space Between made picturable; the prune named** so it's recognisable later. Fixes the quit line and "what is a prune?". *Test:* a reader can say what Pathwell gave up.
- **E3. Ch8–9: local motives** for getting into Shade's car and for the walk back (Plan wave 2). Both target readers didn't believe them.
- **E4. Ch12: the funeral page she can't finish** becomes the diary's reason; it's already mentioned, so the scene is the attempt. Fixes the diary gift reading as a copy (4 readers), and the diary's loss feeling "almost a relief". *Removal test:* without it, the Ch15 loss of the diary is quiet.
- **E5. Ch14: physical clarity** (who is where, and what the cord does). The climax currently blurs.
- **E6. Ch15: what Shade's walk prevents, felt in the scene.** Run the counterfactual from the Plan first, and don't invent a rule. Fixes "convenient", and "I didn't know he was dead".
- **E7. Ch18: one gesture tying the Merritt book to Nana's.** No reader saw the replacement motive.
- **E8. Ch17–18: her want, said in a beat she owns** (from R40's reading of her character), inside an existing scene. Fixes her want being thin. Must not become a return-to-normal scene (Q136).

E1–E8 come to roughly **+2,500–4,500 words** (a rough estimate).

**S1. Shade's own decision, inside the existing morning (a new scene in Ch13, about 1,500–2,500 words).**
- **Engine:** Shade alone with Elizabeth or Milo, with no Pathwell, decides something about his own future that Pathwell can't decide for him. That's the hostile reviewer's single change. Possibly also the anger at Pathwell the character reader missed.
- **If cut:** Ch14's "Can." / "Not will." and Ch15's walk rest on one morning, and the death reads as the plot removing him. That's the only stopper-level gap in the panel.
- **How it fits:** no change to the timeline. It sits before Hearts in Ch13's morning.
- **The conflict:** the Ch13 tenth seat (10-05) decided "no scene is added". That was a reviser's decision under R37, made before the panel, so new evidence can reopen it. But it adds a second "Shade is a person" beat, which Ch13 already does. The scene must *assume* his personhood and use it to make a choice, not prove it again.
- **Depends on:** W19 (the tree-line conversation, which this scene could absorb) and E6.

**S2. Open the middle: days between the museum and the reintegration (about 5,000–8,000 words).**
- **Engine:**
  - Shade's ordinary days (a plan for next week, a job of his own);
  - competent people trying alternatives;
  - the draw worsening;
  - Pathwell asked to handle something that *is* his, and doing it well;
  - then deciding he has waited long enough.
- **What it means for the story:**
  - It changes the climax's meaning. Now he can't wait one morning; then he waited and still couldn't tolerate it.
  - It deepens Shade.
  - It gives Elizabeth time to propose an alternative, which could finally meet the 09-18 climax rule.
- **The cost:** it rebuilds Ch12–15's chronology. The "Good morning" arrival mid-Hearts, Pathwell's fresh museum injuries and "inventory by tonight" would all go. The urgency the current version has would be lost, and the new middle would have to stay as tight as the rest.
- **Removal test:** it passes *only if* the author thinks the one-morning compression is a flaw. If the compression is the design, S2 is padding.

**Rejected as additions here, with reasons:**
- The "night between" (Ch2): E1 covers it, and Ch2 is protected.
- The "small job" (Ch5–6): the Ch3 prune exists; E2 covers it.
- The building or job scene: Q136.
- The client named, plus a restitution visit: P5 is open on purpose; at most one line if the author answers D4.
- Everything in the "conflicting or rejected" list in §3.

**Plainly: there is no honest path to 70,000 words in this story.** Every route that gets there adds material that fails the author's own test. If length becomes necessary for selling, the method's answer isn't to stretch this book. The ending already opens onto the next adventure, and **a second book** adds words that each earn their place. Series and backlist are also where independent authors' income tends to come from. That is a separate decision from this one.

### 8. Kept for later: assumptions, uncertainties, and what would decide

- **Uncertain:** whether readers who aren't simulated feel Shade's death as convenient. *Would decide:* two or three human readers of Ch13–15, before S1 is drafted.
- **Uncertain:** whether S2's slower middle would lose the momentum readers liked. *Would decide:* an outline-level read before any prose.
- **Uncertain:** E6. The counterfactual (what the blob does without his walk) might show the difference is already on the page. *If so:* clarify rather than add.
- **Assumption:** the author rewrites the final prose himself, for authorship and voice. The word counts above are rough, and his drafts will set the real length.

**Stop or reverse when:**
- a fix needs a mechanism the canon doesn't have;
- an addition can't fill the removal blank specifically;
- a protected echo or locked scene would change;
- the author's voice flattens;
- the book starts adding material because it's short.

The rule for every one of these is the same: choose the smaller fix, or leave the scene as it is.
