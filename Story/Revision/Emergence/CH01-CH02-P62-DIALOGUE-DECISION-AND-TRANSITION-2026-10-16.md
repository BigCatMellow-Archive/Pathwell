## The comparison in the scene, not in isolation

### A — prior live Chapter 1

Following “We should probably get going,” Elizabeth objects **“I'm not going anywhere with you.”** Pathwell checks the empty front doorway anyway and turns back; he begins his “marked” claim with **“Understandable. But”**. Elizabeth then refuses **a second time**, this time conditionally: “I'm not going anywhere until you tell me what is going on.” After his evasive responses she later begins “I already told you I'm not—”, and ultimately answers “No” to his “Ready?”

**A does real character work.** Elizabeth explicitly says no before he claims the danger is hers; Pathwell responds with a pleasant-sounding concession that is not a concession at all. This is the novel's larger control/consent dynamic at small scale, and is why these lines were not cut reflexively after R38.

**A's cost:** The exact “I'm not going” framing is repeated in a span already crowded with “we need to get going,” his false “marked” certainty and her more consequential question about Nana's voice. The first “Understandable” becomes the setup for a later “Again, understandable reaction,” making Pathwell sound like a verbal loop rather than a person listening badly. Those repetitions may be intentional, but the draft can still show his flaw without every instance.

### B — paired deletion/trim, adopted under continuing-work direction

- Remove the standalone first **“I'm not going anywhere with you.”**
- Trim **“Understandable. But”** from Pathwell's sentence about the apartment being “marked.”
- **Keep** “I'm not going anywhere until you tell me what is going on.” and “I already told you I'm not—”. Keep her final “No.”
- **Keep both “Perfect.” occurrences**: first after the sugar-cookie identification, second as the final line. The competing author-origin “Good… that's good” variant was **not adopted** by generic continuation.

In B, his sequence is clean: he says leave; physically checks the doorway; returns having convinced himself he knows the danger; uses the unearned “marked” diagnosis; *then* she objects and insists on an explanation. His next answer (“That confusion is quite normal. Do you have shoes?”) is a more naked case of talking past her direct request. Her earlier door investigation and cookbook intervention have already demonstrated that she acts rather than merely accepts instructions.

**Informed verdict:** B saves repetition without letting Pathwell off the hook. It also allows Elizabeth's more precise request for an explanation to carry the pressure. However, it **slightly reduces the earlier consent signal** and is not objectively superior to A on character grounds. The author may legitimately prefer A after hearing the whole chapter.

### The “Perfect” test remains separate

“Perfect” appears after the sugar-cookie recipe and at the novel opening's end. In the author's July draft it did so **twice**, potentially by intention. The October 8 author markup proposed **“Good… that's good”** for the early line; that is available as an alternate [EXP-0014 C](experiments/EXP-0014C-Ch01-Author-Dialogue-Trio-2026-10-16.txt), **but the C full file predates a P61 scent-line grammar change**. Never copy the whole archival file over the current chapter. Assess the dialogue choice *alone* against the saved author lock on the final “Perfect.”

**Current recommendation:** Keep the original pair until a full read establishes that the first use cheapens the last. The second use is not an accidental final one-liner; it reveals how Pathwell dismisses her answer.

## A narrow Chapter 2 transition repair

The October 8 author marked the first Chapter 2 dialogue **“Will you slow down!?”** as difficult to attribute when followed by a new paragraph beginning “Elizabeth struggled to keep up…”.

**Do (single newline deletion only):**

> “Will you slow down!?” Elizabeth struggled to keep up with him as he charged down the stairs. “What about all my stuff?”

The spoken sentence and Elizabeth's movement are now one paragraph. No dialogue, story event or punctuation changed, except removal of the paragraph break between the opening quotation and her name. This realizes the **author's specific ¶185 note** without adding a “she shouted” tag or changing the very first beats of Chapter 2. Pathwell's subsequent “Replaceable” still responds to her question; she still remembers Nana's spoon at home.

**Continuity into Chapter 3:** The conditional refusal is not dropped as a durable character boundary. Chapter 3 opens with Elizabeth demanding answers, refusing Pathwell's “Lizzy,” and calling out the cookbook and diary he has retained. The shorter Chapter 1 exchange preserves the progression: physical rescue does **not** purchase Elizabeth's trust. Later independent choices in the book remain hers.
