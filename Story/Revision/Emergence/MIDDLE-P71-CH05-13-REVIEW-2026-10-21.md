## Findings

### F1 — Ch7's last line states what the scene just showed

**Text:** “Elizabeth drove. The dagger pressed against the back of her waistband. Her hands were steady on the wheel. The headlights found a reflector, then a mile marker, then a long empty stretch of road with nothing coming the other way.” and then, alone: “For the first time since the apartment, nobody was telling Elizabeth where to go.”

**Problem:** The paragraph before already shows it: steady hands, an empty road, the brothers silent after “She's right,”. The last line explains it. It's also slightly off: forty lines earlier Pathwell did tell her what to do (“"Drive," Pathwell said.”), and the scene's argument is that she's driving but not being told anything. In R54 the author cut two closers of the same kind from Ch16 and Ch17.

**Counterargument:** The line marks a turning point. Ch7 is where Elizabeth stops being taken along, and a reader skimming might miss that without it.

**Why the cut still wins:** Ch7's whole last page is that turn, said aloud by her (“"The person driving the car."”, “"Whose decision was that?"”) and agreed to by Stansbury (“"She's right," he said.”). The road image carries what's left.

**Severity:** low to moderate. **Candidate:** B1, cut the last line. **Lean:** take it.

### F2 — Ch12: “For most of the night,” is the wrong night

**Text:** “For most of the night, the diary had been one of the few things that was unequivocally hers. Pathwell had taken it. She'd followed partly because he had it. The Space Between had given it back”

**Problem:** It's Saturday dawn at Camp. Pathwell took the diary on Thursday night (Ch1) and the Space Between gave it back that same night (Ch3). The night that has just ended, the crash, the diner, the museum, is one she spent with the diary in her pocket. So “the night” points the reader at the wrong one, and “most of” has nothing to measure.

**Candidate:** B2 cuts the phrase: `The diary had been one of the few things that was unequivocally hers.` The next sentences give the history, so no time marker is needed.

**Severity:** low (a careful reader's snag, not a plot problem). **Lean:** take it.

### F3 — The diner sign (open since P34)

**Text:** “The diner sat on the far side of a gravel lot under a yellow sign that said DINER 24/7.”

The author's draft spells it "DINNER 24/7", and the narrator-register page quotes his version. P34 asked on October 5 whether the misspelling was deliberate; there's no answer on record.

- **A (now): DINER.** Correct; the sign disappears into the scene.
- **Q1: DINNER.** If it was his joke, it's the kind of small roadside wrongness the book likes (the Ch5 neon “advertising a beer they no longer sold”). It should stay unexplained; no one in the scene should remark on it.

**No lean.** It's the author's spelling either way. Q1 is a one-word patch if he wants his.

### F4 — Ch13: “cooking wagon” twice in one list

**Text:** “a woman shaking flour from a cloth near the cooking wagon, a dog asleep under the cooking wagon.”

**Candidate:** B3 cuts the first location: `a woman shaking flour from a cloth, a dog asleep under the cooking wagon.` Nothing else changes.

**Severity:** low. **Lean:** take it.

## Considered and kept

These were read closely and are left alone. Each is recorded so the next reviewer doesn't raise it again without new evidence.

- **Ch5: “She heard Stansbury's voice in the back of her head. He lies, you know.” / “Pathwell looked at her, perplexed. "I don't lie."”** On a cold read, Pathwell seems to answer a thought she didn't say. The October 8 panel didn't read it that way. One target reader laughed at it, another called it "a good plant" and said they'd "hold both of them to it". He answers her face, not her mind, and Ch18's confession pays the plant off. Keep.
- **Ch8: Shade names himself on the road (W16).** Kept on the reviser's lean in R55; the author can still overrule it. Not re-opened.
- **Ch10: Pathwell says “Camp” first.** P34 flagged it because the lock has Elizabeth choosing Camp. The text has him say it as a deferral (“"Camp," he said.”), she refuses it (“"Not later. Not at Camp. Now."”), and later she chooses it for her own reason (“Elizabeth said, "Camp."”, then “"Because Mama Baga doesn't belong to either of them.”). The choice is hers, which is what the lock is about. Keep; the author can still rule on the order.
- **Ch10: “Leo” and “big cat” (L15).** Still the author's open item. Nothing in Ch5–13 pays them, and no line is proposed.
- **Ch11: “Elizabeth knew plenty about the war. She had never taken the time to see what it did to people.”** Slightly explains the cannon tableau, but it's her own reaction and sets up the Vale letters. Watch only.
- **Ch12: the diary's “missing meetings”.** She can't have written about Friday's missed meeting, but the list is about her life before, and missed meetings fit it. Ledger L1 is unaffected. Keep.
- **Ch13: Shade's hand opens eight times.** Each time is stronger (closed against his thigh, opened at the table, the onion sack dropped, both hands, the cards scattered, held open toward the seam). It's the chapter's clock, not repetition. P38 already did the September "tighten the middle" work. Keep.
- **Fragment stacks:** “Not now. Not here. Not necessarily not her.” (Ch6) and “Not cruel. Worse. Useful.” (Ch7). Both are her thinking, and both are earned by the moment. Watch for a third in the same chapters, not a cut.
- **The shared “"Fair."”:** five uses book-wide (Ch2, Ch8, Ch9 twice, Ch16). It's on the known-weaknesses list. No single use is wrong; watch it in any later pass rather than patch it here.
- **The Ch5 second-person town aside** (“You would be forgiven for not stopping in the town as you drove through”). The panel split on it, and it's from the author's own draft. Keep.
