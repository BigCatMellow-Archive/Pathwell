## Part I — chapter-by-chapter discovery log

### Act opening — Chapters 1–4

**01 — A rescuer enters by violating ordinary boundaries.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_01.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Pathwell attended Elizabeth's welcome party, lingered after guests left, reads her diary and takes Nana's cookbook; a cleanup thing is at the door *before* he works the sugar-cookie page; he calls her apartment “marked” and leaves holding her things. The final “Ready?” / “No.” / “Perfect.” occurs when **he** grabs her hand and leads.
- **CONNECTION:** The question of whose danger this is exists before any revealed Shade lore. More importantly, Pathwell's apparently generous action begins with possession of things that belong to Elizabeth. Its final three-line callback is not just symmetry: Ch18 transfers who asks and who leads.
- **TEST / resistance:** Why *this party, these boxes, now* is partly but not fully observable on the page; do not manufacture a chosen-one explanation, remove the crash hook, or replace the author-approved early ambiguity without a controlled test. See INS-0009.

**02 — Choice offered while property and orientation are withheld.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_02.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Her meeting, dry cleaning and repaired life matter to Elizabeth while Pathwell prioritizes dumplings. The laundry ticket disappears. He declares “The choice is yours” and walks away **still holding her books**. “Lizzy” triggers Nana's sensory echo and makes her run after him.
- **CONNECTION:** A nominally free choice can be practically constrained. Ch17 later restores ordinary errands and a small domestic choice about Nana's spoon; Ch18's invitation does not require either physical pressure or theft.
- **TEST:** Elizabeth follows for concrete property, not simply fascination. A reader should be able to say that without author commentary.

**03 — Futures are spent; objects are not just receipts.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_03.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** In the Space Between, strangers' feelings press against Elizabeth; she collapses and wakes to coffee, shopkeeper and cat. Pathwell buys Camp supplies by pruning his own remaining possibilities. The shopkeeper returns Nana's cookbook and diary because “These were never his to spend.”
- **CONNECTION:** The book explicitly establishes an ownership limit to the commerce that Pathwell regularly circumvents *outside* the shop. Ch14 is his worst attempt to settle another person's identity; Ch18's put-back can be read against this unspent purchase. The shopkeeper enforces a rule Pathwell has not internalized.
- **TEST:** A new welcome-party flashback at the collapse is a separately author-directed **proposal**, not an approved memory projection; compare to unchanged Ch3 as in the existing [Ch3 experiment plan](../Chapter-03-Party-Flashback-THINK-PLAN-2026-10-14.md). Magical price remains hard to weigh for some prior readers.

**04 — Consent costs her something; belonging does not require being special.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_04.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Camp is lived rather than explained: turnip boy, archive, Olan/Hess disagreement, everyday repairs, Mama Baga's care and clothing. Elizabeth gives the *entire* cookbook so a healer can spend a page on a stranger, then consciously entrusts the rest to Camp. The archivist treats the donation as custody and provenance.
- **CONNECTION:** Ch12 reverses the position (Elizabeth gives her diary too) and Ch15 makes her leave both with ordinary fire threatening them. The objects are not expendable symbols: first she gets to experience Camp holding them.
- **COUNTER:** “That was the choice” works as a signature here, but Ch15 echoes its exact formulation. Prior reader panel split between admiration and over-explanation: test the second occurrence before cutting either (R33 protects Ch4).
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0005 and INS-0008.

### Act development — Chapters 5–9

**05 — A useful tool does not confer the right to deploy it.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_05.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Stansbury's school work makes enchanted foam weapons from residual energy, and he explains both their limits and where they come from; Elizabeth takes a small separation dagger. The bar trip begins through food, family teasing and increasingly unequal knowledge.
- **CONNECTION:** Stansbury and Pathwell both convert possibility into a useful result, but Stansbury's work feeds tools whose later use is a choice. The Ch7 dagger frees Pathwell; the Ch15 dagger is consciously not used when Milo needs a two-armed rescue. The tool's **nonuse** can be meaningful without denying it ever works.
- **COUNTER:** Stansbury is not inherently morally superior; his method and the children's use of labor deserve the separate world-rules caveats already in canon. This does not authorize a school subplot.

**06 — The first 'good time' and the first knowingly excluded conversation.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_06.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Elizabeth refuses Pathwell's appletini and orders ginger ale; the bar has a real interval of ease. Pathwell recognizes the draw and mirror wrongness and tells Stansbury “Later,” not Elizabeth. Stansbury makes a crucial distinction between prudence and virtue. The wet thud outside closes the chapter.
- **CONNECTION:** Agency progresses through mundane preference before the extreme crash, and later through listening to a choice at the healing wagon and cookbook. Ch16–17's restraint is credible only against this older habitual withholding.
- **TEST:** A worried reader may reasonably ask why the brothers cannot tell her enough *now*; repeated withholding in Ch7–8 risks sounding like machinery moving her to the ditch.

**07 — She saves the person who is still withholding the truth.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_07.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** A blob goes for Pathwell, not Elizabeth; she cuts multiple seams, pulls him free physically, then drives while the brothers continue a private diagnostic exchange. She says that she has become “the driver” whenever an explanation is due.
- **CONNECTION:** Her later refusal to use the same weapon on Shade is a deliberate tradeoff, **not** failure to remember an easy solution. Her rescue of Pathwell does not create a debt requiring her to rescue him again or save everyone else.
- **COUNTER:** Ch7 demonstrates that Stansbury had anti-blob means *on that day*. It does **not** prove a baton or the school box is at Camp in Ch15. P55's available-help question remains genuinely open.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0006.

**08 — Agency can be decisive and dangerous.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_08.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** “Elizabeth chose the ditch”; she intentionally crashes the Cadillac after another refusal to answer her. She meets Shade and elects the back seat of his sedan, with the dagger still with her.
- **CONNECTION:** The book does not argue that freely chosen actions must be wise. The destruction of Stansbury's car is real harm, unlike the later successful Milo rescue, yet both require Elizabeth to own their consequences. Ch16's admonition to Pathwell about responsibility would ring false if her own choices were treated as consequence-free.
- **COUNTER:** This is not a model of sensible road safety or magically guaranteed control; don't recast it as a flawless emancipation beat.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0006.

**09 — Shade's borrowed memories diverge from his own wants.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_09.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** At the diner Shade remembers bits of a life he never lived, has different habits and choices from Pathwell, and says he did not know Elizabeth existed. The central reversal is “you were never the point.” He refuses to supply Pathwell's moral confession: “He knows what he did. Ask him.”
- **CONNECTION:** In Ch13 Shade learns to play Hearts by reading people, **not** by replaying memories; likes pockets and bad coffee, and chooses to stay. The distinction between origin and experience is performed through activities before Camp explicitly records his personhood in Ch16.
- **TEST:** His stated intention to make Pathwell answer risks eclipsing any independent life until Ch13. The recent nonconsensual death increases the stakes of this contrast.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0007.

### Reversal and cost — Chapters 10–13

**10 — The confession about pruning reveals the moral method, not every fact.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_10.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Stansbury consents to spending a copied-and-kept Ellison letter; a terrible shared coffee memory and used grocery lists locate Elizabeth. She interrogates Pathwell in the stopped van. Someone asked for help; no one asked to be pruned. He chose the method, and that failure produced Shade. Elizabeth chooses Camp.
- **CONNECTION:** The book distinguishes **what is known** (choice without delegated authority) from what remains withheld by author lock (original person's name/problem). The tracking magic works without reading Elizabeth's mind or controlling her direction. This leads into Ch14's violation even after multiple explicit refusals.
- **COUNTER:** Some older reader-panel concerns about the unidentified original requester remain; naming them would alter a deliberate lock and needs author direction, not automatic worldbuilding.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0004, INS-0009.

**11 — 'The information' can be copied, but loss cannot.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_11.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Elizabeth chooses Camp, but Pathwell overrides her “No” in the museum and spends another record, wrecking irreplaceable letters, ledgers and her shoulder. Stansbury can preserve historical information in scans, yet “the information” is not identical to the original page. Elizabeth passes through the graffiti threshold without Pathwell's permission.
- **CONNECTION:** Camp's archive destruction later repeats the **cost** of treating records as fungible, but this time Elizabeth herself chooses the unreplaceable child over the unreplaceable books. Ch17's facsimile and missing original make a visible third term between “fixed” and “nothing.”
- **TEST:** Avoid compressing both museum and Camp into 'Pathwell made the same mistake twice'; Chapter 11 is an imposed action framed as rescue; Chapter 14 adds explicit refusal and a forced working.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0005, INS-0008.

**12 — Keeping a record becomes an ethical relationship.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_12.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Healer requests permission to touch Elizabeth and sets her shoulder; Shade remains nearby without treating; Camp cookbook has a donor card. Elizabeth gives her diary outright but specifies “Ask me before anyone reads it”; the archivist writes “PATHWELL IS NOT DONOR.”
- **CONNECTION:** Ownership and use-rights are not the same thing. Camp learns to hold a life without appropriating it. In Ch16, writing “LIKED POCKETS” beside Shade's name becomes one answer to the violence of declaring him merely a malfunction.
- **COUNTER:** Her giving the diary is a genuine loss even before it burns, not a second gift that should cancel her claim to grieve.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0008.

**13 — He begins a future that never becomes a backstory.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_13.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Shade rejects his torn coat, chooses green jacket pockets, likes dreadful coffee, joins Hearts without remembering having played, learns from his fellow players, helps with onions, and says “I want to stay here.” The draw worsens, but he retrieves the dropped onion anyway.
- **CONNECTION:** Ch14's question “Do I remain myself?” has content: unfinished preferences and social ties. Ch15's death interrupts a life in progress; Ch16's archivist records liking pockets and Milo says Shade owes him a hand, and Ch17 makes Pathwell play Hearts without pretending he replaces him.
- **COUNTER:** A small Camp morning is evidence of real personhood, not a complete backstory or proof that Shade secretly consents to disappearance. Previously proposed extra 'days' conflict with the tight same-morning timeline; R42 blocks padding.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0007.

### Climax and aftermath — Chapters 14–18

**14 — He forces the decision despite three kinds of refusal.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_14.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Shade says no, Mama Baga says stop, Stansbury stands in the way, Elizabeth objects. Pathwell forces the contradictory frame using his own remaining future; recoil throws brass into a lantern and ordinary fire starts. A girl sees something near the trees before Milo is trapped.
- **CONNECTION:** This is not just excessive generosity. He proceeds after his expertise, authority and result have explicitly diverged. The pressure that drew Shade also draws cleanup via newly released spillage; the catastrophe is not an act of fate rescuing a stalled plot.
- **COUNTER:** A reader can dispute where everyone is standing or how quickly the shape crosses Camp, but no additional supernatural rule should be invented to make the argument symmetrical.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0004, SYN-0003 (new).

**15 — The choice not to help is different from choosing whom to save.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_15.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Shade is unexpectedly caught at the shin, struggles, calls for help and tries to pull himself free with a Camp rescuer; Milo is trapped and also calls. Elizabeth has her dagger but only one useful arm; she goes inside, frees Milo and passes both books. Shade is taken before she emerges. Stansbury's salvage earns his scar; the diary and cookbook burn in ordinary flame.
- **CONNECTION:** The revised climax makes a *distinction*, not a moral equivalence, between Elizabeth's positive rescue decision and Pathwell's calculated omission. It also establishes why the second Ch7-style dagger extraction was not obviously practical during Milo's immediate danger.
- **COUNTER / high-priority test:** With one Camp man already pulling Shade free, the concrete *additional* help Pathwell could summon is poorly specified. The story need not establish a guaranteed successful rescue, but “I could have called someone” should refer to a credible available action. See [P55](../QA-2026-10-15-Informed-Reader-Voice-Followup.md) and EXP-0005.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0004.

**16 — An inventory can acknowledge a person without settling blame.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_16.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Camp lists saved and destroyed items; writes “UNKNOWN” for losses it cannot count. Milo and Mama Baga get the archivist to record Shade's name, green jacket and liking pockets. Elizabeth refuses to become Pathwell's moral tutor; Camp assigns restitution without letting him define the value or timing. Stansbury says “Let it be” about his burn.
- **CONNECTION:** This is the strongest collision of the record economy and personhood: an archive is destroyed, yet a new entry documents someone who cannot be restored. The entry is **not a price tag** for his life.
- **COUNTER:** A chapter that verbalizes every aspect of responsibility risks a tribunal-as-theme lecture. The earlier reader panel raised this before P54; only one grief line changed in P54, so this remains worth a read, not a command to delete.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0008.

**17 — Restitution is obedience to another person's specifications.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_17.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Pathwell copies misspellings and smudges rather than improving them, buys specified cabinets, accepts The Lemon, refuses to replace Daniel Vale's absent letters with Henry's and offers the latter only as separate provenance. Elizabeth writes her witness statement and has her own domestic life and walks. Milo plays Hearts with Pathwell; the empty exhibit case stays empty.
- **CONNECTION:** Ch3's insistence on separating ownership from value becomes Ch17's practice of repair under someone else's authority. This is **partial evidence**, not sufficient proof that he would have called for Shade in Ch15. Ch18's confession should complicate, not erase, these improvements.
- **COUNTER:** The brisk montage risks convincing readers Pathwell has already “earned” forgiveness before they know the concealed omission; high-priority emotional test in EXP-0006.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0004, INS-0005.

**18 — The final invitation changes who owns the next step.** [Source](../../Chapters/Chapter_18.txt)  
- **ON PAGE:** Elizabeth arranges a safe return visit to the Space Between, crosses first, browses without supervision. Pathwell sees another family's cookbook and puts it back rather than using the ledger. He confesses that he knowingly declined to call for help for Shade; he rejects Elizabeth's proposed equivalence (“That's not what I did”). She spends another hour browsing, asks about routes, chooses the problematic geese route, and **asks if he wants to come**. This time **she** asks “Are you ready?” and **he** replies “No.”
- **CONNECTION:** The ch1–18 bookends transform the same lines from a man moving a frightened woman to a woman inviting a man who may refuse. Their relationship does not return to its previous power arrangement.
- **COUNTER / high-priority test:** Invitation may seem like absolution because of the buoyant banter after a painful new fact; the book deliberately avoids an explicit pardon. Test comprehension before inserting a speech or extra ordeal.
- **INSIGHT ROUTE:** INS-0010 and EXP-0006.
