## Causal read of the whole opening

1. **Ordinary cause of waking:** An overturned stack of sweaters wakes Elizabeth. Her welcome-party leftovers locate the scene in her own apartment; her nine o'clock meeting and fatigue ground her immediate interests. Nothing in the current opening makes her *know* about an adjacent magical world.
2. **Stranger at the party:** Pathwell was a guest and laughed at her landlord joke. She assumed a coworker had brought him; she noticed him inspecting old notebooks. That is an early *behavioral* clue to his habit of scavenging emotionally charged writing, not a reveal that Elizabeth herself is a magical target. **Still inferred:** Exactly how he first sensed the charge at her apartment. No new sensing powers are spelled out.
3. **The first wet blow:** Pathwell asks “Friend of yours?”; she actually opens the chained door to check whether someone needs help. Dark wet material sags into the gap; he slams and bolts the door. His reaction now visibly takes place before he seeks the cookbook. **Reader risk:** She opens the door despite the obviously anomalous sound, rationalized only by “Someone might have fallen in the hall.” The chain limits the risk, but a cold reader may still question the decision.
4. **Why Nana's cookbook:** Pathwell runs to her boxes and asks for her old cookbook. He selects a favorite recipe rather than an abstract charge value. His casual ownership breach is his fault, not evidence that she volunteered the page.
5. **First magic:** After her Nana's recipe is identified, gold moves across the precise irregular strokes of Nana's ink. Elizabeth leans in before comprehending. The door cracks and the creature forces ooze through; her stomach reacts. **The touchstone remains the author's sensory sugar-cookie transport**, which happens only after she pulls the book and the activated page remains with Pathwell.
6. **Waste and defense:** Her pull destabilizes an already-committed spell, leaving gold threads on the carpet. The blob consumes that loose, unclaimed casting waste. It then lunges toward the continuing bright page in Pathwell's hand. In P61, he raises the **existing** page as a barrier; the blob strikes it, white light flashes, and the impact sings. **This is a staging choice for this instance**; no general ability to fire magical energy is established.
7. **Retreat:** Elizabeth sees the creature recoil, runs sick to the bathroom, hides while Pathwell is knocked down, and hears a physical struggle. The room is covered in residue after it is “mostly gone.” Pathwell's later explanation of the apartment as “marked” is a story he tells from an erroneous theory, not a literal permanent curse established by the narrator.
8. **Exit and book custody:** Pathwell has the diary and cookbook when she cracks the bathroom door and asks what happened. He steps into the empty entrance to check outside, then insists they leave; her reluctance is visible in repeated dialogue. A new distant thud and the demand for shoes precede the last “Ready?” / “No.” / “Perfect.” Chapter 2 begins immediately with Elizabeth struggling to keep up on the stairs, still asking about her possessions; no intervening travel or magical jump is needed.

## What demonstrably improved since the earlier panel

- The reader can see **two unrelated questions** forming before explanation: Pathwell's interest in old notes and **the blob already outside** before any spell is cast. This does not itself verify that readers infer the right causes by Ch3/Ch9.
- Elizabeth makes an earlier independent investigative choice with the chain, then a personal intervention to save Nana's cookbook. This does not make her magically gifted or determine the creature's target.
- The first glow is distinctively Nana's handwritten ink, not generic gold effects. The sugar-cookie memory's text remains author-authored and untouched.
- The creature's first magical contact is now stageable: residual threads are consumed, a still-active page remains, and the blob collides with it.
- Simple prose blockers repaired: a falling box now has an actor, the duplicate impact-note is gone, the low-tide door-breach sentence is grammatical, and the bathroom exit is explicitly an exit rather than a second initial opening.

## Stage/object regression checks

- **Ch1:** front-door material first seen under chain; same door bows, cracks, then collapses. The creature is in the room **after** the activated page is interrupted, not before. The cookbook is under Pathwell's hand/page; she pulls the spine away; the page cannot be saved. The two sources of cleanup signal (ambiguous Pathwell/Shade, interrupted casting waste) are not confused.
- **Ch4:** the cookbook is later returned to Elizabeth by a Camp runner with **one page gone**, before she elects to give the whole remaining book for healing; no on-page loss is reversed by Ch1.
- **Ch5:** Stansbury questions why the creature was already at *her* door; Pathwell's “Her door” is challenged. Do not reveal the later correct target in Chapter 1.
- **Ch7:** the creature turns toward Pathwell directly, not Elizabeth; the later story can finally revise Pathwell's original mistaken interpretation.
- **Ch18:** the book's final “Perfect” follows Elizabeth's independent invitation/decision; keep the author-locked exact last exchange.
