## What the author asked us to protect

1. **Genuine personal style:** lively, associative, conversational narration; body language that *means* something, not a camera following every nod; affectionate ordinary detail, purposeful personification, wrong-priority humor when it suits the scene.
2. **The real improvements already made:** causal clarity, clear position of bodies/objects, emotional choices, point of view, accepted character and plot decisions, deliberate ambiguity, and no redundant back-and-forth blocking.
3. **The book's memory:** callbacks and recurring experiences, meaningful objects, later inversions, and source/payoff timing. The [Promise Ledger](../../Promise-Ledger.md) owns these; the earlier [22-link first-pass audit](FORESHADOWING-CALLBACK-AUDIT-2026-10-26.md) crosswalks them.
4. **No unnecessary new foreshadowing.** The author explicitly argued that useful planting **works backward from the payoff**, and that this character/choice-driven story **does not need much conventional foreshadowing**. The appropriate question is *"Does the later scene need groundwork the reader currently lacks?"* not *"What cool clue could we hide here?"* Do not manufacture future significance for lived-in details, or announce a reprise.
5. **Use scene-dependent restraint.** Recovering an expressive narrator does not mean adding equal amounts of witty commentary to a rescue, death, apology or invitation.

## Review rule for future author selections

Check the affected paragraph *with adjacent paragraphs* and the corresponding **earlier plant or later payoff**, then compare against the **canonical** text, not just V1. Only author-picked alternatives graduate into the normal EXP/ruling workflow. If canon already achieves the effect, **keep canon**, even when a new sentence is agreeable in isolation. If a later moment truly fails for lack of preparation, repair *backward* from that moment to the lightest earlier planting; otherwise do not add foreshadowing.
