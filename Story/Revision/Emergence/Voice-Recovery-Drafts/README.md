## Aim

**Recover the author's experience of telling the story without surrendering the actual progress of the revision.** The first draft showed lively associative narration, figurative actions that make objects feel involved, funny undercutting priorities, compassion for ordinary things, and conversational rhythms. Some early over-description and repeated physical choreography genuinely needed cutting. The fix is to keep the expressive effects while preserving causal clarity, scene direction, and the author's authoritative rulings.

**Never make every chapter sound equally jokey.** When it gets dark, bodily movement and sentence rhythm can carry the same signature voice without quips. Prose must adapt to the situation rather than meet a uniform metaphor-per-paragraph quota.

**A novel remembers what it says.** A line that looks removable in isolation may foreshadow a threat, explain who has a book, recur as a phrase, define a character's default gesture, or set up a deliberately altered return fifteen chapters later. Before revising for voice, compare both earlier **plants** and later **payoffs**. The [canonical Promise Ledger](../../Promise-Ledger.md) owns these relationships; the [18-chapter draft callback audit](FORESHADOWING-CALLBACK-AUDIT-2026-10-26.md) checks how this series treats them. **Do not add new foreshadowing just to make prose feel important, or explain echoes the reader can recognize.**

## Continuity and callback gate — required for every author-selected candidate

Before replacing any sentence with recovered voice, identify the earlier **plant**, any changed **recurrence**, and any later **payoff**. Not all details are promises: label an incidental lived-in observation as **texture** rather than inventing a payoff. Check object custody, cause/time order, information each character could know, and whose decision the later scene means to honor. The [draft-by-draft crosswalk](FORESHADOWING-CALLBACK-AUDIT-2026-10-26.md) lists 22 high-value connections and risk cases. Its status is **initial editorial evidence**, not a pass from an independent reader.

**Two added lines have already been withdrawn for this reason:** Ch8's unnecessary explanation before the bare crash echo, and Ch18's claim that Boots's blood tribute was unpaid even though the existing scar callback proves otherwise. Ch2's new psychological/packing particulars and Ch3's expanded inventory remain author-owned proposals, not confirmed canonical history.

## Process for review and deeper iteration

1. For each alternative, compare it with the full current chapter, its neighbors and the author's locks. The later light-touch alternatives should not be mistaken for a full sentence-by-sentence rewrite.
2. Preserve the plot, relationship information, emotional choices, world mechanics and callback anchors.
3. Restore expressive narrator / bodily dialogue selectively; avoid the AI habit of adding a polished quip to every paragraph.
4. Keep a **full, standalone chapter file**, even where a scene is left exactly as written. Do not store only a suggestion or an outline; the current alternatives all meet the full-chapter file requirement.
5. Compare line by line against canon **and the entire Promise Ledger plant/payoff chain**; flag *new* dialogue, jokes, narrator commentary, future obligations, and potential downstream continuity differences as **author-owned alternatives**. Never replace canonical prose from these drafts without explicit approval.
6. After individual review, author-selected patches belong in numbered EXP records and normal revision workflow, not a wholesale reset.
