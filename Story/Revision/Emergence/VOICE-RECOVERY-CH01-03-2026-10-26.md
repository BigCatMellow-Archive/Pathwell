## Chapter 1 — breach, cookbook, and the magic

### V01 — The entering door has an emotion
- **First draft:** "The door burst open with panic, slamed shut with desperation." ([early Chapter 1](../../Archive/Manuscript/Author_First_Draft_pathwell-work_plaintext_2026-10-26.txt))
- **Current:** "It was the crash that woke her." ([Ch1](../../Chapters/Chapter_01.txt))
- **Gain:** A concrete event and an Elizabeth-centered perception; the current party/diary setup supports later misreading and the author-selected "Reading" joke.
- **Loss:** The first draft's striking transferred emotion and a panicked kinetic entry. The newer scene starts comparatively calmly after the crash.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED on the first line, OPEN on opening *energy***. **R11** locks the exact first line. **Q-Q** already holds the author's hook concern. Don't propose restoring the opening verbatim or replacing the present first line; revisit the energy issue only after the author's paper read.
- **Crucial distinction:** "the door ... with panic" is valid figurative characterization, **not** an impossible event requiring correction.

### V02 — "All the courage a baseball bat could provide"
- **First draft:** "WIth all the courage a baseball bat could proivde, the voice spoke up from behind the unknown guest."
- **Current:** Elizabeth asks the lingering guest "What are you still doing here?" No baseball bat is introduced in the current first scene; his presence at her welcome party is the setup.
- **Gain:** More coherent recognition and diary mystery; fewer props and a cleaner Elizabeth viewpoint.
- **Loss:** Comic fear conveyed through an inanimate object acting as temporary moral support. It is a compressed character observation.
- **Disposition:** **REWORK AS VOICE EVIDENCE**, **not** a bat-restoration suggestion. A literal reinsertion would change the blocking and author-approved hook.

### V03 — The box "seasoned" the floor
- **First draft:** "The box was upended and seasoned the floor with books." This follows the comic aside about a cookbook and a Thai recipe.
- **Current:** He urgently asks for the handwritten cookbook and returns from the kitchen with Nana's book.
- **Gain:** Direct action under threat; fewer comedic detours before the blob strike.
- **Loss:** Culinary verb imported into the nonculinary mess, without any explanation; narrator has fun with the scene's immediate subject.
- **Disposition:** **REWORK AS AN INSTINCT**, not a request to restore the old cookbook search. **Strong** evidence for unexpected everyday verbs; **low** confidence that this exact beat would help the current staging.

### V04 — "Sunshine directed right at her stomach"
- **First draft:** "Sunshine directed right at her stomach." Elizabeth is engulfed in warmth, tastes her grandmother's sugar cookies and cries.
- **Intermediate author's Chapter 1 draft:** the experience condenses to smell → taste → Nana's voice and a childhood baking memory, in some versions with "The impact sang."
- **Current:** "Everything went white, and a scent hit her; buttery and sweet" and the twelve-year-old memory precede "Her body moved back before she did".
- **Gain:** Emotional specificity, Nana's role, body-first reaction; the current figure **still crosses sensory categories**.
- **Loss:** The absurdly physical abdominal sunshine image itself.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED / LEAVE ALONE**. This is evidence that voice recovery isn't a rule to put back every pretty phrase. The later version accomplishes the scene's emotional and plot work more exactly.

### V05 — A stench "splattered" as figurative violence
- **Author pre-August benchmark:** "Recoiling, the blob rippled, the stench of wet-rot permeating the room."
- **Current (author's P78 markup):** "The blob rippled and recoiled, the stench of wet-rot splattered across the room."
- **Gain:** The verb makes the invasion sudden and disgusting, echoing the creature's material violence; it can be felt without being physically literal.
- **Potential cost:** In a crowded action passage, a reader might momentarily interpret "splattered" as literal ooze; test with actual nearby sentences before assuming that is a defect.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED**, with an author-owned taste question, **not** a grammatical or logic correction. A P79 line-note question said "A smell doesn't splatter"; literal impossibility is **insufficient** grounds to alter it. This specific image arose in the author's latest rewrite, not his first draft.

### V06 — Joke immediately after wreckage
- **First draft:** "But right now, we need to to someplace else, preferably somewhere with a front door."
- **Current:** Pathwell calls her house "marked" (a bluff under R27), asks for shoes, and gets them moving before the next thud: "Shoes. Now. I did say please."
- **Gain:** Clear momentum, stronger character manipulation, bluff is planted as the engine of departure, and the scene retains a dry incongruent line.
- **Loss:** A small mundane practical joke about the lack of a front door; not necessarily a loss of the *kind* of humor.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED**. The present beat **already** preserves wrong-detail humor. The old joke is not worth breaking the now-established lie and urgency.

## Chapter 2 — restaurant, food and choice

### V07 — "Perfectly crappy" **survived**
- **First draft:** Pathwell calls the food "perfectly crappy Chinese food" and insists that finding food this good is difficult.
- **Current:** "These things are perfect. Perfectly crappy. They aren't trying to be anything they're not. You have no idea how hard it is to find that kind of quality."
- **Gain:** Current speech is more precise about his admiration of unpretentious ordinariness, while still using the deliberately paradoxical joke.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED — a success story.** A first draft's distinctive wrong-detail reaction need not be copied verbatim to survive; it can be sharpened. It would be inaccurate to list this as lost.

### V08 — From noodle-table discussion to the dry-cleaning ticket and tears
- **First draft Chapter 2:** Begins already at a dim-sum table, then her demand to go home and Pathwell's evasion. Chapter 1 also has an earlier hot-dog interlude; those beats were reorganized in later work.
- **Current Chapter 2:** Starts with flight from the building and her mixing spoon left behind; the dry-cleaning ticket leaves her with something small to lose, followed by unforced crying in the street-side restaurant.
- **Gain:** Elizabeth's ordinary obligations and loss become dramatic causes; Pathwell's offer of one dumpling is an unmistakably *wrong* response to distress.
- **Loss:** A more extended old conversation, not necessarily greater emotional impact.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED.** This is meaningful structural/emotional improvement, not the flattening type of edit the audit is looking to reverse. Preserve R35's unresolved meeting logistics and the author's deliberate removal of the explicit funeral line.

### V09 — Pathwell's manipulative wrong-answer logic
- **First draft:** Asked whether she is being kidnapped, he replies "why would I kidnap you? You came willingly." It provokes her objection that he broke into her apartment and dragged her out.
- **Current:** The encounter becomes "The choice is yours, Lizzy," while he walks off after leaving her the last dumpling. The choice is materially uncomfortable for Elizabeth rather than merely a semantic debate.
- **Gain:** Behavior and consequence instead of additional argument; his irresponsibility stays perceptible.
- **Loss:** The original's brazen comic legalism.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED**, with the old line retained as evidence of his deflecting voice. Reinserting it would risk making the scene repetitive and would reopen a plot arrangement that has changed.

## Chapter 3 — the bookstore and its cost

### V10 — The immense catalogue of human writings
- **First draft:** "Recipes passed from hand to hand and love letters unfisinehd. Sketchbooks and songbooks, full of songs never sung." After the long list: "These are not just paper, and ink, they are a pulse, a breath, they. are. proof."
- **Current:** "Books. Floor to ceiling, wall to wall ..." then "Not books — lives," followed by compact journals, letters and recipes. The shelves induce physical emotion in Elizabeth.
- **Gain:** Tighter pacing, stronger POV, the *magic actually acts on Elizabeth* rather than the narrator giving a world-philosophy speech; shorter quotation does not stop the action.
- **Loss:** Accumulation as a rhythmic device, social breadth of receipts, wills, petitions, diaries, drawings, music, and a storyteller's affectionate delight in ordinary records. "Not books — lives" is efficient but less musically exuberant.
- **Disposition:** **REWORK / FLAG**. **Highest-priority voice-recovery question in the first act**. Look for a *small, author-written* way to preserve the sweep of ordinary lives without restoring the whole catalog or saying the novel's thesis aloud. No replacement text is selected.

### V11 — Original ugly coffee, refined rather than removed
- **First draft:** "It tasted of reused filters, and dirty pots playing host to a dollar store bean." The past returns through a bad cup.
- **Current:** "It was awful. Reused filters, dirty pots, dollar-store beans. It tasted like late nights spent studying. Like the past."
- **Gain:** Keeps the specific mediocre flavor, ties it to her earlier life, and sustains the magic's emotional economy.
- **Loss:** The longer comic syntax "playing host" has been compressed.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED**. The basic distinctive image and its emotional function remain, so this is not good evidence of an erased voice.

### V12 — "The Space Between" lore presentation
- **First draft:** "Welcome to The Space Between. That is, what we are, where we are, who we are, and when we are." The shopkeeper delivers the line reluctantly, like a tired showman; Pathwell says "Yes! I love it when he says that."
- **Current:** Pathwell introduces it as a bookstore "commonly referred to as the Space Between." The shopkeeper guards Elizabeth's property and negotiates payment.
- **Gain:** More purposeful shopkeeper, stronger transactional stakes and Elizabeth's ownership concern.
- **Loss:** Shopkeeper's canned patter and Pathwell's childish enthusiasm for it; a small worldbuilding-as-performance pleasure that could charm a reader.
- **Disposition:** **REWORK / FLAG**, medium confidence that *some* expression of practiced bookstore ritual might enrich the scene, low confidence that these exact lines belong in the now more consequential exchange. Avoid inventing more lore to justify the ritual.

### V13 — Boots as a blood god **survived**
- **First draft:** The shopkeeper calls the orange cat a "blood god"; "We pay him in mice."
- **Current:** "He's the full-time guardian and part-time blood god." Shopkeeper: "We pay him in mice."
- **Gain:** A stronger formulation using the language of employment to domesticate a supernatural threat. The incongruity survives.
- **Disposition:** **KEEP REVISED.** Explicit example of successful preservation; no reason to recover the older wording.

## Cross-cutting pattern (provisional)

The most promising losses are **narrator vocabulary and rhythm** (V01–V03) and **the human breadth of the bookstore catalogue** (V10), *not* the larger early-story blocking or magic exposition. Two conspicuous comic ideas (perfectly crappy food and Boots's mice) demonstrably **survive and improve**; the current first-magic scene is a powerful example of desirable rewrite. Do not claim an across-the-board depletion of voice.

The first draft also has real defects: protracted explanation of why Elizabeth thinks the intruder may be preparing a meal; repeated descriptions of action in long, unpunctuated blocks; many proofing errors; overwritten spells; an early premise of future-Pathwell attempting to kill himself that changed. The audit must not conflate free figurative language with these fixable problems.
