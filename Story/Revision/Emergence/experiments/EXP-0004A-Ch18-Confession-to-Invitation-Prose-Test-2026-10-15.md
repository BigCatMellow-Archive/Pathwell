## Trial passage — full continuous segment (Elizabeth-limited)

She drank half a cup of terrible coffee because she wanted to know whether it was still as bad.

It was. Shade would have liked it.

Pathwell refused his.

“Coward,” Elizabeth said.

“Evidence-based decision making.”

“You've changed.”

“Do not spread that around.”

He turned the refused cup a quarter turn on the counter and left it there.

“I kept thinking resemblance was enough,” he said.

Elizabeth waited. It had taken him a long time to arrive at that sentence.

“It was my fault.”

She looked at him. He was watching the cup he'd refused.

“The working,” she said.

“Yes. And after.”

Pathwell's hand moved toward the cup, stopped, and stayed on the counter.

“I saw him trying to get free. He was shouting. I could see what the thing was doing to him.”

Elizabeth thought of the green jacket. She had seen the darkness around his leg, his face when he looked at her. She had gone through the archive door before she could know what happened next.

“I thought if it took him, it would be finished,” Pathwell said. “The draw. Everything I'd been trying to settle.”

Elizabeth had been holding the shopkeeper's coffee. She put the cup down.

“I could have called someone,” Pathwell said. “I didn't.”

“Because you thought it would work?”

“Yes.”

The shopkeeper was at the far end of the counter, writing something into the ledger. Elizabeth couldn't tell if he'd heard. She found she didn't want to ask.

“I didn't help him either,” she said. “He was calling out. I saw him, and then Milo shouted from inside.”

Pathwell looked at her for the first time since he'd begun.

“I went to Milo.”

“You went to save Milo.”

“Yes.”

“That's not what I did.”

He wasn't arguing with her. That made it harder to find anything to say.

The shopkeeper turned a page in his ledger. His pencil made a dry sound along the paper.

Elizabeth went down the nearest aisle. She passed the postcards and the bird guide and returned by the long way around the shop. She had no particular reason to read another book.

When she came back, Pathwell was where she'd left him. He had not touched the coffee.

“How do I get out?” she asked the shopkeeper.

“Where?”

Elizabeth considered the question.

The first time, she had been offered the sidewalk, and had chosen a forest without knowing what it was. Now home was available. Camp was available. The museum was available through the proper route, though she had no desire to visit it after hours again. Her apartment would still be there tomorrow. Work would still contain email. The plant that survived would probably continue requiring water in a manner she considered manipulative.

Elizabeth looked at Pathwell. “What routes can you actually get us back from?”

Pathwell blinked. “That is a surprisingly responsible version of the question.”

“I contain multitudes,” Elizabeth said.

He considered. “From here? Four without making the Shopkeeper invent anything unpleasant.”

“Five,” the shopkeeper said.

Pathwell looked at him. “The fifth is seasonal.”

“Season began yesterday.”

Pathwell frowned. “I hate when you know things about your own shop.”

“Occupationally.”

Elizabeth leaned against the counter. “Give me the five.”

Pathwell did. She listened.

The first sounded useful. The second sounded beautiful and crowded. The third involved a ferry schedule and therefore too much planning. The fourth Pathwell described in great detail, which made Elizabeth suspicious. The fifth he described badly.

“What's wrong with that one?” she asked.

“Nothing.”

“You made a face.”

“There are geese.”

“Wild geese?”

“Technically.”

“What does technically mean?”

“They have learned commerce.”

Elizabeth looked at the shopkeeper. He kept writing.

“Is that true?”

“They steal buttons.”

Elizabeth looked back at Pathwell. “That one.”

“Of course.”

“Problem?”

Pathwell's old answer almost arrived. She saw it. Then he smiled. “No.”

Elizabeth turned to the shopkeeper. “Can we use it?”

“Yes.”

“Both of us?”

“If he is coming.”

Elizabeth looked at Pathwell.

She had asked him to bring her here. That didn't make the next step his.

“Do you want to come?” she asked.

Pathwell glanced toward the recipe shelf and then back at her.

“Yes.”

Elizabeth nodded. “Then come with me.”

The shopkeeper closed the ledger.

A narrow door appeared beside the shelf of journals. Brass handle. Old wood. A thin line of cold evening air underneath.

Something honked on the other side.

Pathwell's face tightened. “They remember me.”

Elizabeth put her hand on the brass handle. “Good.”

He came to stand beside her.

“Are you ready?”

“No.”

“Perfect.”

## Immediate self-review — do not mistake this for an independent reader result

**Functional beats delivered:** later specific confession rather than vague regret; Pathwell states that convenience influenced his refusal to call; Elizabeth relates rather than comforts; he distinguishes Milo's rescue from his own omission; she has room to move away; she returns and *chooses* whether to invite him; no explicit forgiveness and no speech about the theme. Last three lines unchanged.

**Reader-risk still present:** even with Elizabeth walking an aisle, the revelation of conscious nonintervention is severe enough that the ensuing “commercial geese” exchange may feel emotionally premature. The restored invitation is not necessarily *earned* merely because an author instructed us to retain it. A real cold-reader check on full Ch15–18 is needed.

**Defects to investigate:**
1. **Early knowledge:** Elizabeth's recollection of Shade's face is permissible from proposed shared glance, but the confession must not claim a detailed view of the engulfment she did not witness. It doesn't.
2. **Shame motive:** Pathwell's refusal to use euphemism is convincing; “Because you thought it would work?” risks compressing motive into a slightly too explanatory Q&A. Test against a shorter variant.
3. **Voice:** “He wasn't arguing with her. That made it harder to find anything to say” might be a narration-evaluates-dialogue beat; remove if it feels like a reviewer's annotation.
4. **Spatial continuity:** Shopkeeper remains at his ledger while they speak, and Elizabeth's aisle circuit doesn't create a new scene. Whether he overhears is deliberately Elizabeth-unknown, but withholding this could feel contrived if the counter is small.
5. **Existing ending paragraph trimmed:** The original after Pathwell's “Yes” says “He said it freely: no debt, no Camp order, no blob…” Immediately after the detailed confession, **“no blob” reads untrue to the emotional moment**. Trial cuts that explanatory claim while preserving his yes and the invitation.
6. **Style:** Experiment intentionally retains the existing route conversation nearly verbatim to test whether the tonal shift itself works. Do not treat preserved dialogue as evidence that the revised version is seamless.

**Verdict:** A physically plausible controlled draft **with a significant unresolved tonal and character-consequence risk**. It should not be promoted merely because the specified beats are present.
