# EXP-0001C — Scene test B: explicit break to Shade

**Date:** 2026-10-15
**Status:** NONCANONICAL PROSE TEST / NOT APPROVED
**Parent:** [EXP-0001A timeline](EXP-0001A-Ch14-Ch15-Timeline-and-Blocking-2026-10-15.md)
**Question:** Does a deliberate scene break earn access to Shade's realization without converting the ending into a speech or making him responsible for Pathwell's action?

> This is an experimental passage, NOT new Chapter 15. It shares A's causal setup: failed working; lantern fire; trapped Milo; creature arriving across the resisting boundary. It temporarily switches away from Elizabeth *after her choice* and does not determine Q54 (stillness vs continued help) or Q55 (whether Pathwell witnesses).

---

The first flame climbed the archive door before Elizabeth had finished understanding the noise of the broken working.

Pathwell lay by the ruined brass frame. The pale loose threads around him did not go out when the fire caught. Elizabeth didn't know which was worse.

Something moved at the edge of Camp, between the trees. A child shouted about the woods, but the archivist was shouting for water and there was no room for one more instruction.

The archive gave a heavy wooden crack.

"Milo?"

No answer.

Shade was still near the card table. Elizabeth turned toward him, caught his eye, then looked at the archive again.

He moved toward it.

His foot stopped. He glanced down.

The black shape had taken his boot and was climbing toward his shin.

Wet-rot smell came through the smoke. Elizabeth recognized the thing immediately. She had the foam dagger by its handle before she remembered deciding to reach for it.

"Shade!"

He looked up, startled enough that she knew he hadn't seen it coming.

From the archive: "I'm stuck!"

Elizabeth turned halfway toward the doorway.

It would take more than a moment to cut him out. It had taken more than a moment with Pathwell, and that had been in a parking lot, not beside an archive catching fire. She could not see Milo inside. She could hear him coughing.

She called that the blob had Shade. Mama Baga was pulling people away from the burning wagon. Stansbury was moving toward its side.

Shade said nothing.

Elizabeth went into the archive.

* * *

Shade had meant to follow.

He saw the fire and thought first of buckets. Somebody would know where they were kept. Somebody would need another pair of hands.

Then his right foot refused to come with him.

He looked down.

Blackness moved against his trouser leg, very slowly, and the sight made no sense. A smear of dirt would have made sense. Water from an overturned bucket. One of Mama Baga's children catching him by the ankle because somebody had finally taken the card game too seriously.

The shape had no edge he could keep his eye on.

The low-tide smell reached him a moment after the sight.

"Oh," he said.

The word was smaller than he'd expected.

Beyond the wagons, the trees seemed to pull against something not quite visible. The dark mass had come through anyway. It continued to gather around his leg as though that had always been where it belonged.

His weight shifted toward the archive. The dark shape held his leg, and his right hand opened toward Pathwell under the old demand. For a furious second Shade couldn't tell which part of him was refusing to move.

Elizabeth disappeared through the smoke.

He could hear Milo somewhere inside. Mama Baga was directing people away from a wall whose canvas had started to glow. The archivist stumbled with an empty bucket, and another person took it from him and filled it without stopping to ask permission.

The people of Camp had not asked Shade to explain himself before giving him breakfast.

A jacket. Coffee. An argument about cards he would have lost if the game had gone on.

Mama Baga had asked him whether he wanted to stay.

He had said yes.

He had meant it. That was the worst part.

Shade looked back at the place where the brass frame had been.

Pathwell had forced that working. Everybody had told him to stop, including Shade. That part was not in dispute. But Shade had spent his morning preparing to make a roomful of people see the man exactly as he saw him. He had wanted the audience. The admission. The look on Pathwell's face when somebody finally agreed.

All morning there had been other things he might have done.

He had done a few of them, badly or by accident. Made coffee. Sat with a child. Almost learned a game.

He had been offered a place among people who did not need him to be a lesson about Pathwell.

And he had kept turning back to Pathwell.

That was a thing he knew how to do.

There was a movement in the archive doorway, then another shout and a line of buckets began to change direction. Shade wanted to go toward it.

The blackness had reached his knee.

For most of his life—an absurd expression for the amount of life he'd had—he had been certain he knew what Pathwell ought to become.

He had not become it either.

No one needed to hear him say that. Least of all Pathwell.

He looked toward the people fighting the fire.

The creature continued moving.

## Immediate authoring observations

- **What is newly legible:** Shade's internal realization is plain without assigning him literal responsibility for Pathwell's forced magic. His breakfast/jacket/cards memories provide concrete evidence of a lived alternative. His need for Pathwell's recognition ends without a last accusation.
- **What is preserved:** He genuinely tries to help before noticing the creature at his shin. He is surprised, not volunteering. Elizabeth remembers the dagger and chooses to reach Milo. The scene break is explicit; no Elizabeth mind-reading.
- **What remains intentionally UNDECIDED:** The excerpt stops before confirming whether Shade continues to resist or becomes still (Q54), exactly how fast the blob advances, whether Pathwell witnesses (Q55), and whether Elizabeth emerges in time to see anything.
- **Primary failure risk:** One intimate Shade POV segment would be a significant formal departure from the novel's predominantly Elizabeth-centered viewpoint, especially if introduced only to explain the climax. The authorial explanation is *clearer* than in A but could be too explicit and on-the-nose. It also stretches a very short, urgent physical interval to fit an extended introspection; revise or intercut only after timing validation.
- **Provisional result:** **Passes comprehension of Shade's missed opportunity**, but **conditional/weak on established POV conventions and physical clock**. No promotion.
