## The diagnosis

**Shade.** The book already has what makes him lovable: singing along to the radio and stopping when he hears her (Ch9); "Observation. Not memory." (Ch9); the coffee (Ch13); Hearts and "This child is cheating." (Ch13); "Pockets." / "I changed my mind." (Ch13); "I want to stay here." / "Then stay." (Ch13). The problem is **time and attention**, not material:
- His warm time is mostly **one morning** (Ch13) plus the diner. In Ch10–11 he's the antagonist; Ch14 is the prune.
- **His hope has no concrete form.** "I want to stay here" is a wish. Nothing on the page shows him starting to stay: no plan, no tomorrow, nothing of his waiting to happen. Hope "ripped from the world" needs something to rip.
- **The death is off-page,** which the rulings require: Elizabeth is inside with Milo. On her return it gets eight lines, then Stansbury's window and the books burning come first. It finally arrives as a quick list at the chapter's end ("She thought of the coffee. The green jacket…").

**The diner.** "Then you were never the point." gets one line, then "Elizabeth looked at him." / "So what was?" The scene doesn't let her feel what that cancels. Her walking back reads as Shade's errand ("Ask him what he chose") more than her own need.

**Ruled out:** Shade telling her "Go." at the eye contact. Q59 says the look mustn't imply he approves of her leaving him.

## Not drafted, for the author

- **More time with Shade.** If one morning isn't enough, the place for it is Ch12's night at Camp (the stew) or before the threshold opens in Ch13. That's new scenes and his call. The reviser's lean: try S1–S4 first, which give the time he already has a future to lose.
- **W16.** The August lock had Elizabeth recognize Shade at the diner, not on the road. That would add a second blow at the diner (the man she's with isn't Pathwell) on top of "never the point". The reviser's lean: keep the roadside. The diner's weight should come from "never the point", and reverting costs nine lines in Ch9 plus Ch8's second half.
- **Shade's line "I'm tired of carrying pieces of an answer that belong to a question I never asked."** (Ch9). The aim reviewer called it his only aphorism. "For him to stop deciding what I am before I get to." says the same thing in his own voice.

## Test: two blind readers, A/B

Two fresh readers read the whole book with no goals, logging reactions as they went. [Reader A](../../Reader-Reports/Blind-AB-Shade-and-Diner-2026-10-23/READER-A-CONTROL.md) had the control; [reader B](../../Reader-Reports/Blind-AB-Shade-and-Diner-2026-10-23/READER-B-CANDIDATES.md) had D1–S4. Both were asked the same eight questions only after finishing. One reader per arm is evidence, not proof.

**Both ranked Shade their favourite character** and scored his death **8/10** and the diner **7/10**. The scores didn't move. What moved is *where* the book hurt. Reader B logged a reaction at every candidate:
- D1: "This hurt, in a good way. 'she had taken all of it personally, because she had wanted to' is a brave, unflattering line for a heroine… The second egg skinned over — a quiet, sad image that does the emotional work."
- S1: "He wants the *green jacket* fixed: he wants to keep the thing he chose. That's quietly lovely."
- S2: "'It took him.' Grief. Real grief… Then 'On the card table, the queen of spades still lay face up.' Devastating."
- S3: "'as though there would be time to find out.' / 'That hand was promising.' I teared up."
- S4: "'LET OUT SLEEVES' in slow left-handed capitals / 'REPAIR REQUESTED.' **This is the most moving moment in the book for me.**"

Reader A, without them, named the gap S1–S4 fill: "To get free of the draw on his own terms, with some sign he'd keep living at Camp." Reader B, with them, listed "To get his sleeves let out" among the things it wanted and didn't get. That's the unfairness the author asked for.

**What both readers still wanted** (for the author):
1. **A goodbye.** A: "The look he gives Elizabeth before she goes to Milo is all we get, and I wanted one more word." B: "A goodbye of any kind with Elizabeth." Q59 limits what that word can be: nothing that reads as his approval.
2. **To finish the hand of Hearts**, and one conversation with Stansbury.
3. **More room after Pathwell's confession in Ch18.** A: "a huge thing to learn about this loss, and the book moves past it fast." B: "This is where the book moved on too fast for me." The confession and the hour of browsing are ruled (R47). How long the silence after it lasts isn't.
4. **The diner landed as loss and relief at once.** A: "a gut punch that was also a relief, because if she isn't the point, her life is hers again." B: "I also felt relief, because everything she did afterwards was her own choice." If the author wants it to land as loss first, that's a choice to make on the page.
5. **Walking back from the diner** made both wince practically: miles alone at night, with no money and no plan.

**One risk the readers surfaced:** at the eye contact, reader B read "Shade looked at the doorway… He looked at her." as "Shade himself seems to tell her to go to Milo, without saying it." Q59 says nothing may imply he approves. The narration doesn't claim it, but a reader inferred it. It's the author's call whether that inference is acceptable.

**Lean after the test:** take D1, D2 and S1–S4.
