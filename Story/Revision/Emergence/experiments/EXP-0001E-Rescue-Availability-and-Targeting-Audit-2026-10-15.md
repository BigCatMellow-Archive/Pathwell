# EXP-0001E — Rescue availability, equipment and blob-targeting audit

**Project:** Pathwell | **Date:** 2026-10-15
**Status:** ANALYTICAL STAGING TEST — conditionally viable; **not approved / not canon**

Do **not** decide Shade's movement after the capture (Rung Q54), Pathwell's witnessing (Q55), the POV strategy, or whether the revised climax supersedes the old decisions.

## Verdict up front

**Rescue logistics are conditionally solvable using existing facts, without inventing an anti-magic shield or forgetting any tools.** Elizabeth is the only character **shown to have an enchanted anti-blob blade at Camp**. However, her left shoulder is injured and the established Chapter 7 rescue required repeated cutting *and* a physical pull. The other demonstrated anti-blob tool, Stansbury's foam baton, was last associated with a box carried in his work van; the van is parked at the museum and Stansbury enters Camp with a first-aid box and paper bag. Ordinary hands cannot be assumed capable of peeling a blob away; at the bar Pathwell could not be freed by pulling until the dagger opened seams.

**The harder remaining causal problem is targeting:** in old Chapter 15 Shade deliberately separates himself from Pathwell to test the blob's choice. In the proposed ending he is surprised and makes no such maneuver. Why does the creature reach his leg rather than take the fresh waste or Pathwell? The least invasive answer is **collision along an existing cleanup route**, not a new magic instinct that identifies Shade flawlessly at a distance. Position Shade on the wooded side of the failed frame, in the creature's route toward the fresh pale spillage. Its leading edge reaches him as he tries to help with the archive; contact envelops the **actual unresolved waste anomaly** that Shade is. This is a proposal to test against the World Bible, not a fully established rule.

## B. Critical route: why Shade and not Pathwell or the fresh spill?

**Existing rule:** [World Bible](../../../Story_Files/world_bible.md), blobs: cleaning unclaimed pruning and casting waste; actual Shade is a large unresolved pruning-spillage anomaly; Pathwell and Shade's signatures overlap. Stronger signals affect response, but accessibility matters. Ch15 currently *uses Shade walking away* to make the creature commit to his location.

**Proposed route (geography must be checked in prose):**

```text
 [WOODS / CAMP BORDER]  --- blob has been pushing against boundary
            |
            |  surge draws it toward loose threads by broken frame
            v
 [SHADE: standing on woods-facing side of frame]  <-- tries to head to fire
            |
 [PALE SPILL / PATHWELL ON GROUND / BROKEN FRAME]
            |
 [ARCHIVE DOOR / LANTERN FIRE]
            |
 [MILO, PINNED DEEP INSIDE]
```

The creature does **not** need to know in advance that Shade is the "true" waste source. The beacon can bring it toward the working; it reaches **Shade first**, because he is in or beside the route from the boundary to the loose threads. The leading edge may spread low along the ground amid smoke and moving feet, reaching his boot unnoticed until it climbs his shin. As it begins engulfing Shade, it has actually contacted a large cleanup target rather than merely a superficially matching signature. It can absorb Shade and then clean the nearby fresh spillage, as existing Ch15 already depicts.

**Unproven portion:** The World Bible describes signature ambiguity but does not explicitly promise that direct physical contact resolves it or that a blob always prioritizes larger waste over nearer smaller waste. Avoid declaring a general "contact-recognition" law or a new intelligence. This blocking preserves plausibility but must remain **CONDITIONAL** until tested in prose. If the spatial layout cannot put Shade between incoming creature and spill, another legitimate reason for first contact is required.

## D. Other rescue options and what a good draft must answer

1. **Can Elizabeth make one fast dagger cut and THEN save Milo?** Chapter 7 disproves "one guaranteed tap": she had to make multiple cuts, haul Pathwell out, and collapse backward. One cut *might* temporarily free Shade, but its benefit is unknown. A single speculative cut while Milo is choking is a high-risk delay, not an obvious solution.
2. **Can she hand the dagger to someone else?** There is no rule that prevents it, and the toy is safe to handle, but (a) physically handing off takes time and proximity, (b) Chapter 7 suggests **technique, nerve and close contact** matter, and (c) no other person at her side is prepositioned and shown ready to use it. **If the author stages a capable person within arm's reach, the dilemma fails unless the handoff is plausibly attempted.** Do not invent "only Elizabeth can wield it" or "it goes inert for others."
3. **Can Stansbury use his magic?** His destructive baton is associated with a foam-weapon box in a work van outside Camp; Ch14 documents other items in his hands. Nothing proves he carries a spare, so he shouldn't conveniently have one. If a revision explicitly places the baton on him, another rescue becomes much likelier and the blocking must change accordingly.
4. **Can somebody simply pull Shade away?** A plain pull failed against the mass around Pathwell in Chapter 7 until the dagger opened it. A non-magical attempt may still be attempted; a quick, certain rescue by unarmed people is **not** established.
5. **Could Mama Baga perform an anti-blob spell?** Her competence does not establish a specific anti-cleanup working. Granting a new ability at the climax to solve or obstruct rescue is a lore change requiring author choice. Let her realistically coordinate and react.
6. **Can Pathwell help?** He is down, chest struck, rib-injured, hands near unclaimed spill, and cannot stand. He cannot physically replace Elizabeth. Whether he notices Shade is still Q55 open.
7. **Why doesn't the blob simply clean fresh spill first?** Shade stands directly in the path toward it, and the creature contacts Shade's large actual waste anomaly. This is the **least invasive plausible answer**, but **not a confirmed targeting algorithm**.
8. **Why does Shade not see a huge creature until shin level?** The blob's first advancing edge can remain low in smoke and chaos with witnesses looking toward the fire. A child may see boundary strain but fail to convey a warning. No invisibility, teleport, unusually intelligent stealth, or magic suppression is required.
9. **Why isn't the blob extinguished by ordinary fire?** No existing rule says it is. The fire-producing *enchanted* baton destroyed a previous creature; ordinary flames at the archive are not proven the same specific effect. Keep them separate.
10. **Why does the blob leave?** After actual Shade anomaly and loose waste are removed, existing World Bible says it inspects for remaining unclaimed waste and departs. It does not need to put out the archive blaze.

## F. Residual failure cases (not solved by positioning alone)

- **Creature speed is still unspecified.** Must allow first contact shortly before Elizabeth enters, progression while she is busy freeing Milo, and disappearance before or just after her exit. Too slow = people could intervene. Too fast = Shade's internal realization cannot develop.
- **POV continues to matter.** Elizabeth cannot testify that Shade chooses acceptance after she enters; current experiment B's explicit Shade break has other costs.
- **Why Elizabeth's choice matters to the central magical conflict remains unresolved.** Saving Milo undoubtedly affects human stakes, not the blob's object; the older chapter revision criterion has not been demonstrably met.
- **Q54/Q55 remain explicitly open.** No final gesture or Pathwell sightline is fixed by this audit.
- **Old manuscript is different.** Ch15 currently places Milo rescue and Stansbury's full salvage *before* arrival. The proposed revision deliberately changes it, so late on-page dialogue with Milo and old Shade deliberation cannot simply be retained.

## Outcome / recommendation

**GO to a narrow, noncanonical rescue-window prose test of Configuration 1, NOT to chapter revision.** Establish Stansbury's weapon-box absence once using existing transport continuity, set Shade in the spill approach path, give Elizabeth a brief clear view and Milo an immediately worsening danger, and show Camp's adults *responding* rather than freeze-framing. Then re-evaluate chronology, the POV strategy and author promotion. Do not settle Shade's interior or agency by implication.
