# EXP-0003A — Hybrid test: accepting the capture without abandoning care

**Project:** Pathwell | **Date:** 2026-10-15
**Status:** NONCANONICAL TEST; NOT CHOSEN OR PROMOTED
**Sources:** [Rung Q43](../../Rung-Q28-Q29-Shade-2026-10-14.md) (captured unexpectedly; sees the damage; accepts it), [Q51–54](../../Rung-Q51-onward-Shade-2026-10-15.md) (tries to help, surprises at shin, regrets missed life, Pathwell's opinion irrelevant, after-contact behavior undecided), [EXP-0003 matrix](EXP-0003-Shade-Final-Action-and-POV-Matrix-2026-10-15.md), [author's restrained Elizabeth ruling](../../Author-Ruling-Elizabeth-Agency-2026-10-15.md).
**Purpose:** Test whether the previously offered binary (go still *versus* keep helping) contains a false opposition. Neither option is a new author ruling.

## Core synthesis

**Shade can stop a futile attempt to free himself and still make one small, genuinely useful choice for someone else.** The former can express acceptance of an inescapable physical event, not a moral conviction that death is deserved. The latter gives his regret about unused possibility outward behavior, without making him a pre-planned martyr, forgiving Pathwell, demanding an acknowledgment, or converting cleanup into a moral judge.

This matters because the author Q43 already proposed that Shade, surprised by capture, **accepts** it; Q51 later says Shade is moving **to help the fire** before noticing it. The two can coexist: initial helpful movement → trapped at shin → brief instinctive effort to pull away → sees Camp and accepts futility → does one ordinary thing for the emergency → creature overtakes him. He can accept an outcome without treating his own life as worthless.

**A caution:** This is a subtle framing, not established fact that a blob cannot ever be escaped. Chapter 7 demonstrates the enchanted dagger can cut one, but Shade and the bystander in this prospective scene don't have that tool; the author-approved Elizabeth rescue prevents her making the same multi-step extraction simultaneously. Avoid an omniscient assertion that rescue was metaphysically impossible.

## Short noncanonical scene — controlled, deliberately brief Shade view

> The passage begins AFTER the Chapter 14 failed working, Milo's entrapment, Elizabeth's warning and her move into the burning archive. It does not show Pathwell witnessing anything or prescribe a full rewrite.

* * *

The man pulling at Shade's sleeve was stronger than he looked. Shade's shoulder turned. The blackness around his right boot did not.

"Again," the man said.

They tried. This time Shade almost went down.

He let go of the man's arm.

From the archive came another crack of wood. The man had dropped a bucket to grab him; it lay on its side between them. Behind the man, people were running for water.

Shade had spent his morning waiting for Pathwell to understand what he'd done. He could remember every word he had wanted to say.

He could also remember Milo dealing him another hand when he ought to have been running errands, Mama Baga letting him stay, and a woman asking whether he needed both arms to carry onions.

None of that had required Pathwell to become a better man first.

The man started toward Shade again.

Shade caught the bucket by its handle with his left hand.

"Take it."

The man hesitated, looking at Shade's leg.

Shade tipped the bucket toward the line. "They need it."

The man took it. Shade watched him run toward the fire.

The blackness rose over the green jacket.

Shade looked at the place where Elizabeth had gone in to find Milo. He didn't call to Pathwell.

## Findings from the hybrid (assistant's craft assessment)

**Works provisionally**
- Preserves Q43 **acceptance** and Q51 **initial desire to help** without deriving a suicidal or punitive motive. The man attempts extraction; it fails visibly; Shade doesn't simply choose death.
- His regret focuses on a missed *mode of living*, not the false claim that he started the fire. The alternatives were all ordinary: cards, welcome, carrying onions, accepting help. He now makes another such choice while he can.
- “None of that had required Pathwell...” translates Q53 (Pathwell's judgment irrelevant) with very little overt thematic explanation; this thought might still be too explicit and should be tested against Pathwell's narrator voice.
- The green jacket and the missed game are established. The bucket line and the man's abandoned bucket already exist in the rescue-window probe; no magical method introduced.
- No scene in which Elizabeth must save Shade, and no false consent to being pruned.

**Still at risk**
- A scene break into Shade's thoughts interrupts **Elizabeth's rescue**; he may steal the emotional center. Could reduce to one exterior observable beat (bucket transfer) while relying on Ch13 for reader inference. This is not automatically the right POV.
- It is possible to read “Take it / They need it” as a *conventional noble sacrifice*, especially if the bystander might have secured magical help. The reader must see that an ordinary pull has failed, not infer Shade has chosen to refuse an actual rescue.
- The swift progression from shin to torso implies a timeline. It has not been synchronized to Milo's fulcrum rescue, Stansbury's salvage and injury or the fire's physical growth.
- The *ordinary* bucket may be too convenient: a final meaningful act should not look like a narrative symbol conveniently rolling to the dying character. If needed, keep only the earlier attempt to reach the fire (Q51) and let the ending be quiet.
- The narration's line about not requiring Pathwell to change may articulate the theme too bluntly (Writing Principles 9, 16). A second leaner treatment should delete it and compare reader comprehension.
- Although Shade's disappointment about his missed potential is authored (Q52), his actual morning contains generosity. The novel should not call his life wasted or his death deserved.
- Q55 remains OPEN: the scene neither confirms nor denies Pathwell noticing. Q54 remains OPEN: this hybrid is one candidate for author review, not an implicit final action decision.

## Formal alternatives to test next

1. **E-only:** Elizabeth witnesses just the unsuccessful jacket pull and first effort to help, then goes inside. The reader must infer Shade's turn from Ch13; his last moments remain outside her knowledge.
2. **External short break:** Show the bucket being redirected by Shade while Elizabeth's rescue takes place, without accessing his thoughts. This can be short and still legible as a human choice, but removes explicit Q52 regret.
3. **Brief Shade interior (this trial):** Reader hears his recognition while Elizabeth is inside. Clearer but risks over-explanation and stealing the protagonist's climax.
