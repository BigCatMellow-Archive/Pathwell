## Working extract: Elizabeth only

The brass loop struck the lantern.

Glass broke, and oil went across the archive doorway. There was a moment in which somebody could have put a foot on the flame. Then the lower wall took.

"Water!" the archivist shouted.

Elizabeth turned toward the door. Her diary had fallen out of the intake rack. Nana's cookbook was still on the shelf beyond it.

A little girl came running from the wagons, empty-handed.

"There's something in the trees."

"Bucket!" someone shouted to her.

"No, I—"

The sound from the archive swallowed the rest. It was heavy enough to shift the floor beneath the open door.

"Milo?" the archivist called.

A smaller voice answered, muffled. "Here!"

Shade was moving already. He had been standing on the side of the broken brass frame nearest the woods, with Pathwell on the ground behind him. His right hand still pointed back toward Pathwell, but Shade was facing the archive.

He took a step.

His next step stopped halfway.

Shade looked down. There was something around his boot that Elizabeth had taken, for an instant, to be another shadow from the failed working.

It wasn't pale.

He lifted his leg, and it didn't come free.

Darkness had folded over the top of his boot and was working up his shin. Its outer edge was almost level with the dirt. Beyond the frame, the dark mass extended toward the woods, where two tree trunks stood a little too close together for anything that size to have passed between them.

The smell reached Elizabeth: low tide under the oil smoke.

The blob was coming toward the pale threads scattered around Pathwell. Shade was in the way.

He stared at his leg, then at the thing coming through the trees. He hadn't seen it.

From inside the archive came the sound of another shelf going over.

"I'm stuck!"

Elizabeth's hand went to the dagger at her back. She had cut Pathwell out of one of these. The seam had opened beneath the blade, but not all at once; she had cut more than once and pulled him free with both arms.

Her left shoulder still hurt when she breathed.

She could reach Shade. He was only across the clearing.

She could not see Milo at all.

"Mama Baga!" Elizabeth shouted. "The thing's got Shade!"

Mama Baga turned from the bucket line. A man with an empty bucket moved toward Shade before she could tell him anything. He caught the sleeve of the green jacket and pulled. Shade's shoulder moved. His boot did not.

Shade looked toward the archive again.

Milo coughed. The next call came smaller.

On the far side of the wagon, Stansbury was trying to reach the side window through the people hauling water. He had a hammer. Elizabeth could not see an orange baton.

Elizabeth took the dagger out. She held it a moment, looking from the man still pulling at Shade's jacket to the archive door.

She knew what happened when someone pulled without first opening a seam. She had tried it at the bar.

She could not safely throw the blade through the crowded clearing. Handing it over meant moving away from the child she could hear but could not see.

"Elizabeth!" Milo called.

She put the dagger back behind her waistband and ran inside.

Her diary lay across the entrance aisle. The cookbook sat beyond it, close enough to touch.

She passed both.

"Milo!"

"Here!"

He was farther in.

Elizabeth went to him.

## What this does and does not prove

**Improvement over tests A/B:**
- Stansbury's known weapon **box** is not silently conjured into Camp; the last established location is the work van at the museum. He is physically on the opposite side of the archive doing immediate emergency work.
- Elizabeth recalls exactly how the dagger worked, has a genuine line of sight to Shade and hears Milo trapped. She tries to get others' attention instead of silently abandoning Shade.
- Mama Baga and an ordinary Camp resident **respond** to the cry. A human hand alone is not a confirmed instant extraction (Ch7), but the scene does not declare rescue impossible by authorial decree.
- The creature arrives through the *route to the fresh waste*, reaching Shade as an object in that route before any deliberate target-selection trick. His surprise is visible to Elizabeth.
- Milo is the single trapped child; a different child is a tentative observer.
- Shade's **first** action is to move toward the fire, preserving the author's Q51 answer.

**Still unproved:**
- The distance and duration between woods, frame and doorway are not established in any measured way. “Only across the clearing” makes the danger physically close but raises how easily another person could rush in.
- The man makes an ordinary attempt to pull Shade out, and it fails at the boot, consistent with Chapter 7's extraction difficulty. He still requires follow-through after Elizabeth enters; we do not know if a later attempt succeeds or can change the outcome.
- The line about Stansbury not carrying a baton may be too expository for polished prose; the **audit** establishes provenance, so actual revision may merely show his hands and existing work.
- The excerpt now avoids a contrived explanation shouted about the dagger. Throwing/carrying the blade, the distance and rescue method still need a full-scene test; a credible handoff might be possible if a capable person comes within reach.
- The blob might be delayed or redirected by direct contact with the person attempting to help. This should not be solved with a new rule without author review.
- Elizabeth entering for Milo does not change the blob's objective. Shade's internal realization, final bodily choice, and Pathwell's observation remain open.
- Archive fire movement, smoke and ordinary physical damage still need full Ch15 pacing after the entrance.

**Result:** **TENTATIVE PASS** on the existence of a conceivable competing rescue window; **NO PASS YET** for the entire aftermath or for the proof that Shade could not be rescued. This is a test, not canonical prose.
