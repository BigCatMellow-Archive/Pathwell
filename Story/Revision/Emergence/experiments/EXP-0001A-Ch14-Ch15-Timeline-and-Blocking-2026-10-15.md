## 4. Candidate A / B fork (only AFTER Q51's attempt to help)

**Shared locked author-direction:** Shade *tries to help with the fire*, discovers blob at shin in surprise, and recognizes he did not become who he thought Pathwell could be. The previous demand for Pathwell's acknowledgment becomes irrelevant. No voluntary approach.

### A — Stillness after realizing his loss
- After the surprise at shin, he becomes still and watches what has happened.
- Pros: allows loss and regret without a last speech or heroic self-portrait; contrasts earlier angry public demands.
- Critical risk: the creature may already make further motion impossible. The stillness can be **paralysis**, not a conscious choice; Elizabeth cannot verify the interior state, and treating it as chosen acceptance may be narratorial overreach.
- Experiment verdict: **CONDITIONAL**, only if another earlier voluntary behavior or an unequivocal observable ability to resist establishes the difference. Do not fabricate it merely to force proof.

### B — Continues trying to help while already caught
- After noticing his shin, Shade tries to move toward a person or active fire task rather than withdrawing into a speech about himself.
- Pros: his care for Camp is visible; he had the capacity to act beyond resentment.
- Critical risk: moving against a heavy, pursuing blob may just look futile; if framed as martyrdom it contradicts surprise capture. Also the author expressly left post-capture behavior **undecided**.
- Experiment verdict: **CONDITIONAL; slightly stronger visible agency**, but does not itself establish the final emotional realization.

### C — Hybrid (secondary test idea only; NOT chosen)
- He first moves to help (Q51), realizes the blob has him, and the external observer notices a change in how he regards Camp. Later movement after the capture is left **ambiguous** rather than authoritatively “still” or “struggling.”
- Pros: doesn't force Q54; exploits one unequivocally chosen caring action before he understands danger.
- Critical risk: can feel underwritten if reader doesn't infer Shade's regret from earlier evidence; cannot substitute for fully realized scene. This is a **control option**, not another approved ending.

## 5. Equipment and intervention audit (important new obstacle)

1. Elizabeth **still carries the imbued foam dagger** at her waistband (Ch14 ~160, ~466; Ch7 proves it can cut a blob and free Pathwell). Do not make her forget or mysteriously lose it. In Ch7, rescue requires getting close, several cuts and *physically pulling* Pathwell out—**not a one-second tap**. At B4/B5 she cannot also enter a collapsing archive fast enough if the scene genuinely establishes simultaneous immediate jeopardy. This argument only works with spatially honest distances and a distressed, trapped Milo.
2. Stansbury **has magical anti-blob tools in Chapter 7** (sword, enchanted baton that destroys one). But **at the Chapter 14 arrival** the visible inventory is the museum's green first-aid box and a paper bag. Chapter 11 shows that box obtained from the museum; the text does *not* establish that he has a baton on him at Camp. Do not assert a baton is unavailable without inventory evidence. If the writer wants Stansbury unable to interfere, stage his commitments and inventory on page. A mere claim that 'nobody could save Shade' is insufficient given Chapter 7.
3. Mama Baga is near the failed working and directs Camp evacuation. Her absence from the blob moment would be an error if she has an obvious view and opportunity. She need not be omnipotent; her realistic attention and response should be traceable.
4. Pathwell is injured and on the ground after the failed working; do not restore his mobility without cost. His **Q55 sight of Shade remains undecided**. His ability to call for help or speak may matter even if he cannot stand.

**Decision gate:** If B5 gives Elizabeth enough time for the Ch7-style repeated cuts *and* to reach Milo, the false dilemma fails. If another bystander has an available enchanted baton and a safe route, the unrescuable capture is unearned. The new sequence must survive these counterexamples.

## 7. Why the revision is not yet causally closed

### Camp and blob
- Prior pursuit + stronger spillage signal = supported attraction.
- A boundary that **compresses and resists** entry is an author worldbuilding proposal consistent with Camp's movement/accessibility being unusual, but not yet an existing World Bible rule.
- A six-foot dark blob coming to shin unnoticed needs *smoke, urgency, angle and approach*, not literal invisibility; a child witnessing and failing to get attention can justify audience warning but only if POV allows it.

### Shade and fault
- He didn't choose to be captured. Even if he goes still, non-resistance may be **incapacity** rather than agency.
- His realization that he missed his chance at Camp is a powerful **self-assessment**, not an omniscient verdict that a person granted one morning under magical compulsion wasted a full fair chance.
- Pathwell ignites the fire through his coerced working. Shade's resentment/public demand may help set the emotional context but does **not** transfer causal responsibility for the ignition.

### Elizabeth's central effect
- She saves a living child who would remain trapped without her; the choice materially affects the **human stakes**. Her loss of diary/cookbook has narrative consequence independent of Shade's fate.
- It **does not currently alter the cleanup's magical target or final outcome**. The older author-specified criterion “her final choice materially alters the central conflict” (see Decisions.md Q-E/P40) is therefore **not demonstrated** merely by concurrent timing.
- Do not try to force a new magical power or make Elizabeth morally culpable for Shade's absorption. It may be necessary to revisit how “central conflict” is defined, or find another real causal effect, under author review.

### What must be preserved after the clock changes
- Milo's north-route errand is why he is trapped, not arbitrary return into fire.
- Queen-of-spades / unfinished Hearts; green jacket/pockets; Milo's eventual naming/acknowledgment of Shade in Ch16. The **current final dialogue with Milo in Ch15 cannot simply survive** if Milo is pinned inside at the same moment. Preserve the emotional thread earlier or accept its absence.
- Chapter 16: the salvaged road notebook and family letters, Stansbury's burned hand, archive losses, healer checks, Pathwell's restitution. Stansbury's *current* salvage happens after Milo comes out: if left there, this can occur after the blob sequence instead; he need not be magically fighting the blob to keep the scar.
- Blob's post-cleanup behavior: after taking Shade it can collect loose spillage and leave; no need for its removal to extinguish ordinary fire.

## 9. Verdict and decision register

**Verdict: REVISE; no promotion.** New staging can probably accommodate the **three causal effects and Elizabeth's human rescue** if the creature is already near Camp. It is **not yet** a proven full climax. The blocking and older central-agency criterion remain open.

**Best provisional beat order** (planning default only): Pathwell's refusal-override → failed working creates spill / causes lantern fire → Milo trapped as creature struggles through Camp boundary → warning lost amid emergency → Elizabeth/Shade register archive danger → Shade attempts to move to help and finds blob up to shin → Elizabeth chooses to go in for Milo → Shade outcome outside, treated honestly under chosen POV → cleanup of waste while ordinary fire continues → Elizabeth emerges with Milo → preserve Stansbury's separate salvage/injury and Camp's reckoning.
