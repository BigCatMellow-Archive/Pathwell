## Why this is the next slice

The prior Chapter 1 fix is now in the manuscript. R38 already supplies the next two unusually specific author-origin concerns:

- **R38 ¶63:** “I feel like this should be more fantastical. It's the first time we are seeing magic. Maybe it should feel like slow motion to Elizabeth? While the rule is to ‘write the sequel’ that doesn't mean we need to rush over this part.”
- **R38 ¶69:** “Need to convey her panic and confusion better, she is seeing two things here, magic, and something happen to her grandmothers cookbook.”

The reader must experience **an impossible thing happening to a familiar physical book at the same moment**; the scene should not turn into a magician's lecture, slower chase, or invented lore. The September/October reader feedback also criticizes explanatory narration. This experiment isolates the first spell and her snatch rather than trying to implement R38's entire annotated chapter in one pass.

## Control A — current author-approved manuscript

[Live Chapter 1](../../../Chapters/Chapter_01.txt) already includes the two R48 party-notebook sentences. In the magic sequence it currently says:

> He placed his hand flat on the page, the letters glowed a soft gold.

> The glow from the letters now consumed the page, a pool of light forming under his hand.

> Elizabeth snatched the book, leaving the page behind. “What are you doing!?”

**Advantages:** Tempo stays hard; the sugar-cookie memory lands promptly; the original July passage is recognizable.  
**Liability:** First magic is one comma-spliced sentence, then a second description of gold. The reader sees a glow but has little time to experience Elizabeth's astonishment or recognize that **Nana's pages are being altered** before she acts.

## Candidate B — two bounded changes, no plot reordering

**[Complete alternative Chapter 1 — EXP-0009B](EXP-0009B-Ch01-First-Magic-Cookbook-Candidate-2026-10-16.txt)**. It is a separate **noncanonical** full chapter built from the **now-published** R48 version.

**Change 1, first magic** (replaces only the one comma-splice paragraph):

> He placed his hand flat on the page. The letters glowed a soft gold.
>
> Elizabeth leaned closer. She could still read Nana's writing through the light. The ink shone even in the places his fingers didn't touch. For a second the thudding at the door sounded far away.

**Change 2, her snatch** (replaces the one snatch-and-cry line):

> Elizabeth grabbed the cookbook by the spine and pulled it from his hands. The sugar-cookie page stayed under his palm.
>
> “What are you doing!?”

**What changed physically:** Elizabeth has a perceptual beat while she watches ink emit light; the physical book is visibly *held and contested*, not just a generic prop. Her first move remains **a grab that interrupts an already-started working**, causing the original spillage. The page remains with Pathwell. No new magical sensitivity, control or ability is assigned to her.

**Change footprint:** Exactly **two** designated replacements, adding approximately **262 characters net** to the approved Chapter 1. All other sentences, including the party/notebook clue, the ordinary creature arrival before the spell, Nana's sensory memory, the “marked” bluff, and the locked ending remain identical to the current manuscript.

### Counterargument against B

The current scene already produces the strongest evidence of Nana's value **immediately afterward**, in the sensory cookie memory. Making it too elaborate before the interruption risks reducing that payoff. The author might prefer **only change 1** (magic experience) or **only change 2** (more tangible book rescue), preserving the sharper old cadence elsewhere. These are two natural controls without needing another magical invention.
