The cells differ only in **what Shade does after initial capture** and **how the reader can observe it**.

## P1 — Elizabeth-only / stillness (approximately a doorway beat)

The man pulled harder on Shade's green sleeve. Shade's shoulder turned, but the blackness around his boot remained.

Shade caught the man's wrist with his free hand. For an instant Elizabeth thought he meant to pull himself toward him.

Then he let go.

His eyes went to the archive door, where smoke was beginning to crawl along the roof. He didn't look at Pathwell.

"Milo!" the archivist shouted.

The answer came thinly from inside.

Elizabeth had the dagger in her hand. She had cut a blob before. She had also needed two working arms to get Pathwell free.

Shade stood with his hand open, no longer holding anyone.

Elizabeth called for help and went inside.

**Test note:** A reader can infer resignation, but cannot know whether his grip eased because he accepted anything, because pulling failed, because he wanted the man to move, or because he was losing strength. It is **not** enough to prove his regret. The unspecified fate outside may work as deliberate loss-of-knowledge, but misses the specific Q52 insight unless preparation elsewhere carries it.

## P2 — Elizabeth-only / practical action before the doorway

The man dropped his bucket when he caught Shade's jacket. He pulled once. Shade moved an inch. His boot stayed inside the blackness.

The empty bucket rolled toward the broken frame.

Shade looked down at it, then toward the archive. His right hand was still held open toward Pathwell. His left was free.

He reached with the left, stopped the bucket with his fingers, and pushed it back toward the people running for water.

"Take that," he said.

The man stared at him.

"For the fire."

There was no time to argue. The man took the handle.

From inside the archive Milo cried out, and the cry ended in a cough.

Elizabeth still had her fingers around the dagger. She could see the darkness climbing Shade's leg. She could hear the boy behind the smoke. She called to Mama Baga, then went to Milo.

**Test note:** The reader sees Shade make an independently intelligible **human action** after learning he is caught. He doesn't announce repentance, consent to die or make himself the hero. He also does not turn to Pathwell to see whether he has noticed. His missed-chance realization remains a **reader inference**, not something Elizabeth can certify. **Strongest Elizabeth-camera candidate,** but a bucket transfer could be too inconsequential/contrived if a reader notices it as manufactured symbolism. Needs a natural prop from the bucket line.

## P3 — Short Shade interior / becomes still

* * *

The blackness held him at the shin.

Shade tried to pull back. It moved with him and tightened when he stopped. The man holding his jacket couldn't move him either.

The archive was burning. Somebody was asking for more buckets. Milo was still in there.

Shade had spent all morning rehearsing what Pathwell should be made to understand.

He had also carried onions, drunk bad coffee, and said yes when Mama Baga asked whether he wanted to stay. There had been another sort of morning available. He had seen it and kept coming back to the same argument.

The man's hand slipped from his sleeve.

Shade looked toward the fire.

He stopped pulling.

* * *

**Test note:** Explicitly recognizes the life Shade was offered, without an accusatory farewell to Pathwell. However, **"He stopped pulling" after an inescapable restraint is not clear free agency**. Worse, paired with self-blame, it may suggest he chose death as punishment for ordinary resentment. The interior asks the reader to accept an ethically fraught reading; Chapter 13 proves he also made kind choices. **High risk / weak as final solution.**

## P4 — Short Shade interior / practical outward action

* * *

The blackness had reached his shin before Shade knew it was there.

He'd been moving toward the archive. That part was still true, however useless his legs had become.

Someone caught his jacket and pulled. The fabric tightened against his shoulder. The dark mass stayed where it was.

A bucket rolled from the man's feet.

Shade could see the cooking wagon, the card table, people shouting for water. He had spent his morning making sure they heard what Pathwell had done. They had given him coffee anyway. Milo had wanted another hand of Hearts.

He had wanted that, too.

He reached for the bucket with his left hand and shoved it toward the water line.

"For the fire," he said.

He did not look back toward Pathwell.

The blackness kept climbing.

* * *

**Test note:** The best **compressed** interior candidate because it expresses regret through remembered Camp welcome and a practical choice, not the abstract sentence “he wasted his chance.” Does not claim that Shade caused the fire, chose to be engulfed, stopped resisting or could still escape. **But the scene break steals narrative time from Elizabeth's archive rescue**, and direct Shade thoughts would need a deliberate viewpoint/camera judgment.

## Neutral external camera (fifth, control possibility — not full probe)

An external short cutaway could show the worker pulling Shade, Shade nudging the bucket back into use, and the blob continuing upward, then **immediately return** to Elizabeth and Milo. It would make the physical action visible without claiming to know Shade's thoughts. Chapter 11 permits scene-location/camera movement after Elizabeth passes a threshold, but the novel doesn't thereby license arbitrary unannounced Shade interior at its climax. Such a cutaway is a **formal/style experiment**, not a solved emotional revelation.

## What the comparisons prove and don't

### Supporting existing character evidence

- Ch13: Shade worries about Elizabeth's pain, carries onions, begins another round of Hearts, recognizes he likes pockets, coffee and the chance to stay. The author's Q52 insight that he **believes** he didn't become the person Pathwell could be should NOT overrule this on-page evidence of kindness.
- Ch14: Shade has specifically **refused** reintegration. Pathwell overrides the refusal and **alone** causes the working that ignites the archive. Shade's regret is neither culpability for the fire nor proof he deserves cleanup.
- Author Q51: he tries to move **toward the fire**, not toward the cleanup creature, then discovers the blackness around his shin. Every probe preserves this earlier voluntary attempt.
- Author Q53: Pathwell's recognition is irrelevant to Shade at the end; none requires Pathwell to watch (Q55 remains open).
- Existing Ch15 Shade death and post-cleanup events depend on Shade being the actual unresolved pruning anomaly; emotional choice alone cannot magically stop blob absorption.

### Strong conclusion

**The meaningful choice is not obviously whether Shade stops physically struggling**. If the blob has already immobilized his leg, inactivity may be forced, not freely selected. The more legible final human choice is whether he can redirect his remaining attention **from demanding that Pathwell acknowledge him toward helping Camp in a small available way**, without implying that helping buys redemption or salvation. The author must still select the final action, so this is a **craft recommendation, not a ruling**.

### Limitations

- The **bucket** is an illustrative prop, not a requested plot object; the scene must earn its presence. Ch14–16 already show an active bucket line, so it does not require new magic or extra people, but having one roll directly to Shade may look contrived.
- The proposed blob approach and rate of absorption are untested in finished scene rhythm. How long Shade has for a small action and a recognition is still open.
- P1/P2 can't verify his final private interpretation because Elizabeth enters the archive. P3/P4 require a consciously chosen POV structure. **We should not solve this by having Elizabeth mind-read Shade or by retrospective narration declaring what he understood.**
- The possibility of saving Shade cannot be categorically excluded just because the event ends in tragedy; the previous rescue audit identified remaining intervention windows. A purposeful choice to release a helper from a failed pull isn't the same as choosing death.
- The earlier original ending's detailed final Milo/Hearts banter is **unavailable** in new simultaneity because Milo is pinned inside. Ch13 and the Ch16–17 remembrance can carry the unfinished game.
- Elizabeth's author-approved **restrained approach** is not a license to push her out of Chapter 15's dramatic foreground. The most successful scene should remain the archive rescue, not a second exterior climax competing for the same reader attention.
