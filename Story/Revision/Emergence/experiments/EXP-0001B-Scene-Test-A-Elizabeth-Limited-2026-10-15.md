# EXP-0001B — Scene test A: Elizabeth-limited viewpoint

**Date:** 2026-10-15
**Status:** NONCANONICAL PROSE TEST / NOT APPROVED
**Parent:** [EXP-0001A timeline](EXP-0001A-Ch14-Ch15-Timeline-and-Blocking-2026-10-15.md)
**Question:** Can the reader experience Elizabeth's competing emergencies without a viewpoint shift, while accepting that Shade's private realization remains unavailable?

> This is an alternative *test insert* in the Ch14–15 transition, not replacement chapter prose. The existing longer Milo rescue remains the baseline; the marked transition is an intentionally abbreviated bridge. This sample temporarily assumes the creature was already near Camp's boundary and reaches Shade while Milo is trapped. It does not decide Q54 or Q55.

---

The lantern came down with the brass loop.

Elizabeth saw the glass go and the oil follow it, a bright line crossing the archive threshold. She was still looking at Pathwell, flat on his back among the pale loose threads, when the first yellow flame climbed the doorframe.

The archivist shouted for water.

Someone behind the wagons shouted something else.

Elizabeth turned. The person speaking was a girl carrying two empty buckets, looking the wrong way—toward the trees instead of the fire.

"It's stuck," the girl said.

A second voice demanded the buckets. The girl turned, still pointing.

Inside the archive, something fell.

"Milo?" called the archivist.

The next crash answered him.

Shade stood a few steps from the ruined frame, his right arm held out toward Pathwell. The rest of him was looking at the fire. For an instant Elizabeth caught his eye.

There had been no shortage of things he wanted to say to Pathwell that morning. He didn't say any of them now.

He moved toward the archive.

Or tried to.

His left foot went forward. His right stayed where it was. Shade looked down, annoyed at first, as if it were another trick of the draw.

Something black held his boot.

Elizabeth had seen that blackness at the bar. She knew the smell when it reached her, wet rot beneath burning oil.

The shape was already up to Shade's shin. It came out of the direction of the wagons in a slow folding movement, the outer edge dragging against the earth. Beyond it, between two trees, the air had the unpleasant look of fabric pulled through a hole too small for it.

Shade stared at his leg. His head came up sharply.

"Milo!" someone shouted.

From inside the archive: "I'm stuck!"

Elizabeth's right hand was already behind her, fingers finding the foam dagger. The ridiculous orange handle still fitted her palm. She had cut Pathwell out of one of those things. She knew what the blade could do.

She knew, too, that it had taken more than one cut.

Shade looked at her again. Behind him the creature was still coming, black gathering around his boot, and behind her the archive doorway breathed smoke. She could take three steps toward him. She could not know how many it would take to get him free.

Milo cried out, and the cry ended in a cough.

Elizabeth tried to say Shade's name. What came out was "It's got him!"

Mama Baga was sending people away from the burning side of the wagon. Stansbury had gone around that side; Elizabeth could no longer see him. Somebody turned toward her voice. She didn't wait to find out who.

She put the dagger back at her waistband.

Not away. Back.

Then she ran toward the archive.

The diary lay open where the intake rack had fallen. Nana's cookbook was on its support behind the first shelf. She passed both.

"Milo!"

"Here!"

His voice came from farther in.

She went to him.

---

**Abbreviated bridge for this experiment only:** Elizabeth reaches the pinned Milo and frees him using the already established shelf/strut sequence, sacrificing the chance to collect either book. There is no exterior cutaway. When she comes out, both she and Milo have been occupied inside; the writer must still decide when Stansbury's salvage and injury occur.

---

Outside, the bucket line had moved.

The fire was taller. Mama Baga was shouting instructions, but the words reached Elizabeth in pieces.

The dark shape was no longer where Shade had stood.

Neither was Shade.

Elizabeth caught Milo before his hurt leg folded under him. Beyond the failed brass frame, Pathwell lay among ground that looked suddenly ordinary.

There was no way to tell, from the place where she stood, whether Shade had tried to get free.

She held Milo upright.

He was breathing.

## Immediate authoring observations

- **What is legible:** Elizabeth recognizes two live dangers; remembers her dagger; knows rescuing somebody from a blob takes time; chooses to reach Milo. She sees Shade's capture as a surprise rather than a willing approach. She does not ignore a child's warning maliciously.
- **What is deliberately unavailable:** Shade's interior recognition of his own squandered chance, whether he physically resists afterward, and whether Pathwell watches. Elizabeth cannot narrate these from the archive.
- **Test-specific assumptions:** The boundary's visible strain, observer's bucket errand, brief sightline from frame to archive, and Stansbury's offscreen position are provisional staging devices, not established facts.
- **Primary failure risk:** The reader may reasonably ask why Elizabeth does not attempt even one cut, call for a capable helper more effectively, or revisit Shade once Milo is free. The sample does not solve Stansbury's equipment or the precise time between Shade's capture and his disappearance.
- **Provisional result:** Strongest continuity with the book's Elizabeth POV; only **partial success** at expressing Shade's emotional climax. No promotion.
