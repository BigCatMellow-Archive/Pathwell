## Test A — minimal exterior cutaway, no access to Shade's thoughts

> Assumed to run as a *clearly separated*, brief exterior beat while Elizabeth is inside freeing Milo. Does not rewrite the actual Milo rescue, which retains its full uninterrupted internal action in the current manuscript.

* * *

The man tried once more to pull Shade free. His hands slid against the green jacket, leaving two black streaks on the cloth.

He stepped back, looking at the thing climbing Shade's leg.

A bucket lay where the man had dropped it. Shade's right hand was still stretched toward Pathwell. With his left, he picked up the handle.

"Here."

The man looked at the bucket.

"They need water."

The man took it and ran toward the line.

Shade stood by the broken frame. The dark mass continued up his coat. He was looking at the archive doorway, not at Pathwell.

* * *

### Cold logic read
- Readers see a **small final useful choice**, not a bold last stand or speech. It can be understood as acceptance: he has stopped a visibly failing tug and uses the arm still free.
- But nobody can responsibly conclude from external action alone the exact thought “I didn't become who Pathwell could be.” The reader may infer grief, regret, a change of attention, or simply practical triage. **That ambiguity is legitimate but must be intentional.**
- The cutaway still interrupts Elizabeth, and no actual novel convention has been formally chosen; Chapter 11 has an exterior scene after Elizabeth departs but is not blanket permission for unlimited viewpoint changes.
- The man helping cannot vanish: he tried twice, failed to free Shade, and returns to the water line. No magical weapon demonstrated at hand. The author is not claiming that a magically assisted rescue was impossible.

## Test B — same action with no exterior cutaway (Elizabeth-only control)

Elizabeth sees the first failed pull and Shade's attempted movement toward the burning archive **before she goes in**. She then enters and rescues Milo without interruption. Outside, his choice after capture and his private realization are deliberately unknown; she may emerge to discover only that Shade is gone.

**Gain:** strongest protagonist focus and fewest camera disruptions.  
**Loss:** Shade's newly authored Q52–53 inner turn gets **no direct dramatic expression** in Chapter 15 beyond the earlier attempt to help and Camp scenes. The reader may reasonably miss it. One must accept the mystery or seed it more forcefully in earlier scenes without invalidating his still-consuming anger from Q41.

## Test C — compressed Shade interior (EXP-0003A)

The hybrid Shade-focused beat shows his memory of kindness (Milo's game, Mama letting him stay, helping with onions) and his unchosen different future, followed by passing the bucket. There is no speech addressed to Pathwell.

**Gain:** his regret and change of priorities are readable, not guessed.  
**Loss:** it gives the scene a late, explicit interior witness, risks over-explaining the theme, and could feel like Shade's tragic recognition rather than Elizabeth's difficult rescue is the entire climax. **Must be very short if selected.**

### Adversarial objections before a rewrite

1. **A dying man is not obliged to help others.** If the story's emotional test becomes “Shade earned dignity by doing a useful chore before dying,” it fails the character and the novel's human-choice thesis. His ordinary life has already included good decisions; regret is his, not the narrator's moral verdict.
2. **Accepting capture isn't choosing death.** A blob at shin height is not yet proven unavoidable; the attempted pull failing plus Elizabeth's rescue window makes aid difficult, not metaphysically impossible. Do not make death morally proper because Shade is angry.
3. **Don't manufacture a bucket icon.** A brief action may feel like an obvious "thematic device" if the prop rolls into his hand too neatly. The rescue-window prose already uses a worker dropping a bucket to try pulling Shade; retain only if natural in full staging.
4. **Whose chapter?** The decisive long passage belongs to Elizabeth and Milo. If the exterior insert is longer or more pointed than her rescue, the “restrained Elizabeth” ruling will be defeated at the level of reading experience.
5. **Pathwell caused the archive fire.** Shade's final look around is about his own missed potential and attention, not proof he made Pathwell coerce him or ignite a wagon.
6. **Final Shade moment Q54/Q55 still open.** A chosen hybrid would supersede the literal either/or only with author approval; this experiment cannot assign the final action itself.
7. **Book's existing shade deaths are after Milo comes out.** Any new action during Elizabeth's interior rescue requires the larger Chapter 14–16 chronology to be rebuilt, and the prior Milo/Shade final Hearts banter cannot be left in its old place.
