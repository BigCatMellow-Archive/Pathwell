## Hypothesis
One of the two camera strategies will present Elizabeth's child-rescue and Shade's realization coherently while preserving the known story mechanics and author Q51–54 intentions.

## Controlled elements

Both experimental snippets assume:
- Pathwell, not Shade, forced the failed working that produced fresh unclaimed pruning waste, physically knocked the lantern and ignited the archive.
- A cleanup creature was already pursuing Shade's anomaly; fresh spillage strengthens its attraction, while Camp's difficult boundary resists entry.
- Milo is the **one confirmed trapped child**, sent to the archive for the north-route copy before Pathwell's decision. An **unnamed other child** may notice the boundary strain.
- Shade looks toward the fire, genuinely means to help, then is surprised to discover the creature at his shin. He has **not** elected capture.
- Elizabeth remembers the enchanted dagger and knows from Chapter 7 that freeing someone takes several cuts and bodily extraction; Milo needs urgent rescue.
- No ruling has been made on whether Shade keeps attempting to help after capture (Q54), or whether Pathwell watches (Q55). No invented speech of forgiveness, self-sacrifice or final public indictment.

## Close reading: what improves or breaks

### A — Elizabeth-limited

**Works:** Readers experience an actionable dilemma directly. Her body goes toward Shade's dagger, then toward Milo's voice. Passing the diary and cookbook carries the prior thematic decision. The child's incomplete warning is visible to Elizabeth, so the camera does not become omniscient.

**Fails / risk:** Because Shade disappears outside while Elizabeth is rescuing Milo, the story cannot *show* his changed relationship to Pathwell, his decision not to resist, or even whether he had that option. The empty space when she emerges may be emotionally effective, but it underdelivers the key author-defined Q52–53 insight. The thought “There was no way to tell whether Shade had tried to get free” deliberately foregrounds that lack of evidence. A more economical and honest version may leave Shade's final private insight unconfirmed to Elizabeth while preparing the reader with stronger earlier signs.

**Blocking still missing:** Where exactly is Mama Baga? Did anyone answer Elizabeth's warning? If Stansbury has a loaded enchanted baton, why is it not used? Stansbury's offscreen move around the archive cannot silently erase his previously demonstrated competence.

### B — Explicit Shade scene break

**Works:** The regret is based on concrete one-morning experiences: coffee, cards, the jacket, being welcomed without having to be Pathwell. Shade actually tries to help before he notices danger, and the emotional change isn't framed as apology or a lecture. The forced working is clearly Pathwell's act; Shade's regret concerns what he chose to center.

**Fails / risk:** The introspective passage is substantially longer than the depicted crossing from shin upward. The fire rescue is suspended while the narrative inventories Shade's life. Several declarative reflections repeat the theme rather than letting it arise from physical behavior. Direct interior Shade POV might be viable—the novel is not absolutely limited to Elizabeth, and Ch11 follows Stansbury and Pathwell after she leaves—but it must be deliberately established and paced. A single late full interior detour merely to explain the ending risks authorial convenience.

**Blocking still missing:** The reader still doesn't know whether the creature's approach could be interrupted by Stansbury, Mama Baga or the dagger. The passage currently ends before actual removal so it proves the realization's readability, **not** that the complete ending is earned.

### A critical author-intent constraint on *both*

Shade's real Camp opportunity lasts around **one morning**, under a deterministic draw and a real cleanup threat. His judgment that he failed to become the person he wanted to be can be painfully sincere without being an objective sentence that he deserves to die. His wish for a public confrontation is not mechanically the cause of the fire. If the novel equates his self-condemnation with actual responsibility for Pathwell's dangerous nonconsensual choice, it would undermine the whole conflict.

Similarly, Elizabeth choosing Milo is a sound and consequential human act. Presenting her as choosing to *let Shade die* assigns her an obligation to rescue two people simultaneously she might have no way to fulfill. Keep her positive action as rescuing Milo, rather than making guilt about Shade her arc's primary reward.

## Assessment of established detail

- Chapter 14's Milo north-route copy errand is retained in both as the causal setup, though not restated explicitly in prose.
- The flame's lantern origin is retained; the archival emotions are not used as magical cleanup fuel.
- The blob's exceptional forced passage across a moving Camp boundary is treated as **new proposed rule**, not old settled World Bible fact.
- Elizabeth's prior Ch7 dagger use is acknowledged. **Critical unresolved fact:** Stansbury's Ch7 enchanted baton could destroy a blob; Ch14 confirms him arriving with medical box and bag but is not a comprehensive inventory.
- Q54 and Q55 are not answered by either test, as required.
- The **exact rate of absorption and geography** remain unproven: a large blob at shin height during active fire must have physical progression; it cannot invisibly teleport into place.
- The current Ch15 has the familiar Milo/Shade exchange after Milo's escape. That line must be moved earlier, replaced by more modest payoff, or deliberately removed before adopting concurrent peril. The Ch16 naming/record of Shade is available.

## Decision

**Experiment outcome: REVISE BOTH.** Neither sample is ready for the manuscript.

**Safest next drafting target:** Test a *shorter, deliberately framed Shade interior beat* against Elizabeth-only continuity, but do it only after fixing the rescue logistics and establishing whether the book's voice supports a separate Shade-centered scene. Do not choose Q54's physical action by implication. Another viable path is to retain Elizabeth's POV and intentionally leave Shade's interior unknowable—this would change what the audience is allowed to know about the arc and requires the author's judgment.

**Priority gates before implementation:**
1. **Rescue availability:** Show Stansbury's actual items, Mama Baga's position, when the blob seizes Shade and how far Milo is inside. Prove why no other rescue can simply be attempted in available time.
2. **Camera contract:** Decide whether Shade can hold a short independent viewpoint at all, then how to avoid too much explanatory introspection during fast danger.
3. **Character fairness:** Preserve Shade's real desire to help, limited lifespan, and the distinction between regret and Pathwell's objectively caused catastrophe.
4. **Elizabeth's central agency:** Explicitly assess whether her human action fulfills the project's previously recorded requirement to alter the central conflict, without inventing magical powers or blaming her for Shade's death.
5. **Aftermath continuity:** Keep Stansbury's hand injury, saved records, Milo's recovery, Camp naming Shade, and Pathwell's restitution.
