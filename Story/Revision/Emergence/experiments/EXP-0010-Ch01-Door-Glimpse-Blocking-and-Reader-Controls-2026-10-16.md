## Why test this independently?

In the existing opening, the sound reaches them, Pathwell asks “Friend of yours?”, Elizabeth says “What?”, and his attention changes after he smells low tide and sees the door bow. **She never chooses to inspect the threat**, and the first creature's body is wholly unveiled only when it breaks through during the cookbook working.

The author's suggested reversal gives Elizabeth a small independent act: an ordinary person hears an impact at her own door and tries to see whether someone needs help. Pathwell then physically overrules her by shutting the door, showing his pattern of action without consultation. The benefit could be worth the slight sacrifice in monster-reveal suspense; this **has not been established by reader testing**.

The later breaking-through reveal is modified only from “It was some sort of blob…” to **“Now she could see the whole thing: some sort of blob…”**. These are the **three replacements** used for the [complete B trial](EXP-0010B-Ch01-Door-Glance-Chain-Candidate-2026-10-16.txt); they make a coherent scene rather than simply inserting a new glimpse and leaving an impossible second discovery / duplicate “he was on his feet.”

**Physical ledger:** Elizabeth moves to the door from the living room; it had been shut and locked since the party. The chain limits the opening. The wet substance presses into that gap, but has not yet broken into the apartment. Pathwell runs over **with the diary still under his arm**, shuts the door and secures the bolt. He then resumes the frantic search for the cookbook. Later the same door physically fails under the advancing creature. This is stageable without magical protection, extra characters, or telekinesis.

**Information ledger:** The glance reveals only a wet, dark surface — not a full silhouette or a rule about its nature. Neither Elizabeth nor the reader needs to identify the cleanup creature. The blob was already at her door before any cookbook magic was worked. Pathwell still has no reliable reason to infer Elizabeth is marked; the later claim remains his bluff.

### Strong argument *against* applying B

The current Chapter 1 already has **Elizabeth's more personal and causally crucial action**: she rescues Nana's cookbook from a stranger and disrupts an active spell. The first opening would be an investigative action, but might borrow urgency from the more important grab and give away the body-horror reveal too early. The very detail that makes B physically plausible — a chain — also costs sentences and introduces mundane door machinery during a supernatural attack. It should only survive if a fresh reader finds the early agency worth the cost.

### Strong argument *for* B

Elizabeth acts **before** Pathwell controls her responses; she is not simply watching him react to the danger he has imported. The instant refusal to let her look also dramatizes his character. The earlier glimpse and later full reveal create two stages of discovery, making the blob feel physically adjacent rather than arriving through narration.

## Result and recommendation

**B is a valid stageable controlled trial, but not an automatic upgrade.** Keep the canonical A unchanged until the author/reader decides whether the early deliberate action improves Elizabeth, suspense and the book's rhythm. Unlike the notebook cue or cookbook action, this is **not** a nearly consequence-free line edit.

**Recommended order of next work:** (1) obtain the author's impression of this full-context draft; (2) test A/B with uninformed readers or at least a new reader not given the answer; (3) if selected, make those three changes together and inspect scene geography; (4) only then return to the remaining R38 requests: richer first magic, Pathwell's “Good… that's good”, cutting the doubled impact note, and fixing the rogue falling box and bathroom-door blocking.

**No canon approval claimed; no reader panel or independent L4 run has occurred.** The author saying “go for it” in this turn was applied to the **recommended physical cookbook action and creating this door test**, not treated as retrospective approval of a full new door sequence.
