## Control 1: How the strike reads

**Original (live):**

> It darted for the light in the man's hand, where it was met sharply by a blinding strike.
>
> The impact sang.

This is compact, but the subordinate passive construction conceals **whether Pathwell, the page's existing defensive magic, or the creature** initiates the strike. R38 explicitly flagged the awkward clause.

**A — [Active Pathwell movement](EXP-0012A-Ch01-Spell-Strike-Active-2026-10-16.txt):**

> It darted for the light in the man's hand. He thrust that hand forward. Light struck the blob.
>
> The impact sang.

**Informed judgment:** **Best physical clarity.** Pathwell moves the hand already holding the magical light, which physically connects his action, the established glowing page and the creature's recoil. No additional resource or magical talent introduced. **Caveat:** “thrust that hand forward” is workmanlike, and “Light struck” is still a depiction of effect, not a mechanical explanation. The precise protective spell's operation is not established beyond its observed outcome, and the revision should not imply this effect always obeys commands.

**B — [Unattributed flash, no new action](EXP-0012B-Ch01-Spell-Strike-Flash-2026-10-16.txt):**

> It darted for the light in the man's hand. A blinding flash burst between them.
>
> The impact sang.

**Informed judgment:** Smoothest quick delivery and least lore-dense, but it **does not answer the actor question**. It merely rewrites the passive “met sharply” into a flash. If the reader is disoriented by the original's physical sequence, B might be insufficient.

**C — Retain original:** Best if the mystery of whether the light acts by itself is intentional. The author has *not* locked what exact protective mechanism is performed by the page at that instant, and a forced “Pathwell attacks” reading risks inventing intent. A should be **preferred for author review**, not silently adopted while the mechanical ambiguity remains real.

## Control 2: “Good… that's good” versus “Perfect”

The first “Perfect” follows the question about Nana's sugar cookies. The book's last line is **locked** as “Perfect.” in an inverted “Ready?” / “No.” / “Perfect.” exchange. The original July Chapter 1 already used “Perfect” at both locations, so repetition might be **deliberate character voice**, not merely a tic. The author explicitly marked an alternative on Oct 8.

**Live / D0:**

> “My Nana always made the best sugar cookies?”
>
> “Perfect.”

**D1 — [Author-proposed response, one-line replacement](EXP-0012C-Ch01-Good-Thats-Good-Dialogue-2026-10-16.txt):**

> “My Nana always made the best sugar cookies?”
>
> “Good… that's good.”

**Benefits:** Pathwell sounds genuinely occupied with working out whether the recipe is usable, rather than prematurely declaring victory; reserves an especially distinct “Perfect” for the end. **Costs:** Replaces an efficient joke with self-reassurance, and there may be good character reasons that Pathwell would say the wrong, dismissive thing while her home is in danger. Changing both occurrences into an intentional setup/payoff may be stronger than making the final line a unique word.

**Judgment:** **No automatic substitution.** This is the author's Oct 8 dialogue idea and worth an explicit listen for comedic rhythm. Full variant D1 is derived from *current Chapter 1*, changes only the first “Perfect”, and preserves the final one. It can be judged separately from strike A/B (no bundled test that confounds them).
