## Primary observations — ranked for falsifiable follow-up

### F1 — HIGH: the dinner-table books are ambiguously retrieved

**Evidence:** Ch2 says he “dropped her books on the table beside the tray,” then later “stood up, brushing his hands off and gathering up his things.” It ends on “He still has it.” Ch3 confirms the cookbook under one arm and diary under the other.

**Issue:** The pronoun “his” in “his things” could be read as his possessions *excluding* Elizabeth's books, which were last expressly placed on the table. Alternatively, the omission might intentionally place readers in Elizabeth's distracted perspective. Both readings are defensible. **A reader who believes he left the books on the diner table may experience the Ch2 cliffhanger as a continuity trick rather than her realization.** This is an actual object-blocking uncertainty, not a new plot-motive question.

**Control A (current):** Keep the retrieval off-page; read the end of Ch2 without prompting. **Control B (not promoted):** Add a near-invisible action at the point of departure: “He stood, wiped his hands and took the cookbook and diary from the table.” This makes physical transfer certain, **but it may spoil the intentional discovery**, weaken “He still has it” and make Elizabeth seem unobservant. Do not implement B absent unfamiliar reader evidence.

**Discriminating question after Ch2:** “At the end of the diner scene, where do you think the main characters and their belongings are?” Then, **only after that answer**, “What triggered Elizabeth to run after him?” Track whether the reader volunteers that he took the books.

### F2 — MEDIUM/HIGH: the Ch1 target misdirection is intentional but easily mistaken for narrator truth

**Evidence:** Pathwell says “You really don't know?”, then unambiguously asserts that her house “has now been marked, magically speaking.” The creature is visibly at the door **before** the cookbook is touched. She experiences Nana's voice under the spell and Pathwell uses her private nickname in Ch2–3.

**Risk:** A first-time reader may take his early confident tone, Nana-memory knowledge and repeated “Lizzy” as confirmation that **Elizabeth is specially marked or magically selected**. That mistaken belief is an allowable **provisional theory** at Ch3; it becomes a failed reading only if the Ch5–9 correction cannot overturn it. Do **not** make the real target explicit just to “fix” readers making the exact incorrect guess the story sets up.

**Discriminating question after Ch3:** “Which facts about the creature seem certain, and which are based on what another character said?” Ask readers what explanation they expect and how sure they are. Test again after Ch9. Do not insist at Ch3 that they know the hidden solution.

### F3 — MEDIUM/HIGH: why this guest searched *this* flat is only partly established

**Evidence:** Ch1 now gives the notebook-peering party guest, his reading of a diary and hunting an old cookbook. Ch3 shows a broader collection of papers bought for Camp and a shopkeeper challenging whether Camp requested them.

**Reading:** The *kind* of thing Pathwell values is now much easier to infer. **Exactly why he picked Elizabeth's particular apartment/party**, rather than an adjacent building, is still not answered. A reader need not demand his entrance logistics on page one; however earlier simulated readers' “Why her?” complaint was sufficiently common to require actual retesting.

**Discriminating question at end of Ch3:** “What do you think the stranger was doing at the party, before the noises at the door?” Do not call him “the scavenger” in the reader materials; record literal inference and confidence.

### F4 — MEDIUM: consent pressure survives P62 but “choice” can be misread

**Evidence:** Ch1 after the paired cuts still contains Elizabeth's explicit conditional refusal and another interrupted protest. Ch2 Pathwell says “The choice is yours” **while he has custody of what she needs back**. She decides to seek him after remembering the cookbook. Ch3 starts with renewed refusal, and her eventual entry into the Space Between is tied to the books he is holding.

**Reading:** Shorter dialogue does not turn following him into unpressured agreement; the plot keeps her agency constrained and her resistance continuous. But readers may find the repeated jokes about her having no choice funny enough to discount his trespass. The reader needs to recognize that **he created or prolonged the problem** rather than accepting his view that saving her settles everything.

**Discriminating question:** “Why did Elizabeth go after Pathwell after the diner?”; “Where did you feel someone made a choice?” Only in the **editor-only analysis** compare answers to the expected custody/pressured-action distinction.

### F5 — MEDIUM: the first pruning transaction may cause vocabulary overload before consequence is legible

**Evidence:** Ch3 asks for “a Rai Stone,” then “thirty Lydian coins,” then Pathwell offers “potential”; the shopkeeper responds “You did.” The immediate sensory episode is substantial and the closing question “You just sold your potential?” gives a plain-language summary *after* the extended ritual.

**Risk:** Prior [Oct 8 simulated panel](../Reader-Reports/Panel-2026-10-08/Findings-Report.md) flagged the Rai Stone exchange as a reluctant-reader stopper. The current chapter still has the unfamiliar proper nouns and a sustained roots/flames depiction. On a manuscript-only read, the **price is presented before the currency concept is anchored**. A reader may understand that the price was severe without knowing what the terms mean; that may be acceptable for Chapter 3, but a true stumble is plausible.

**Test:** “What was exchanged at the counter, and what did it cost?” and a nonleading attention-drop prompt. Do not add an instructional magic lecture before gathering actual responses.

### F6 — MEDIUM: Pathwell knows “Lizzy” before readers learn how he knows it

**Evidence:** In Chapter 2 he calls her “Lizzy”; she then hears Nana say “Lizzy” as an internal memory echo. In Chapter 3 he answers Elizabeth's rebuke with “Isn't that what your grandmother called you?”

**Interpretations:** It may suggest he absorbed something from the cookbook casting, that he heard the magically released memory, or that he knows more about her than he admits. **The first three chapters do not explain the channel with certainty.** This may fuel the desired misdirection but combines with F2 to create a heightened “he targeted her personally” reading. Do not invent telepathy or past contact to resolve it.

**Test:** “How did you think Pathwell knew what to call her?” If readers are merely curious, keep it. If they confidently infer unplanned prior acquaintance or Ch1 preselection as fact, reconsider when to clarify later.

### F7 — LOW/MEDIUM: chained door response is plausible, but the reader must buy Elizabeth's move

**Evidence:** After a wet thud, she thinks someone may have fallen in the hallway and opens against the chain; something dark and wet is there; Pathwell slams it shut. The same door later bends and breaks.

**Test:** “Did anyone do something you didn't believe they would do?” Do not prompt “Was opening the chained door too reckless?” upfront. This is an author-approved R50 choice, not an automatic edit target.

### F8 — POSITIVE: the first three chapters preserve physical consequence and distinctive voice

**Evidence:** Nana's handwriting turns gold but the recipe page is spent; the first spell is interrupted by an **ordinary physical grab**; Elizabeth loses her domestic normalcy first in the apartment, then in the diner ticket/gutter beat. Pathwell's carelessness with belongings continues through his book custody and unrequested Camp purchase. The shopkeeper returns the cookbook and diary as **never his to spend**; her thumb traces the ragged edge; Pathwell looks away. The last minute of Ch3 puts Elizabeth's ownership back in her hand rather than using magic to unburn the page.

**Protection:** Keep the author's original sensory kitchen memory, the diner “He still has it”, the custody payoff at Ch3, the Page under Pathwell's hand while he uses its protective working, and Ch1's fixed “Ready? / No. / Perfect.” The manuscript can improve readability without explaining what the reader is already able to experience.
