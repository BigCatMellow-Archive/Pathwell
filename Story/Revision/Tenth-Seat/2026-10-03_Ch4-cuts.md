# Minority report: P20, the author's own Chapter 4 cut

## Claim challenged
"A lock or the story's current shape requires leaving out every non-arrival piece of the author's Chapter 4." Disproved for any one piece if the record shows no lock or later chapter excluding it, or shows a record that wants it.

## Assumptions it depends on
1. All the left-out pieces belong to the old fortune-teller errand ("the healing takes their place"). The pass log gives this as the reason; no lock is cited for Mishka, the clothes or the circle.
2. Chapters 2-4 are one night, so the morning goes.
3. No later chapter or ruling needs the dropped pieces.
4. R23 ("existing text and canon preferred over new material") favours the August scenes over his draft.

## Weakest assumption
1 (and so 3). Healing fills the slot; it does nothing Mishka or the clothes did.

## Strongest alternative
Mishka and the clothes come back, because the record wants them. The author ruled on 06-16 "yeah that works, keep it the existing girl" (the hand-taking girl, Story_logic_test ~L2234-2250) for the child Elizabeth runs back for: led in, then she runs to the child, "the same inversion structure as the book's other bookends". Aug C2 §4 locks "the girl" for the runner and the climax child. The author's Ch2 line "Nothing to wear to it" (survives in Ch2 L143) is paid by his Ch4 "Take what you like".

## Evidence that would exist if the alternative is true, and whether it does
- A record the pass ignored. **Yes:** JUN 16 child (Decision-Timeline L50); C2 §4 (L351). Triage 13.2 dismisses "girl" as "belonged to a discarded draft", i.e. his own draft. Zero "AUTHOR" items in the triage, so it was never put to him. W8: a later record that differs from a lock "is put to him, not assumed".
- A planted line left unpaid. **Yes:** Ch2 L143, not in the Ledger.
- A gap a reader would hit. **Partly:** Ch1 L7/L171 puts her in bed, then "shoes and a coat"; the reader notes ask "presumably sleepwear" (1.17); nobody has read Ch5-12 for it, and her clothes are never mentioned again. Q136 leaves ordinary-life logistics open.
- Precedent. **Yes:** R29 ("why did we cut the leath tome, the vow, the fire...?"), the same pattern, reversed.

## Evidence against the alternative
- **Milo is built in.** Ch4, 12-17; Ledger R11 "paid"; Ch15 "His sock had finally stayed up." Swapping to a girl rewrites paid beats (R23 cost), and no record has the author rejecting Milo.
- **Ch4 is the longest chapter** (4,041 words; next 3,831). The cold reader skimmed the catalogue (4.11) and felt the chapter "slow here while I waited for Stansbury" (4.38).
- **Clothes carry a footprint.** Pastel linen, gold chains, a blazer and a bandana would have to appear in Ch5-12; no later chapter mentions any. Q126 shows care through Mama Baga; a stranger's wardrobe is a different shape.
- **Q136** says logistics stay unresolved, which weakens the "hole" claim.
- Nothing in Ch5-18 mentions Mishka, linen, bandana, "Belíssimo", the grandmothers, a bookkeeper-or-amoeba exchange or fortune (grep). No later dependency breaks either way.

## Cost if the claim is wrong
Low and late. A plant stays unpaid and the climax child stays a boy against two author records. If he wants the girl, the rewrite grows with each chapter built on Milo.
