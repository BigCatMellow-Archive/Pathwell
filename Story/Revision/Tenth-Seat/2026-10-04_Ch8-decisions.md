## F2. R11: "It was the crash that woke her." placed bare

**Claim.** The Ch1 line (Chapter_01.txt:3) repeats exactly, in its own paragraph, after the car stops and before the hubcap (Ch8:131), with no narration pointing at it. It is read figuratively: she is awake; the crash is what wakes her to herself. The opener "Elizabeth chose the ditch." (Ch8:3) stays.

**Assumptions.**
1. The reader recognizes the line as Ch1's, seven chapters back.
2. The figurative reading comes without help, although Ch7-8 contain no sleep or waking image (grep for asleep/awake/woke/dream/drows in Ch7-8: none).
3. The hubcap, two lines on, does not undercut it.
4. Ch1's "crash" (now the boxes, R27, Chapter_01.txt:5) and Ch8's crash rhyme rather than clash.

**Weakest: 2.**

The sentence is causally backward for the scene. Ch8 has just spent 120 lines on her choosing; she is awake and in control (hands on the wheel 133, heart 135). A literal-minded reader sees a claim that the impact woke her. In a book with spells and a time-stopped bar, "woke" could even read as magic. The lock itself ("causes the crash that wakes her by choice", refinements §9) is a paradox; the sentence has to carry it alone.

**Strongest alternative.** Place it a beat later, where the waking is visible: after she steps out and "The night was bigger outside the car" (Ch8:231). Or first line of the chapter, ahead of "Elizabeth chose the ditch." (Plan row 8's second candidate, Plan.md:123). Or after the hubcap and the "heart… steering column alive" paragraph (133-135), where her body is the evidence.

**For F2's placement.**
- Right after the impact is the exact parallel of Ch1, where the line follows the noise that woke her. A reader who remembers Ch1 will hear the rhyme there and nowhere else.
- The self-correction is built in: 133-135 ("kept both hands on the wheel… heart… steering column feel alive") shows her alert within three lines. A one-second literal flicker that then turns figurative is a good reading.
- R27 improved the rhyme. Ch1's crash is now boxes Pathwell knocked over; Ch8's is a car she drove. Acted upon, then acting. That is the lock's point, and it needs no narration.
- Opening with the line would pre-empt "chose" and the reveal that she did it on purpose. The opener reads as the active-voice answer to Ch1 ("Elizabeth chose the ditch." vs. "It was the crash that woke her."); the middle echo then completes the pair. Registry.md:70 already lists the opener and the closer ("Too late to call it an accident.") as the chapter's linked phrases.
- The triage placed it after "Stopped." (triage.md:128).

**Against F2's placement.**
- The hubcap joke (131: "fell over in the grass") is the book's dry deflation; the lock says the crash is "an awakening rather than a triumphant empowerment beat", and a joke after it helps that. But only if the line has room first: "Stopped." / line / "Dust moved…" / "Nobody spoke." / hubcap puts three beats between them; the line directly before the hubcap puts none.
- F2 says "after the car stops and before the hubcap". That leaves 125-129 ("Stopped." / "Dust moved through the headlights." / "Nobody spoke.") ambiguous. I read the best order as: **"Stopped." then the line, then the dust and the silence as its ring, then the hubcap.**
- F5 is the real threat. If F5 contracts or "tells-cuts" 133-135, the sentence loses its self-correction. And "She had time to choose. / That mattered." (7-9) is exactly the kind of narration pointing at the theme the lock forbids; cutting it is right, and it should not be replaced.

**Cost if wrong.** One paragraph moves. The loss is the line landing as a stumble in a chapter whose point is that line. Cheap to fix; expensive to notice, since the author asked for it ("I'd like to repeat it", R11) and will read it first.

**Verdict: YELLOW (holds). Do it if:**
- (a) The line is the exact Ch1 sentence, one paragraph, directly after "Stopped." (125), with the dust, the silence and the hubcap following.
- (b) Nothing within about ten lines on either side names waking, choosing or deciding. "That mattered." (9) goes; "Not injured. / Just late to the decision." (227-229) is far enough away.
- (c) F5 keeps 133-135 (hands, heart, "steering column feel alive"). That paragraph is what turns "woke" from literal to figurative.
- (d) The reviser reads lines 119-135 aloud once with the line in both places (after "Stopped." and after 135) and keeps the one with no literal flicker. If neither passes, fall back to the chapter's first line.
- (e) Promise-Ledger R2 (line 46) is marked paid, and Registry row 8 gets the echo listed.
