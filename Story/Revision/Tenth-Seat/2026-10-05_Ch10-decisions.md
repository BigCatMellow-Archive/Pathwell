# Minority report: Chapter 10 decisions

## G1. Rebuild the brothers' half from the author's draft

**Claim.** Replace Ch10:62-517 and 550-618 with a rebuild from draft:1866-1912 and 1941-1955, keeping the current spell mechanics, arriving at the house "well before midnight", holding the spell until dawn (Pathwell's hands still shake, so Stansbury makes him eat and rest), and having Stansbury carry the foam box out of the wreck.

**Assumptions.**
1. W14 covers a half-chapter rebuild, not only voice and small things.
2. The draft's scene transplants. It was written for drunk brothers in a bar, a mind-reading nudge, and a catch "ten minutes" after the diner coffee (draft:1941).
3. The kept current mechanics and the kept draft lines fit together.
4. The timeline fix works: arrive before midnight, spell at dawn, "rest" as the reason.
5. The result stays inside the locks' size limits ("keep the house/library setup brief", Continued 10; "very brief ... Pathwell-limited exception", chapter matrix, W10 POV constraint).
6. The author will take the result as his own text.

**Weakest: 4, with 5 behind it.**

The clock. The book gives: sunset at the bar (Ch6:278), the diner less than an hour after the crash (Ch9:198), "still full night" when she leaves it (Ch9:370), and "She had been walking for most of the night" (Ch10:3). Arrival at about eleven and a spell at first grey is roughly seven hours. The reason offered is Pathwell's shaking hands (Ch7:117), which explains an hour, not a night. During those hours the man who says "We have to find Elizabeth" (draft:1867, 1877) and who stood in the road "less angry than afraid" (Ch8:383) eats and rests. Triage 8.8 (Revision/Currency-Audit-2026-10-01/triage.md:126) kept the roadside standoff partly because it "explains why the brothers need to find her in Ch10, so the stakes hold". The draft's own joke at the catch, "I just didn't expect it to work so soon" (draft:1947), is false in this timeline.

The deeper problem is where the clock error comes from. Ch10:70 now has the brothers reach the kitchen at grey dawn after a two-mile walk, which is the bug G1 fixes. That bug exists because P32 moved the Space Between coffee recall out of the diner and onto the road (Pass-Log.md:1464, 1477). The lock says the opposite: "At the diner, Elizabeth's notably bad coffee naturally recalls the awful coffee ... the simultaneity is therefore causally motivated ... rather than arbitrary coincidence" (Continued 7, LOCKED A; Decisions.md:90 lists it as binding). P32 recorded the move only as an R36 lean (Pass-Log.md:1477), with no W-row, and no answer from the author is recorded. G1 and G4 now build a fix on top of it. With the recall hours after the diner, the "motivation" is a lingering taste (Ch10:19, 531), which is closer to the coincidence the lock was written to avoid. G1's claim that the current mechanics "match the locks" is therefore not quite true for the simultaneity.

## G2. "Leo" and "big cat" / "grande gatto"

**Claim.** Introduce Stansbury's first name, used only by Pathwell, and Stansbury's old nickname for Pathwell, because both are the author's inventions "and nowhere else in the book"; list them for him.

**Assumptions.**
1. "Both ... nowhere else" is accurate, so they are his and unconstrained by anything in the record.
2. The book can take a first name for one brother and a nickname for the other without contradicting its naming habits.
3. Two or three uses in one scene will read as texture, not as set-up.
4. "Leo" is unambiguously Stansbury.

**Weakest: 3 (the one-off), with 1 needing correction.**

**Corrections to the claim.**
- The draft uses "Leo" three times, not two: draft:1869, 1879 and 1890 ("Children's stories? Leo you musnt be so desperate"). G1's own keep list contains all three.
- "Leo" is not only in the draft. Archive/Notes-2026-06-to-08/Pathwell_convenience_paradox.md:1637 has the author's own June note: "Leo Stansbury is the brother of Pathwell." That makes the name his from the start, which strengthens G2. Archive/Manuscript/Review_Notes.txt:41 also says "Leo and the ledger scene: cut" (an older Ch4); I could not tell whether that Leo is Stansbury, so I rely only on the June note.
- "big cat" / "grande gatto" appears only at draft:1884-1886. No chapter, bible or registry file has either (grep, whole repo).

**Strongest alternative.** Do not introduce them, and let "Stansbury" stay the only name, as in every chapter (Ch5:149, 262; Ch11 onward) and in the glossary (glossary.md:14, 60: surnames are the old practitioners' names). The case: the book's own Ch2 gag is a man with one name (Ch2:127-133, "First name?" / "Pathwell." / "Last name?" / "Also Pathwell, if the situation becomes formal."), and a first name for his brother muddies it. A nickname with "again" in it promises a history; the draft never pays it, and neither would the book.

**Evidence for G2.**
- It is the author's own name for the character (June note), used in his own draft, in exactly the intimate moments (earnest, teasing, rueful).
- "Please don't start calling me that again." / "Too late, grande gatto." is history in a line, which is the job the matrix gives the brothers: "show Pathwell/Stansbury history without exposition dump" (chapter matrix, W05 setup/payoff jobs). It matches the June note's "fallouts and come back together more times than they can remember".
- Cheap and reversible; listing it for the author (R36) costs nothing.

**Evidence against.**
- **Unpaid.** Both appear in this one scene and in no later draft line (draft grep). Craft rule 3 ("make it matter before it's lost", Sunday-Morning/Craft.md:172) and the ledger discipline say a planted name should come back. The Ledger has no row for it.
- **The cat.** The book's only cat is the Space Between's (Ch3:75-93, which draws her blood and "licked its chops"; Ch18:220-246, 397, 723). Pathwell's own working goes through "Orange cat." and "The cat." (Ch10:27, 370). A nickname "big cat" on Pathwell, a few hundred words before that memory, invites a link nobody planned (Pathwell as the shop's creature). Also, Leo is a lion; the nickname goes to Pathwell, not to Leo. Deliberate swap or accident, the author should say.
- **Italian in a book with none.** One phrase, "grande gatto", is the only Italian in the chapters (I did not find another). Harmless in a nickname, but "keeps people small, corrects people" Stansbury would more likely say "gattone". Minor, the author's call.
- **Registry.** Registry.md:52 lists open letters and L is already taken; no clash, but a name table row and glossary row are needed.

**Cost if wrong.** Two lines come out of one scene; the cat read, if it appears, is a reader's question, not a break. Cheapest decision of the six.

**Verdict: YELLOW (holds). Do it if:**
- (a) The record is corrected: "Leo" is the author's own name for Stansbury (June note), used three times in the draft; "big cat" is draft-only.
- (b) "Leo" appears only in Pathwell's speech, never in narration or in Elizabeth-POV, and each use has a narration tag or an unmistakable addressee (the draft's "She is our responsibility Leo" has one).
- (c) The nickname exchange stays as the draft has it, without explanation, and sits well away from the Ch10 "The cat." memory. If it cannot, drop "big cat" before "Leo".
- (d) It is listed for the author as "unpaid on purpose or not": one later use (Ch15-17, Stansbury and the scar) would pay it, and he decides.
- (e) Registry and glossary get a row.

**Reopening indicators.** The author wants Stansbury kept surname-only; a reader reads "big cat" as the shop's cat; a reader asks who Leo is.

## G3. The letter: a lovesick kid's, confiscated at school, replacing the Ellison estate letter

**Claim.** Replace the "ELLISON ESTATE" scene (Ch10:169-257: a woman's six years of unsent letters, "copy before I buy") with the draft's letter, "confiscated off some lovesick kid" (draft:1878, 1893). It fits Stansbury's school job (Ch5), neither Ellison nor the "copy before I buy" ethic is in the author's text or the locks, and the cost beat survives in one exchange ("If I use this, it's gone.").

**Assumptions.**
1. The kid's letter carries the same weight in the story: "smaller and just as real".
2. Nothing downstream rests on the Ellison scene except the scene itself.
3. It fits Ch5: Stansbury "keeps what kids would throw away".
4. A confiscated minor's letter spent without asking is morally neutral in the chapter where the Ask follows.
5. The author prefers his throwaway line to a scene the readers called the best magic in the book.

**Weakest: 2, then 4.**

**2. A dependency G3 does not list: R9.** Promise-Ledger.md:53 (R9): "Stansbury's 'copy before you buy'; the information is not the thing", planted Ch10, paid Ch11 and Ch17. The plant is Ch10:225-249: "If I use this, it's gone." / "The copy keeps the words." / "Not the thing." / "You asked me once why I copy before I buy." / "It makes the cost mine to approve." The pay-offs: Ch11:1209-1211 ("The information can be copied from the museum scans." / "The information."); Ch17:385-397 ("FACSIMILE ... The information survived. / The page did not."); Ch17:164 ("Copy the smudge", which the Assessment lists among the lines to protect, Assessment-2026-09-29.md:42). The world rule it demonstrates is locked (Bible decisions 08-21:145-146, "Information can sometimes be copied; the original human record cannot") and written up in world_bible.md:174-180. G3 keeps only "If I use this, it's gone." A confiscated note was not bought, so "copy before I buy" cannot move across as written. Without a relocated plant, Stansbury's "The information." in Ch11 is an echo of nothing.

Also depending on it: Ch10:663 ("A letter that deserved better, two grocery lists, and the worst cup of coffee I've had this century", a Cold-Read pull-quote, :374) and Ledger O10 and the Registry Ch10 row.

**The cost weight.** Cold-Read:408 reads Ch11's museum disaster as "the point" because Ch10 shows Pathwell understanding the cost of originals ("If I use this, it's gone"). That reading needs a letter with something at stake. A schoolchild's crush note is a small cost by design ("smaller"), so Pathwell's pause carries less, and Ch11's reversion loses a rung. Triage 8.7 (triage.md:125) itself calls the letter "Stansbury's irreplaceable letter".

**4. The moral grammar.** The scene is the foil for the Ask in the same chapter. In the Ellison scene the chain of consent is whole (she wrote, the niece sold, Stansbury bought and copied, and "the cost [is] mine to approve"). The Ask then condemns the opposite: paying with your own future and deciding for people who did not ask (Ch10:983-987; Continued 10 on Shade's accusation). A letter Stansbury took from a child and Pathwell burns for convenience reproduces the sin one scene early, and a joke ("lovesick kid") hides it. It also sits against Ch5:258, where Stansbury defends himself with "I don't steal anything. The kids get their work back." Mitigation worth noting: Stansbury is centuries old (canon), so "some kid" could be long dead, but only if the page says so; a present-day school (Ch5) suggests the opposite.

**3. The Ch5 fit.** Ch5:252-258 has him say he has not taught in years and that kids get their work back; nothing in Ch5 has him keeping or confiscating anything. "Keeps what kids would throw away" is the draft's idea (draft:1890, the yellow children's papers), not Ch5's.

**Strongest alternative.** "Both, minimally": keep the Ellison scene (existing text, protected, R9-bearing, locks-compliant) and add the author's lines around it: "Children's stories? Leo ..." and "you really are terrible at this" as the banter, and his "lovesick kid" as Stansbury's offhand description of a different item in the box (the one Pathwell does not use). This honours W14's "adapted as little as the lock needs": the lock needs an unrequited-love letter, and Ellison is one. R23 (Decisions.md:47) points the same way: prefer the existing text to new material.

**Evidence for G3.**
- The Ellison scene is the 08-28 rewrite, not the author's text (W14, W9), and it is new emotional text written by the reviser, the category Craft marks "AI is cautious with" (Sunday-Morning/Craft.md:192-193). Its sentences ("A woman's patience worn raw around one particular absence. A porch in summer. The idea of a train arriving.", Ch10:302) carry the register the watch-list flags.
- The draft's wording ("a letter of unrequited love", draft:1893) matches Continued 10's "unrequited-love/longing letter" exactly; Ellison is not in any lock.
- The kids' papers in the draft library (draft:1890) agree with Ch5's children's-work source (world_bible.md:174).
- Ellison also adds to the E-name crowd (Registry.md:52-55 expects Ellen/Ellison); dropping it removes the clash.

**Evidence against.** R9; Cold-Read:374 and :408; Assessment-2026-09-29.md:42 (protect list) and :116 ("Keep the working whole"); R23; Ch5:258. None of the above is an author ruling, so the swap is allowed under W14, but it is the one G1 consequence with a three-chapter dependency list.

**Cost if wrong.** If Ch11 and Ch17 lose their plant, two protected payoffs go flat and a Ch11 reading the cold reader found clear gets harder; the repair is a new plant somewhere else in Ch10 (cheap if done now, a re-open of three chapters if found later). If the swap is wrong in taste only, one scene is restored from this repository.

**Verdict: ORANGE as written.** The evidence cannot show the kid's letter does the Ellison letter's three jobs (the R9 plant, the weight Ch11 relies on, the foil for the Ask). Narrow the claim: keep Ellison and use the author's lines as banter. Move to YELLOW if all of these hold:
- (a) The R9 plant is carried over or relocated in Ch10: "The copy keeps the words." / "Not the thing." and a plain reason Stansbury copies (he has copied the note before it goes in the box, or he copies everything).
- (b) The kid's note has one plain line that makes "If I use this, it's gone" cost something: the only trace of someone, who or how long ago.
- (c) The page settles whose it was and why Stansbury still has it (long gone, never claimed), so it does not contradict Ch5:258 or the Ask.
- (d) The new wording is the author's own, flagged plain, not new emotional prose from the reviser.
- (e) Ch10:663, Ledger O10 and R9, the Registry Ch10 row and the Assessment protect-list entry are updated in the same pass.
- (f) The author is shown both: his kid's letter or the Ellison letter, as an R36 lean he can overrule.

**Reopening indicators.** The author asks for Ellison back; the Ch11 "The information." line reads as unearned in the next read; a reader finds the kid's letter a throwaway.
