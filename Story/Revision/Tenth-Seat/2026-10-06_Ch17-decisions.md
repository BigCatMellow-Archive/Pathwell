# Minority report: Chapter 17 decisions

## K1. The museum section in Elizabeth's view (overrides triage 17.1)

**Claim, stated so it can be proven wrong.** Opening the museum section with why Elizabeth is there, seating her at a side table writing her statement, and running the scene as she sees and hears it (a) is required by Q140 and not merely permitted, (b) loses nothing the Henry Vale refusal needs, so "No," / "No," he repeated still reads as a fight he wins, (c) leaves no callback in Ch18 broken, and (d) cures the cold reader's can't-follow.

**Assumptions it depends on.**
1. Q140's exceptions are exactly two (the bar mirror and the coda prune) and a third needs a new lock or a "genuine structural need" the reviser cannot self-certify.
2. The refusal's weight comes from what is visible (the pause, the empty case, the packet going back in the bag, the curator's "No.", Stansbury's stopped fingers) and from what Elizabeth and the reader already know of his old answer, not from the four-line interior.
3. Elizabeth can know the old answer. The page has to have taught her.
4. The Elizabeth-in-the-room geometry does not falsify lines K1 leaves alone ("No request that Elizabeth ... sit beside Pathwell while he felt bad", Stansbury's "standing").
5. The scans, photographs and catalog copies he has been supplying can come from Stansbury or the curator without losing the setup the packet offer needs.
6. Ch18 (and the Ledger) call back nothing from the museum scene that K1 moves or cuts.

**Weakest: 2, with 5 close behind.** Assumption 2 is the one K1's own risk note asks. The scene as written does two things at once. It shows the visible beat (offer, the curator's "No.", the stop, "No," he repeated, the packet back in the bag, "May I donate them separately?"), and it narrates the temptation ("The old answer was already assembled behind his teeth. / Same family. Same period. Genuine originals. Better than an empty case."). Take away the second and the first has to carry.

**Strongest alternative.** Keep the scene Pathwell-limited, but stage it as a clean section (Q140c: "POV switches should be clearly staged at scene/section boundaries"), with Elizabeth absent for the whole of it and no "Then Elizabeth walked past" in the last paragraph. A capable defender would say: the refusal is his private choice and is "itself the dramatic event"; the author's own Ch10 brothers' half is Pathwell-limited (W17; Assessment:113, "the library and van sections are the brothers' (ruling E1)"); and the Assessment itself says this refusal "should carry the chapter". Under this alternative the blur is cured by staging alone and the interior stays. It is the only alternative that keeps the interior line.

## K3. How she reaches Camp

**Claim, stated so it can be proven wrong.** One line, "She went the way she had the first night, through the Space Between and down the dirt road; the shopkeeper had stopped asking her business after the second time.", placed at "Elizabeth came by Camp that afternoon because she wanted to.", uses established places only, invents no rule, and answers the cold reader's question.

**Assumptions.**
1. Ch3-Ch4 establish the Space Between to dirt road to Camp route, and it is still usable.
2. Elizabeth can pass through the Space Between without Pathwell.
3. The shopkeeper can have "stopped asking" without contradicting any later scene.
4. The line does not collide with Ch18's coda setup.
5. Camp is where it was.

**Weakest: all of 2, 3 and 4. They all fail on the page.**

### What I tested

**Does anything on the page establish that the Space Between's door or the dirt road takes her without Pathwell?** No.
- Ch3:29-33 (the door at the wall) is made by Pathwell pressing a folded page against concrete; the shopkeeper's own door in the shop (:195-197) is his. Ch3 ends inside the shop (:277). Ch4 begins on the road (:9) with the handcart and, at :15, "after the shopkeeper had offered to send her back to the sidewalk." So the page shows the exit to the sidewalk offered and the exit to the forest taken, with the mechanism of the forest exit never shown. The "dirt road" is Ch4's first image; how they got there is a cut. The cold reader's question ("Thresholds require a practitioner ... Can she open them? Does someone escort her?", Cold-Read, Ch17 "Confused") is the right one and the page does not answer it.
- The page's other thresholds are all operated by practitioners: Pathwell's portal out of Camp (Ch5:3-13, after "Pathwell wouldn't open anything near the wagons"), Shade's opening of the museum wall (Ch11:277-311, Ch12:5-11; lock C2§13a, Timeline:362: "Do not give Elizabeth solo threshold expertise"), and Pathwell's brass tag in Ch18 (:153-183).
- The Interview says the same. 08-22 §1 (Timeline:141): access depends on the threshold's own conditions, and a non-practitioner can use a valid threshold, but Space Between thresholds are not necessarily two-way (:142) and "established anchors are much more reliable than temporary or improvised thresholds" (:143). Camp moves (08-21 §2, Timeline:83); Q114 (Timeline:553) puts the exit near Camp's current approach, not a fixed place. The first-night route was a Pathwell-made door, so "the way she had the first night" is not a route she owns.

**Does it contradict Chapter 18?** Yes, in five places.
- Ch18:3: "Take me to the Space Between." If she had been through the shop several times a week, the request is odd.
- Ch18:153: "The entrance was not the same alley. / Elizabeth had expected it to be." The first-night entrance is not repeatable on the page.
- Ch18:218-232: "The Space Between had not improved its coffee. / Elizabeth learned this within four minutes."; the shopkeeper "took in Elizabeth, Pathwell behind her ... and frowned. / 'What happened?'" / "I dislike novelty." The shopkeeper is greeting a return, not a regular.
- Ch18:250 ("He's mellowed.", the cat) and :283 ("The first time she had been here, the shelves had pressed other people's feeling against her"): she has not been back.
- Ch18:762-786: "How do I get out?" / "Where?"; "The first time, she had been offered the sidewalk or a forest she had not chosen until the choice was finally put in front of her."; "What routes can you actually get us back from?" She depends on Pathwell for routes.
Of these, the shopkeeper clause ("had stopped asking her business after the second time") is the worst: it tells the reader she has been in the shop at least three times.

**Does it break a lock?** Yes. Q137 (Timeline:620; Interview CONTINUED_65, § Q137): "Elizabeth does **not** need an independently operable Space Between threshold, a special token, or a newly invented Camp route." "Do not manufacture a special Elizabeth-only access mechanism." And "Elizabeth asks Pathwell to take her to the Space Between, and Pathwell does so." K3's line is an Elizabeth-only access mechanism, stated as routine. Q137b (Timeline:621) says access assistance is not the same as choosing, which is the lock's own answer to the cold reader's worry. Writing Principles 17 (rules are sacred once established) is the principle the reviser's risk note names, and it is right.

**The other options K3 raises.**
- *Stansbury's car.* The page does not support it. Camp is not on a drivable road: in Ch10:582-586 Pathwell says "There isn't a reliable threshold from here" and they drive to the museum for the wall; Q114 rejects mundane travel (Option C). The van gets her to a place with a threshold, not to Camp. Stansbury is also never shown opening a threshold.
- *The museum's graffiti wall.* It is a real route (Ch10:586; Ch12:5-7; Ch18:776, "available through the proper route") but it needs a practitioner (Shade opened it, Ch11:277-311; Ch18:776 puts it "after hours"), it sits in the gallery under repair and in the curator's custody, and K1 puts the curator in that gallery. Using it adds a quiet new fact about who can open it and when.
- *A Camp guide.* The bible allows it (08-21 §2: "a mixture of guides, signs, known routes, and established thresholds", Timeline:83) but nothing on the page shows one, and Camp's own people are not shown leaving Camp.
- *Pathwell.* He is the only person the page shows opening thresholds for her (Ch3, Ch5, Ch18). It is lock-legal (Q137b), but if he ferries her the three-week "because she wanted to" (:142) is shadowed by him carrying her through, the Q137 ask in Ch18 loses some of its first, and Q134b ("does not ... use restitution as leverage") gets a small pressure.
- *Silence.* Nothing in the locks asks for the line. Q136 (Timeline, Q136; Interview CONTINUED_65, § Q136) says her logistics "need not" be resolved. Q137 says Ch18 is where her dependence on his access is shown, and Ch18:786 does exactly that.

**The cross-decision effect.** K4 keeps the last image: "She looked toward the far end of Camp where the road bent between the trees." (Ch17:843). If K3 has her walking that road in and out, the closing "somewhere strange" is less strange.

**Evidence for K3.** The cold reader's question is real and the Plan row asks for a line; Ch4:9 and Ch18:770 ("a forest") do put a road and a forest on the page.

**Evidence against K3.** Ch18:3, :153, :218-232, :250, :283, :762-786; Q137 and Q137b; lock C2§13a; 08-21 §2; 08-22 §1; Q114; Ch5:3-13 (leaving Camp took Pathwell's portal).

**Cost if wrong.** Moderate and silent. The line would pass a single read and then contradict Ch18's opening at the one place the book is most careful about her agency, which is the Q137 ask. It would also teach the reader a rule (she can come and go) that Ch18 then breaks. The Writing Principles 17 test fails.

**Verdict: RED as written.** The line contradicts Ch18 and breaks Q137. It becomes GREEN if it is replaced by either of these (the reviser's choice):
- (1) **No line.** Close the Plan's "a line on how she reaches Camp" by noting that Q137 and Ch18:762-786 answer it (she depends on Pathwell for routes, and says so), and record this in the Ledger. Cost: the cold reader's question stays open for one chapter.
- (2) **One line that says she was brought and names no route.** It must: say someone with the means took her (so it does not read as a solo threshold); not name the Space Between, the dirt road, the shopkeeper, the museum wall, or a vehicle; not set a pattern ("after the second time", "as usual"); and not make Pathwell the only ferryman by implication, since nothing else on the page can open a door. If the reviser cannot write it without a name, choose (1).
Under either, ":144 'That distinction had become easier to notice once nobody was carrying her books away from her.'" is the line that carries her agency, and it needs nothing added.

**Reopening indicators.** The author wants an explicit route on the page; the Ch18 pass changes the Q137 ask or the shopkeeper's greeting; a reader asks "wait, can she go there on her own?".
