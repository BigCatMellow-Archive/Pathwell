# Minority report: Chapter 14 decisions

## M1. Her "Let it fail" is what makes him let go after release

**Claim, stated so it can be proven wrong.** After the payment has released, Elizabeth's "Let it fail." / "I can hold it." / "That's what you said at the museum." (Ch14:1091-1097) is followed by Pathwell taking his hands off the loop. The failure still happens physically (frame screams, pins tear out, collapse toward him, loop into the lantern). What her line changes is that he stops forcing the settlement, "so Shade isn't folded into him." This gives the 2026-09-18 rule ("Elizabeth's final major choice must materially alter the central conflict") "a real answer without making her a magician," and keeps the locks: she only speaks, he decides, the failure stays his.

**Assumptions.**
1. Shade being folded in is a live outcome that Pathwell's hands-on grip would have produced, so that removing her line changes what happens to Shade.
2. The failure can have a physical cause without his grip.
3. His letting go does not make her the decider (the witness lock) or turn Pathwell into someone who stopped (W15: "No one else causes the failure for him").
4. A late lift in Ch14 does not soften the lift in Ch18 ("Nothing had released." / "Then lifted his hand.").
5. R37's "her 'Let it fail' is what makes Pathwell take his hands off" can be shown on the page by sequence alone.
6. The 09-18 test ("if the choice is removed, the climax should unfold differently") is met.

**Weakest: 1 and 6, which are the same assumption. 3 is the one that makes the others expensive.**

**Strongest alternative.** Drop M1. Keep "Let it fail" as the ignored line the locks and the audit treat it as (Assessment B3: "ignored ... as intended"; Assessment:143; triage 14.1: the scene "is a run of refusals he overrides one by one ... That is the tragedy"). Let option 2 (Ch15, what she carries out of the fire sets off Shade's locked reason) carry the 09-18 rule, since it is the only half of R37 where removing her choice changes what happens. Record Q-E honestly as "option 2 and 3b; option 1 not taken, because it cannot change the outcome inside the locks", and put the rule, not M1, to the author. Chapter 14's other work (M3, M4, M5, and M2 if kept) stands.

If the beat is kept anyway, the defensible version is not "she makes him"; it is "he yields too late and it changes nothing", which is what 09-18 §10 says the book is about ("Causing a problem does not guarantee that he can repair it, and understanding the harm does not make the harm reversible"). That is a craft decision the reviser can make; it is not an answer to the rule.

### Does it change the outcome? Is Shade "folded in" in the current text?

No, and the current text already has the result M1 claims to buy.

- Ch14:1111-1117: "The frame collapsed toward him. / Not toward Shade. / Not toward Elizabeth. / Toward the practitioner who had supplied the contradiction and the payment." This is with his hands on the loop, in the unmodified text.

- Ch15:545-547, 645-647: Shade's right hand "open toward him. Still. Even now." and still "pointed at Pathwell" after the failure.
- The Plan says the same thing: option 1 row, "the frame collapses toward him (as it does now) instead of settling Shade into him" (Plan.md:78). The thing M1 avoids is already not happening.
- Assessment B3 (Assessment-2026-09-29.md:32): "the Pathwell-Shade outcome would run the same without [her climax choice]." M1 does not touch that sentence.
- The Plan's own counterfactual for "her choice removed" is "the working keeps trying to fold Shade in until it breaks; whether Shade survives that as himself is unclear" (Plan.md:78). "Unclear" is not "different". On the page the answer is already decided: he survives as himself, and he must, because Ch15 needs him standing and choosing (Ch15:543-883). A Ch14 in which Shade could be folded in would break Ch15's setup.
- The locks do not leave a branch for it. Q87 (Timeline:479): "the payment cannot settle into the intended working → malformed spillage/recoil erupts from Pathwell." Q145c (Timeline:648): "the malformed failure is still caused by Pathwell forcing a prune through a fundamental contradiction in intent, not by another character interrupting the magic." So M1 faces a dilemma it does not state:
- If her line changes nothing about Shade, M1 fails the 09-18 test (remove it and the climax runs the same), and R37's "but Shade isn't folded in" is a sentence about a risk that never existed on the page.

That is a mechanism no lock sets up, it makes Shade endangered twice in two chapters, and it conflicts with Q87/C28 and with Ch15, where the blob's reason for taking Shade is the unresolved anomaly and the fresh spill, not what Pathwell's hands did (Ch15:609-683).

### Does Shade's headshake speak for her? Does it read as a refused solution?

Both risks are real, and neither is fatal.

Evidence it reads as a refused solution:
- Ch7:49-69: the dagger cut "a seam" in the blob and freed Pathwell ("Not flesh. A seam."). Ch5:242-244: "Adhesions. Clinging effects. Certain magical bindings."
