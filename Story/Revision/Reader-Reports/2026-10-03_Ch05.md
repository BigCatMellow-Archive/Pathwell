## 4. Neutral questions for the author
1. What is Elizabeth carrying into this chapter, from the cookbook, the page and the meeting? Is her silence about Nana here the choice?
2. At L274, what does she mean by "what you did at the bookstore"? Is "Neither of them answered that either" meant as a second echo of L226?
3. Is the rain-shaking sword Stansbury's prank, or that sword's job?
4. In "That was enough," what is enough? Does she arm herself because of his answer or in spite of it?
5. The bible gives Elizabeth small, defensive jokes. Are "I'm the missus" and "useful information yesterday" hers, or has the dry register spread to her?
6. Do you want the narrator speaking to "you" at L309, with the radio and "migrate" lines around it?
7. What season is it? Corn and baseball say late summer; Ch2's night was cold.
8. If the climax takes plan option 3 (the dagger cutting the draw), should Stansbury's honesty about "certain magical bindings" (L258) go here?
9. Pathwell is wrong or ignorant five times (Saturday, lockers, school, waiting, which room). Is that one joke, used on purpose?

## 5. Opinions (mine, not findings)
- **The replay at L184-210 is the slowest stretch.** I would keep "I broke it", "Normally?", "unpleasant rather than murderous" and "Her door." / "Was it?", and let the rest go. The author's call.
- **The brothers' repartee trades one move.** "That's not an amount." / "That is a more compelling version." / "It's also a worse description." share a shape, and Pathwell's sounds like Stansbury's. His best lines here are the ignorant, practical ones ("Is this what waiting is like?").
- **Elizabeth is the sharpest person in the chapter.** I enjoyed it, but it drifts from the sincere, flustered voice the bible gives her.
- **The soft ending ("Elizabeth followed him inside") is the book's pattern (ledger R3).** The hook is Pathwell stopping at the steps (L323).
