## 1. Would I read the second half?

Yes, without hesitation. Three things pull me.

The first is **Elizabeth**. She starts as someone things happen to: pajamas under a coat, no answer to "what do you normally have?" (6:15). Nine chapters later she crashes a Cadillac on purpose to get a straight answer (8:3), then orders exactly what she wants in a diner (9:98). I want to see what she does when she finally corners Pathwell.

The second is **the question Shade hands her**, "Ask him what he chose" (9:336). It's a better question than "what happened," and it puts Pathwell's charm, which I liked from page one, on trial.

The third is **the warmth**. I want more time at Camp Cunnan, with Mama Baga and the archivist, and a reason to go back for Nana's cookbook. Or a reason not to.

## 2. What worked

- **The grief is specific, and it's funny.** "She believed soup was an argument you could win" (4:264). The pencil note "MORE IF THEY'RE SICK. THEY WON'T EAT ENOUGH" (4:260) did more for me than any magic scene. Giving the whole cookbook ("If I keep holding the rest, I'm going to spend the next ten minutes finding a reason this page is the wrong one," 4:292) is the emotional center of the half.
- **Comfort arrives as a joke.** "Oh, fine, you can have a dumpling" (2:97) lands right after she cries. "Criticism received" (7:91) comes right after he nearly dies. The book keeps catching me after a fall.
- **Setups pay off.** "You'll get them back at the counter" becomes "And look. A counter" (3:17/3:129). The dagger that "cuts separation" (5:244) cuts Pathwell free (7:63). "I am an excellent driver" (6:304, 8:41). "That is not a posture" (4:142) returns as "apparently a posture" (6:168). These made me trust that the book is paying attention.
- **Dread that's slipped in quietly.** The muddler thud that comes "a hair before his hand did" (6:115) and the mirror that's wrong "in the smallest possible way" (6:204) unsettled me more than the blobs did.
- **The magic always costs something.** The prune (3:211–235) is horrible, and the handcart joke afterward is a mercy. Pages are "Not burned. Spent" (4:324). The archive's "Not hidden. Not displayed. Available" (4:470) makes the camp feel like a place with an ethic, not a backdrop.
- **The comic rhythm.** The short exchanges ("You broke my car." / "Yes.") work almost every time.

## 3. The people

**Enjoyed:** Elizabeth, increasingly. Mama Baga ("Sit before you fall over politely"). The archivist ("Specificity is expensive"). The turnip boy and his sock. Boots. The shopkeeper ("Will that be paper or plastic?"). Stansbury's dry suffering ("My axle is in a drainage culvert. I'm evaluating several positions"). And, to my surprise, Shade.

**Less enjoyed:** Pathwell in Chapters 7–8. His withholding stops being charming at "Drive" (7:243), which seems intended. I also could have done with less of Stansbury's hand-slapping (5:222, 6:73).

**Do they sound alike?** The leads are distinct:
- **Pathwell** fills the air with flourishes, lists and nicknames ("Lizzy", "Perfect", "I tip historically").
- **Mama Baga** never uses contractions ("We did not ask for this," "That is different").
- **Shade** is clipped and literal ("Enough name," "Correct," "Observation. Not memory"), and he's distinct from Pathwell in exactly the way 8:249 describes.

The book's dry "straight men" do blur together, though. Stansbury ("It's the useful version," "That fixes it"), the shopkeeper, the archivist ("That narrows it"), the bartender and Shade all deliver the same deadpan correction. "Fair" is said by Pathwell (2:21), Elizabeth (8:103) and Shade (9:47, 9:358). Almost every minor character gets a polished one-liner, so the world sometimes feels like one witty voice in several costumes. The same staging recurs too: "X looked at Y. Y looked at X" at 4:573, 6:294, 7:151, 7:191 and 8:257.

## 7. Opinions (these are opinions)

- **Opinion:** Chapter 4 is the best chapter, and the camp is the book's real home. I missed it from Chapter 5 on.
- **Opinion:** Chapter 5 is the weakest. The school, the foam-sword demonstration and the drive feel like moving people into position.
- **Opinion:** Echoing the first line at 8:71 is the one place I felt the book winking at me. The other callbacks feel natural; that one feels placed.
- **Opinion:** Nearly everyone being quick and dry is the book's charm and its risk. By the diner I wanted one person who just said a plain thing badly.
- **Opinion:** The pajamas reveal (4:360) and the grits order (9:98) are better character work than any of the speeches, including 7:291.
- **Opinion:** Shade is the best new idea in this half. He made me rethink Pathwell rather than just adding a mystery.
- **Opinion:** Leaving so many small things unexplained (the neighborhood, Thumper, the chain, Celia) mostly felt like texture, not neglect. The neighborhood at 2:51–57 is the exception: it read like a promise.
