### 1. Verdict in three lines

The first half passes the author's test. I would happily leave Chapters 1–6 on if I came across them halfway through, and the bar chapter is the clearest Sunday Morning stretch in the book.

Chapters 7–9 give up some comfort for plot attention (the pull, "which prune", Shade). That's the weight R24 keeps, and each hard beat still gets a landing: the ditch comedy, then the diner.

The main real cost under the lens is the cast's voices. One corrective deadpan ("No. [correction]." / "That isn't X.") is spoken by too many of them, and by Chapters 7–9 Elizabeth has started speaking it too.

### 3. Where it delivers (what a revision must protect)

1. **The little world arrives in one paragraph and plays sincerely.** "half a sheet cake that now said only ELIZ, because her coworkers had started at the wrong end" (1:7) and "The cake, somehow, was fine." (1:121). The second is the book's first soft landing.
2. **Every hard beat in Ch1–4 lands on food, warmth or a small absurdity.** "Oh, fine, you can have a dumpling." / "He left the last dumpling on her side of the tray." (2:97, 2:147). The terrible coffee, "Familiar things are useful." (3:87). The handcart, "For decades." (3:271). The soup line, "She believed soup was an argument you could win" (4:264). The chain and "You'll fit in here." (4:380).
3. **Camp is a sitcom world built to movie standards.** "almost everything had been repaired by someone who believed replacement was a personal failure" (4:57). The beetle children, "It's practicing." / "Being dead." (4:63–67). Hess, "dead twenty-two years" / "He'd still argue." (4:484–486). The turnip boy's sock. Nearly everyone in it is pleasant to watch, which is the "cast better than the story needs" trait.
4. **The bar is the half's purest Sunday Morning chapter.** "What, then, is family for?" / "Usually this argument." (6:81–83). "I tip historically." (6:270). "The mounted fish went on withholding judgment." (6:247). And the line that names the mode: "It was a decent time. She let it be one." (6:119).
5. **Elizabeth gets a pleasant underdog arc of small wins.** "Mrs. Pathwell… Don't be rude, honey." (5:137). The ginger ale (6:33–37). The dagger (7:63). The ditch (8:3). "Perfect," Elizabeth said (8:155) borrows his word and sets up R9.
6. **The crash aftermath is the book allowed to be a little stupid, and earning it.** "You broke my car." / "Yes." (8:75–77). "My axle is in a drainage culvert. I'm evaluating several positions." (8:117). "how upset the farmer was going to be" (8:133, the author's own).
7. **The diner is a soft landing that carries plot.** The bad coffee rhymes with the Space Between (9:78). "A motel vacancy sign missing the V." (9:7). The shared song (9:41). The waitress writing the directions on the receipt (9:372).

### 4. Where it doesn't

- **Ch3:165–195, the counter (Rai Stone, Lydian coins, "potential to sell" / "You did.").** This is the heaviest lore in the half, and Elizabeth's "What does that mean?" goes unanswered (3:189–191). **The weight overrides the lens here.** It's the author's draft (W14), the prune that follows is R29's, and the haggling keeps it watchable without understanding.
- **Ch3:65–73 then 3:197–237: two hard beats in one chapter** (the panic attack, then the prune). Both land (the coffee; the handcart). **Not a problem.**
- **Ch2:51–57, the changed neighbourhood.** A casual reader files it as dread with no payoff. **This is the author's open Q-O, not mine to settle.** On the lens alone, the displacement reading costs nothing.
- **Ch4:543–545, "It changed targets. Fresh waste."** This is lore a chapter before Stansbury explains it (5:202). **Not a problem:** being told twice actually helps a reader re-enter.
- **Ch6:186–216 (the mirror) and Ch7:219–275 (the pull in the car).** This is where the book starts to need memory. A reader dropping in at Ch7–8 meets "the pull", "which prune" and "started" all at once. **The weight overrides it:** this is the book's turn. "Which prune?" is asked six times (7:237, 8:11, 8:15, 8:27, 8:137, 8:255), which works as both the engine and a re-entry aid. Keep it.
- **Ch7 has no soft landing at its end** (7:315, the empty road). **The author ruled this (R56 B1)**, and "Elizabeth chose the ditch." lands it.
- **Ch8:159–293, Shade's arrival.** This is the most plot-attentive scene in the half ("pieces", "Existing. Pick a word."). **The weight overrides it.** W16 keeps the roadside reveal, and the diner then lands it.
- **Ch9:220–222, the recap of clues.** **Protected by R39.**
- **The shared deadpan. This one is a real Sunday-lens problem.** The "No. [correction]." shape is spoken by five different people:
  - the archivist: "No. That is generally the opposite of an archive." (4:524)
  - Stansbury: "No. You said something came through a door and tried to kill you." (5:194)
  - Stansbury: "No. I ordered it because I enjoy disappointment." (6:71)
  - Shade: "No. I'm getting coffee." (8:321)
  - Elizabeth: "No. It's one of them." (8:337)

  Elizabeth's correction lines also pile up: "That wasn't the question." (7:113), then "That wasn't my question." (8:19), then "That's not the normal response." (9:86). "Fair." is said four times: by Pathwell (2:21, his under R28), Elizabeth (8:103), and Shade twice (9:47, 9:358). "Pathwell looked at X. Y looked back." look-chains appear five times (4:573, 6:294, 7:151, 7:191, 8:257).

### 5. Voice check

- **Elizabeth.** Sincere and literal, with mundane panic ("Where am I going to get a new door?" 2:31; "I really am sorry." 8:129). She's distinct in Ch1–6. By Ch7–9 she has picked up the house corrector ("That wasn't my question."; "Was that a question?" 8:329; "Don't start doing that." 9:258). The text marks the borrowing once ("This is spreading." 6:41), which makes the first instance a joke, but the later ones aren't marked.
- **Pathwell.** The most distinct voice: flourishes, food, lists, nicknames, evasive logistics ("Replaceable." 2:5; "Company?" 2:77; "layover" 4:119; "Several dignities." 7:131; "I tip historically."). Nobody blurs into him except Shade, on purpose.
- **The shopkeeper.** Clerical courtesy ("All bills come due eventually, Mr. Pathwell." 3:151; "Will that be paper or plastic?" 3:239; "Yes." / "Yes." 3:265–269). Distinct.
- **Mama Baga.** Uncontracted speech, with care given as orders ("Sit before you fall over politely." 4:138; "Hush now." 4:374). She's distinct when she's caring. When she's facing Pathwell she joins the deadpan ("You appointed yourself a delivery." / "That does not make you less annoying." 4:561–565).
- **The healer (Ch4).** Plain, as R33 set her ("Then she wakes up sore and hungry." 4:336). Good.
- **The archivist.** The sanctioned dry wit ("Everything is new when you are twelve." 4:424; "Specificity is expensive." 4:512). He works because he's the only one in his scene.
- **The turnip boy (Milo, unnamed here).** A child's bluntness ("I'm eleven." / "It wasn't me." / "You were going to." 4:426–498). Distinct.
- **Ken and the waitress.** Plain and ordinary. Good.
- **The bartender.** Mildly dry ("You complained about the batter then too." 6:107). Fine as one line.
- **Stansbury.** In Ch5 he's vain and theatrical, from the author's draft ("Oh, heavens no." 5:252; "Always so crass, Pathwell." 5:264; shaking the tree and staying dry, 5:236). From Ch6 on he flattens into the correct, precise man ("Survive it." 7:205; "That one is gone." 7:111), which is the same register as the archivist and the Mama Baga who faces Pathwell. **He's the speaker who blurs most.**
- **Shade.** Clipped and literal ("Enough name." 8:239; "Observation. Not memory." 9:92). He's distinct in brevity. His "Fair." and "Would you prefer applause?" (9:166) are the house quip rather than his own exactness. The diner smile, "smaller, and meaner around the edges, and his own" (9:260), is the right note for him.

**Do two or more blur?** Yes. Stansbury (Ch6–7), Mama Baga facing Pathwell, the archivist, Shade at his quippiest, and late Elizabeth share one corrective deadpan. Pathwell, the shopkeeper, the children, Ken and the waitress stay distinct.

### 6. Candidate changes (smallest first)

1. **4:401–403**
   - Current: `He nodded. "This wheel is new."` / `"Newer," Pathwell amended. "Relatively."`
   - Change: join the two lines into one paragraph and change "Pathwell" to "he": `He nodded. "This wheel is new." "Newer," he amended. "Relatively."`
   - Why: a new paragraph signals a new speaker. The 10-08 panel misread this as amending "a line that isn't there."
   - Authorship: not in his drafts; reviser text.
   - Rulings: none touch this.
2. **9:47**
   - Current: `"Fair."` (Shade's reply to "So is the seat I'm in. Mostly.")
   - Change: cut it. The next paragraph ("He checked his mirrors often…") becomes his answer.
   - Why: it's Shade's first of two "Fair."s in one chapter and the fourth in the half. Keeping 9:358 ("Shade considered the correction.") keeps the better one.
   - Authorship: his Ch8 car draft has no "Fair."; not his.
   - Rulings: none touch this.
3. **8:19**
   - Current: `"That wasn't my question."`
   - Change: cut it. The scene goes from "We don't know that it matters yet." to "Stansbury looked out the passenger window. Elizabeth waited…"
   - Why: it nearly repeats 7:113 ("That wasn't the question."), which has to stay because "It's the answer I have." answers it. The cut also thins Elizabeth's drift into the corrector.
   - Authorship: reviser text.
   - Rulings: none touch this.
4. **Flag for the author: the "No. [correction]." shape.** The five lines are 4:524, 5:194, 6:71, 8:321 and 8:337 (quoted in section 4).
   - My lean: keep the archivist's (R33) and Shade's (his literalness), and let him reconsider Stansbury's 6:71 and Elizabeth's 8:337. Both are character-defining lines, so I've written nothing.
   - Rulings: no conflict. This is the same question as EXP-0019 Q1, extended past the healer.
5. **Flag for the author: Stansbury's vanity after Ch5.**
   - The author's Ch5 Stansbury is showy and a little pompous. From Ch6 on he only corrects. A trace of the Ch5 vanity somewhere in Ch6–7 would separate him from the archivist and Mama Baga.
   - This needs a new character-defining line, so it's his to write.
   - Rulings: no conflict.

### 8. Opinions (marked as opinions)

- *Opinion:* Ch4 and Ch6 are what the whole book should come back to. If a reader remembers one place, it should be the Camp ring. If they remember one hour, it should be the fish argument.
- *Opinion:* the cast really is better than the story strictly needs, and the main thing standing in its way is the deadpan. Pathwell's voice is so distinct that the others don't need to be dry to keep up with him. Plain, warm or showy would set him off better.
- *Opinion:* the Ch7 car is the least pleasant company in the half, because both brothers go quiet and the scene runs on withholding. That's the right cost to pay, and the ditch pays it back within a page.
- *Opinion:* a reader could put the book down after any chapter except Ch6, whose thud is the half's only true cliffhanger. That's about the right number.
