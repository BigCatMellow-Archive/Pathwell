## Ch12, the shoulder

**H1** (cut 12:68, "I ask because people point at the wrong thing surprisingly often.")
1. Without it, "Which shoulder?" / "Left." is plain and in character. The line is wry, but it's practical and explains her question rather than correcting Elizabeth.
2. Losing it costs a little: "Elizabeth looked at her. The healer looked back." is a nice beat.
3. It adds no new problems.
4. **Opinion, low confidence: keep the current text.** One gentle, explanatory line fits a plain healer, and R59 asks for "plain", not "humorless". If the author wants her strictly plain, take H1. Nothing breaks either way.

**H2a / H2b** ("Briefly? Very." / "Excellent." / "Afterward, less.")
1. H2a, "It'll hurt a lot, and then it'll hurt less.", is honest and plain and has the same two-part shape as Ch4's "sore and hungry". H2b, "Badly, for a moment.", is still the clipped, inverted house rhythm: a smaller "Briefly? Very."
2. "Strong sales pitch." still answers both. Elizabeth's "Excellent." is lost, which is fine.
3. H2a's "and then it'll hurt less" comes back in the narration at 12:144: "and then less pain. Not gone. Different." That reads as a payoff, not a slip.
4. **Take H2a.**

**H3** (cut "Keep them that way.")
1–2. "Still mine." becomes Elizabeth's joke, and nobody answers it, the same way Ch4's "That seems fair." goes unanswered. Nothing is lost.
4. **Take.**

**H4** ("Define using." → "Lifting. Carrying. Pulling. Any of it.")
1. The healer's answer is plain. "Define using." stays, as Elizabeth's line.
2. Losing "Probably." gives up a small self-aware admission. But the setup Ch15 needs is intact: Ch15:138–140 recalls "You are not using this arm today." (12:156, which no patch touches). "Lifting" names exactly what she then does with the shelf in Ch15:158–186, so the payoff gets sharper. Ch16:103, "You strained everything I told you not to strain.", now has concrete instructions behind it.
3. H4 also removes an echo the candidates don't name. Ch12:160–164 ("I wasn't." / "You were going to.") copies Ch6:51–55 ("I was not." / "You were about to."), where Stansbury and Elizabeth do the same bit.
4. **Take.** One small worry: the three-fragment list is a slightly writerly rhythm.

## Ch16, after the fire

**H5a / H5b** ("Useful." / "For who?" / "Me." / "Milo considered that deeply unfair.")

H5b keeps it through the narrator ("Milo considered that insufficient."). "Insufficient" is a little arch for a child's point of view, but so was "deeply unfair".
4. **Take H5b.** (An observation only, the pick is the author's: the reviser could set H5a's "Sorry. That's the one." next to H5b's Milo line.)

**H6** (cut "A low standard." / "It has been a morning.")

3. EXP-0019's B1 row still says "'A low standard.' / 'It has been a morning.' stay". R59 overrides that row, but the record is now stale.
4. **Take.**

**H7** ("Shelves are famous…" / "This one was especially difficult." → "I know.")
2. Plain and warm. She has just treated the leg the shelf pinned, so she knows. Elizabeth's "especially difficult" was a good line, and it goes.
3. "I know." is a loaded phrase in Ch15:132–134 ("The answer came out in Shade's voice and Pathwell's voice and her own."). Here it's a faint echo, probably harmless, possibly resonant.
4. **Take.**

**H8** (cut "Fine. I will ask you very firmly…" / "less effective" / "It is what we have.")
2. The exchange still works: the threat, then "Is that allowed?", then Mama Baga's "No.", then "I'll try". Mama Baga's "No." becomes the button and keeps the laugh. "I'll try" now comes straight after Mama Baga, but readers will see that it answers the healer.

**H9** (cut "Define a while." / "Longer than you want." / "Everything does.")

## Stansbury, Ch6–7

**S1a / S1b** (6:71, "No. I ordered it because I enjoy disappointment.")
1. S1a, "Stansbury closed his eyes over the next bite. 'Don't talk to me for a minute.'", comes straight from the author's own "So good he said to himself". It's appetite and self-absorption, nowhere near the corrective deadpan. S1b, "Exceptional. I have exceptional taste in fish.", is invented pomp. It announces the trait rather than showing it.
2. With S1a, the slap at 6:73 gets funnier: his eyes are shut and he still catches Pathwell, which fits 6:61, "Stansbury knew which dart Pathwell would reach for before he reached for it." S1b adds a third "fish" in a short run (6:79 "my fish", 6:89 "the fish").
4. **Take S1a.**

**S2** (7:111, "brushed something off his vest that wasn't there")
1. This restores the author's own beat at the same moment (benchmark Ch5:111, dusting himself off after the blob), and the vest was set up in Ch5:147.
2. The risk is that vanity right after Pathwell nearly died could read as indifference. Two things answer that: "It's the answer I have." and Stansbury checking Pathwell's pupils (7:121–127) follow straight after. "That wasn't there" is a slightly stock phrase.
4. **Take.** S1a is appetite, not vanity, so S1a plus S2 gives one trace of vanity, as EXP-0020 Q2 asked. If only one of S1b and S2 is wanted, S2 is better grounded in the author's text.
