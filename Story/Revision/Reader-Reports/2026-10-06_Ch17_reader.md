# Reader report: Chapter 17 (three weeks of restitution), working copy

Reader: independent, no edits to any repo file. Text read: Story/Chapters/Chapter_17.txt as it stands in the working tree (420 lines, 2,599 words). Line numbers are that file's. Read order: protocol; the Ch16 report (format only); Ch1-15 at reading speed (Ch11 closely); Ch16 closely; Ch17 cold in four chunks, logged before any rule, lock, record, decision or pass log was opened; Ch18 L1-200 as the hand-off; then the records.

## Headline

No stoppers. Seven snags, all cuts or one-word fixes, and about fifteen quibbles. The chapter now does what the Ch16 hand-off needed: the correction pattern is shown a handful of times and then stops being the point, and the Henry Vale refusal (L162-182) is the one place where the change is an event between people rather than a habit. Seen from the side table it reads as a man catching the second half of a sentence he has already been told not to say, not as a fight he wins, and it works better that way. The sore spots are all the narrator stepping in to tell me what the montage meant (L225, L410-412, L408), one scene with no Elizabeth in it (L377-383), and a few Pathwell-looked beats. Elizabeth's own scenes (the apartment spoon, the side table, "bought lunch because she was hungry", the walk) are the best writing in the chapter. The ending lands ("Because she wanted to go."), and Ch18 L3 picks it up without a seam.

## Pass 1b: the questions the launching agent set

1. Are three weeks enough evidence, or thin? Enough, and the chapter knows it: I counted eleven separate occasions (rack height, brace, cabinets, copying, Susquehanna, Henry Vale, board, scar, wagon, Hearts, label) across four settings with four different people. Where it is thin is not number but kind: nearly all of it is "he asks, is told no, complies". The ones that are different in kind are the best ones (Susquehanna, where he did not ask; Henry Vale, where he says the no himself; the wagon, where he supplies the questions; "Again."). Thin only in one respect: what showing up costs him once the novelty is over (the days between day 12 and day 21 are three paragraphs of Camp and none of the grind). The chapter is modest about what it claims ("evidence"), which is right.
2. Does the Henry Vale refusal land as a fight he wins, seen from Elizabeth's side table? Not as a fight, and I do not think it should. The curator says "No." first. What Elizabeth, and I, see is the second half of an old sentence arriving ("It is better than an empty—") and Pathwell stopping himself: the stop, the look at the blank space, "No," he said, then the packet put away, "They are not Daniel's." The detail that carries it is Elizabeth's pen stopping (L172), which tells me she is watching him and not the page. Nothing is lost by not being in his head. The thing I did not see is any sign that the refusal costs him; "Understood." at L182 is flat. That is probably the point.
3. Does anything feel like a "he got corrected and complied" montage? Mostly no, after the first week. The first half still has a repeated shape: rack (L11-41), cabinets (L48-54), notebook (L56-80), board (L227-239). L225 then names the pattern outright ("corrected often enough that correction stopped looking like a dramatic event"), which is the sentence that makes it feel like a montage because the narrator is counting it for me. The cabinets (L48-54) are the weakest beat: they say the same thing as the rack in less time, and the board is the cabinets again with a better exchange attached.
4. Where is Elizabeth in each section, and did I lose her? See the table below. I lost her at L377-383 (the reopening), where the narrator surveys what Pathwell paid for and carried with no one present; I never learn how Elizabeth knows any of it. I half lost her at L248-284 (the wagon): she is there only to watch him ("Elizabeth could see an entire better plan forming in him", L268). Everywhere else she is physically placed and speaks or acts.
5. Do I wonder how she gets to Camp? Once, and it is answered: L82 "Getting there meant asking Pathwell to open the way." I then wondered how it opens now and what it costs; Ch18 L153-191 shows a brass tag on a laundromat door, which is a fair answer for the reader of Ch18, not of Ch17. I am not asking for more in Ch17.
6. Every speaker attribution I stopped on: L190 ("Continue writing." has no tag; it has to be Stansbury because Pathwell answers him next); L198 (the "either"; see quibble 6); L328 ("He was bad." has no tag; I read it as Milo, and Elizabeth's "Very." confirms); L344 (Pathwell's "No." is clear, but what it refers to is not said; "Nothing." covers it, I took it as Shade's card). Nothing else stopped me. All other lines were clean, including the fast stretches at L192-202 and L360-374.
7. Any line that tells me what to think about Pathwell: L225 (the narrator names the pattern); L245 ("Pathwell never mentioned changing it again."); L383 (the curator's distinction, which is a good line and which I would keep); L410-412 ("Pathwell had kept showing up..." / "Elizabeth didn't call that redemption. She called it evidence."). The last is the strongest. Also L291 and L414 give Elizabeth a neat self-diagnosis about adventure and being dragged that is more tidy than she has been anywhere else in the book.

## Opinions (marked as opinions)

- The Henry Vale refusal is the chapter's best scene and does not need the inside view. Keep it as it is.
- "Copy the smudge." is the right size of joke for the book. Keep.
- I would cut L410 and L408's last three sentences before anything else; the label scene already carries the arc and the chapter would end closer to Elizabeth.
