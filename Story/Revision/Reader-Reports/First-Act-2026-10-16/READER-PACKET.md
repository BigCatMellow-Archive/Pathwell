# First-act reading packet — Chapters 1–3

**Reader copy only.** This packet contains no explanations of intended plot, themes or world rules. It is for someone who has **not** read these chapters or the project's planning and revision notes.

## What to read

Read these chapters in order, without opening any other documents in the repository:

1. [Chapter 1](../../../Chapters/Chapter_01.txt)
2. [Chapter 2](../../../Chapters/Chapter_02.txt)
3. [Chapter 3](../../../Chapters/Chapter_03.txt)

Reading should be at ordinary pace, as you would read a book. If you need to go back and reread something, do so naturally, but note what and why. Stop after Chapter 3; don't look at later chapters or at the repository's author notes, critique reports, decision records, story bible, answers or experimental drafts.

You can stop reading at any point. If you would have stopped reading the book voluntarily, write down **where and why** instead of forcing yourself to finish. Uncertainty, mistaken guesses, annoyance and “I don't know” are all useful responses.

## After each chapter — write before reading the next one

Write a short paragraph or a few numbered answers:

1. What happened in this chapter, as you'd tell someone who hasn't read it?
2. What stayed with you most? A moment you liked, disliked, found funny, frightening, moving, or strange?
3. What, if anything, made you pause, reread, or work to understand what was happening?
4. What questions are you carrying forward? Which ones are important enough that you expect the story to answer?
5. What do you think might happen next, if you have a guess?
6. Where did your attention drop, if it did?
7. Which character are you most interested in right now? Why?

**Keep these chapter notes unchanged.** Do not improve your Chapter 1 account after finishing Chapter 3.

## Only after Chapter 3

Answer in your own words, without checking notes or earlier passages first:

1. Tell someone the story so far in six to ten sentences. What seems to be driving it?
2. What do you think each of the two main characters wants right now? What evidence gave you that impression?
3. Which actions did each character take by choice, and which seemed forced by circumstances?
4. Which possessions or objects mattered, and why? Where do you think those objects are at the end of Chapter 3?
5. What do you believe about the unusual events so far? Separate things the narrative *showed* from guesses or things characters merely *claimed*.
6. What is your strongest unanswered question? What explanation are you tentatively expecting?
7. Was there any point where an object's location, a person's movement, or the order of events stopped making sense?
8. If you had found this book on your own, would you read Chapter 4? Explain the reason either way.

After writing everything above, you may glance back at the chapters and add a **separate** “after checking” note. Do not overwrite the original responses.

## About your reading circumstances

At the top of your answers, please state (a) whether you knew any plot or spoilers beforehand, (b) whether you have read this text before, (c) whether you reviewed any notes from this project, (d) whether you read in one sitting, and (e) approximately when you read it. Do not supply personal information beyond what you want to share.

**Submission:** Return your answers as plain text or Markdown to the person who provided the packet. You do not need to register an account, fill out an external form or use a particular format. The response is evidence of **your reading experience**, not a test with right answers.
