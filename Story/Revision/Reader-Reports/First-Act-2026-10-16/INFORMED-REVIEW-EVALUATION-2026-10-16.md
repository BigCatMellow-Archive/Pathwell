## Executive assessment

**The reviewer would continue after every chapter**, more confidently after Chapter 3 than Chapter 2. They praise the emotional magic, Nana's cookbook memory, Elizabeth's breakdown, the Space Between, the horrible-but-familiar coffee, the potential-payment tree, and the handcart release. **The binding problem is purpose and moral framing, not general inability to understand the world.** By the end of Chapter 3 they can summarize the objects, the movement, Pathwell's payment and Elizabeth's constrained choice, but cannot confidently say why Pathwell was searching *this apartment*, what the purchased materials are urgently for, or when he will stop withholding elementary information.

Most consequential sentence from supplied Part 3: **“The opening has secured my curiosity. The review finds Pathwell funny and distinctive *without absolving him*. The reviewer especially values the shopkeeper's line **“These were never his to spend”** because it recognizes his intrusion as wrong. This is positive evidence that existing text can maintain sympathy and moral accountability simultaneously; do not flatten Pathwell into an innocent guide or make Elizabeth's participation sound freely chosen.
