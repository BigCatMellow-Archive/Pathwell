# Running log

## Chapter 1
1. What happened: Elizabeth, new in her apartment after a welcome party, wakes to find a lingering party guest reading her diary. A smelly blob monster breaks in; he uses her Nana's cookbook (a sugar-cookie recipe page, glowing gold) as a weapon/ward and she briefly relives a memory of Nana. The blob is "mostly gone," her flat is "marked, magically speaking," and he hustles her out the door.
2. Hardest hit: the cookie memory ("She was twelve again... Elizabeth felt something she hadn't felt in years"), and then her asking "Why could I hear my Nana?" and him stopping for a second. Leaned in. The cake being fine made me smile.
3. Confusion: What exactly he did with the recipe page (he tore it out? it "went wrong"); why the blob wants the gold; what "marked" means. I guessed it's a magic-system thing where memories/recipes are power. Why he took the diary too.
4. Want to know: Who he is. What the blob is. Why she heard Nana's voice "because it was hers." What the torn page being left behind means. Why he went still in the hallway.
5. Guess: He's a lowkey con man/guide who needs her cookbook, and she'll get dragged into a hidden magical world.
6. Attention dips: none; short and quick.
7. With: Elizabeth, because of the Nana moment; the man is entertaining but slippery (reading her diary like a magazine is a bad sign).

## Chapter 2
1. What happened: They flee downstairs; her neighborhood has subtly changed (no coffee shop, a park that shouldn't be there). Pathwell (that's his whole name) takes her to a Chinese place and eats dumplings. She loses her dry-cleaning ticket to the gutter, breaks down crying, he reluctantly gives her a dumpling and tells her "the choice is yours, Lizzy" and walks off. She hears Nana say "Lizzy," realizes he still has the cookbook, and jumps up.
2. Hardest hit: the dry-cleaning ticket skipping into the gutter and then the crying: "a life she'd packed into garbage bags... leaving didn't feel like anything at all." That felt real; it was the first time the book told me why she was numb. Also the dumpling he finally gives her. And Pathwell leaving the last dumpling on her side.
3. Confusion: The neighborhood has changed with no explanation (guessed it's the magical world leaking, or she was moved). "Just a really weird Tuesday" / "It's Thursday" - a joke, I think. He calls her Lizzy before she told him; presumably from the diary. Whether he left the cookbook on purpose as bait is unclear. I'm not sure if the dumplings and the cookbook (he "dropped her books on the table") are on her side now - the last line says he still has it, so apparently he took it back.
4. Want to know: Is he leaving her the "choice" as manipulation? Why does Nana's voice come when the cookbook is near? What's the "mess" he cleans up? What did she come from (the "staying felt worse than leaving" bit)?
5. Guess: She chases him, and that's how she gets pulled in; he engineered it.
6. Dip: none.
7. With: Elizabeth. Pathwell is funny, but I'm wary: he's too casual about wrecking her life.

## Chapter 3
1. What happened: She finds Pathwell, who opens a paper-folded door into a bookstore (the Space Between) full of other people's journals/letters/recipes. The place overwhelms her with others' emotions until she faints; she wakes to terrible coffee, a blood-god cat (Boots) who cuts her hand, and a shopkeeper. Pathwell is in debt; he pays by "selling potential" to a tree-of-roots book, in a painful ritual that burns away a chunk of him. The shopkeeper returns her cookbook and diary ("These were never his to spend"), and gives Pathwell a handcart.
2. Hardest hit: the ritual: "Pathwell screamed in a million voices, voices lost to time, burning out to become memories never made." Winced; first time he seemed to pay a real price. Also the shopkeeper's flat "You had that the whole time? / Yes." Funny. And her laugh at the end.
3. Confusion: What "potential" is and what exactly he lost ("Some of it." "If I knew that, I'd make worse decisions with more confidence"); I guess it's his possible futures/memories never made. The "settlement" mechanics (outside property in an open transaction - was he trying to put her cookbook/diary on the tab? "never his to spend" suggests he'd have spent them). A Rai Stone, Lydian coins: money jokes, fine. Camp Cunnan is named and the shopkeeper clearly disapproves of what they asked for. The fainting in the shelves: emotions of the books, I think.
4. Want to know: What's Camp Cunnan and why does Pathwell owe for it? What did he lose? Why did he need her cookbook and diary? What was the first-chapter blob? Is Pathwell ruined by this, or does he get away with everything?
5. Guess: The cookbook is the key to what he wants from her; she'll go to Camp Cunnan with him.
6. Dip: briefly during the bartering over Rai Stones, but the jokes carried it.
7. With: Pathwell is getting more interesting: he's hurt and a bit pathetic, and I trust him less and like him more. Elizabeth is still the lens.

Pathwell and Mama Baga discuss something that came to the door before the working failed; Pathwell has an older brother, Stansbury, he has to see. Elizabeth decides to go with him.

4. Want to know: What happened to the other page, the cookie one (still missing)? Who is the hurt young woman? Why is Stansbury a problem? What are the creatures? What's the magic system's cost: pages "spent."

6. Dip: the archivist scene ran a touch long but it was charming; the opening trek was a bit slow. Not bad.
7. With: Elizabeth firmly; I now care about her choices. Mama Baga is very appealing. Pathwell got slightly more complicated (the camp finds him annoying and fond at once).

3. Confusion: None on what happens. A few spots where the narrator explains a feeling I'd already felt ("Elizabeth thought of the side window... ## Chapter 17
1. What happened: A three-week montage of restitution. Pathwell builds a drying rack three inches wrong, copies the blue notebook "mistakes included," orders the boring cabinets, finds a wagon (The Lemon) and lets it be ugly, is made to write incident accounts for the museum, and refuses to hand the curator a replacement letter packet ("They are not Daniel's"). Elizabeth goes back to work, tidies her apartment, puts Nana's spoon on the counter then in a drawer then back, gives the museum her witness statement, and stays as a guest at Camp. Pathwell plays Hearts and loses worse than Shade ("Then I have work to do"). On day 21 the Lemon becomes the archive; Pathwell relabels a crooked label. Elizabeth, looking down the road, smiles: she wants to go somewhere.
2. Hardest hit: "They are not Daniel's." Pathwell stopping himself mid-sentence is the first time I really believed he was changing because it was a small thing and he caught it alone. Also the Hearts table: "Shade said that." / "Did he." and "Then I have work to do."
3. Confusion: None. The timeline jumps (first week, second week, twelfth day, eighteenth, twenty-first) were clear. I wasn't sure if a montage is a bit too tidy for such a catastrophic loss, but the "no debt vanished" paragraph keeps it honest.
4. Want to know: Where she goes next; what final beat the book is saving. How Pathwell and Elizabeth stand at the end.
5. Guess: The last chapter will be a quiet leaving or a new job; Pathwell goes to the shop and meets something (the questionnaire told me about a cookbook; I'll note that only because it was in the page I read first).
6. Dip: the Pathwell-and-the-rack comedy repeated the same structure three or four times (he proposes better, archivist says no, he obeys); it's charming but I felt it by the third time. The apartment section is a good short one.
7. With: Elizabeth in the apartment, and Pathwell (grudgingly; he's earned some respect). The archivist is wonderful.

## Chapter 18
1. What happened: Three weeks and four days after the fire, Elizabeth calls Pathwell and asks him to take her to the Space Between; he says "All right" and asks nothing. They go through a laundromat's OUT OF ORDER door. The shopkeeper is unsettled by a visit with nothing wrong; the cat has mellowed. She wanders the shelves, now less afraid of the pressure, and finds recipe books and a new acquisition, the Merritt family cookbook (four generations; the family copied it and consigned the original). Pathwell reads a page, then closes it, puts it back square, and takes his hand off. Later he tells her "I kept thinking resemblance was enough... It was my fault." Elizabeth picks the fifth exit (geese that steal buttons) and asks him, "Do you want to come?" He says yes, freely. At the door: "Are you ready?" "No." "Perfect."
2. Hardest hit: Pathwell putting the Merritt cookbook back, "square to the edge," and taking his hand off the cover; then the shopkeeper saying "I may need to sit down." I laughed and also felt something loosen. And the last exchange flipping Chapter 1's: warm, but I knew it was coming (I'd also been told the lines in the questionnaire).
3. Confusion: What exactly Pathwell is struggling with at the Merritt cookbook: his eyes go to the shopkeeper's ledger (his debt), so I took it as: he could use the book to pay or to do something, and chose not to. But the book doesn't say outright. "I kept thinking resemblance was enough": I took it as about Shade; the Ch 15 "our signatures overlap" idea, or treating Shade as a version of himself. "It was my fault": about Shade's death, I think, but it could also include the archive. Also: who was the speaker of "Perfect": Elizabeth (flipped lines).
4. Want to know: Nothing urgent; where they're going (the geese); whether Pathwell keeps this up; what Elizabeth wants.
5. Guess: They go off on a small new adventure with her leading.
6. Dip: the "five routes" bit is cute but a little long; none otherwise.
7. With: Elizabeth, leading; Pathwell changing at a believable pace.
