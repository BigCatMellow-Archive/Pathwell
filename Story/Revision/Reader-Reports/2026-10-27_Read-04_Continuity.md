# Read 04: Continuity and Logistics Report: *Pathwell*

Read in full, lines 1 to 7740, Chapters One to Eighteen, in order.

## 1. Errors

These are clear contradictions, ranked from most to least noticeable.

### E1. The diner "at four in the morning" does not fit the evening sequence (Ch. 6 to 10 vs. Ch. 17)

Ch. 17 says:
> "Her standards for adventure had been set by a diner at four in the morning, a museum before opening, and one ditch she had chosen herself"

The chapters that show that night put the diner in the early-to-mid evening:
- Ch. 6, leaving the bar: "Outside, evening had settled over the parking lot. The sky was streaked with wisps of orange and red"
- Ch. 7, the blob fight in the lot: "Skin gray in the last of the red light."
- Ch. 7 and 8, the drive to the ditch is short: "Pathwell and Stansbury had been quiet for almost a mile". Ch. 10 adds: "Stansbury lived two miles from the ditch." The ditch is near his town, and the bar is in his town.
- Ch. 8, right after the crash: "stars starting to show through breaks in the cloud."
- Ch. 9, at the diner: "I met you on the road twenty-five minutes ago." and "She had deliberately crashed a Cadillac less than an hour ago"

That puts the diner roughly one to two hours after sunset. Nothing accounts for the hours needed to reach 4 a.m. There is a second strain from the other direction. If the diner really was in the evening, then Ch. 11's "The drive took most of what was left of the night" means a drive of about seven hours to the museum, which the book never mentions. One of the two time markers has to move.

### E2. Pathwell says scars are outside his skills, then offers to reduce a scar with magic (Ch. 3 vs. Ch. 16)

Ch. 3:
> "Unfortunately, scars are outside my wheelhouse."

Ch. 16:
> "There are ways to reduce what remains after the skin closes."
> Stansbury looked at his brother. "Ordinary ways?"
> Pathwell didn't answer quickly enough. "No."

He is offering a non-ordinary scar treatment, which his own earlier line rules out. The text leaves some room: "reduce" is not "remove", and the method might "require somebody's record". A reader who remembers Ch. 3 will still notice.

### E3. The cookbook's position on the shelf changes (Ch. 12 vs. Ch. 15)

Ch. 12:
> "On the front reserve shelf, Nana's cookbook rested spine-out between a weather ledger and a bundle of family letters wrapped in cloth."

Ch. 15:
> "Nana's cookbook sat exactly where it had that morning, spine supported, flour-stained cover turned slightly toward the aisle."

A book shelved spine-out does not show its cover. The words "exactly where it had that morning" make the change stand out. The wagon "lurched" in Ch. 14, which could explain it, but the text doesn't connect the two.

### E4. The biscuit is broken and then "untouched" (Ch. 14)

> "Pathwell broke the biscuit in half and didn't eat either half."

Shortly after, on the same page:
> "Pathwell put the untouched biscuit on the paper bag."

This is minor. "Uneaten" would fix it.

## 2. Soft problems

These are possible but strained, unexplained, or dropped.

**Objects**

- **The card deck survives the fire.** Ch. 14: "Milo. Archive. Put these back in the drawer and bring me the north-route copy." and "Milo took the deck and ran toward the archive wagon". The archive then burns ("Intake is gone. Weather shelf, mostly gone."). Ch. 17 says "The archivist had the deck." and "The queen of spades appeared in Pathwell's hand." There is one hedge. Ch. 14 has the archivist lay the queen "on the card table" separately, and Ch. 16 mentions "several wet playing cards had been gathered into a stack". So the queen may have survived, but the full deck in Ch. 17 is unexplained. It may be a different deck; the text doesn't say.
- **The foam dagger is never accounted for.** Its last appearance is Ch. 15: "Her right hand had found the foam dagger behind her waist." After that it is never mentioned again and never returned to Stansbury. In Ch. 8 he said "Keep it." Ch. 18 lists what she was not told to bring, "coat, knife, heirloom", which is the closest the book comes.
- **The dagger reaches the diner table without being moved.** Ch. 8: "The dagger pressed into the seat at the small of her back." Ch. 9: "the foam dagger where it lay beside her in the booth". Nothing shows her taking it out, although Ch. 10 notes "She'd moved it there before leaving the diner."
- **Milo's sock, minutes apart.** Ch. 14: "his sock falling as he went". Ch. 15: "His sock had finally stayed up." He could have pulled it up, but "finally" reads oddly so soon after.
- **Milo's right sock disappears with the boot.** Ch. 15: "The boot came off, and his leg came free". Then: "Milo stood: bare right foot, one sock still somehow present on the left." Nothing says the sock came off. Ch. 16 is consistent with that outcome: "His right foot was bare."
- **Three scraps, two lists.** Ch. 10: "I have a letter of longing stacked away somewhere, and a couple of grocery lists." Then: "Stansbury came down the steps with three scraps of paper in his hand." Only two lists are used: "A letter that deserved better, two grocery lists". The third scrap is never accounted for.
- **An extra brass piece.** Ch. 14 lists "three brass pins … a narrow loop of dull metal, and a length of pale waxed cord", then adds "He pulled another small brass piece from a pocket and set it beside the others." The frame uses only the three pins and the loop. The extra piece may be the "narrow brass tag" of Ch. 18, but nothing links them.
- **Vehicles left behind.** Shade's sedan stays in the museum lot (Ch. 11) and is never mentioned again. Stansbury's van is also left at the museum when he goes to Camp through the wall (Ch. 14). In Ch. 17, "I drove." implies he got it back, off-page.
- **Coins.** Ch. 2: "her fingers toying with the coins in them … She dumped the contents of her pockets on the table". Ch. 9: "I don't have any money." She may have left the coins at the Chinese place; the book doesn't say.

**Injuries that stop mattering**

- **Stansbury's ribs.** Ch. 8: "one hand pressed to his ribs". Ch. 10 still has "his other hand pressed to his ribs". Afterwards, in Ch. 11 he "caught Elizabeth around the waist" and "hit her from the side", and in Ch. 15 he climbs halfway through a broken window and back. The ribs are never mentioned again.
- **Pathwell's neck.** Ch. 8: "one hand at the back of his neck". The peas are held "against the back of his neck" through Ch. 10. The Ch. 16 healer's list ("Ribs bruised. Chest impact. Exhaustion. Pruning shock.") leaves the neck out.
- **Pathwell's tremor.** It is tracked carefully from Ch. 3 ("His right arm was shaking") through Ch. 5, 6, 7 ("Both of them now") and 10, up to Ch. 14 ("His right arm shook hard enough that his hand blurred"). It is never mentioned again, even though Ch. 17 has him hand-copying documents and writing a long handwritten incident account.
- **Stansbury drives with a burned hand.** Ch. 17 has "his burned right hand wrapped in a lighter bandage now" and "I drove." This is possible but strained.
- **Pathwell's "concussion".** Ch. 17 has Stansbury say "I am deciding whether the concussion was permanent." The Ch. 16 diagnosis lists no concussion. It reads as a jibe, but it is an unstated injury.

**Timing and logistics**

- **The tracking spell catches too quickly.** Ch. 10 says the brothers "set out a few minutes after the sedan's taillights were gone". They walk two miles with injured ribs, get peas, go down to the library, wait while Stansbury goes upstairs, use up the letter, and try several memories before one catches. Pathwell then says: "She's thinking about it. Right now, somewhere, she's drinking something terrible." Elizabeth's only sip on the page is her first one on arriving (Ch. 9), within about 25 minutes of leaving the wreck. She later refuses a refill: "No, thank you." This works only if she was still drinking the same cup well into the meal. Ch. 10's "Her mouth still tasted like the diner's coffee" allows that.
- **The van comes from the wrong side.** Elizabeth walks from the diner toward the bar, "and the wreck was somewhere on the way". The van leaves a house "two miles from the ditch", but "came up behind her on the far side of the road". It also takes the same left after the water tower that she did ("Left. Soon." / "There's a water tower up ahead."). Together these mean the van followed her route from the diner side, which is hard to square with a starting point near a ditch she has not yet reached. The book's geography is loose enough that this is a query, not an error.
- **"Three weeks" on the account.** Ch. 18: "Your account remains at zero." … "Three weeks is not a character trait." The account was zeroed on Thursday night (Ch. 3). Ch. 18 takes place "Three weeks and four days after the archive burned", and the archive burned on Saturday. That is about three weeks and six days, nearly four weeks. "Three weeks" works as a round number, but it is short.
- **The order of Milo's and Shade's cries.** Ch. 18 has Elizabeth say: "He was calling out, and then Milo shouted from inside." In the scene itself, Milo cries first, at the end of Ch. 14: "A shelf inside went over. Milo cried out. 'I'm stuck!'" Then "Elizabeth was already moving." Shade's "Help!" comes after that, in Ch. 15. Her memory flattens the order.

**Threads not followed up**

- **The second presence at the apartment.** Ch. 1: "It's gone… mostly." Then "A wet thud hit from down the hall." Ch. 2: "She looked back one more time at her apartment window. A shadow filled the space." Ch. 1 also says "Your house has now been marked." The book later explains the first blob as following Pathwell (Ch. 9, 14). The second thud and the shadow in the window are never explained, and Elizabeth later lives there with no trouble. The door itself is paid off well in Ch. 18: "The landlord had replaced it". The ooze cleanup and her return trip home happen off-page, between Ch. 16 and Ch. 17.
- **The meeting she "cannot miss".** Ch. 2: "I have a meeting that I cannot miss in the morning." It is missed (Ch. 5), but Ch. 17 has no fallout: "Her work email contained forty-seven messages and only six that mattered."
- **The Daniel Vale letters.** Ch. 11 shows only one page being destroyed: "One framed page slid free … and tore cleanly through the middle," and later "Shade looked at the ruined Daniel Vale letter." Ch. 17 treats the whole set as lost: "Daniel Vale letters. … All originals. All irrecoverable." and "The letters stayed missing." Ch. 11 does imply wider loss ("the Daniel Vale letters, the household notebook, the medical ledger. Things that had survived … Until now."), so this is soft rather than an error.
- **The Henry Vale dates.** Ch. 11's card says "COAT FOUND IN HENRY VALE'S ATTIC, 1978." Ch. 17 has "Henry Vale, 1882 through 1886." If this is one Henry, he would be well over 100 by 1978, unless "his attic" means a house still known by his name. It may be two men with the same name; the text doesn't say.

## 3. The timeline

### Day 0: Thursday night ("It's Thursday." Ch. 2)
- **Ch. 1.** There is a welcome party in the apartment, where she has lived three weeks. The guests left "hours ago". She wakes, Pathwell is reading her diary, the blob breaks in, the sugar-cookie page is torn and spent, and they leave. She is wearing pajamas, shoes and a coat.
- **Ch. 2.** They go to the Chinese place on foot. The dry-cleaning ticket goes into the gutter. Pathwell leaves, and she follows.
- **Ch. 3.** Pathwell opens a paper door on a concrete wall into the Space Between. Boots cuts her hand and Pathwell heals it with a "Love note". Pathwell prunes to pay the bill, and the cookbook and diary are returned to her.
- **Ch. 4.** They go to Camp Cunnan with the handcart, "after midnight". Mama Baga finds her in the woods. The chicken-and-dumplings page is spent on the injured young woman, and the cookbook goes to the archive ("Pages used? One tonight. Before that? One. Also tonight."). Elizabeth changes clothes, receives the gold chain, and leaves with Pathwell.

### Day 1: Friday
- **Ch. 5.** They walk until "the sky went gray" (dawn). Pathwell sleeps in a field "until well past noon". The portal circle takes them to a school closet. Pathwell says "It's Saturday"; a child says "Friday." The nine o'clock meeting has been missed. They meet Stansbury ("Leo") and wait 20 minutes at the maroon Cadillac. Stansbury shows them the foam weapons. They drive to the bar "late in the afternoon", and Elizabeth takes the dagger.
- **Ch. 6.** They spend afternoon into evening at the bar. Pathwell looks into the mirror in the back hall. They leave at sunset ("Red sky at night"), and Elizabeth is given the keys.
- **Ch. 7.** The blob swallows Pathwell, Elizabeth cuts him out, and Stansbury burns it. Elizabeth drives.
- **Ch. 8.** She crashes into the ditch on purpose (stars coming out). Stansbury has bruised ribs, Pathwell's neck is hurt, and her seatbelt catches her shoulder. Shade arrives in a dark sedan, and Elizabeth leaves with him.
- **Ch. 9.** At the 24/7 diner, about 25 minutes after the road, she drinks the coffee and talks with Shade. She leaves on foot with the receipt.
- **Ch. 10.** She walks "a little over an hour" and past the water tower. Meanwhile, the brothers walk two miles to Stansbury's house, where the letter catches on the coffee memory and two grocery lists guide them. They drive the work van east, find her, and Pathwell confesses in the van. They decide on Camp by way of the museum.
- **Ch. 11.** The drive takes "most of what was left of the night". *(Conflicts with Ch. 17's "four in the morning"; see E1.)*

### Day 2: Saturday
- **Ch. 11.** They reach the museum lot as the windows are "just started to go gray". Shade's sedan is there. The museum incident follows: Elizabeth's left shoulder is dislocated, Pathwell's temple is cut and his ribs are hurt, and Shade's palm is cut. Shade opens the graffiti-wall route and Elizabeth and Shade go to Camp. In the gallery, "it was daylight."
- **Ch. 12.** At Camp "here it was still dark". The shoulder is put back and Elizabeth gets a sling. Shade's left palm is bandaged. They eat stew, Elizabeth donates the diary, and "dawn had finally arrived."
- **Ch. 13.** By "full morning" Shade swaps his coat for the green jacket, makes coffee, and plays Hearts. Milo is named. The draw grows stronger.
- **Ch. 14.** Pathwell and Stansbury arrive by threshold ("Good morning"). Pathwell builds the brass frame over objections, and it fails. The loop smashes a lantern, the archive catches fire, and Milo is trapped.
- **Ch. 15.** A blob takes Shade. Elizabeth gets Milo out past the diary and the cookbook, both of which burn. Stansbury burns his right hand at the side window.
- **Ch. 16.** The healer works through the injured ("Then Shade died here this morning."). The fire is ash by late morning. Camp holds the reckoning "after lunch", Stansbury's dressing is changed "by evening", and Elizabeth and Mama Baga sit together that night.

### Days 3 to 25 (counted from the fire)
- **"By the third morning"** (Monday, if the fire morning counts as the first): Pathwell builds the drying rack three inches too low.
- **First week:** The blue notebook spends three days drying. Pathwell copies "HESS SAYS FOUR" and Elizabeth visits Camp one afternoon. Elizabeth is back in her apartment (the trip home is not shown) and moves the spoon around. The museum session happens: Elizabeth gives her statement and Pathwell's Henry Vale packet is refused.
- **Second week:** The archival-board catalog is thrown away. Milo's ankle heals and he gets mismatched boots. Elizabeth goes back to work.
- **Day 12:** The replacement wagon is chosen.
- **Day 14:** The yellow wagon arrives and is named "The Lemon".
- **End of second week:** Pathwell plays Hearts and loses by 26.
- **Day 18:** The museum's damaged gallery reopens.
- **Day 21:** The Lemon becomes the archive and the crooked label is fixed.
- **Day 25** ("Three weeks and four days after the archive burned"), which falls on a Wednesday if the fire was on Saturday: Elizabeth phones Pathwell, he arrives at 7:03 p.m., they go through the laundromat door to the Space Between, and they leave by the fifth route, the one with geese.

## 4. Rules ledger

**Blobs (the "cleanup")**
- **As stated.** Ch. 5: "Things like that clean up loose magical waste. Failed workings. Unsettled messes. If you don't stand between them and what they're after, they're unpleasant rather than murderous."
- **How they behave.** Ch. 1: "The blob rolled over the fallen threads of gold. Taking their light with it." Ch. 4: "It changed targets. Fresh waste."
- **How they arrive.** Every arrival has the "Wet. Heavy." thud and the low-tide smell (Ch. 1, 6/7, 15).
- **The deliberate exception.** Ch. 7 has a blob go for Pathwell with no waste present ("It turned toward Pathwell, with no hesitation at all."). The book explains this through Shade: "The blobs keep confusing the two of you." (Ch. 10), and "If I leave this unresolved … The blobs stay confused." (Ch. 14).
- **Obeyed.** Elizabeth cuts right next to the blob and is not attacked (Ch. 7). The man pulling at Shade is not attacked (Ch. 15). In Ch. 15 the blob "crossed one, and the loose light vanished beneath it", it took Shade, it "leaned toward" Pathwell, and it withdrew. After that, "The draw did not return." This is consistent.
- **A new, unexplained detail.** Ch. 15: "The air there pulled inward around its edges, as though Camp were making the gap too small for it." This is the first suggestion that Camp resists blobs.

**Charged pages and records**
- **Using one spends it.** Ch. 4: "Then disappeared one line at a time, and the paper itself followed. Not burned. Spent." Ch. 10: "If I use this, it's gone." The book obeys this every time: the Ellison letter (Ch. 10), the lists (Ch. 10), the quarantine letter (Ch. 11), and the portal pages ("The pages in his hand were gone.", Ch. 5).
- **A copy keeps the information, not the thing.** Ch. 4: "The copy won't be the same thing." Ch. 10: "The copy keeps the words." / "Not the thing." The idea is obeyed and echoed in Ch. 11 ("The information.") and Ch. 17 ("The information survived. The page didn't.").
- **Destroying a charged record without using it releases loose feeling.** Ch. 11: "They weren't memories. They were pieces flung loose without anyone shaping them." Ch. 14: "The fire reached the first shelf of charged records, and only then did the room change." Ch. 15 obeys it: "A handwritten page tore in the heat, and for one second Elizabeth was desperately homesick".
  - **Possible bend.** The cookbook was charged (a healer used a page from it, and its card read "RESERVE / ASK BEFORE USE"). Yet it burns with no release noted. Ch. 15/16: "There was no glow, and nothing magical was consuming it. Ordinary flame moved through old paper". Elizabeth, who heard Nana's voice in Ch. 1, registers nothing of Nana's as the book burns. The diary is explicitly "ordinary", so "Just fire." fits the diary.
- **The tracking working.** Ch. 10: "You're going to have to be thinking of the same thing she is, at the same time". Obeyed: it fails on the dumplings, Camp and the archive, and catches on the coffee. The lists give only "where the next step in the sequence pointed" and "Can you talk to her?" / "No."

**Doors and thresholds**
- **The paper door.** Ch. 3: a page on a wall unfolds into a doorway, and "She pulled the door shut and the frame dissolved into the wall behind her."
- **No doors at Camp.** Ch. 5: "Pathwell wouldn't open anything near the wagons". Obeyed: he walks to a field first. The later thresholds at Camp are an existing route ("Graffiti wall. Camp route."), not new openings.
- **The portal circle.** Ch. 5 needs pages, and "Sometimes where you want to go and where you need to go aren't the same place."
- **Anchors need history.** Ch. 10: "There isn't a reliable threshold from here." and "If it's still carrying enough history, it can anchor the door." Obeyed: in Ch. 11 the wall still works after the damage ("the original boards were still standing"), and a route mark is traced.
- **Thresholds close behind people.** Ch. 12, 14 and 18 all obey this.
- **Elizabeth can't open doors herself.** Ch. 17: "Getting there meant asking Pathwell to open the way." Obeyed in Ch. 18, where she phones him.
- **The shopkeeper makes doors at will.** Ch. 4 (the offer of the sidewalk) and Ch. 18.

**Pruning**
- **As stated.** Ch. 3: "I understand the cost of my needs." / "I give what may be, to protect what will." / "You don't have much left to give, Mr. Pathwell." The cost shown is a tremor in the right arm ("His right arm was shaking, but there wasn't a mark on it.").
- **Generalized.** Ch. 10: "The working could accept future possibility as payment."
- **Obeyed.** Ch. 14: "It accepts my pruning directly." The branches are "thinner and older, and still his", which fits "not much left". The Ch. 14 image (pale light from his shadow) differs from Ch. 3's (blue then orange, growing from the tome), but the text frames it as "something like it", and the chest-height detail matches Ch. 3.
- **Camp bans it.** Ch. 16: "You will not prune to buy any of it." Nothing breaks the ban.

**The Space Between**
- Boots takes a blood tribute (Ch. 3). Ch. 18 pays it off: "He remembers winning."
- Outside property is held until the bill is settled: "These were never his to spend."
- The shelves press other people's feelings on you. Ch. 3, and again in Ch. 18: "It hadn't become weaker for her."
- The account stays at zero after it is paid (Ch. 3 → Ch. 18).

**Healing by paper**
- **As stated.** Ch. 3: "Love note … Cures most wounds." and "scars are outside my wheelhouse."
- **Obeyed.** After Ch. 3 all healing is ordinary because there is no paper. The healer has used up what she had: "What we had that would help, I've already used."
- **Bent.** Ch. 16 offers a non-ordinary way to reduce a scar (E2).

**Foam weapons**
- **As stated.** Ch. 5: "You hit the thing you mean to hit, and it does what it was made to do." The dagger: "cuts separation into things that don't separate easily." / "People?" / "It's designed not to."
- **Obeyed.** Ch. 7 (the seam, four cuts). In Ch. 14 Elizabeth reasons from the stated rule, uncertain whether the draw is a binding or Shade himself.

**The draw**
- **As stated.** Ch. 8: "Not who. Not where. Which way." / "I can ignore it for a while. Then it gets worse."
- **Obeyed.** It strengthens with distance closing (Ch. 13: "Stronger." / "Closer?" / "Probably."), it is mutual and faint on Pathwell's side (Ch. 10: "Barely is still him"), and it ends when Shade dies (Ch. 15).

**Camp's clock**
- Not stated as a rule. The museum shows "daylight" while Camp is "still dark" (Ch. 11/12). The narration marks it with "here", but never explains it.

## 5. Things that are correct and worth protecting

- **The days of the week.** Pathwell's "Tuesday" is corrected to "Thursday". The child at school says "Friday". The missed nine o'clock meeting is set up in Ch. 2 and Ch. 4 and paid off in Ch. 5. Pathwell's wrong "It's Saturday" turns out to be the day of the fire.
- **Clothing continuity.** She leaves in pajamas under her coat (Ch. 1). The "pajama legs … since the lobby" detail appears in Ch. 4. The bandana is singed in half in Ch. 5 and never reappears; the Ch. 9 waitress sees only "coat, the pastel linen under it and the gold chain". The gold chain runs from Ch. 4 to Ch. 9 to Ch. 17.
- **The cookbook count.** It has two pages used everywhere: Ch. 4 ("One tonight … One. Also tonight."), Ch. 12's card, and Ch. 15. "MORE IF THEY'RE SICK. THEY WON'T EAT ENOUGH." is quoted verbatim in Ch. 18. The bowl in Ch. 16 has "more in it than she could possibly eat", brought by the young woman who was saved in Ch. 4.
- **The diary's list.** CALL ELECTRIC / BUY BOXES / CHANGE ADDRESS / FIND NANA'S SPOON appears identically in Ch. 12 and Ch. 15. The spoon sits on the counter in Ch. 2 and is put away in Ch. 17.
- **The scar.** Ch. 3: "between her thumb and wrist" and "scars are outside my wheelhouse". Ch. 18: "the thin scar between her thumb and wrist".
- **Pathwell's pages.** The portal uses up his handful of pages (Ch. 5). That sets up "zero books" (Ch. 7), and he steals the quarantine letter from Stansbury's library (Ch. 11: "You took that while I was upstairs.").
- **The coffee chain.** The Space Between's "That's terrible." / "I know." (Ch. 3) runs through the diner (Ch. 9), the tracking working (Ch. 10), Shade's coffee (Ch. 13, "After you already existed"), and Ch. 18 ("Shade would have liked it.").
- **The directions.** The receipt's "second left after the water tower" is underlined twice (Ch. 9) and checked at the water tower (Ch. 10). The van's "Left. Soon." / "There's a water tower up ahead." matches it.
- **Errands and saved records.** The north-route errand (Ch. 14) pays off when the copy is found in Milo's pocket (Ch. 16). Stansbury's two boxes become one saved box (Ch. 15), matching "One flat box" (Ch. 16). Milo's lost boot leads to "One was brown and one was black." (Ch. 17).
- **Injury sides and mechanics.** Elizabeth's left shoulder, Stansbury's right hand, and Shade's bandaged left hand with his drawn right hand are consistent throughout. "You are not using this arm today." is quoted back verbatim in Ch. 15.
- **The foam weapons at Camp.** The swords stay in the van, so Elizabeth's dagger is the only anti-blob tool at Camp. That makes the logic of her choice in Ch. 15 sound.
- **The brothers.** Who is older is consistent ("Older" in Ch. 4; "I am older than you." in Ch. 11; "Little brother." in Ch. 14). So are "Leo" and "big cat".
- **Shade's memories.** "A room in Prague I have never entered" (Ch. 9) matches "Prague." / "I've never been there." (Ch. 14).
- **Small details that hold.**
  - The bar tab matches what was ordered: "Two beers, one ginger ale, two baskets of fish".
  - "Down to six sheets" recurs, and the blank paper is replaced.
  - The climate-control donation pays off as "two sensors out of calibration".
  - The Hess note (three days of rain) gets "HESS SAYS FOUR".
  - "Less than two weeks ago" in Ch. 18 is correct: 13 days after the wagon on day 12.
- **Closing echoes.** "It was the crash that woke her." appears in both Ch. 1 and Ch. 8. "Ready?" / "No." / "Perfect." (Ch. 1) returns with the roles reversed in Ch. 18.
