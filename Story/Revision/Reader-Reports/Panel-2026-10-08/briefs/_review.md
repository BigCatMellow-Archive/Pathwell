## Cross-brief problems (rule 4, and rule 1 where noted)

1. **Rivers of London** appears in target, skimmer, auditor and hostile. Target and hostile both say "the first three." Keep it in **target** only.
2. **Dresden Files** appears in target, skimmer and rules. Keep it in **rules** only.
3. **Pratchett** appears in skimmer and character. Keep it in **character** (Vimes).
4. **Diana Wynne Jones** appears in skimmer and hostile. Keep it in **hostile** (Fire and Hemlock).
5. **Le Guin** appears in character and prose. Keep it in **prose**.
6. **Gaiman** appears in target (Neverwhere) and prose. Keep it in **target**.
7. **Susanna Clarke / Jonathan Strange** appears in prose and hostile. Keep it in **prose**.
8. **The grandmother's cookbook as the reason for picking the book up** appears in target, character and prose. Keep it in **prose**, which adds its own reason: food books.
9. **"Chosen-one" complaint** appears in skimmer, rules, character and hostile. Skimmer and character use the identical phrase "chosen-one prologues." Keep it in **rules** ("power-ups") only.
10. **Everyone talks in one quippy or wisecracking voice** appears in character, prose and hostile. Keep it in **character** only.
11. **Repeated jokes and stinger chapter endings** appear in target, prose and hostile. Keep them in **prose** only.
    - Rule 1: items 10 and 11 together have four readers primed to look for a jokey voice, recycled jokes and stinger endings. That reads as a suspicion about the book's tone, and the cover line doesn't support it. Spreading it across four briefs leaks it.
12. **Burned by a series that went wrong in a later book** appears in target, skimmer, rules, character and prose. Keep it in **rules** and **character** only. The others need burns that aren't about a series.
13. **Burned by front-loaded setup or lore** appears in reluctant, skimmer and hostile. Keep it in **reluctant** and **skimmer** (slow start is central to that brief).
14. **Reads on the train** appears in reluctant, skimmer, auditor, character, prose and hostile. Auditor and character also share the exact wording "on the train … and late at night." Keep the train in **skimmer** and **reluctant** only.
15. **Reads with a pencil or pen** appears in prose, hostile and rules. Drop it from **hostile**. That leaves it in prose and rules, which is acceptable.

## Required changes per brief

- **target**
  - Remove Dresden (item 2).
  - Give a reason other than the cookbook. The apartment building alone is enough, or something else (item 8).
  - Replace "a series that … repeats its own jokes" with a different burn and a different distrust, not repetition and not a series (items 11 and 12).
- **skimmer**
  - Replace Pratchett, Diana Wynne Jones, Dresden and Rivers of London with a different reading history (items 1–4). This also breaks its overlap with hostile.
  - Drop "chosen-one prologues" (item 9).
  - Make the long-series burn something other than a series, such as a single slow book or a genre habit (item 12).
  - Rule 1: "sounded like a fun one-week read" assumes the book's tone and length. Give a reason that doesn't (price, a sale, a stranger reading it).
- **auditor**
  - Remove Rivers of London. Use a different urban fantasy with a mystery spine (item 1).
  - "You picked this one up from its cover line" gives no reason. Give a specific one (item 8).
  - Change "on the train home and again late at night" (item 14).
- **character**
  - Remove Earthsea (item 5).
  - Give a reason other than the cookbook (item 8).
  - Drop "chosen-one prologues" (item 9).
  - Change "on the train and late at night" (item 14).
- **prose**
  - Remove Gaiman (item 6).
  - Make the repeated-joke burn a single book, not "the third book" of a series (item 12).
  - Drop "same quippy register." The voice complaint stays with character (item 10).
  - Drop "on a train" (item 14).
- **hostile**
  - Remove Rivers of London and Jonathan Strange (items 1 and 7).
  - Replace the whole tired-of and burned-by set with distrusts nobody else holds: no chosen-one, jokey narrator, stinger endings or mystery-that-is-setup (items 9, 10, 11 and 13).
  - Drop the train and the pencil (items 14 and 15).
  - Rule 3: "You write sharp one-star and two-star reviews … you aren't unfair, but you're hard to impress" works as a role label plus a temperament. Recast it as reading history: what they've read and rejected, and why.
- **reluctant** and **rules**: no changes. Their shared elements (train, pen, Dresden, chosen-one, series burn) are the ones kept above.
