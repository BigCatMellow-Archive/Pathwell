# Ten things I'd most want to tell the author, ranked

1. Answer or deliberately frame the central question: who asked Pathwell for help, what the problem was, and why pruning was his answer (ch. 5, 6, 10, 17).
2. Explain or cut the stand-alone "It was the crash that woke her." in the middle of the car crash; as it stands it reads as a hint, an echo, or a slip, and nothing pays it off (ch. 8, echoing 1).
3. Cut the lines that explain a scene the scene already earned: both "That was the choice" lines, the crying list, and "She called it evidence" (ch. 4, 15, 16, 17).
4. Cut or compress the second donation scene; it repeats the cookbook scene beat for beat and costs her less (ch. 4, 12).
5. Don't let the brothers stall with "Not here" more than once; each repeat made me trust the withholding less (ch. 5, 6, 7, 8).
6. Keep Shade's death and don't undo it; it is the best scene in the book, and everything that follows depends on it holding (ch. 14, 15, 16).
7. Replace the day-count montage with one or two full scenes, and stop restating that Pathwell has changed; the Henry Vale letters moment already shows it (ch. 17).
8. Give the apartment building and its neighbors something to do, or stop promising them; after chapter 2 the setting I picked the book up for disappears (ch. 1, 2, 17, 18).
9. Cut "Pathwell went still" down to two or three uses; it appears in at least six chapters, and the book notices it in 6 and 8 and keeps doing it (ch. 1, 4, 5, 6, 8, 11, 14).
10. Tidy the loose names and lines: Thumper, "Leo"/"big cat," Camp Cunnan's missing wheel line, the Bakhtak aside, and the cleanup creatures' origin; each opens a question the book doesn't close (ch. 4, 5, 10, 12).
