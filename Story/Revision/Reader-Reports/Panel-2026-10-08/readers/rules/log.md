# Running rules sheet (append-only)

Entries are added as I read. Earlier entries are not revised. Refinements and contradictions are new entries that name the older entry and its chapter.

## Chapter 1

R1.1 (Ch 1) Recipes in the cookbook can be activated by touch. A tagged recipe, hand flat on the page, makes the letters glow gold and then a pool of light forms under the hand. Quote: "He placed his hand flat on the page, the letters glowed a soft gold."

R1.2 (Ch 1) Activation is quick and personal. The chooser is asked for a favorite recipe and told not to think. Quote: "Don't think. What's your favorite recipe?" / "My Nana always made the best sugar cookies?" / "Perfect." The recipe that matches the caster's memory is what gets pulled. Quote: "Flipping through worn and stained pages, he found a tagged recipe. 'Nana Jones Sugar Cookies'."

R1.3 (Ch 1) Using a recipe brings back the memory and sensation tied to it, including someone's voice. Elizabeth gets smell, taste, sun through the window, and her grandmother's voice. Quote: "Everything went white, and the scent hit her; buttery and sweet. Then a taste… and something else. A voice that couldn't be here." Asked why she heard her Nana, he says: "Because it was hers."

R1.4 (Ch 1) Using a recipe costs the page. The page is consumed by the light. Quote: "The glow from the letters now consumed the page, a pool of light forming under his hand." Also: "A ragged edge stuck out where the page had been." (Note: Ch 1 also says "Elizabeth snatched the book, leaving the page behind." It is unclear whether the page was torn out or consumed. Stated as seen.)

R1.5 (Ch 1) Light is a weapon against the creature. The gold light can be struck into the creature, and the strike makes a sound. Quote: "It darted for the light in the man's hand, where it was met sharply by a blinding strike. / The impact sang. / A clear bright note that rang through the room." The creature recoils from it.

R1.6 (Ch 1) Gold light goes out when the creature rolls over it. Quote: "It rolled over the gold on the carpet, and the gold went out." Loose gold threads also drop to the floor when the light "spat and flickered." Quote: "The light under his palm had gone wrong. It spat and flickered, and loose threads of gold dropped onto the carpet."

R1.7 (Ch 1) Creature: a blob/ooze, "wet-rot," smells of low tide "a long way from any sea." It breaks doors and pushes through frames. Quote: "Low tide, a long way from any sea." / "It was some sort of blob, pushing its way through the door frame, taking pieces with it." It seems to target magic. Quote: "It darted for the light in the man's hand."

R1.8 (Ch 1) A house is marked after a magical attack. The mark seems to make it unsafe to stay. Quote: "your house has now been marked, magically speaking. So we should probably be anywhere but here." The mark's mechanics are not explained.

R1.9 (Ch 1) Unexplained: how the man survived or where he is at the end, and what happened to the creature beyond "mostly gone." Quote: "Then nothing." / "It's… mostly gone." Also: "'You really don't know?' ... 'Huh.'" Elizabeth's own part is unexplained. She seems to be a latent user or a target, and nothing is said.

R1.10 (Ch 1) Flag, not a rule. The man is a stranger who reads her diary and handles her grandmother's cookbook. He says "Don't think" and gives orders, and she goes along with it quickly. This is characterization. I will watch whether this is ever explained.

## Chapter 2

R2.1 (Ch 2) The marked house stays dangerous. Pathwell says he would not go back there tonight. Quote: "First off, I wouldn't go back there tonight. But that's just me." This refines R1.8 (marking). Nothing says how long the mark lasts or what triggers it.

R2.2 (Ch 2) The neighborhood has changed, and the change is not explained. The coffee shop she passes every morning is gone, replaced by "a shuttered laundromat she'd never seen before." Quote: "Beyond that, a park that shouldn't have been there." Quote: "Three weeks in this neighborhood and she didn't recognize a single thing." This is a new rule of the world (place shifts), not yet explained. Flag, see Ch 2 entry F2.1.

R2.3 (Ch 2) Nana's voice and the sensory memory come back without any object in hand. Quote: "She heard her again. / Lizzy. / Then a warmth, the taste of butter and sugar, the heat of an oven she hadn't stood in front of since she was twelve." This refines R1.3: the echo can happen when the book is not being used, and it can happen after the recipe page is gone. The trigger is unstated.

R2.4 (Ch 2) Pathwell's last words suggest the cookbook is still with him, and the voice seems to say so. Quote: "He still has it." The "it" is not named. Refines R1.4 (Ch 1, the recipe page was consumed). Not stated whether the cookbook itself carries the power.

R2.5 (Ch 2) Pathwell gives a name and no more. Quote: "'Pathwell.' / 'That's it?' / 'That's it.'" / "'Don't need one. Saves everybody time.'" His nickname for her, "Lizzy," is used without explanation, though she only ever heard her name from the party banner (Ch 1). He also read her diary (Ch 1). Flag: how he knows her nickname is not explained.

R2.6 (Ch 2) Pathwell says the choice is hers. Quote: "That being said, it's up to you what you want to do next. The choice is yours, Lizzy." He does not force her and does not explain. Characterization and an unexplained motive.

R2.7 (Ch 2) Night-time city and the dry-cleaning ticket. Elizabeth loses the ticket to a passing truck. Quote: "As she reached for it, a truck roared past close enough to rattle the table. The ticket skipped off the edge, caught the gutter, and was gone." Mundane cost, no magical rule. Little to do with the world's rules, but it is a loss she feels.

F2.1 (flag, Ch 2) Unexplained: the change in the neighborhood. Her street is not the street she knew. "Why does a coffee shop she passed every morning turn into a laundromat?" Nothing in Ch 1 or Ch 2 covers it.

F2.2 (flag, Ch 2) Why is the flat "marked" and why does that mean she can't go home? She is clearly in danger, and Pathwell refuses to say what he saved her from. "Saved you" is his only explanation. Quote: "I saved you. Most people are thankful when someone does that."

## Chapter 3

R3.1 (Ch 3) A folded paper, pressed flat against a wall with a short phrase in the presence of light, opens a doorway. Light spreads through the creases and doubles with each layer peeled back. Quote: "Light bled through the creases… He peeled back a layer and the square doubled. Another layer, and it doubled again… a doorway stood where the wall had been." Cost not stated. Pathwell says "You don't [know if it's a trap]." Expands R1.1 (light and paper can open the world).

R3.2 (Ch 3) The Space Between is a bookstore that opens onto an unreal place. Sound stops at the threshold. Quote: "The street noise cut out like someone had closed a window." Its stock is "Not books — lives. Journals with cracked spines, letters bundled in ribbon, recipes on index cards." It "had been here practicing for years." Shelves bow under weight. The door frame dissolves once shut. Quote: "the frame dissolved into the wall behind her. Just shelves now."

R3.3 (Ch 3) The books carry the feelings of the people who wrote them, and touching them can take those feelings into the body. Elizabeth's chest tightens, her eyes burn, she wants to cry over something she cannot name. Quote: "The spine of a journal was warm to the touch." Then: "None of that had been hers, and she held on to that." She can resist for a time. Quote: "Then something was [hers]." She then has a panic attack and faints. Quote: "The last thing she registered was the carpet against her knees." Limits: she can resist for a time; the source is each book.

R3.4 (Ch 3) Coffee (bad, familiar) calms a person coming out of a shock. Quote: "'Why does it help?' / 'Familiar things are useful.'" The mechanism is not explained. Flag as unexplained, see F3.1.

R3.5 (Ch 3) The guardian cat, Boots, takes blood as tribute. Quote: "'He wanted his tribute,' the man behind the counter said." Boots "did indeed sample the blood from your cut. He's the full-time guardian and part-time blood god." He is paid in mice. Quote: "We pay him in mice." Cost to Elizabeth: a single cut from a claw, which is then "healed."

R3.6 (Ch 3) Pathwell's healing is a "love note": a strip of paper, held on the wound while he whispers. The paper glows and fades into the skin. The pain and the wound go into a feeling. Quote: "The wound and the pain went with it, leaving behind a lingering feeling that reminded her of a boy she knew in middle school." He says: "Cures most wounds." Also: "scars are outside my wheelhouse." So the cure has a limit (scars) and a hidden cost (the feeling is passed on, presumably to the patient, but the source of the feeling is unexplained). Refines the cost rule in Ch 3.

R3.7 (Ch 3) Debts and bills at the shop accumulate and come due. Quote: "All bills come due eventually, Mr. Pathwell." The shop keeps a ledger and does not forget. Currency and barter: "a Rai Stone," "thirty Lydian coins." The shopkeeper says Pathwell "already owe[s] more than I allow most." The limit on his credit is not explained.

R3.8 (Ch 3) Pathwell's "potential" is a tradeable good with a market value. Quote: "I've got potential to sell. Lots." / "You did." Pathwell has sold some already and later says "Some of it" when asked which part of his potential he sold. Quote: "'If I knew that, I'd make worse decisions with more confidence.'" His potential is partly spent and partly still held.

R3.9 (Ch 3) Pathwell pays a bill with his potential through a tome: a leather-bound book with silver-gilt edges and a branded dot. The rite: he says "I understand the cost of my needs." / "I give what may be, to protect what will." / "Perdat quod esse poterat." The Latin reads as "let (him) lose what could have been," or similar. Roots of light grasp his forearm, enter his arm, and grow a tree up his body. Some branches wither. Quote: "Pathwell screamed in a million voices, voices lost to time, burning out to become memories never made." Costs: the arm is shaking afterward but "there wasn't a mark on it." The tome's price is his potential; the shopkeeper warns, "Mr. Pathwell, are you sure this is worth what it will cost?"

R3.10 (Ch 3) The tome's cost is a limited supply. The shopkeeper says, "You don't have much left to give, Mr. Pathwell." Pathwell replies, "Take what you will, and spare me the lesson." Quote: "The fire burned down, taking all but a few living branches at the core." Some of his potential is left, but how much is unknown.

R3.11 (Ch 3) A bill can be paid with a ritual, not only coin. Quote: "'Your bill has been paid in full. Will that be paper or plastic?'" Payment closes the account. Quote: "'Your account is settled now.'" The shop's own cart is always there: "'You had that the whole time?' / 'Yes.'"

R3.12 (Ch 3) Outside property can be pulled into a transaction. Quote: "He brought outside property into an open transaction, and I dislike paperwork." The shopkeeper requires settlement before he will hand back Elizabeth's books, which are not Pathwell's to pay with. Quote: "These were never his to spend." (Pathwell apparently spent something that was not his.)

F3.1 (flag, Ch 3) "Familiar things are useful" is the only explanation given for why the bad coffee steadies her. The rule behind it is never stated.

F3.2 (flag, Ch 3) The cookbook is heavier after the page is gone. Quote: "The cookbook felt heavier, which was stupid. Missing a page should have made it lighter." She names it as stupid, but nothing explains it. Possible rule: the book's weight tracks something other than paper.

F3.3 (flag, Ch 3) Pathwell promised answers, and then did not give any. Quote: "I'll answer any questions." / "I can answer all your questions, even the ones you'll have next." After the coffee, the shopkeeper's transaction takes over and nothing is answered. A broken or dodged promise. Also the cat is "guardian" of what? Not stated.

F3.4 (flag, Ch 3) Pathwell calls her "Lizzy," and says it is what her grandmother called her. Quote: "'Isn't that what your grandmother called you?'" How he knows this is unexplained; the book may carry the answer.

## Chapter 4

R4.1 (Ch 4) A recipe can heal a person when the healer holds the page and lays a hand on the patient's sternum. No light is shown this time, unlike Ch 1. Quote: "No flash. No burst of gold. The page simply began to give off the particular heat of a kitchen in winter." The healing comes with the smells and the memories of the cooking. Quote: "Broth. Pepper. Flour dust against the back of a hand." This refines R1.1 (Ch 1, gold light). Gold is the light used for Elizabeth's recipe and for Pathwell's attack. The healer's method has no visible light, and the mechanism is not explained.

R4.2 (Ch 4) A used recipe page is spent, and it vanishes. Quote: "Nana's handwriting darkened on the page. Then thinned. Then disappeared one line at a time, and the paper itself followed. / Not burned. Spent." This confirms R1.4 (Ch 1). The page is consumed by use; it is not an optional cost. Quote on the book: "A clean gap remained where the page had been."

R4.3 (Ch 4) The recipe's effect is strong but limited. The healer says: "I can keep her where she is. What we had that would help, I've already used. I don't have enough here to move her forward." The healing gets the patient through the night, and the result is ordinary recovery. Quote: "'She'll sleep.' … 'Then she wakes up sore and hungry.'" This shows a limit: the recipe does not restore everything; it returns the patient to a normal state. Refines R3.6 (Ch 3, love note cures most wounds).

R4.4 (Ch 4) Reading a recipe's margin notes and touching the page brings back Elizabeth's own memories, not the writer's. Quote: "Elizabeth ran one finger down the edge of the recipe. A different memory this time. Not twelve. Seventeen, maybe, sitting at Nana's table with the flu." Then: "The page was ordinary paper." This refines R2.3 (Ch 2): the cookbook prompts her memories even without a working. The rule for when the memory comes is not stated.

R4.5 (Ch 4) Records are donated to an archive and the original is kept. The copy is not the same thing. Quote: "'We keep the original,' he said. 'Someone may copy the recipes for information. The copy won't be the same thing.'" Healers may request a page: "If a page is needed badly enough and fits the work, a healer may request it." Quote: "'If it survives long enough, someone may spill tea on it… Someone may not touch it for thirty years.'" The archive tracks page use with marks in the margin. Quote: "'Pages used?' 'One tonight.'"

R4.6 (Ch 4) The archive's reserve shelf holds records "Available," not hidden or displayed. Quote: "Not hidden. Not displayed. Available." The book can be retrieved later, but nothing says how or by whom.

R4.7 (Ch 4) The creature was already at the door before the cookbook was used, and it changed targets after the working broke. Quote: "'It was already at the door before you used the cookbook?' 'Yes.' … 'It changed targets. Fresh waste. That part made sense.' 'The first part did not.'" This is a new rule of the world: something (unknown) brought the creature to her door, and the working drew it again. Flag, see F4.2.

R4.8 (Ch 4) Elizabeth gave the whole book, not the page, and the book went to the archive. Quote: "'The whole book.' … 'We only need the page.' … 'If I keep holding the rest, I'm going to spend the next ten minutes finding a reason this page is the wrong one.'" Quote: "That was the choice. / Everything afterward was consequence." She did not go back for it: "Behind her, Nana's cookbook stayed on the shelf, and Elizabeth didn't go back for it." This is a real, self-chosen cost.

R4.9 (Ch 4) The woods are hostile to the careless. Quote: "'Now, you really do have to be careful, young lady. These trees are very spiteful.'" Also: a path that splits, a bear she did not see. Pathwell: "'Not if you didn't see her.'" The rule is not stated beyond the spite.

R4.10 (Ch 4) Camp Cunnan is a refuge for many magic practices. Quote: "'Welcome, Elizabeth, to Camp Cunnan, home of the Cunning Folk. Dwelling of charmers, blessers, wizards and witches.'" The camp "kept being a place without asking permission." Its rules are not given.

R4.11 (Ch 4) Pathwell's "delivery" to Camp was not authorised. Mama Baga: "'We did not ask for this.' … 'You could have written first.'" Pathwell: "'On what?'" Mama Baga: "'You appointed yourself a delivery.'" His older brother, Stansbury, is the one he should have gone to first. Quote: "'You should have gone to him before this.'" Flag, see F4.4.

F4.1 (flag, Ch 4) Why does Elizabeth's pajama-legs-under-a-coat matter? Only the clothes change. Not a rule.

F4.2 (flag, Ch 4) The working that brought the creature to the door, and what "the working" was, is not explained. Pathwell says "The first part did not [make sense]" and "No." This is an open question for the book.

F4.3 (flag, Ch 4) "Fresh waste" is never defined. The creature is apparently a thing that comes from waste, or a waste-eater, or an agent of waste. Unexplained.

F4.4 (flag, Ch 4) Pathwell's brother, Stansbury, is named but not seen. Pathwell is deflecting. Stansbury is "Older," and Mama Baga is amused. The brother's role is unknown.

F4.5 (flag, Ch 4) Elizabeth's answer "One. Also tonight." to "Before that?" is unclear. It could mean the book has already been used once, or it could be a joke. The archive writes "two small marks in the margin" either way.

F4.6 (flag, Ch 4) The Camp healer had no more supplies, and the recipe filled the gap. Is that the cost of a resource that has to be spent? The page was the only thing that had enough. Good cost, but the healer had to use Nana's page, which means Elizabeth paid, because she gave the book up.

## Chapter 5

R5.1 (Ch 5) A portal is made by crumpling folded pages in the hand, with yellow light, then drawing a circle and spinning. The pages are consumed. Quote: "He gripped them tight, crumpling them in his hand, and a yellow light danced out in every direction." Later: "The pages in his hand were gone." Cost: paper (pages), plus a violent wind that singes objects. Quote: "the bandana in her hand had been singed in half." Expands R3.1 (Ch 3): folded paper opens doors, and the working is violent.

R5.2 (Ch 5) A portal's destination is set by need, not by the caster's will. Quote: "'We went where we needed to go. That doesn't mean I know where that is. Sometimes where you want to go and where you need to go aren't the same place.'" Pathwell does not know where "here" is, and does not know what the destination was for. Limit: the caster cannot choose the destination. Quote: "'That was a portal.' 'To where?' 'Here.'"

R5.3 (Ch 5) Stansbury's foam weapons are magic. They glow when activated, and they are sold as a set of stock. Quote: "They're magic... They don't need to be pointy." The swords glow "a bright orange, interlocking plates of light wrapping it from the base up." A strike shakes a tree and knocks rain off it. Quote: "'You don't need to know any magic to use one. You hit the thing you mean to hit, and it does what it was made to do.'" Limits: "They aren't indestructible. If one cracks, splits or starts heating when it shouldn't, stop using it."

R5.4 (Ch 5) The dagger's rule: it cuts "separation into things that don't separate easily. Adhesions. Clinging effects. Certain magical bindings." Limit on people: "'People?' 'It's designed not to.'" Quote: "'It's designed not to.'" Possible use on the roots and bindings in Ch 3 (see clever-move candidates). Note the qualifier "designed not to," which is not "cannot."

R5.5 (Ch 5) Stansbury's trade: he harnesses the latent magical energy in children's work and redirects it into items, then sells the items. Quote: "'I harness the latent magic energy created by the children's work and redirect it into items. Then I sell those items to interested buyers. I don't steal anything.'" Elizabeth sees it as the same as Pathwell's sale of potential in Ch 3. Quote: "'Selling off potential nobody was going to use.'" Stansbury says the work would never be used otherwise. This is a cost issue, see Ch 5 costs.

R5.6 (Ch 5) The creature is a scavenger of loose magical waste. Quote: "'Things like that clean up loose magical waste. Failed workings. Unsettled messes. If you don't stand between them and what they're after, they're unpleasant rather than murderous.'" This is the most direct statement of what the creature is. It refines R1.7 (Ch 1) and R4.3's "fresh waste" (Ch 4). Also, the creature only becomes lethal when something stands in its way.

R5.7 (Ch 5) Pathwell's sale of potential shows as physical decline: his right hand still shakes. Stansbury: "'You pruned.'" and "'How thin?'" Pathwell: "'He said I didn't have much left to give.'" This refines R3.10. Pruned = potential sold; "thin" = how much is left.

R5.8 (Ch 5) The creature was already at the door, and Stansbury doubts whose door it was. Quote: "'The part that doesn't fit is why it was already at your door.' 'Her door,' Pathwell said. Stansbury looked at him. 'Was it?'" Flag, see F5.1.

R5.9 (Ch 5) Time and calendar. Pathwell says "It's Saturday." A child says "Friday." Quote: "'It's Saturday.'" / "'Friday.'" Elizabeth's Friday meeting at nine has passed. Quote: "Somewhere back in the city, her nine o'clock meeting had come and gone while she sat in a field." Refines R2 (her Thursday night meeting). Pathwell's time claim was wrong.

R5.10 (Ch 5) Pathwell's "I don't know" and "I don't lie." Stansbury says Pathwell "lies" and "only partially remembers his own half-truths." Quote: "'He lies, you know. Don't listen to him when it comes to old stories.'" Pathwell: "'I don't lie.'" This is a claim the book does not resolve. Flag only.

R5.11 (Ch 5) Mama Baga's camp sends word through its own channels. Quote: "'Mama Baga sent word that something followed you into a city apartment before you started a working.'" Refines R4.10 (Camp Cunnan).

F5.1 (flag, Ch 5) Why is the creature at a door that might not be Elizabeth's? Stansbury presses the point, and Pathwell cannot answer. Unexplained.

F5.2 (flag, Ch 5) The hidden dagger and the foam sword taken from the box. Elizabeth takes a foam dagger, unasked, and goes into the bar with it hidden in her waistband. Not a rule, but the book has given her a tool.

F5.3 (flag, Ch 5) Stansbury says of the creature: "'If you don't stand between them and what they're after…'" What does the creature go after in Elizabeth's case? The book has not yet said.

## Chapter 6

R6.1 (Ch 6) Pathwell feels a directional pull, a thread drawn taut behind his ribs. It is "A pull without a destination." It sharpens or eases as he turns, and he cannot read it reliably. Quote: "Not a direction he trusted. Barely a direction at all." He has felt it for a long time: "It had been with him in small ways for longer than he cared to count." This refines R5.7 (pruning, potential): the pull is a consequence he has "collected and declined to inventory." Mechanism not stated.

R6.2 (Ch 6) The creature's pull points to Pathwell himself. Quote: "'Something points in an unpleasant direction,' he said. / 'Which direction?' / Pathwell looked down at his hand. 'Mine.'" The creature came to the door before the cookbook was used, and its arrival only made sense after the working broke (Pathwell's own reasoning). Quote: "The apartment door. The blob already there before the cookbook. The way it had only made sense after Elizabeth broke the working." This refines R4.7 and R5.8: the thing was drawn by something of his, possibly a waste or a debt. Not confirmed in the text.

R6.3 (Ch 6) Mirror and reflection: when the pull is strong, the reflection can be "wrong in the smallest possible way." Quote: "Then, for one heartbeat, it didn't [look ordinary]." This is an unexplained effect, not a stated rule, and it implies that his self-reading can be wrong. Flag, see F6.2.

R6.4 (Ch 6) Pathwell's consequences are many and mostly unrecorded. Quote: "one of the many consequences he had collected and declined to inventory properly." Cost is now partly a personal weakness, not only a sale.

R6.5 (Ch 6) Stansbury's reason to stay quiet with Elizabeth is to keep her from being pulled into his brother's business: "'Nobody was chasing you?' 'No.'" Quote: "'Most people seem to have an opinion about what I should do next.' 'I have several.' 'And?' 'You didn't ask for them.'" Not a magic rule. Characterization.

R6.6 (Ch 6) Pathwell's "Not here" is a refusal to talk about his own problem in public, and Elizabeth gives him a pass for the moment. Quote: "'Not here.' Not now. Not here. Not necessarily not her." Flag, see F6.3.

R6.7 (Ch 6) Pathwell is asked to explain the pull and says "I don't know yet." Quote: "'What does that mean?' 'I don't know yet.' It sounded strange coming from Pathwell." Refines R5.10: when he says "I don't know," he may be saying the truth.

R6.8 (Ch 6) The bartender may be ahead of his own actions. Quote: "She could have sworn one of them came a hair before his hand did." This is a hint, not a stated rule. Flag, see F6.4.

R6.9 (Ch 6) Elizabeth has the keys to the Cadillac. She drives. Pathwell wants to watch his brother, possibly stop him from "opening a door in a moving car." Quote: "Possibly stop him from opening a door in a moving car." The chapter ends with a wet, heavy thud from the far side of the Cadillac. Quote: "Then, from the far side of the Cadillac, a thud. / Wet. Heavy."

F6.1 (flag, Ch 6) The creature is back, at the end of the chapter, with no explanation of how it found them. It followed the pull, or it followed Pathwell. Unexplained so far.

F6.2 (flag, Ch 6) The mirror is a strange image, and it is never explained. Is it an effect of the pull, or a warning? The book does not say.

F6.3 (flag, Ch 6) Pathwell's "Mine" is the strongest hint so far that the whole thing is his. Elizabeth decides to let him have "that much, for the moment." The book has not yet said what "mine" means.

F6.4 (flag, Ch 6) The bartender, with his muddler, may have the sort of foreknowledge that Pathwell has. "Came a hair before his hand did." Nothing tells us what he is.

F6.5 (flag, Ch 6) Stansbury's "Don't confuse restraint with virtue." He is not asking, and he has a plan that is not stated.

## Chapter 7

R7.1 (Ch 7) Foam sword: it cuts the blob's leading edge, and it does not stop the blob. Quote: "The sword sank halfway into blackness. The thing shuddered. It didn't stop." The sword alone does not destroy the creature. This refines R5.3 (swords are magic; hit what you mean to hit): the sword is a poor tool against the creature.

R7.2 (Ch 7) Foam baton: it strikes the ground and sends heat and orange-white light through the cracks under the creature. Quote: "Heat hit the parking lot. Dry, immediate, furnace-door heat." Striking the creature directly: "For one instant flame moved through the thing without catching on anything else. The blob collapsed inward." Quote: "Its shape broke into heavy wet sections that flattened, hissed and thinned until only black streaks were left on the dirt." The baton is the thing that kills the creature. Limit: "That one is gone." (Stansbury, see F7.3). Refines R5.3 (swords and batons can heat when used, and stop when they crack).

R7.3 (Ch 7) Dagger cuts seams, not flesh. Quote: "The dagger should have bent. It didn't. Something opened. Not flesh. A seam." The blob recoils from the cut. Elizabeth frees Pathwell by cutting "across the band holding his ribs." Quote: "The thing released him all at once." This is the first on-page test of R5.4 ("designed not to" harm people) and it held. Refines R5.4.

R7.4 (Ch 7) Pathwell's workings need paper or books on his person. He has none and it nearly costs him. Quote: "'You mean to tell me you brought zero books with you!?' ... 'Yes! It slipped my mind! Would you please focus!?'" Pathwell "went for his coat, patted it, and came up with nothing." Refines R5.1 (pages consumed) and R3.1 (paper opens doors). This is a limit on his power that is visible in the scene.

R7.5 (Ch 7) The creature goes for a particular person and ignores others. Quote: "It turned toward Pathwell, with no hesitation at all." And: "'It went for you.' Pathwell said nothing." Then: "'Not me,' she said. Pathwell looked at her. 'No.'" The creature is attracted to whatever it is that is his, not to Elizabeth. Refines R5.6 (scavenger of waste) and R6.2 ("Mine").

R7.6 (Ch 7) Pathwell's own remark: "'The first one found you in her apartment before there was fresh waste.'" Stansbury says this, not Pathwell: "And the first one found you in her apartment before there was fresh waste." Then Elizabeth: "My apartment." Stansbury: "If whatever it keeps finding is close enough to you that it can't tell the difference—". Pathwell: "Not me." Then: "I can't yet." Refines R4.7 (the creature was at the door first).

R7.7 (Ch 7) The "prune that went wrong" is the likely source of the pull. Quote: "'Since the prune that went wrong?' 'Maybe.' 'Which prune?'" Pathwell does not answer. Refines R5.7 (pruned = potential sold). Flag, see F7.2.

## Chapter 14

R14.1 (Ch 14) A "rejoining frame" is a working built from brass pins, a loop and waxed cord. Two anchors pin the cord, and a return line runs between them. Quote: "'Two anchors. One return.'" Shade: "'You're building a rejoining frame.'" Pathwell: "'A way to look at the connection without you dislocating a shoulder every time I get close.'" The aim is to make the working treat the two as "one settled structure instead of two." Quote: "'make the working decide that we're one settled structure instead of two.'" Refines R10.1 (the shared memory link) by making the link physical.

R14.2 (Ch 14) Pathwell's frame takes payment from his own potential, and the payment is what fails. Quote: "'It accepts my pruning directly.'" Mama Baga: "'Do not release payment.'" Stansbury: "'You are not paying for this.'" Refines R10.7 (pruning takes future possibility as payment).

R14.3 (Ch 14) A working follows the intent it is given, not the wish behind it. Quote: "'The working knows what you mean, not what you wish you meant. You are giving it two incompatible answers.'" (Mama Baga.) Pathwell wanted to rejoin Shade and keep him separate; the frame could not do both. This is the book's clearest statement of how workings fail. It also explains the museum failure (R11.2 and R11.3): the intent was too wide.

R14.4 (Ch 14) A payment released mid-working is not recovered. It spills as "ownerless threads." Quote: "Pale branches snapped inward around his body and then burst apart into loose, ownerless threads across the dirt. / Fresh spillage." Refines R10.8 (unclaimed possibility). The released possibility is unowned and goes wherever it lands.

R14.5 (Ch 14) Released possibility breaks loose records. When it reaches charged records, the impressions are freed to the room. Quote: "The fire reached the first shelf of charged records, and only then did the room change. A hundred small human impressions broke loose into the heat at once." Refines R3.3 and R11.4 (impressions in books). The archive's charged records are a store of other people's feelings, and damage releases them.

R14.6 (Ch 14) Ordinary fire is a danger to the archive. The lantern is knocked over by the brass loop. Quote: "Oil crossed the wooden threshold in a bright ordinary sheet, and flame followed it." The "ordinary fire had found history, and history answered badly." This is a new effect: ordinary fire and magic interact through history.

R14.7 (Ch 14) Shade can refuse a working by standing still. Quote: "'Then whatever happens next is yours.'" and: "'He didn't try to tear the frame apart. He simply stood where he was and refused.'" Refines R9.3 and R12.7 (resisting the draw). Refusal does not undo a working; it only stops one side from pulling.

R14.8 (Ch 14) Shade's identity question is the core of the working. Quote: "'Do I remain myself?'" Pathwell: "'I think the distinction can survive.'" Shade: "'Can.' … 'Not will.'" Pathwell: "'No.'" This answers R8.2 and R13.6 in part: the working threatens Shade's separateness, and Pathwell cannot promise it.

R14.9 (Ch 14) The dagger was not used when Elizabeth could have used it. Quote: "'Whatever held Shade's arm out now might be a binding, and might be him. She didn't know what it would cut, or whose it was to cut. She left it where it was.'" Stansbury's rule (R5.4): the dagger "cuts separation into things that don't separate easily… designed not to cut people." The binding here was the frame. This is a missed move, see section (c).

R14.10 (Ch 14) Pathwell's failure is the release of his own potential into a frame that cannot hold it. The cost falls on him: "The future he had offered entered the structure, and the structure had nowhere coherent to put it." Quote for physical cost: "The gauze at his temple darkened. His right arm shook hard enough that his hand blurred." He is left on the ground, conscious but unable to stand.

R14.11 (Ch 14) Mama Baga states a clear rule about who may stop a working: the practitioner can stop it by lifting his hand. Quote: "'Lift your hand,' she said." "'Lift it,' Mama Baga said. 'Let the frame fall. Nothing has released yet.'" Refines R11.6. Stopping is possible before release. After release, the working must be left to finish or fail.

R14.12 (Ch 14) Stansbury stepped between Pathwell and the frame, and then stood aside when Shade asked him to. Quote: "'If this fails because Stansbury stood there, he'll spend the rest of his life explaining that part instead of the choice.'" Stansbury: "'Then he stepped aside.'" Not a magic rule. Consent-based restraint of another person's working.

R14.13 (Ch 14) "Let it fail" is what Elizabeth says, and Pathwell refuses. Quote: "'Let it fail,' she said. … 'I can contain it.' … 'That's what you said at the museum.'" Refines R11.6. The working does not stop when he refuses to end it.

R14.14 (Ch 14) A lamp or flame can destroy a whole archive. Milo is trapped inside. Quote: "'I'm stuck!'" Elizabeth's shoulder is injured and she moves anyway. Elizabeth's diary is near the threshold, and the cookbook is on the reserve shelf, inside the burning wagon.

F14.1 (flag, Ch 14) Elizabeth's decision not to cut the frame with the dagger. She had the tool and the authority and chose to wait. "She didn't know what it would cut, or whose it was to cut." The book makes this a missed move, but also a sensible one. See section (c).

F14.2 (flag, Ch 14) Stansbury's "You are not paying for this" and then his stepping aside. He could have stopped the working by force and did not. The text gives Shade's reason: "Don't make him the reason." Stansbury's choice is the book's position on consent.

F14.3 (flag, Ch 14) Pathwell says "I can preserve him inside the settlement." Mama Baga: "'You don't know that.'" Pathwell agrees: "'No.'" The book is clear that the working's outcome is unknown and he proceeds anyway.

F14.4 (flag, Ch 14) The archive fire is the first real consequence to people other than Pathwell. Milo is stuck. The book has not yet said whether he survives.

F14.5 (flag, Ch 14) Why the archive and why now. The frame's released possibility goes into the archive, not into Camp or into the brothers. The book does not explain why the spillage chose the records.

## Chapter 15

R15.1 (Ch 15) The creature takes unclaimed waste, and a person who has become unclaimed can be taken as waste. Shade walks away from Pathwell, and the blob "bent after Shade." Quote: "The blob changed course, slowly and completely. Its whole dark mass bent after Shade." Shade is taken. Quote: "Then there was no Shade standing in the clearing." R15.2 (Ch 15) The creature removes spillage as waste, and it does not return it to its source. Quote: "The spillage vanished into the dark surface. It wasn't settled, or returned to Pathwell. R15.3 (Ch 15) The creature ignores emotional residue from the fire. Quote: "The blob had ignored all of it." Also: "The emotional fallout kept breaking loose in fragments." Refines R14.5 (impressions). The creature takes only what is unclaimed and tied to a working, not what is only felt.

R15.4 (Ch 15) The creature's targeting is reading "signals," and it can be split by movement. Shade's theory: "'I think it's been trying to decide between us because it can't read the difference cleanly.'" Shade: "'I'd like to find out which problem it thinks is biggest.'" Quote: "'By separating the signals.'" Refines R7.5. Shade's walk away from Pathwell did work: the creature followed the one moving, but it also took him.

R15.5 (Ch 15) Pathwell cannot stop the creature from taking his unclaimed spillage, and he cannot stand. Quote: "'Pathwell tried to stand, and his arm failed.'" Cost: physical and lasting. R15.6 (Ch 15) Ordinary fire destroys records without any magic. Quote: "Nana's cookbook had caught along the bottom edge. There was no glow, and nothing magical was consuming it. The cookbook is lost to ordinary fire, not to a working.

R15.7 (Ch 15) Elizabeth chooses to save Milo over the books. Quote: "'She got Milo through the door.' 'That was the choice.' 'Everything else burned afterward.'" Quote: "'She didn't regret the order.'" She tore off the sling to free Milo, and the shoulder took more damage. Quote: "Her left arm dropped three inches, and pain went white behind her eyes." Cost: real.

R15.8 (Ch 15) Stansbury saves two archive boxes and the blue road notebook, and burns his right hand doing it. Quote: "The skin across the back of his right hand was already red and shining." Quote: "'You don't need to save everything.' 'I know.'" He saves what he can, but not the back shelf. Quote: "'Not that shelf.'" The blue notebook and a family letters bundle survive. Quote: "'Not everything. Something.'"

R15.9 (Ch 15) Pathwell's last-minute plan to use spillage as bait fails in its premise. Quote: "'Fresh pruning spill by the frame. It should be what it's here for.' … 'Should.'" The creature does take the spill, but it also takes Shade, which Pathwell did not plan.

R15.10 (Ch 15) Shade chooses to leave Pathwell and to draw the creature. Quote: "'Shade turned toward the open side of the clearing, away from Pathwell.'" Quote: "'No,' he said" (to Pathwell's "Will you stay?"). This is a deliberate choice, and the book frames it as self-directed.

R15.11 (Ch 15) Shade's last words are in character and not a speech. Quote: "'You still owe me a hand,' Milo called." Shade: "'I was winning.'" / "'Corrupt institution.'" The book refuses to let the death be a grand scene: "Shade had never been particularly interested in deserving scenes."

F15.1 (flag, Ch 15) The draw between Shade and Pathwell "did not return. Shade did not either." The text says Shade is gone. Whether this is death, absorption, or a rule about unclaimed beings is not stated. The book has not explained what happens to a piece once it is taken as waste.

F15.2 (flag, Ch 15) Elizabeth's dagger, again. She has it in her hand, and she does not use it. Quote: "She had used it on one of these before. This one was coming for him because he had let it, and it wasn't hers to cut." Refines R14.9: the second missed use. For section (c).

F15.3 (flag, Ch 15) The creature's stated "rule" of waste is now shown with a person. The book has broken its earlier claim (R5.6, "unpleasant rather than murderous") because Shade was the thing taken, not Elizabeth or Pathwell. The creature is murderous if it takes a person as waste.

F15.4 (flag, Ch 15) Stansbury stays and works the archive even after Elizabeth asks him not to. He refuses to let the fire take "one more shelf." Not a rule. For section (d): his burn is paid and real.

F15.5 (flag, Ch 15) "The queen of spades lay on the card table where the archivist had left it." The token is left behind after Shade's removal. Whether the queen has any power is never shown.
