# Rules reader: lens output (saved by the orchestrator from the reader's reply; the reader's harness refused the write)

## (a) Rules sheet, final version

**Recipes and the cookbook**

- A1. A tagged recipe lights under a hand, and use spends the page. Ch 1: "He placed his hand flat on the page, the letters glowed a soft gold." Ch 4: "Nana's handwriting darkened on the page. Then thinned… Not burned. Spent."
- A2. Use brings the writer's sensations and voices. Ch 1: "A voice that couldn't be here." Asked why, he says: "'Because it was hers.'"
- A3. A recipe heals to stability, not to full health, and needs no light. Ch 4: "No flash. No burst of gold. The page simply began to give off the particular heat of a kitchen in winter." Limit, Ch 4: "'I don't have enough here to move her forward.'" Outcome: "'Then she wakes up sore and hungry.'"
- A4. Ordinary medicine heals ordinary injury with no paper. Ch 12: "There was no paper in it anywhere: just hands, cloth, a bitter-smelling salve."

**Paper, doors, thresholds**

- A5. Folded paper and light open a door. Ch 3: "He peeled back a layer and the square doubled." The paper is consumed (Ch 5: "The pages in his hand were gone").
- A6. Destination is set by need, not by the caster. Ch 5: "Sometimes where you want to go and where you need to go aren't the same place."
- A7. A threshold needs history and a route mark. Ch 10: "If it's still carrying enough history, it can anchor the door." Ch 11: "'that's where the mark used to be.'"
- A8. A pull can open a seam where it points. Ch 13: "a seam of pale light appeared between two trees."
- A9. A brass tag rotated against a sign opens a utility door. Ch 18: "Pathwell rotated the tag."
- A10. A door appears after a decision. Ch 18: "'Then come with me.'" Then "A narrow door appeared beside the shelf of journals."
- A11. The far side can remember a person. Ch 18: "'They remember me.'"

**Memory link and search**

- A12. The only link is a shared memory, and it cannot be planted. Ch 10: "'You had to be remembering something we both actually remembered.'"
- A13. The search consumes its object, and a copy does not work. Ch 10: "'If I use this, it's gone.' … 'The copy keeps the words.' 'Not the thing.'"
- A14. A list gives direction only. Ch 10: "Nothing about Elizabeth's thoughts, or what she wanted. Only where the next step in the sequence pointed."

**The pull (draw)**

- A15. The pull has a direction and no destination. Ch 6: "A pull without a destination." Ch 8: "'Not who. Not where. Which way.'"
- A16. It can be resisted, and resisting costs over time. Ch 9: "'You can resist it,' she said. 'Yes.' … 'Does it hurt?' 'Eventually.'"

**Workings in general**

- A17. Workings take payment from the practitioner's future possibility. Ch 10: "The working could accept future possibility as payment… I paid with mine."
- A18. A working does what the intent says, not what is wished. Ch 14: "'The working knows what you mean, not what you wish you meant. You are giving it two incompatible answers.'"
- A19. Stopping works only before release. Ch 14, Mama Baga: "'Lift your hand… Let the frame fall. Nothing has released yet.'" After release it runs out. Ch 11: "The page released. The handwriting vanished all at once."
- A20. Range is not capped. Ch 11: "The working tried to make distance physical, and it shoved chairs, signs, the broken barrier…"
- A21. A failed working leaves spillage that belongs to no one. Ch 14: "loose, ownerless threads across the dirt. / Fresh spillage."
- A22. Released charge frees the impressions held in charged records. Ch 14: "A hundred small human impressions broke loose into the heat at once."

**Potential and the shop**

- A23. Potential is currency, paid to the shop. Ch 3: "'I've got potential to sell. Lots.' / 'You did.'"
- A24. The rite costs the body and other lives. Ch 3, the tome: "I give what may be, to protect what will." The tree "bloomed in patches, sick and breathless." Pathwell "screamed in a million voices, voices lost to time, burning out to become memories never made."
- A25. Spent potential shows. Ch 5: "'You pruned.' … 'How thin?'" / "'He said I didn't have much left to give.'"
- A26. The shop keeps a ledger and closes accounts. Ch 3: "'All bills come due eventually, Mr. Pathwell.'" Ch 18: "'Your account remains at zero.'"
- A27. Unclaimed possibility becomes something. Ch 10: "I know what unclaimed possibility looks like afterward."

**Pieces**

- A28. A bad prune splits off pieces that carry memories of a life not lived, and the feeling of the decision. Ch 8: "'I remember things I did not live.'" Ch 9: "'His certainty. A problem he had decided belonged to him.'"
- A29. Pieces are usable but cannot be trusted. Ch 13: "The knowledge came easily enough that Elizabeth could see him distrust it."

**The creature (blob)**

- A30. It cleans up loose magical waste. Ch 5: "Things like that clean up loose magical waste. Failed workings. Unsettled messes."
- A31. It goes for what it is after, and signals can split it. Ch 15: "'it can't read the difference cleanly.'"
- A32. It takes unclaimed things, including a person who has become unclaimed. Ch 15: "Its whole dark mass bent after Shade." / "Then there was no Shade standing in the clearing."
- A33. Heat and flame destroy it. Ch 7: "Its shape broke into heavy wet sections that flattened, hissed and thinned."
- A34. It leaves when nothing it came for remains. Ch 15: "found nothing left that it had come for, and withdrew."

**Foam weapons and the dagger**

- A35. Foam weapons are magic and need no skill. Ch 5: "You don't need to know any magic to use one. You hit the thing you mean to hit, and it does what it was made to do." Fragile: "If one cracks, splits or starts heating when it shouldn't, stop using it."
- A36. The baton delivers heat through the ground and kills the creature. Ch 7: "Heat hit the parking lot. Dry, immediate, furnace-door heat." The sword alone does not stop it: "The thing shuddered. It didn't stop."
- A37. The dagger cuts seams, adhesions, clinging effects and magical bindings. Ch 5: "'Adhesions. Clinging effects. Certain magical bindings.'" Ch 7: "Something opened. Not flesh. A seam." Ch 5: "'People?' 'It's designed not to.'"

**Records and the archive**

- A38. Archives keep originals and track use. Ch 4: "'We keep the original… The copy won't be the same thing.'" Ch 4: "'Pages used?' 'One tonight.'"
- A39. Donors control access, and owners and donors can differ. Ch 12: "'That's different from ownership.'" Ch 12 ledger: "DONOR PERMISSION REQUIRED. PATHWELL IS NOT DONOR." Ch 16: "'Both can be true.'"
- A40. A substitute does not replace a lost original. Ch 17: "'They are not Daniel's.' … 'After provenance review. Not as replacement.'"
- A41. Ordinary fire destroys records with no magic. Ch 15: "Ordinary flame moved through old paper… because that was what fire did."

**Space Between and the shop's other rules**

- A42. Books hold other people's feeling, and a visitor must hold on to what is their own. Ch 3: "None of that had been hers, and she held on to that."
- A43. Outside property can be pulled into an open transaction. Ch 3: "He brought outside property into an open transaction, and I dislike paperwork."
- A44. A love note cures most wounds and moves the pain into a feeling. Ch 3: "The wound and the pain went with it, leaving behind a lingering feeling…" Limit: "'scars are outside my wheelhouse.'"
- A45. Bad familiar coffee steadies people. Ch 3: "'Why does it help?' 'Familiar things are useful.'" The mechanism is never given (see B10).

**Camp**

- A46. Camp sets the terms of restitution, not the wrongdoer. Ch 16: "'The person who broke the thing does not get to set the schedule because waiting makes him uncomfortable.'"
- A47. Impossible payment is not accountability. Ch 16: "'Can he replace everything we lost?' 'No.'"
- A48. Personhood is a matter of choice. Ch 16: "'He chose to stay here… He died.' … 'Person is the word I have for that.'"
- A49. The woods are hostile to the careless. Ch 4: "'These trees are very spiteful.'"

## (b) Rules broken, bent, contradicted, or never explained

1. Contradicted: the cookbook's page count. Ch 1 consumed the sugar-cookie page ("the glow… consumed the page"). Ch 4 spent the chicken-and-dumplings page. The archivist asks "'Pages used?' 'One tonight.' 'Before that?' 'One. Also tonight.'" and adds "two small marks in the margin." The Ch 12 card then reads "ONE PAGE USED — CHICKEN & DUMPLINGS." Two pages were used. The book does not say which record is wrong.

2. Contradicted: is the flat marked against Elizabeth or against Pathwell? Ch 1: "your house has now been marked, magically speaking." Ch 4: "'Her door,' Pathwell said." Stansbury: "'Was it?'" Ch 6: "'Something points in an unpleasant direction… Mine.'" Ch 7: "'It went for you.' … 'Not me,' she said. Pathwell looked at her. 'No.'" The flat is never called cleared or still marked in Ch 17 or 18. The book holds both readings and does not choose.

3. Bent: the creature is harmless unless something blocks it. Ch 5, Stansbury: "If you don't stand between them and what they're after, they're unpleasant rather than murderous." Ch 15: Shade walks away from Pathwell, and "The blob changed course, slowly and completely. Its whole dark mass bent after Shade." Shade was not between the creature and its target. Either he was the target or he was taken as unclaimed. Ch 5's rule does not cover the event as written.

4. Contradicted: scars. Ch 3: "'Unfortunately, scars are outside my wheelhouse.'" Ch 16: "'There are ways to reduce what remains after the skin closes.'" Pathwell adds "'Possibly. Not necessarily.'" Stansbury refuses. Ch 17: "Pathwell never mentioned changing it again." The offer is never explained.

5. Broken: Pathwell's promise to answer. Ch 3: "'You finish that coffee there, and I'll answer any questions.'" Then: "'I can answer all your questions, even the ones you'll have next.'" The coffee is finished and the shopkeeper's transaction takes over. No questions are answered. Ch 5, Pathwell: "'It's Saturday.'" Child: "'Friday.'" The same chapter has "'I don't lie.'" The book never resolves whether he lies.

6. Never explained: why the creature was at the door before the cookbook was used. Ch 4: "'It was already at the door before you used the cookbook?' 'Yes.'" Ch 4: "'The first part did not [make sense].'" Ch 5: "'The part that doesn't fit is why it was already at your door.'" Ch 6: "The blob already there before the cookbook." Ch 4: "'Fresh waste. That part made sense.'" The book never says what was summoned, or by whom.

7. Never explained: how Pathwell knows Elizabeth's nickname. Ch 2: "'Lizzy, I can answer all your questions…'" Ch 3: "'Isn't that what your grandmother called you?'" He read her diary (Ch 1), but she never gave him the name. Nothing explains it.

8. Never explained: how Pathwell knew about the cookbook. Ch 1: "'Where's your cookbook?' he said without looking up… 'The old one.' … 'We need it.'" He knows what the book does. Why he was in her flat, and how he knew, are not explained.

9. Never explained: the neighborhood. Ch 2: "a shuttered laundromat she'd never seen before. Beyond that, a park that shouldn't have been there." Her morning coffee shop is gone. The cause and scope are never given. Ch 18 uses a laundromat as a door, which may or may not be the same one.

10. Never explained: why bad coffee steadies people. Ch 3: "'Why does it help?' 'Familiar things are useful.'" Ch 9: "'You looked less tired after you drank it.'" The effect repeats in Ch 3, 9, 13 and 18. The rule never appears.

11. Never explained: the cookbook's weight. Ch 3: "The cookbook felt heavier, which was stupid. Missing a page should have made it lighter." Ch 4: "Somehow that made the cookbook heavier." The book hints that weight tracks something other than paper and says nothing more.

12. Never explained: is "potential" one resource? Ch 3 calls the tome's price "what may be." Ch 10 calls the prune's price "future possibility." Ch 5 calls Stansbury's children's work "potential nobody was going to use." The book treats these as one currency and never says so. It also never says where Pathwell's sold potential went. The shop's "zero" in Ch 18 is about money.

13. Never explained: the client. Ch 10: "'They had a problem. A real one. They came to me because I knew things they didn't.'" "'They did not ask me to prune. I did not ask whether they wanted me to.'" This starts the whole chain, and the client is never named again.

14. Never explained: whether pieces can become people, and what Shade became. Ch 10: "'But you don't know how the failure became a person.' 'No.' 'Does anyone?' 'Not that I've found.'" Ch 8: "'Existing. Pick a word.'" Ch 15: "The draw did not return. Shade did not either." Ch 16 records "SHADE — DIED AT CAMP." The book never says whether a taken piece is dead, absorbed or returned.

15. Bent: the draw opens thresholds when the plot needs it. Ch 10, Pathwell: "'There isn't a reliable threshold from here.'" Ch 13: the pull opens "a seam of pale light… between two trees," with no route mark, only Shade's hand pointing. Ch 11 says a threshold needs a route mark.

16. Never explained: the dagger and a bound person. Ch 5: "'It's designed not to.'" Ch 14: "Whatever held Shade's arm out now might be a binding, and might be him. She didn't know what it would cut." "Designed not to" is not "cannot." No scene tests it on a person who is part of the binding.

17. Bent (minor): the sword's rule. Ch 5, Stansbury: "it does what it was made to do." Ch 7: "The sword sank halfway into blackness. The thing shuddered. It didn't stop." The sentence in Ch 5 overpromises.

18. Never explained: why Pathwell could not stop the Ch 11 working. Ch 11, Stansbury: "'Cancel it.'" Pathwell: "'I can contain this.'" The book never says whether that working was stoppable. Ch 14 gives the rule that a working can be stopped before release, and the gap is not closed.

What I'd cut first. Item 2, the flat-mark thread. It never pays off, and it competes with Pathwell's "Mine" as a second and weaker reveal. The "copy keeps the words, not the thing" rule is restated in Ch 4, 10, 11, 17 and 18. One statement in Ch 4 is enough. The card-game scenes in Ch 13 and 17 run long for what they carry. Ch 17's restitution list is accurate but could be two lines.

What I'd keep. The Ch 3 cost structure (potential as currency, a visible tree, a body that shakes). The Ch 14 frame failing on intent, with the release spilling into the room. Ch 16's Camp terms are the best rules writing in the book, because they say who sets the price.

## (c) The clever move

1. Ch 4: Elizabeth keeps control of the book. The move is to donate the page use only, under "reserve / ask before use," and keep ownership. The archive lets donors restrict use (Ch 12: "'Ask me before anyone reads it.' … 'That's different from ownership.'"). The healer needed only one page (Ch 4: "'We only need the page.'"). Why nobody did it: Elizabeth gives a reason, and it is about herself. "'If I keep holding the rest, I'm going to spend the next ten minutes finding a reason this page is the wrong one.'" She learns the donor tool a chapter later, with the diary.

2. Ch 5 to Ch 13: cut the draw with the dagger. Stansbury's list includes "Clinging effects. Certain magical bindings" (Ch 5). Pathwell's pull is "A thread caught somewhere behind the ribs" (Ch 6). Shade's pull keeps his arm open toward Pathwell (Ch 9, 12, 13). If the draw is a binding, a cut would end it without touching flesh, which is what the dagger is designed for. Ch 8 is the best moment: Shade is in the car, the dagger is in Elizabeth's waistband, and his hand is fighting him. Why nobody did it: the book does not say. Nobody identifies the draw as a binding on the page. Elizabeth carries the dagger through Ch 8, 9 and 13 and never asks.

3. Ch 7 and Ch 15: decoy the creature. The creature takes "loose magical waste. Failed workings" (Ch 5). Pathwell could plant a failed working, or a torn letter, far from people and let it go there. It could work: Ch 15 has Shade say the creature goes for what overlaps, and Pathwell says "'Because our signatures overlap.'" Why nobody did it in time: Pathwell had no books in Ch 7 ("'You mean to tell me you brought zero books with you!?' … 'Yes! It slipped my mind!'"). The decoy idea only appears in Ch 15, too late: "'Fresh pruning spill by the frame. It should be what it's here for.' … 'Should.'" The book gives the no-books reason and does not say why the idea never came up earlier.

4. Ch 10: search with a copy of the letter. Stansbury keeps copies, and "The copy keeps the words." The move is to use the copy and keep the original. Why nobody did it: the book says so directly. "'The copy keeps the words.' 'Not the thing.'" The copy does not carry the link. Here the rule closes the door, not the character.

5. Ch 14: cut the cord before payment. The frame is "a narrow line between two fixed points." The cord is a binding, and the dagger cuts bindings. If Elizabeth had cut the cord before Pathwell drove the third pin, there would be no return line for payment to travel. Why nobody did it: the book gives the reason. "She didn't know what it would cut, or whose it was to cut. She left it where it was."

6. Ch 14: lift the hand. Mama Baga gives the rule ("'Lift your hand… Nothing has released yet'"). This is the cheapest clever move in the book: stop before release and lose nothing. Why nobody did it: Pathwell gives a reason. "'I can't keep leaving damage behind me.'" He refuses Elizabeth's "Let it fail," because for him the working is the repair.

7. Ch 11: scope the quarantine. The letter says "Care that meant stay away." Pathwell could have used it to separate only Shade's pieces from the room, not to make "distance physical" across the gallery. Why nobody did it: the book gives his certainty, not a reason it would fail. "'I can contain this.'" Elizabeth hears "Quickest. Cleanest. I can handle it." The same overreach as Ch 14: the intent is too wide, and the working takes the room.

## (d) Costs

**Real: paid, with consequences later.**

- Pathwell's potential. Ch 3: "'Some of it.'" Ch 5: "'You pruned.'" Ch 7: "Both of them now" (both hands shake). Ch 14: "The gauze at his temple darkened. His right arm shook hard enough that his hand blurred." Ch 16: the healer lists "Pruning shock."
- Elizabeth's shoulder. Ch 11: the case "caught her left shoulder." Ch 12: reset by hand. Ch 14: "Her shoulder screamed inside the sling." Ch 15: she tears the sling off, and "Her left arm dropped three inches." Ch 17: "a shoulder that hurt when rain moved in."
- The cookbook and the diary. Ch 4: she gives the whole book, and it goes to the archive. Ch 12: she gives the diary. Ch 15: both burn. Ch 16: "The diary was gone. The cookbook was gone." The burning is not her choice, but the giving was: "'She got Milo through the door.' 'That was the choice.' 'Everything else burned afterward.'"
- Shade. Ch 15: "Then there was no Shade standing in the clearing." Ch 16: "SHADE — DIED AT CAMP." He chose to walk away from Pathwell ("'No,' he said").
- Stansbury's hand. Ch 15: "The skin across the back of his right hand was already red and shining." Ch 16: "'You will scar.'" Ch 17: "The scar grew clearer." He refuses to have it reduced: "'Let it be.'" (Ch 16)
- The Daniel Vale letters. Ch 11: "The original was destroyed before anything magical happened to it." Ch 17: "The letters stayed missing." The museum refuses substitutes: "'Not as replacement.'"
- Elizabeth's statement. Ch 17: "She wrote that she told him not to use the letter. She wrote that he used it anyway." The record stays honest.

**Waved away: the book lets it go.**

- Elizabeth's Friday meeting and her job. Ch 5: "her nine o'clock meeting had come and gone while she sat in a field." Ch 17 mentions only "forty-seven messages and only six that mattered." No consequence follows.
- The Cadillac. Ch 8: "'You broke my car.'" "'You cannot.'" Ch 10 switches to the work van. The Cadillac is never mentioned again.
- The landlord and the door. Ch 4: "the missing front door." Ch 18: "The landlord had replaced it with something brown, heavy, and aggressively ordinary." Nobody asks about the mark or the cost.
- The young woman from the wagon. Ch 4 saves her with the page. Ch 12: "The young woman from the wagon accident was gone from the cot." Her later life is not covered.
- Milo's boot. Ch 16: "'My boot burned too.'" Ch 17: a mismatched replacement appears. It was a real loss, and it is solved in a line.
- The children's energy. Ch 5, Stansbury: "'I don't steal anything. The kids get their work back.'" Elizabeth's objection, "Selling off potential nobody was going to use," gets one answer ("It would never be used otherwise") and is left there.
- Pathwell's ribs and temple. Ch 18: "His face had mostly healed."
- The draw. Ch 15: "The draw did not return." The pull that cost Pathwell for years, and that drove Shade toward the creature, ends with Shade's death. The book shows no further cost to Pathwell, so the cost passes to Shade.

**Mixed.** Pathwell's account with the shop. Ch 3 closes it: "Your account settled now." Ch 18: "Your account remains at zero." The money is clear. The potential he sold is still gone, and the book does not count it.
