# Report on Shade (Chapters 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15)

**What I'd cut, before any praise:**
1. Ch. 14: Shade's explanation speech ("You are going to use the existing connection as the return line. You're going to put the unresolved part back against its source..."). His line just before, "You're building a rejoining frame," already did the job. This is where he turns into an exposition engine.
2. Ch. 14: "Nothing changing is acceptable to me." I read it three times and can't parse it. It's the one line of his that doesn't sound like him.
3. Ch. 12: the narrator explaining him ("Whatever pieces of old Pathwell knowledge he carried, he kept them out of a treatment nobody had asked him to direct"). Show it or skip it.
4. Ch. 11: the stumble where the pull knocks him into Elizabeth so the scene can go wrong. He causes nothing; the plot uses his hand as a lever.
5. One of the two "wise child sees through everyone" gags (turnip boy in Ch. 12, Milo's card tricks in Ch. 13).

## (a) What Shade wants
- **Start (Ch. 8):** to be rid of the pull. "Something keeps telling me which way you are... I can ignore it for a while. Then it gets worse. So I drove." Also coffee: "No. I'm getting coffee."
- **Ch. 9 (the real want):** "For him to stop deciding what I am before I get to." He admits Elizabeth is "Partly" leverage ("Because he'll listen to you longer than he'll listen to me").
- **Ch. 11:** his goal swaps to Camp the moment Elizabeth names it: "Something like recognition passed over Shade's face. 'Yes,' he said. 'Camp.'" No reason is given.
- **Ch. 13:** first want stated outright. "You want to leave?" / "No." / "I want to stay here."
- **End (Ch. 15):** I don't know. He says "I'd like to find out which problem it thinks is biggest" and "I told Pathwell to leave it unresolved. I thought I'd be the one carrying it." My guess is he doesn't want to leave the cost of his refusal to others. The book doesn't say.

## (b) How he changes
He goes from reacting to a pull and a pile of borrowed memories to having likes and a place he wants to be, then gives it up. The moments:
- **Ch. 9:** "We used to—" corrected to "He used to." Then "Observation. Not memory." He polices the line between his mind and what he was handed.
- **Ch. 12:** the boy asks "Are you Pathwell?" / "No." / "Good." / "I don't know what that means." I wanted him to mind it more.
- **Ch. 13 (the turn):** four Camp people ask what he wants instead of telling him what he is. "People keep doing that." / "Asking." / "Disorienting." He starts having preferences (green jacket, Hearts, "Pockets," then "I don't know yet.").
- **Ch. 13:** the coffee. "That's terrible." / "I know." Elizabeth stops because Pathwell said exactly that. "Then I don't remember it." Later, "He was right once."
- **Ch. 14:** "Do I remain myself?" / "Can." / "Not will." / "Then no."
- **Ch. 15:** "Will you stay?" / "No."

I can't follow his feeling toward Pathwell step by step. It starts wary and ends with a long look ("He just looked").

## (c) His voice
Short declaratives, literal questions ("What prune?"), pronoun and tense self-corrections, one-word rulings ("Fair." "Accurate."), refusal to elaborate ("First name?" / "Enough name."), flat understatement ("Would you prefer applause?" "More driving. Worse coffee."). Elizabeth names the contrast in Ch. 8: Pathwell "filled silence because silence looked undefended. Shade seemed willing to let it suffer."

He mostly sounds like himself and clearly unlike Pathwell. Two caveats:
- **Ensemble drift (Ch. 12-13):** Camp characters share one dry-comeback register (Stansbury's "History has casualties," the healer's volleys, Mama Baga, Milo's "Pathwell stupid?"). Shade's "Diversifying." and "Corruption" join it. Funny, but not the diner Shade.
- **Ch. 14:** the technical vocabulary ("rejoining frame," "Two anchors. One return.") makes him sound like the man he's refusing to be. That's partly intentional ("There he is"), but the speech runs long.

## (d) Best and worst moments
**Best**
1. Ch. 13: "People keep doing that." / "Asking." / "Disorienting." Four scenes of setup, and it pays.
2. Ch. 14: "Don't make him the reason... If this fails because Stansbury stood there, he'll spend the rest of his life explaining that part instead of the choice." The man with least reason to care about Stansbury's pride sees what it would cost. A person, not a device.
3. Ch. 14: "For who?" (after "I am trying to make this right.")
4. Ch. 9: "Observation. Not memory." and "You were never the point... Don't make that sentence larger than it is."
5. Ch. 13: "That's terrible." / "I know."
6. Ch. 15: Milo: "You still owe me a hand." Shade: "I was winning." / "You were not." / "Corrupt institution." Preceded by the narrator's "Shade had never been particularly interested in deserving scenes."
7. Ch. 15: "Will you stay?" / "No." Pathwell takes it without arguing, for the first time.
8. Ch. 11: stepping on the squeaky floorboard on purpose.

**Worst**
1. The Ch. 14 speech. 2. "Nothing changing is acceptable to me." 3. The Ch. 11 grab. 4. Ch. 12, where he is by the door with no want and the chapter isn't about him ("This is humiliating" and the spoon are fine, but he's close to a prop). 5. The logic of the Ch. 15 sacrifice (see f).

## (e) Out of character
- **Responsibility (Ch. 14 vs. 15):** Ch. 14: "Then whatever happens next is yours." Ch. 15: "I told Pathwell to leave it unresolved. I thought I'd be the one carrying it," as if the fire were partly his. I can believe he feels it, but the book never says why.
- **Passivity (Ch. 14):** he stays in reach of the pins saying "I am not fighting you." I wondered why he didn't step out of range. Ch. 15 answers it: he walks twenty feet with the arm dragging. So it was a choice, but I waited a chapter to learn that.
- **Ch. 11:** drops his own goal for Camp on Elizabeth's word.
- **Ch. 13 to 15:** "I want to stay here" to walking into the blob. Biggest shift, and the only one that reads plot-driven.
- **Smaller:** "Diversifying" (Ch. 12) and "Corruption" / "No one suspects him" (Ch. 13) are in the Camp register. A drift, not a break.

## (f) Does the ending follow?
Partly.
- **What follows:** he refused merger, found people who asked what he wanted, and looked at Milo and Elizabeth's arm before leaving. "I am the person living with it" (Ch. 14) becomes "I'd like to find out which problem it thinks is biggest" (Ch. 15); both insist he is the one who decides. The Milo callback earns the walk.
- **What doesn't:** I can't tell what his death accomplished. His theory is the blob "can't read the difference cleanly" and the spill is new while what it's been following "isn't." But Elizabeth says "It could just take the fresh spill." and he answers "Yes." After it takes him, it goes back, eats the spill anyway and leaves. So it wanted both, and I don't see what he changed. If the point was keeping the blob out of Camp, show me.
- **Cleanup by him:** the draw ends, the spill is removed, the blobs leave. That is what Pathwell's rejoining was meant to do and Shade refused ("Can... Not will."), so he gets a worse version of the outcome he wouldn't accept, and the plot's problem is solved by him going away. I'm wary of that pattern, though the writing keeps me from being angry.
- **Ch. 13 to 15:** one afternoon of wanting something for himself, then he gives it up. Maybe that's the point; maybe a missed beat.
- Last line, "Shade did not either," is good. I don't know if he returns.

## (g) What I had to guess
- What the prune is, what Pathwell "chose," and what "marked" meant ("Ask him what he chose," Ch. 9). I never got the answer.
- What "Lizzy" vs. "Elizabeth" costs Pathwell (Ch. 14); I read it as a boundary she's set.
- How Camp, the Space Between, the museum and the blobs fit together; I took the rules (spill, payment, pruning, "signatures overlap") on the characters' word.
- Stansbury's history with Pathwell and with Shade ("Fragments of him. Before me," Ch. 9).
- Whether the draw is magic, a flaw in the working, or deliberate. I read it as a consequence, not a leash.
- Whether Shade is "a person" in the book's world or a leftover. Everyone, including him, treats him as one, and I did too. If other chapters say otherwise, my read of the ending is off.
- Whether he returns after Ch. 15.
- How much of the dry register is his and how much is the narrator's house style. I'd need Elizabeth and Pathwell without him to say.
