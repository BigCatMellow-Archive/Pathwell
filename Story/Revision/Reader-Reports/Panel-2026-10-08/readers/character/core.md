# Core questions (reader of Shade's chapters only: 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15)

## 1. What is this book about?
It's about people who keep deciding, on someone else's behalf, what is best for them, and what that costs the people on the receiving end. Pathwell's habit of "I can contain this" and "quickest, cleanest" makes messes (a wrecked museum, a burning archive), and the book keeps asking what it means to own a choice, to be asked instead of told, and to keep things (a diary, letters, a cookbook, a self) that matter to someone even if no museum wants them. The magic is the vehicle; the subject is care versus control. That is all I can say from seven chapters.

## 2. The moment that worked best, and why
Ch. 13, the coffee. Elizabeth sips Shade's awful brew and says "That's terrible." He says "I know." She stops, because Pathwell said exactly that to her once. He says "Then I don't remember it," and later "He was right once." In four lines it shows the whole problem of him: he is not Pathwell, he can't stop sounding like him, and he knows both things. Nothing is explained and nothing needs to be. Close second: "People keep doing that." / "Asking." / "Disorienting." in the same chapter.

## 3. The moment that worked least, and why
Ch. 14, Shade's speech laying out the working ("You are going to use the existing connection as the return line..."). He had already said "You're building a rejoining frame," and the reader had it. The speech is there to brief me, and it makes the quietest man in the book talk like a lecturer. Runner-up: the Ch. 11 fall that makes his hand land on Elizabeth's arm, which is a plot lever, not a choice.

## 4. Where did you get confused or stop believing it?
- Ch. 15: I stopped believing his sacrifice was needed. Elizabeth says the blob "could just take the fresh spill," he says "Yes," and after it takes him it eats the spill anyway and leaves. I can't tell what he changed.
- Ch. 14: "Nothing changing is acceptable to me." I could not parse it.
- Ch. 11: he adopts Camp as his goal the second Elizabeth says it, after two chapters of wanting something else.
- Ch. 14 to 15: he says whatever happens is Pathwell's to own, then says he thought he'd be the one carrying it.
- Throughout: I never learned what the prune is or what Pathwell "chose," and Shade keeps pointing at it. I held that as a gap, not a flaw, since I didn't read those chapters.

## 5. What did you want from the book that you didn't get?
- One scene of Shade being angry at Pathwell for what Pathwell is. He's wary and dry throughout; I never see the thing under it.
- One line from him about Elizabeth's shoulder after the museum (Ch. 11-12). He looks at her first, then goes quiet.
- More days. He gets one afternoon of wanting something for himself (Ch. 13) before it's taken. I wanted him to have to live with the wanting for a while.
- A reason his death mattered beyond ending the pull.

## 6. One thing you'd tell the author
You've made a character who is good at being himself in small objects, a green jacket with too many pockets, a spoon in the stew, a queen of spades. Trust that, cut the explaining speech in Ch. 14, and make his ending cost the plot something it didn't already want to lose.
