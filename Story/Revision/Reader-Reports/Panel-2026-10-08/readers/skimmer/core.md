# Core questions, reader: skimmer

**1. What is this book about?**
As far as I can tell, it's about Elizabeth, whose ordinary life gets pulled into a hidden world of shops, camps and magic after a party, and about Pathwell, a man who keeps trying to settle other people's problems by paying for them with his own future. Underneath that is a question about who gets to decide what happens to other people's things, like a grandmother's cookbook or a diary, and whether someone who made a serious mistake can be trusted again. I'm not sure the book knows which of those it cares about most, and I didn't get far enough to settle it for myself.

**2. The moment that worked best for me.**
Chapter 15, the fire. Elizabeth runs into the burning archive, passes her own diary on the floor and Nana's cookbook on the shelf, and pulls Milo out with a broken strut. The passage then drops the explaining and just says, "No spell. No sacrifice. No last message. Just fire." That was the first time I stopped reading the magic as a puzzle and just felt the scene. It's the one I'd show someone on a train. Chapter 18 comes close, with the cookbook margins ("RUTH LIED ABOUT THE LARD." / "SHE DID NOT. YOU USED THE WRONG CUP.") doing the same job: four generations of family notes made me laugh and then made me feel how much a kitchen can hold.

**3. The moment that worked least for me.**
Chapter 14, the Mama Baga "Stop" section, roughly lines 379 to 463. It's about eighty lines of one objection, repeated, while Pathwell drives the pin in. I lost track of who was arguing with whom and kept waiting for the scene to move. It's where I came closest to quitting, and I only kept going because I wanted the fire that followed. The "What pull?" / "Which prune?" loop in Chapter 7 is a close second.

**4. Where I got confused or stopped believing it.**
- The magic. The prune, the working, the draw, "cuts separation," the pins and cord, the mirror, the Space Between. I never got a sentence I could hold on a train that said what any of them do, so by Chapter 15 I was taking "the draw" on trust.
- Shade's death. In Chapter 15 the text says "there was no Shade standing in the clearing" and never says what happened to him. I only believed he was dead because Mama Baga says so in Chapter 16, and I didn't feel it in the scene.
- Names. The nameless archivist, Stansbury versus the archivist, the sock kid and the turnip boy, and the lists of Vales and Bells in Chapter 17. I couldn't hold who was who between sittings, and nothing in the text helped me hold on to them.
- The narrator explaining the scenes after they happen. In Chapter 7 ("A day ago Elizabeth might have missed it...") and Chapter 17 ("Adventure had been difficult to separate from being dragged somewhere..."), I'd already seen what was going on, and being told lowered my trust in the rest.

**5. What I wanted from the book that I didn't get.**
A plain, short account of what the magic is and what it costs, stated once and then used consistently. I wanted to know what the Space Between is. I wanted the mirror and Celia and the pears to come back in a way I could follow. And I wanted the scenes to trust me, so I didn't have to be told what a look or a silence meant.

**6. One thing I'd tell the author.**
Trust the scenes you've already written. Chapter 18's cookbook page works because it just shows the margins and lets me find the joke and the grief myself. Several other places (Chapter 7, Chapter 11, Chapter 17) stop to explain what I just saw, and the explanations are weaker than the scenes.
