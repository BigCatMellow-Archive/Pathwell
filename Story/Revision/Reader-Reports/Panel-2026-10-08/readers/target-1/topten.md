# Ten things I'd most want to tell the author (1 = most important)

1. Stop repeating a beat once it has landed: the archivist's "Store? / Loan?" scene (Ch 4, again in Ch 12), the "he wants to improve it, he's told no, he complies" joke (Ch 17), and "That was the choice" (Ch 4, Ch 15).
2. Cut the restitution tribunal and the lines that explain the moral (Ch 16: "Defined by whom?", "Person is the word I have for that", "Both things were true"). The ledger entry "LIKED POCKETS" already did it.
3. Tell me what the original problem was that Pathwell pruned for (Ch 10: "They had a problem. A real one."), or at least admit it plainly. The whole plot rests on it.
4. Close the blob logic and the question "Her door... Was it?" (Ch 1, 5, 7, 15). Why was the first one already at her door, and why does it behave differently each time?
5. Delete "It was the crash that woke her." in the middle of the wreck (Ch 8). It reads as a stray paste.
6. Give Pathwell a real change between his two bad decisions (Ch 11, Ch 14). The second is the same mistake, not a worse one, and I could write his lines.
7. Let each character have a voice of their own; "Important distinction," "Occupationally," "Disorienting," "Cruel," "Perfect" and "Not X. Y." fragments run through everyone (Ch 7, 13, 17, 18).
8. Make Elizabeth's decision to get into Shade's car and to walk back alone (Ch 8, 9, 10) cost her something, or give her a stronger reason than "he has pieces."
9. Trim the long set-ups that tell me what I'll understand in a line: the portal and school halls (Ch 5), the three memory attempts (Ch 10), the extra Hearts hands (Ch 13), the postcard list (Ch 18).
10. Bring the building and her ordinary life back into the story, especially her coworkers, her landlord and the nine o'clock meeting (Ch 1, 2, 17), so the magic and the everyday touch more than at the door.
