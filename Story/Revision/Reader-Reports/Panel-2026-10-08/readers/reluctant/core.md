# Core questions

**1. What is this book about, in three or four sentences, without listing the plot?**

It is about a woman who has just moved to a new city, is grieving a grandmother whose cookbook she has been unable to let go of, and finds that her new life has a hidden side full of folk magic. The magic is mostly a way of making people pay for shortcuts, and the man who guides her has paid for one of his own with part of his future. The book is really about what it costs to decide, for other people, that a problem is yours to solve, and whether a person who made that choice can own it.

**2. The moment that worked best for you, and why.**

Chapter 4, when Elizabeth gives the whole cookbook to the healer, not just the page she needs. "That was the choice. Everything afterward was consequence." Until then the magic had been happening to her. Here she makes a decision that costs her something she wants back, and I stopped reading for the lore and started reading for her. It was also the first time the grief in the cookbook had weight that the magic could not explain away.

**3. The moment that worked least, and why.**

Chapter 3, the Space Between's shopkeeper and his prices: "We could settle today, past bills and all, for… a Rai Stone." Rai Stones, Lydian coins, a blood-god cat, and a binding in Latin came in one chapter with no definitions. The sacrifice that follows is well written, but I could not weigh its cost against a currency I had never been shown, so the scene had no stakes for me. This is the line where I would have put the book down.

**4. Where did you get confused or stopped believing it?**

I was confused by the magic almost everywhere before Chapter 4: what the working is, what the "draw" and "pull" are, what "charged" means, what "pruning" does until Chapter 10. The confusion was not a problem in itself. The problem was that the book often kept the answer back long after the character had asked the question, most visibly "Which prune?" in Chapters 7 and 8, which I had to wait until Chapter 10 to hear. I stopped believing the magic was a system and started believing it was a set of excuses people used for their choices, which the book may have intended, but then it needed to say so earlier. Chapter 14's climax is where I stopped being able to follow the physical action, because the jargon ("released possibility," "ownerless threads," "fresh spillage") piled up faster than I could picture it.

**5. What did you want from the book that you didn't get?**

I wanted a plain account of the magic's rules, one or two of them stated clearly, so I could judge what the characters chose. I wanted Nana's death in its own scene, not as a line in a list of diary contents in Chapter 12. I wanted to know what Shade is, not only that he is "pieces" of Pathwell and has inherited memories. I wanted the Space Between described as a place I could picture. I got almost none of that. I got the emotional argument instead, and it was enough to keep reading, but I wanted both.

**6. One thing you'd tell the author.**

Put the human stakes and one clear rule of the magic in the first fifty pages, and cut the lore until it has earned its place. Elizabeth's grief and her cookbook are the strongest things in the book, and they only show up as a real thing in Chapter 4. Start there, and the currency and the glossary can wait.
