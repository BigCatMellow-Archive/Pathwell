## Neutral questions for the author
- Who says "Let's roll, big cat": Pathwell to Stansbury, or Stansbury to Pathwell?
- "Leo" and "grande gatto" never come back in Ch11-18. Do you want one of them to return once, at a serious moment (the museum, Ch15), so the joke has a second life?
- Is "before sunrise" (L7) a joke about her night or a promise that dawn comes soon? What time of night should the museum be?
- When Pathwell says "A letter that deserved better," should Elizabeth react to someone's unsent letter being destroyed to find her, or is the silence the point?
- "Mama Baga doesn't belong to either of them" (L608): is that Elizabeth's honest view of a woman who calls him by a mother's name, or should the reader hear her being wrong?
- The brothers leave the box of swords in the hall. Is that deliberate (they are heading to a threshold, not a fight), or should the van carry them?

## Opinions (mine)
- The Ask is the chapter. Take only L568-578 out of it and leave the rest. "That isn't an answer." / "It's the answer I use when I don't want to say the other one." is the best exchange the book has had.
- The tracking scene is the second best: it has stakes (the letter), rules (shared memory, passive), cost ("mine to approve") and a joke ("I can feel the shoe polish.") and none of them gets in the other's way. The only repair is the clock, and it is one clause.
- I'd keep "Bank it." and the two slaps. They give Stansbury the book's first physical comedy and show how easy the brothers are with each other before the scene turns on Pathwell's confession.
