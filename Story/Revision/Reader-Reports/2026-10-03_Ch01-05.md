## Verdict in one paragraph

The five chapters hold. I never lost belief in the premise, and I wanted to keep reading at every chapter end. There are no true plot holes. There are three small holes in the page itself (a staging confusion in Ch1, one analogy in Ch5 that I could not parse, and two unpaid beats: the shadow in her window and "thief"), a cluster of snags, and a style cluster in Ch4. Most of what I wondered is deliberate or already tracked in the ledger; where a row is missing or stale, I say so.

## 1. What worked (keep)

- **Ch3 handcart.** "You've watched me carry things out of here by hand." / "Yes." / "For decades." Then her one laugh and "Don't make anything of that." The best joke in the five chapters, and it works because it pays off a real debt (he has just spent a piece of himself).
- **Ch3 prune.** "Pathwell screamed in a million voices, voices lost to time, burning out to become memories never made." The cost is felt, and the next beat is a joke ("Will that be paper or plastic?" / "Surprise me.") that doesn't cancel it. "If I knew that, I'd make worse decisions with more confidence."
- **Ch4 the wagon.** The Camp's rule is shown, not told: "Nobody looked at Elizabeth. Somehow that made the cookbook heavier." / "Family?" / "Handwritten?" / the healer does not ask. Then "MORE IF THEY'RE SICK. THEY WON'T EAT ENOUGH." and "She believed soup was an argument you could win." Her reason for giving the whole book ("If I keep holding the rest, I'm going to spend the next ten minutes finding a reason this page is the wrong one.") is a person thinking like a person.
- **Ch4 absence as characterisation.** "Elizabeth waited for Pathwell to appear in the doorway and turn the whole thing into a problem he had already solved. He didn't." Then the wheel: "This wheel is new." / "Newer. Relatively."
- **Ch5 "I'm the missus."** The first time Elizabeth leads a scene, and it is funny because it is real (Ken's lanyard, her elbow). "Mrs. Pathwell. And this is my husband, Mr. Pathwell."
- **Ch1 and Ch2 small things:** "The cake, somehow, was fine."; "Ready?" / "No." / "Perfect."; the dry-cleaning ticket going into the gutter and what it releases ("Three weeks of bare walls and boxes she couldn't bring herself to open."); and the running joke that Pathwell is wrong about the day ("It's Thursday." / Ch5 "Friday.").

## 3. Stoppers

Every place the reader was pulled out, one line each. None broke belief; each cost a reread.

1. **Ch1 L109-L165, the doors.** I had to count door actions to place her in the bathroom doorway through the whole conversation.
2. **Ch4 L184, the cot she "hadn't noticed".** A patient and a healer sit in the wagon through the tea and the dressing; the curtain at L134 is the only plant.
3. **Ch5 L264, "Spending something nobody was going to use."** I could not tell what she meant, and it comes just after the best explanation of Stansbury's work.

Near-stoppers (snags I read through): Ch2 L99 (a new fact, "her books", that she does not react to; answered at L163); Ch3 L53-59 (he leaves her in the stacks); Ch4 L19-21 (the cart gets ahead); Ch5 L3/L91 (the field and the meeting); Ch5 L145 (Stansbury's timing).

## 6. Opinions (marked as opinions, last)

- *Opinion.* Ch4 is the strongest chapter of the five. The wagon-to-archive stretch is where the book earns its warmth. I would not touch Elizabeth's "ten minutes finding a reason" line even though the Craft rules mark it as a flagged category; it is the one place a character names her own motive and it reads as a person talking.
- *Opinion.* The Ch4 "Not X. / Y." cluster is the one place I felt the prose arranging itself. "Not burned. Spent." is right. "Not hidden. Not displayed. Available." and "Not gratitude. Not consolation. Just contact." are the same move twice within about 120 lines. One could go.
- *Opinion.* The Ch5 sword-and-dagger rules (L244-252) are the only place I skimmed. They plant a dagger that the ledger already says is under-paid later; the plant is good, the length is slightly more than the bar scene needs.
- *Opinion.* Pathwell walking off from Elizabeth is a pattern (stacks in Ch3, forest path in Ch4, the dumpling in Ch2). It is fine as character, but the three feel the same; the Ch4 one is the weakest because it is the one where she "picked a side" with no consequence beyond a fall.
- *Opinion.* Ch5 turns from comedy into set-up in its last third (the town paragraph, the bar). The reader's attention goes forward, not into the car. That is fine for a chapter that hands off to a bar fight, but the Ch5 ending is the least felt of the five.
