# Pathwell: voice review against the author's aim

**Reviewer:** independent helper, read-only. **Lens:** Aim (his voice guide, the reading list, prose voice, the narrator register, forbidden_patterns, Writing-Against-Sameness, AI-Tells, his drafts of Ch1–7, the quote bank skimmed). Then all 18 chapters, in order. This is evidence, not rulings. Where I give an opinion, I label it.

**Headline.** The book sounds most like him wherever an ordinary object or a sincere child carries the scene. It drifts in two ways. First, the narration and the characters keep saying aloud what the scene has just shown, mostly in Ch8–9 and Ch14–18. Second, from Ch6 onward the cast shares one clipped, corrective deadpan. Almost every fix is a cut.

## 1. Where the book is most his (protect)

1. **Ch4:256–268.** "Chicken and dumplings. Of course." / `MORE IF THEY'RE SICK. THEY WON'T EAT ENOUGH.` / "She believed soup was an argument you could win." The voice guide's own recipe: "Objects and habits should carry emotional history", and the grief arrives through a grease mark. The joke belongs to Elizabeth and is about Nana.
2. **Ch12:353–365.** `FIND NANA'S SPOON` / "The last item was checked twice." / "Elizabeth smiled." The diary decision rests on a to-do list, with no explanation. This is the "stained recipe card" principle working exactly.
3. **Ch16:686–690.** "I considered asking him to leave on that basis alone." / "Elizabeth laughed before she could stop herself, and the laugh broke in the middle. Then she was crying." The guide's test, done literally: "laugh shortly before or after something emotionally serious without making either moment feel fake."
4. **Ch16:300–312 and 350.** "My boot burned too." / "It was a good boot." / "I could give it to somebody with one foot." … "He still owes me a hand." The funniest moment comes "because a character is completely sincere about something ridiculous", and the grief follows two lines later.
5. **Ch16:376–392.** `LIKED POCKETS.` / "That is not an archive category." / "Archive burned. Categories are temporarily flexible." A funeral done as paperwork, which is the guide's "profound… may still end with paperwork".
6. **Ch11:221–227.** `SAM HOME TODAY. THIN AS A FENCE RAIL. ATE THREE EGGS.` / "The war ends and he gets three eggs." The mundane holds the enormous.
7. **Ch11:15–19 and Ch10:68–90** (his own lines, per W17 and P-pass notes). "just because we're trying to find a bad guy doesn't mean we need to be one." The slaps / "Bank it." / "Let's roll, big cat." These are the guide's *Imperfection*: "Some jokes may be stupid." They're rough, sincere and unpolished, and the most him of anything in the middle.
8. **Ch18:263–283 and 410–416.** `RUTH LIED ABOUT THE LARD.` / `SHE DID NOT. YOU USED THE WRONG CUP.` … `ALSO GOOD WHEN YOU HAVE BEEN FIRED.` / `NOT THAT GOOD.`, and the geese: "They have learned commerce." / "They steal buttons." Strange without being whimsical, and grounded in family argument.

Honourable mention: Ch15:106, "His sock had finally stayed up. Elizabeth hated that detail immediately." It's the wrong-detail response inside the danger, done right.

## 3. Character voices

- **Pathwell.** He's distinct, and like the author's drafts, through Ch5: "Home is such a permanent term. I prefer… layover." (Ch4:119, his line). From Ch10 he slips into the house's clipped formal register ("I am developing standards." Ch10:265; "Good to preserve novelty." Ch14:235). The author's Pathwell is looser and chattier, and runs on ("Runemasters!").
- **Elizabeth.** Hers in Ch2, Ch5 and Ch12: "It was a lucky guess," she snapped (Ch5:79), and "You people are committed to making trees unpleasant." (Ch12:174). In the back half she becomes the book's articulate auditor. The author's Elizabeth snaps and rants ("Upset!? Might be Upset!?"); she doesn't diagnose.
- **Stansbury.** Theatrical and appetitive in Ch5 and Ch11:19, as in his drafts. Elsewhere he's mostly a corrector ("Do not anthropomorphize the evidence." Ch10:343). I agree with EXP-0021's S1a and S2.
- **Shade.** The most distinct voice in the book: literal, sparing, and consistent with the register guide's clinical Shade ("Pockets." Ch13:357). His only drift is the aphorism: "I'm tired of carrying pieces of an answer that belong to a question I never asked." (Ch9:314). (The author's draft Shade was a different, warmer man. Later rulings replaced him, so I'm not raising it.)
- **Mama Baga.** In Ch4 she's "dry in speech and warm in action" (R33): "And the occasional no-good thief." By Ch16 she lectures in aphorisms: "Then stop naming impossible payment as if saying a large enough number is accountability." (Ch16:508), and 576. Opinion: those two are the place to look.
- **The archivist.** Consistent as the sanctioned dry wit (R33): "Everything is new when you are twelve." / "I'm eleven." / "That explains the filing." (Ch4:422–426). The risk is that the others copy him.
- **The healer.** Plain and right in Ch4: "Then she wakes up sore and hungry." Deadpan in Ch12 and Ch16, which is already EXP-0021. I agree with its leans (H2a, H3, H4, H5b, H6–H9).
- **Milo.** Distinct and consistent: a child's sincerity, never adult deadpan. "That counts as dying, right?" (Ch16:354).

## 5. Rhythm

The voice guide asks for "contrast", and in reflection to "allow thoughts to stretch". I measured narration only (my script, so treat it as a pointer):

| Manuscript Ch12–18 | 8.5–9.4 | 2–4% | **26–35%** (Ch18 35, Ch13 33, Ch16 33, Ch15 32) |

- **All fragments where it should stretch:** Ch16's grief night (49–61, "Alive." / "The diary was gone." / …; 694–708, the "For…" list), Ch17:400's run of "still"s, and Ch18:185–209, the wandering. These are reflection scenes, and the guide says they should carry "accumulation, uncertainty and memory". The few long sentences the book has are its best (Ch2:93, his; Ch10:193, the unsent letter; Ch18:372). Opinion: his drafts' run-ons shouldn't come back, but the stretch should, in two or three places he writes.
- **All polish:** Ch6 and Ch14 are almost entirely quick exchanges, so comedy timing has nothing to contrast with. Ch7:291's five-part "Important enough…" speech is built rather than snapped (reviser lean). That one is NEW TEXT if he wants her rant back.
- **Ch5** has the most of his own text left, and it's the only chapter whose rhythm varies like his drafts.
