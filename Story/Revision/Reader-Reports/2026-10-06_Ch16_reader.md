## Headline

No stoppers. The chapter now reads as people, not as a ruling: the hearing is Mama Baga working a problem with a man who keeps agreeing, and Milo, Mama Baga's "Not me", the terrible coffee and "Person is the word I have for that." are the best sustained stretch since the museum. I did not feel managed in the first two-thirds; I felt managed in a short, countable set of places, all of the same kind: the narrator listing what Pathwell does not do, or telling me what a beat meant after it has worked (L370, L415, L635, L649, L670, L726, L732). ## Pass 1b: the five questions

**1. Did I feel managed, told how to judge Pathwell?** Not in the opening third, and not at the hearing: Mama Baga says the harsh things in her own voice and the narrator stays out. Yes in seven places, all in one mode (the narrator vouching for Pathwell's restraint, or naming what a beat meant):
- L401-409: "He did not say Lizzy." (L401; works, pays Ch15 / ledger P2) then "He looked exhausted enough that under different circumstances she might have wanted to help him. Those circumstances weren't available." The second is a narrator verdict.
- L415: "Pathwell didn't add a because. He didn't say he had been trying to fix it, or mention how much future he had lost." The line before already showed it (the apology without a because); this lists what he declines.
- L635: "The archive had burned because Pathwell chose not to stop. Both things were true." The first two sentences are Elizabeth's thought and carry the weight; "Both things were true." is the narrator naming a balance already shown, and is Milo's "Both can be true" (L280) in a new coat.
- L649: "He didn't explain what he could have done, or offer a better version, or reach for his coat." Same shape as L415.
- L670: "because the archivist had told him where they belonged. He didn't rearrange them." The action pays "You will ask before reorganizing" (L540) and is clear unaided; the "because" and the second sentence vouch.
- L726: "He didn't look toward Elizabeth." This one is fine (see item 2).
- L732: "...for once Pathwell wouldn't be the person deciding what those things were." The last line tells me what the chapter meant for him; "for once" is Ch15 L578's phrase.
The pattern is not the old graded commentary (that is gone; the checker's commentary count is 4, down from 30). It is the replacement: the verdict has moved from "That helped less than he hoped." into "didn't <verb>" runs. Counts under Pass 3 item 5.

**2. Can I see Pathwell behaving differently from what he does alone?** Yes, partly, and that is the right amount. What I can see: he waits to be told he may stand (L395); stops several feet away; says "Elizabeth" (L401-403); apologises "for which part?" with the parts and accepts "Those aren't one thing" with "All right." (L411-429); asks "What do you need?" and takes her answer (L441-453); agrees to terms with a single word each (L522-540); stops the compensation line the moment she says "That's not what I said." What I cannot see is the contrast: the book has no scene of him alone in this chapter, so "different from what he does alone" is only available through the memory of Ch13-15 (a reader who has them gets it; a reader who is handed the chapter does not). The one beat where I saw it without checking whether she sees it is L726. Lean: leave it; Q134 wants observation, not demonstration.
