Three soft problems (none is a contradiction):

1. **Pre-echo of a Ch18 beat.** Ch18:5-7 stages the pause on the phone as new ("a man with too many habitual answers was checking whether any of them had actually been requested") and Ch18:96-98 makes it an observation ("Pathwell did not ask why she wanted to go back until they had gone three blocks. Elizabeth noticed because the old Pathwell would have asked before saying yes and then answered the question himself"). Ch17:82 "he did it without asking what she wanted there" is that same observation, three weeks earlier and in the same shape (he doesn't ask what she wants). The Ch18 beat is now the second statement of it.
2. **Option-(2) conditions of the seat.** The seat said the replacement line must (i) say someone with the means took her: met ("asking Pathwell to open the way"); (ii) name no Space Between, dirt road, shopkeeper, museum wall or vehicle: met; (iii) not set a pattern ("after the second time", "as usual"): **partly** ("still meant" makes it a standing arrangement; "he did it" reads as every time); (iv) not make Pathwell the only ferryman by implication: **partly** ("Getting there still meant asking Pathwell" says getting there requires him). Q137b allows it; the seat asked for less.
3. **Where is he?** "open the way ... and went back to his table" has him opening it and then returning to a table at Camp, which reads as a way opened from Camp's side, without leaving his seat, for someone who is not there. Ch5:3 ("Pathwell wouldn't open anything near the wagons"; a field, a nap "the way she imagined people slept after giving blood") makes his portals costly and kept away from Camp, and Ch18:153-183 has him escorting her with the brass tag. A reader will ask how it can be done from the table. Also, "went back to his table" (82) followed by "She found him copying" (84) says the same thing twice.

Smallest fix (keeps the Q137b-compatible content, drops all three): `Getting there meant asking Pathwell to open the way.` (cut "still", and cut "; he did it without asking what she wanted there, and went back to his table"). If the author prefers option (1), cut the sentence entirely; nothing else in the chapter depends on it, and Ch18:762-786 answers the cold reader's question.
