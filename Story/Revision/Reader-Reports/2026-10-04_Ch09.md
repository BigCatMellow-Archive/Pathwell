# Reader report: Chapter 9, 2026-10-04 (read cold after Ch 1-8; hand-off Ch 10 L1-60)

**Headline.** No stoppers. Six snags: the clock (dusk at the crash, pre-dawn an hour later), a comparison to Pathwell she has no source for, a meal she orders knowing she has no wallet, a certainty Shade has no evidence for, three recaps of things the reader just saw, and one tangled attribution. Nothing breaks a lock or a world rule. Shade stays inside "old fragments, no current memories, no mind-reading" the whole way, and W16 holds on its own terms. Fourteen quibbles, plus one gap the ledger already tracks.

## What worked (keep)
- "Elizabeth sat in the back seat because she had chosen the back seat, and the distinction felt more important the farther they got from the wreck." (L5). It picks up Ch8's "Front is empty." without repeating it.
- The noun list: "A room in Prague I have never entered. A woman named Celia who hated pears. ... A staircase with a loose fourth step." (L122), then "We used to—" / "He used to," he corrected. (L132-136). Shade's grammar slipping is the best new fact in the chapter, and it needed no comment.
- "Observation. Not memory." / "She believed him, mostly because he'd bothered to make the distinction." (L92-94). The rule that he can't read her is enforced by his own manners, not by the narrator. Same at L29-33 ("Do you know what he's thinking?" / "No." / "Good.").
- "Would you prefer applause?" (L168), and "Shade lifted... 'Good.'" (L84, L164-172). The deadpan belongs to him and not to Pathwell: "Pathwell would have smiled after saying it. Shade didn't." (L112).
- "Not that he was right... That he had reached the point where being uncertain no longer mattered to him." (L286). The "Certainty" answer to "What did he choose?" is the right size for what Ch10's Ask needs.
- "I don't have any money. All I have to my name right now is my diary and a foam dagger." (L358), and the close: "Nobody had sent her, and nobody had called her back." (L374).

## Smallest fixes (holes and snags)
1. **L370 (and Ch10 L3):** change "the morning hadn't started yet but was trying" to a night line ("Outside, the night had gone cold and still."). If the author wants dawn at Ch10's opening, put one clause there: she has walked "most of the night", which is honest, since the diner is a twenty-five-minute drive from the ditch and a long walk back. L62-70 of Ch10 (gray windows two miles from the ditch) needs the same treatment; that is outside this report.
2. **L49:** cut "the way Pathwell did": "He checked his mirrors, and tapped the wheel twice with one finger before every turn."
3. **L354:** one clause that puts the knowledge on the page: "Elizabeth slid out of the booth and checked her coat pockets, though she'd known since the dumpling place what was in them. The diary, and nothing else."
4. **L154:** give Shade his evidence: "One came through my apartment door while he was standing in my living room. Another one swallowed Pathwell in a parking lot." Then "whatever followed him" has a basis.
5. **L198 and L210:** cut L198 to its first sentence ("She had deliberately crashed a Cadillac...") or cut it, and cut L210 to its last line ("Shade driving toward Pathwell without knowing her name."). Keep L290.
6. **L362:** move the tag to the end: `"Which way is the bar? Back toward the fields. There's a fish over the register," Elizabeth told the waitress.`

- I'd keep "twenty-five minutes ago" at L106 and L246 as the running joke and let the clock be fixed at L370 instead.
