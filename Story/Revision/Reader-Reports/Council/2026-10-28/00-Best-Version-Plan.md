## The verdict in one paragraph

Every seat thinks the book is worth the work, and most think its best material is as good as published comparables: the Nana flashback, the museum, Shade at Hearts, the fire and LIKED POCKETS. The 17 developmental editor's estimate is "one major revision and one line pass from being ready to query." The readers agree on what holds it back. Three load-bearing things are told abstractly or off the page: the prune, Shade's taking, and why Pathwell was in Elizabeth's apartment. The confession in Ch18 isn't given room to land. Elizabeth chooses well but mostly by refusing. And from Ch6 on, nearly everyone speaks in the same dry voice. These overlap heavily with [Read 8](../../2026-10-27_Read-08_Honest-Editor.md), which a different reader reached a day earlier.

## 2. Where the readers converge (strongest first)

"Seats" lists who reached the finding without being prompted. **Locks** are bt 7274's own rulings that the finding runs into. Those items are his call, and the recommendation is shown only as a lean.

### C1. The prune is a diagram, not an event
**Seats:** 17, 19, 20, 26, 32, 44, plus 09 ("put the reader inside his certainty") and 34 ("what does potential contain?"). That's eight of nine seats across five families, and Read 8 said it too. 20 calls it "a feeling without a fact" and wants it as a flashback.

The Ch10 van confession gives the prune's moral shape ("They did not ask me to prune. I did not ask whether they wanted me to.") but not its content: "Someone asked me for help." / "They had a problem. A real one." Readers say they "can't picture a single noun in it". Shade is the result of this event, so he is built on a hole. The 34 rules lawyer adds a logic gap. A prune burns "memories never made", which are futures, so why does Shade carry Pathwell's *past* (Prague, Celia, pears)?

**Lock:** the bible decision (08-22, Q141) says the problem "remains open **unless the story later needs it**", and Pathwell needn't know the exact prune. Plan.md marks "the reader needs to know who asked" FALSE by that lock.

**The council's position:** the story does need it. This is the most-agreed finding of the round.

**Options:**
- **(a)** One concrete person, place and problem in the van. It needn't be long. 17 and 32 suggest 1,500–2,000 words, but a few hundred specific ones would do.
- **(b)** Make the not-knowing the content. Pathwell genuinely can't recall it, because the prune took that part, and Shade holds a fragment of it. This also answers 34's past-versus-future gap.
- **(c)** Keep it open.

**Lean: (b), or a small (a).** (b) keeps the lock's spirit (Pathwell needn't know) while giving readers something concrete. Diagnosis: A, an actual problem. Ten readers across two rounds report confusion at the book's central scene.

### C2. The Ch18 confession is followed too fast by comedy, and Shade disappears from the end
**Seats:** 17, 19, 44, with 32 adding "show it in Ch15". That's craft, adversarial and market.

The darkest fact in the book ("I thought that if the creature took him, it would be finished") is followed within about forty lines by bird personalities, geese and the bookend. After the confession the word "Shade" never appears again. The ending skeptic: the invitation "reads as the forgiveness Elizabeth refused in Ch16", and the shape implies the death was worth it, which is what she said she wouldn't do.

**Locks:**
- R47 (Version C): the confession comes late, the hour among the shelves follows it, and she freely invites him, "without a pardon speech".
- R54: "Yes, it stays with Elizabeth". Nobody at Camp learns, on purpose.

17's fix (make it public, so the Ch16 hearing accounts for it) **overrules R54**, and I don't recommend that.

**Options that keep both rulings:**
- **(a)** After the confession, give Elizabeth one visible beat of deciding before any comedy returns. This is an action or a condition, not a speech: what she does with what she's just heard. Keep the hour in the stacks, but let it be on the page for a few lines rather than summarised.
- **(b)** Keep Shade's name or an object of his (the pocket, the coffee) present in the closing movement, so the warmth is chosen with him in the room.
- **(c)** Let the shopkeeper's ledger take an entry, since 44 noted that the moral account isn't in the shop's.

**Lean: (a) + (b), small.** This changes the music, not the structure. The invitation still comes without a pardon speech, and Camp still doesn't learn. Diagnosis: A, an unintended effect. Three seats from three families read the current order as absolution.

### C3. Shade's taking is reported, not seen, and the dagger sits unused in Elizabeth's hand
**Seats:** 34 (its top finding), 17, 20, 44, 32, and 09 (Shade's refusal blurred). That's audit, craft, adversarial, market and experience. 20: "Shade's capture was off-camera and abrupt; I didn't quite believe it, and then the book asked me to."

The text explains why *she* doesn't cut (her shoulder, needing both arms), but not why the dagger never passes to the man pulling Shade free. **Locks:**

- R46: Pathwell consciously doesn't interfere, and Shade's fate is nonconsensual.

Neither lock is threatened. These fixes make the locked outcome *legible*; they don't change it.

**Options:**

- **(b)** Elizabeth tries to hand it off and it fails for a stated rule reason.

**Lean: (a) + (c), with (d) as a question for bt 7274.** Diagnosis: A, a contradiction. The climax depends on an unused tool the book itself established.

## 7. The shape of the best version

This draws together the nine "shape" sections.

It opens the same way: the party, ELIZ, the man reading her diary, "Perfect." Elizabeth is funny on her own from the first chapter. Somewhere early there's a faint wrongness about Pathwell, and one plain line about what pruning costs. When he confesses in the van, the reader learns *something*, a person or the specific hole where one should be. Shade then reads as a consequence, not a mystery.

The middle moves faster, with fewer glances, fewer deferrals and fewer quips while people bleed. The cast gets louder and more various: a shopkeeper who enjoys the bill, an archivist who loves a mistake, a healer who talks like a nurse. The museum stays the museum.

At the fire, every tool the book handed out is in play or visibly gone. Shade's look means something, and Elizabeth carries Milo out knowing what she left. The cookbook and diary burn as ordinary paper.

The aftermath is a little shorter and repeats itself less. In Ch18 the confession stays private and late, as bt 7274 chose. But after it Elizabeth decides something in front of us, and Shade is still in the room when the jokes come back. The geese still steal buttons. "Are you ready?" "No." "Perfect." now reads, in 19's phrase, as "a promise made with open eyes."
