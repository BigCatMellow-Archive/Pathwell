## 2. Every rule broken, bent or never explained

Most serious first.

1. **What potential is. Shade contradicts the Ch3 definition.** Pruning burns "memories never made" (Ch3), and Pathwell paid with "future possibility" (Ch10). Yet Shade holds Pathwell's *past*: "I remember things I did not live… A room in Prague… A woman named Celia who hated pears… I know things that were already there when I started" (Ch8–9). If what Pathwell sold was futures, why does the leftover carry his history? A reader can invent an answer (a future grows from its past, so the past comes with it), but the book never offers one. Shade is the emotional centre and the central magical fact, so this is the one place the rules most need to be airtight. *Confidence: high that readers who track rules will notice; medium that general readers will.*
2. **The intent rule (A8) and the brass frame (D6) are introduced at the climax.** Mama Baga explains why the working fails *as* it fails. The hardware has no earlier presence; Ch14 even has to cover for it ("You carry an unreasonable amount of hardware"). Seeding it would make the failure feel inevitable rather than merely announced. The book already has the seed: Ch1, "Don't think… Don't. Think." Pathwell is asking Elizabeth for a clean, unsplit intent. If that line were later shown to be *the* rule, Ch14 would land as a payoff.
3. **Ch1's sugar-cookie working doesn't obey "fits the work" (A3),** and a broken working still kills the blob (C2). The opening sets the reader's contract, and the first working is the one that's least lawful.
4. **"Marked. Magically speaking." (Ch1)** is never confirmed or withdrawn. Elizabeth later calls it Pathwell's error ("because you decided a monster was after me", Ch8), but the shadow in the window (Ch2) and the "wet thud… from down the hall" (Ch1) suggest a second blob that never resolves.
5. **The neighbourhood shift (Ch2):** "there was no coffee shop. Just a shuttered laundromat she'd never seen before. Beyond that, a park that shouldn't have been there." That's a big uncanny event, never explained or returned to. (The Ch18 laundromat may be a nod. If so, it's too faint.)
6. **Harvest vs. spend (A2 / Ch5):** Stansbury's "The kids get their work back" sits uneasily with "Spent." Either harvesting is a different, non-consuming act (say so once), or Stansbury is shading the truth (let someone call it out; Pathwell's "He steals children's energy" is already half that).
7. **Who can work a page (A9).** Never stated. It matters for Elizabeth's agency and for Camp during the fire.
8. **Scale/containment (Ch11):** the plane grows beyond what he meant. The book teaches this by example, which is fine, but it's the same unstated rule as #2.
9. **Pruning cost has no lasting cost on the page (B2).** Beautiful on the page in Ch3, but what it costs afterwards is a tremor and a nap. "Pruning shock" in Ch16 heals in three weeks. No specific future is shown to be gone. For a book whose ethics are about cost, Pathwell's own big cost is the softest one in it.
10. **Thresholds (D1/D2/D3).** The need-portal (Ch5) makes "There isn't a reliable threshold from here" (Ch10) look like a choice, not a constraint. The Space Between door works on any wall in Ch3 and needs a brass tag in Ch18. Both are defensible, but neither is explained.
11. **Camp's ward (C3)** is implied twice, never stated. Fine as atmosphere. Noting it for the rules sheet.
12. **Tiny snag, low confidence:** Ch4, "You could have written first." / "On what?" implies no paper for messages, yet in Ch5 "Mama Baga sent word" to Stansbury. Perhaps Camp has other means. One phrase would settle it.

## 4. Is the climax decided by the rules or in spite of them?

**Mostly by them, with one hole that carries weight.**

- **The frame fails by the rules,** but by a rule stated in the same scene ("The working knows what you mean…"). The fail state is fair: it collapses "toward the practitioner who had supplied the contradiction and the payment", and the released payment becomes "Fresh spillage." That follows directly from Ch5's cleanup rule. Good.
- **The blob takes Shade by the rules.** Shade is unclaimed possibility, which is exactly what the cleanup cleans. "If you don't stand between them and what they're after, they're unpleasant rather than murderous." Shade *is* what it's after. It leans toward Pathwell, then leaves. Grim and consistent. This is the best rule payoff in the book.
- **The fire starts by physics, not magic** (the loop hits a lantern), and grows by A5 (destroyed originals release charge). That's consistent.
- **Elizabeth's deciding choice is deliberately non-magical,** and it costs her: the shoulder she was told not to use, the cookbook, the diary. "Her own ordinary handwriting disappeared without being used for anything." This is the book's thesis made into plot, and I think it's right. It also echoes Ch4 ("That was the choice. Everything afterward was consequence." / Ch15 "That was the choice. Everything else burned afterward.").
- **The hole:** the dagger. The book armed her with a key to the exact lock in front of her, and the climax depends on nobody turning it. If that's deliberate (the dagger "might be him"; she doesn't know whose binding it is to cut), it must be said in Ch15 as clearly as it is in Ch14. As written, a GM's table would stop the game here.

## 5. Ranked top ten

1. **Close the dagger hole in Ch15.** Either take the dagger off the board before the climax (best: pay off Stansbury's Ch5 warning, "If one cracks… stop using it", by having it crack in the museum discharge), or have Elizabeth try to hand it off and fail for a *rule* reason (it opens a seam, but Shade isn't a binding, he's the target, so the blob closes again). Either way the climax stops depending on an unused tool. *Small–medium. Highest priority.*
2. **Decide what pruned potential contains, and make Shade fit it.** Right now Ch3 says "memories never made" and Ch9 says Shade remembers the past. One clarifying exchange in Ch10 or Ch14 would let Shade's existence follow from the rules instead of from a mystery. *Small in words, large in effect.*
3. **Plant the intent rule and the hardware before Ch14.** Turn Ch1's "Don't think" into the first statement of the intent rule. Show the brass tag or frames earlier (Ch3 shopkeeper's cube, Ch5 portal, Ch10 Stansbury's library). Then Mama Baga's Ch14 line becomes a reminder rather than news. *Medium.*
4. **Make pruning cost something specific and lasting.** Show one concrete future Pathwell no longer has, something he reaches for and finds gone. The ceremony in Ch3 is the most vivid magic in the book. The aftermath should match it. *Medium.*
5. **Bring the Ch1–2 threads either to a resolution or a cut:** "marked," the second thud, the shadow in the window, the missing coffee shop and impossible park, the sugar-cookie page that fits nothing, the broken working that still kills the blob. The opening chapters are where a reader learns whether to trust your rules. *Small–medium.*
6. **Have Camp act like a camp of practitioners in the fire.** One reason why Mama Baga's strength, the healer, or a charged page isn't used for the shelf or the blob. *Small.*
7. **Answer the love-note question once** (scarcity, consent, or "love notes don't touch what's this deep"). Readers will ask it at Ch12 and again at Ch16. *Small.*
8. **Square the travel rules.** One line in Ch10 explaining why the need-portal isn't an option (out of pages, or it can't target Camp because of the ward). *Small.*
9. **Fire or cut the unfired setups:** the foam-weapon warning, the gold chain ("Now it's complete"), Boots, harvesting versus spending, the shopkeeper as unconsulted expert. Each one builds an expectation the book doesn't meet. *Small each.*
10. **Protect the originals-versus-copies economy and use it to solve problems 1–9.** It's the best magic thinking in the book. "The copy keeps the words." / "Not the thing." is a rule that costs something, keeps showing up in the plot, and carries the theme. Most of the fixes above can come from it rather than from new rules.

## 6. The best version

### What to protect
- **Spent, not burned.** "Nana's handwriting darkened on the page. Then thinned. Then disappeared one line at a time… Not burned. Spent." (Ch4)
- **The copy is not the thing.** "The copy keeps the words." / "Not the thing." (Ch10) and "The information survived. The page didn't." (Ch17)
- **The finding spell's stated limits.** "Can she feel this?" "No." "Can you make her turn around?" "No." (Ch10) Constraints set out before they matter.
- **Fit-the-work logic:** grocery lists as "Movement in sequence. Nothing more romantic than errands" (Ch10), and the quarantine letter, "Care that meant stay away" (Ch11).
- **The cleanup rule taking Shade.** "If you don't stand between them and what they're after…" (Ch5) paid off in Ch15.
- **Ordinary loss.** "Her own ordinary handwriting disappeared without being used for anything." (Ch15)
- **The restitution terms as rules.** "You will not prune to buy any of it." / "Copy the smudge." (Ch16–17)
- **The confession.** "I could have called someone. I didn't." (Ch18)
- **The answered why-didn't-they-justs:** "You could have written first." / "On what?" (Ch4); "Don't make him the reason." (Ch14)

### The changes, in order of impact
1. **Dagger resolution, Ch11 and Ch15.** Have the dagger crack in the museum discharge (paying off Ch5's warning), or have Elizabeth hand it off in Ch15 and fail for a stated reason. *For the reader:* the climax feels inevitable instead of stopped by convenience. *Small–medium.*
2. **Define potential, Ch3, Ch10 and Ch14.** Adjust the Ch3 imagery or add a Ch10 line so that pruned futures are shown to carry their past roots. Shade's memories then follow from the rules. *For the reader:* Shade becomes a consequence they can reason about, and his death lands as the rule's price. *Small.*
3. **Seed intent and hardware, Ch1, Ch3, Ch5 and Ch10.** "Don't think" becomes the first statement of the intent rule. Brass objects appear in passing earlier (in the shopkeeper's cube, Pathwell's pockets, Stansbury's library). *For the reader:* Ch14 reads as a payoff. Mama Baga confirms what they already suspected. *Medium.*
4. **Make pruning's cost concrete, Ch3 and Ch6 onward.** Show one specific lost possibility: a skill, a place he can't go back to, a person whose name he now can't reach. Keep it unknowable in advance ("Which part?"), but let it be noticed afterwards. *For the reader:* the Ch14 "Do not release payment" carries real dread. *Medium.*
5. **Close the opening, Ch1–2.** Resolve or cut "marked," the second thud, the window shadow and the neighbourhood shift. Make the sugar-cookie working fit (comfort, home, warding?), or let someone later name it as the wrong page, which would be on theme. *For the reader:* trust in the rules from page one. *Small–medium.*
6. **Camp in the fire, Ch15.** A sentence or two on why practitioners and Mama Baga's strength don't solve the shelf or the blob. *Small.*
7. **Healing and travel lines, Ch10, Ch12 and Ch16.** One line each: why no love notes, why no need-portal. *Small.*
8. **Pay off or trim the loose setups.** The chain, Boots, harvesting versus spending, the unconsulted shopkeeper. A shopkeeper consultation in Ch18 about unclaimed possibility could even replace the Merritt scene's silence with one hard fact. *Small.*

### The shape of the best version
A reader meets the magic in Chapter One with a single strange instruction: "Don't think." By Chapter Four they understand the system. Handwritten originals hold lives, using one spends it for good, it has to fit the work, and someone has to own the cost. Every working after that teaches them a little more: the soup that heals, the errands that point a direction, the quarantine letter that shoves people apart. The pruning in the Space Between costs Pathwell something they later watch him reach for and not find. When Shade appears, the reader can work out what he must be, and that is worse than not knowing.

At the climax, every lever the book has handed out is either in play or visibly gone. The dagger cracked in the museum, the baton is in a van two worlds away, and the frame fails for a reason the reader has known since the first night. The blob takes Shade because that is exactly what it was always for. Elizabeth's choice to go into the fire for Milo becomes the one decision no rule makes for her, and the cookbook and diary burn as ordinary paper. The ending's quieter scenes (copying the smudge, the yellow wagon, the cookbook put back unbought) then read as a world keeping its accounts. As a GM I'd say this: it's a soft magic system with Earthsea's sense of cost, and the players never get to ask "why didn't they just…" because the book asked it first.
