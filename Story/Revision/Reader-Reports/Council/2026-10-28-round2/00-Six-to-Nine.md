## Is 6 to 9 harder than 3 to 6? All eight said yes, and gave the same reason

Every seat put the book in the same place: **past competent, on the plateau where good manuscripts stall.** Each one, unprompted, said polish has stopped paying.

- **28 acquisitions editor:** "Authors who reach 'distinctive' tend to polish sentences when what's needed is structural surgery on the parts they're fondest of."
- **05 literary-fantasy reader:** "The work that moves it past competent is cutting lines that are individually good … It's harder than adding, because the cut lines will be some of the author's favorites."
- **13 classic-fantasy elder:** "A 9 is reachable … but it means letting the book be less likeable … the nerve to sit inside something unpleasant without a joke as an exit."
- **38 former caregiver:** "It has everything except the nerve."
- **01 Discworld devotee:** "A 7 has a few great chapters. A 9 has no chapter you would skip."
- **43 hostile reviewer:** "the plateau where line-level and scene-level fixes stop paying. The next gains come from structure and moral pressure, not polish."

**Why the jump is different in kind.** Getting from 3 to 6 is mostly fixing errors: things a checklist can find, where each fix makes the book safely better. Getting from 6 to 9 is **choosing**. You decide what the book is, then you cut good material that doesn't serve that, and you write the uncomfortable thing the jokes have been protecting. None of those moves is safe, and several of them cut against what made the book likable in the first place.

**Is a 9 in reach?** Seven of eight said yes, as the book is conceived. One said no:

- **43 hostile reviewer:** an 8 as conceived; a 9 needs a tempting philosophy for Pathwell, which is "a somewhat different book".
- **13 classic-fantasy elder:** yes, but "less likeable".
- **05 literary-fantasy reader:** yes, if the quiet voice governs. If the comic voice governs, "a very good 8."

## What it takes, by agreement and by points

Points are the readers' own estimates. They don't add up neatly, and several readers warned about diminishing returns.

### 1. Shade's taking, and Pathwell's silence, carried at full weight: 8 of 8 seats, about +0.5 to +1
This is the strongest finding of either round. In round 1 it was C2 and C3; now all eight seats name it, with no prompting.

The darkest fact in the book ("I could have called someone. I didn't.") comes about 170 lines from the end. It gets a page, and then the geese.

Proposed fixes:

- **Inside Pathwell during Ch15** (13, 05): a passage where he hears Shade calling and doesn't call out.
- **Move the confession into the Ch16 apology** (28, 05, 27). 05 points out that a long apology list that leaves out the worst thing would itself be devastating.
- **Put it on the page at the climax, seen by Elizabeth** (43, 27).
- Whichever is chosen, "nothing light should follow it directly" (05).

**Collides with:** R47 (Version C: the confession comes late, the hour among the shelves follows it, then the invitation) and R54 (it stays with Elizabeth). R65 added the "Say his name" beat inside those rulings. This round read the text with that beat in it, and the readers still find the confession underweighted.

### 2. One governing voice: 7 of 8 seats, about +0.3 to +0.75
Every reader hears two books:

- a **quiet voice**: Nana, the cookbook, the fire, LIKED POCKETS, the dumplings. They call it "already at a 9".
- a **sitcom voice**: the shared three-beat deadpan with a one-word topper. They put it at about a 6.

05: "All ten best sentences come from the quiet voice, and none of them is a joke." The line editor counts the shared engine, the "pedantic correction delivered as a verdict", across eight speakers, along with a no-contractions house style.

**The disagreement that matters:** which voice should govern?

- **05 literary-fantasy reader:** the quiet voice should govern. Take the quips out of the narrator, and leave the comedy to particular characters.
- **01 Discworld devotee:** the opposite fix. Add a **comic narrator**, with asides that widen into observation, and let the world generate jokes.

Both agree the book can't keep switching. This is bt 7274's decision more than any other in this round. His Scrubs note ("irreverence at times, with heart") points to a third shape: a comic surface that drops into earnest on purpose, with the drop as the payoff. That shape still needs one narrator who does both.

### 3. The first 40% written to the standard of the back half: 7 of 8 seats, about +0.3 to +1
The readers want:

- Ch1 brought up to Ch4's level, with a Nana memory as specific as the Ch4 flu memory (18).
- Ch5's portal and school cut hard (01, 13, 05, 38).
- The loud magic rewritten in the document register: the Ch1 blob, the Ch3 pruning tree, the Ch5 portal (28, 05, 27).
- The withholding loop ended sooner (28, 43, 27).

28: "It needs the first 40% to be written by the author who wrote Chapters 11–18."

**Honest note for bt 7274:** Ch1–5 are mostly his own draft. Ch6 onward have been heavily drafted and revised by the revising sessions. In round 1, seat 26 said the two halves read like different hands, and that the later half is where the machine-smoothing sits. So the readers here are preferring the revised voice for its control, and also faulting it for its sameness. The fix in item 2 and the fix in item 3 are the same job seen from two sides. Ch1–5 also carry rulings: R29 (his own Ch3 pruning ritual) and R31 (the Ch4 choice placement).

### 4. Elizabeth beyond grief, and the caregiving years on the page: 7 of 8 seats, about +0.25 to +1
The readers ask for:

- Nana's name.
- Elizabeth's job, her mother, and what moving was supposed to fix.
- The diary's voice heard before it burns (05, 27, 28, 13).

The caregiver reader goes furthest:

- put Nana on the page as a person, with mood, temper, and a failing body;
- show one decision Elizabeth made for Nana that she still isn't sure about;
- **turn the care-versus-tyranny lens on Elizabeth.** She spent years deciding for Nana: the pills, the stairs, the plates. Let her hear her own sentences in Pathwell's, and give her one moment of relief she's ashamed of.

The caregiver reader calls this the thing the book "should know about this experience that it doesn't yet". **It's the most original 9-level idea in either round.**

### 5. The climax as Elizabeth's own hardest choice, with opposition that costs something: 4 of 8 seats, about +0.75 to +1

- **13 and 01** say the cleanup creatures could carry real weight: "a system that rates people as waste".

## Decisions for bt 7274 (leans in italics)

1. **Which voice governs?**
   - quiet, with comedy living in characters (05);
   - comic narrator (01);
   - *or Scrubs-shaped: a comic surface, one narrator, deliberate drops into earnest. Lean: Scrubs-shaped, since it's his stated goal, applied by giving the narrator one voice and cutting the shared deadpan from the cast.*
2. **Reopen R47 and R54?** Eight of eight seats want Pathwell's silence carried at full weight. Options:
   - (a) a short passage inside Pathwell in Ch15;
   - (b) move the confession into the Ch16 apology;
   - (c) keep it in Ch18, but give it a scene of its own with nothing light after it.

   *Lean: (a) plus (c). It keeps his late-confession design and the privacy, but stops the reader being surprised by a fact the book had in its hands all along.*
3. **Ch1–5: who rewrites?** These are his chapters, and seven seats say they're the gap. *Lean: he leads. The revising session drafts candidates only where he asks, beginning with the Ch1 Nana memory and the line editor's Ch1 fixes, which are mechanical.*
4. **Turn the care-versus-tyranny lens on Elizabeth?** *Lean: yes, small and specific. One remembered override of Nana, and one moment of relief. It changes meaning (C), so it's his call.*
5. **Climax opposition.** Should the unresolved draw visibly endanger Camp before the frame, so that the "no" costs something? *Lean: ask. It touches R45 and R46.*
