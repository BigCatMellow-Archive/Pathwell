# How to get from a 7 to a 9: guide index

**What this is.** bt 7274 read the round 2 synthesis and asked for "real help and guidance. Not just 'have more nerve', but examples, and understanding of how to get there." Each of the eight round 2 readers took the three items from their own gap list that they thought were worth the most points, and wrote a working guide for each. Every guide has the same six parts:

1. what the text is doing now, mechanically;
2. numbered steps to take at the desk;
3. a published book that does it well, and how;
4. a worked **ILLUSTRATION** on a real passage, with every choice explained;
5. a check to tell whether it worked;
6. traps, and passages to protect.

Each guide ends with a revision order and a practice scene for this week.

**The illustrations are demonstrations, not text for the book.** Their facts (a name, a city, a job) are placeholders. Nothing from these guides has been applied to the chapters.

## Where the guides agree on *how*

1. **Do the paper work before the prose.** Every guide starts with a document the author writes for himself and never puts in the book:
   - a question ledger (28, 13, 43, 01);
   - scene cards (01, 38);
   - a causal spine, covering why he was at the party, the prune and the kitchen table (13, 43);
   - a voice sheet (05, 13, 18, 27);
   - Elizabeth's dossier (05) or a carer's inventory (38);
   - a one-sentence verdict on Pathwell plus a two-column dilemma (27).

   The shared idea: most of the gap is undecided facts, and prose can't fix a fact that hasn't been decided.
2. **Questions should pay out, not stall.** Each time Elizabeth asks, she should get one true fact and a sharper question (28, 43, 13). Cap the bare "Later." / "Not here." lines: three or fewer in Ch1–10 (28), two (43, 38).
3. **The Shade moment has to be seen.** The guides offer four mechanisms, and they can be combined:
   - (a) a short passage inside Pathwell during Ch15 (13);
   - (b) Elizabeth looks back and sees him not call out (43, 27);
   - (c) the Ch16 apology list breaks at "Shade" (28, 05, 27);
   - (d) the confession is written into the loss sheet under LIKED POCKETS (27).

   All of them say nothing light should follow it directly.
4. **A joke test for the voice pass.** Does the joke cost the speaker something or reveal them? Keep it. Is it a topper, or could anyone have said it? Cut it (05, 13, 27, 18). Use a "quiet window" of about a page after a loss, where only a joke that fails is allowed (05). One person per scene should lose an exchange (13).
5. **Trade stated theme for an object or a question.** For example, "Who else paid?" in place of "the method was yours to choose" (01). Cut the narrator's verdict once the scene earns it (27, 01, 38).
6. **Elizabeth needs specifics that aren't Nana:** a want that pulls against her devotion (05), and Nana on the page as a person, with Elizabeth overriding her once (38).

## Where they disagree

- **Order.** Five guides (28, 05, 13, 43, 27) say *structure first, voice pass last*: don't polish scenes that are about to move. The line editor (18) says *voices first*, because who owns which habit decides which beats survive. The devotee and the caregiver say *cut first* (Ch5, the middle), because it's cheap and shows the real shape. The majority view is to decide on paper, then do structure, then do lines.
- **Where the Shade fact goes.** Some put it inside Pathwell in Ch15 (13). Some have Elizabeth see it in Ch15 (43, 27). Some move it into the Ch16 apology (28, 05, 27). Several combine Ch15 with Ch16. This touches R47 and R54, so it's bt 7274's decision.
- **The prune.** Name a client and a problem (43), give it in layers (28, 13), or keep it unnamed but remembered (R65). Four guides think the current version is still too vague.

## Suggested way in (reviser's lean)

1. **One sitting of paper work.** Write the causal spine (the party, the prune, the kitchen table), the one-sentence verdict on Pathwell, and voice sheets for eight speakers. These are decisions only bt 7274 can make. The reviser can draft starting versions for him to correct.
2. **One practice scene: Ch15, the fire, plus Ch16, the apology.** All eight readers converge here, and three guides (43, 27, 05) give worked examples on these exact passages. It tests the Shade question, Elizabeth's choice and the voice rules at once.
3. Then use the guides' checks (questions answered by the end of each scene, a blind test of who's speaking, counts of quip endings, reading aloud) to decide whether to roll the approach out across the book.

## Checks gathered from all eight guides

- **Speaker test** (05, 13, 18, 27): remove the speech tags and see whether you can tell who's talking. Target 80% or more.
- **Swap test** (28, 18, 38): could this passage sit in another novel, or could this line be another character's?
- **Ledger test** (28, 13, 43): no type-(c) question open for more than about two chapters without a partial answer.
- **Seen versus reported** (43): every turn that changes a relationship is witnessed.
- **Defence-attorney test** (43): can you argue for Pathwell's pin using only facts on the page?
- **Two-column test** (27): both of Elizabeth's options cost her a person or a consequence, not just an object.
- **Date test and body count** (38): can you say the day and the hour of each caregiving scene?
- **Narration-only read** (01): read only the narration and count the smiles. Tag each laugh as character, world or narrator.
- **"Is it shorter?"** Nearly every practice exercise sets the rewrite at no longer than the current version.
