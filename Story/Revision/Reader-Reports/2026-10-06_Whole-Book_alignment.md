# Whole-book read against the author's goals, 2026-10-06

**Evidence, closed.** A fresh reader (no prior contact with the book or its records) read Chapters 1–18 in order with the [Reader questionnaire](../Reader-Questionnaire.md), wrote a running log after each chapter ([log](2026-10-06_Whole-Book_reader_log.md)) and answered the 54 open questions after the last page ([answers](2026-10-06_Whole-Book_answers.md)). It opened nothing but the questionnaire and the chapters. It read the questionnaire first, so it knew a few late beats existed (the cookbook in the shop, "It was my fault", the last exchange) and said so. This page compares its answers with the [goals key](../Reader-Questionnaire-Goals.md), which it never saw. Text read: the chapters as of P43 (568dd16).

**Marks:** *aligned* = found unprompted, in its own words; *partly* = found with a gap, or only where the question pointed; *missed* = not found, or found the opposite.

## Summary

The book's heart lands. The reader found Elizabeth's change, Pathwell's flaw and slow turn, Shade as a person, the losses as permanent, and the ending's reversal, all in its own words and mostly unprompted. Its favourite moments are the beats the records protect. Where it diverges, it diverges in five places, three of them already known:

1. **The opening premise doesn't land.** Why Pathwell was in her apartment, why "Lizzy", what happened to the cookie page: "It's the book's first question and I never got an answer." (Q30, Q49, Q54). The canon calls this "the engine of his whole involvement" (the Chapter 1 misread). **New, and the biggest.**
2. **The climax is Shade's, and Elizabeth's rescue doesn't change the outcome** (Q33–34). This is the 2026-09-18 rule recorded as not met in P40. The reader confirms it independently. **Known; the author's call.**
3. **The narrator still tells it what to think**, and the lines it names are mostly ones the passes kept as leans: "That was the choice" (Ch4 and Ch15), "Both things were true", "She called it evidence", the "freely" paragraph, the Ch16 crying list (Q29, Q46, Q54: "Trust your scenes"). **Known; the leans are now in question.**
4. **The early rungs read as passive** (Ch1–3), and the diary gift (Ch12) reads as set up to burn (Q6–7). **Partly known.**
5. **The Ch18 cookbook motive reads wrong.** It saw the temptation but read it as using or paying with the book, not as buying it to replace Nana's for her (Q16). **New.**

## The protected beats

Its five favourites are all on the Assessment's protect list: "The whole book." (Ch4), Shade's last exchange with Milo and "Shade closed it himself." (Ch15), "I want to stay here." / "Then stay." with the coffee and Hearts (Ch13), the museum records (Ch11), the ditch and "Same ingredients. Different recipe." (Ch8). Honourable mentions: UNKNOWN and LIKED POCKETS. (Ch16), "They are not Daniel's." (Ch17).

Its three weakest: the Ch5 school detour and the guard; the Ch16 hearing and the crying list; the Ch10 van (Elizabeth's analysis of him, and the "Bank it" slap gag). None is protected, though the Ch10 van is the chapter of the Ask: what it dislikes is her speech, not "But you still chose it." / "Yes."

## What needs tuning, from this read

In rough order of weight. None is applied; the author asked to hold the open questions.

1. **The opening premise (Ch1–3).** Make legible, early, why Pathwell was in her apartment (he followed the charged material, per the canon's Chapter 1 misread), where "Lizzy" came from (the R21 echo through the sugar-cookie page), and what became of the cookie page. Also "Whose door was it?" (Ch5) and why the first creature was at her door.
2. **The climax rule** (09-18 §3). The reader confirms P40's finding. The author's question stands.
3. **The kept narrator lines.** The reader names most of the leans from P38–P43 as the book telling it what to think. Revisit: "That was the choice. / Everything afterward was consequence." and its Ch15 rhyme, "Both things were true.", "She called it evidence.", "He said it freely: …", the Ch16 crying list, "At the time, it looked like fidgeting.", "Shade was still alive. / The draw between them was still there."
4. **Rungs 1–2.** Ch3's portal should read as her choice (she takes her books back, then chooses to go); Ch4's choice of the forest over the sidewalk is reported, not shown.
5. **The diary gift's want (Ch12).** It reads as set up to burn. One stronger beat of why she gives it.
6. **The Merritt cookbook's motive (Ch18).** The replacement thought (Nana's book, for her) doesn't come through the silent beat; the ledger glance reads as debt or spending. Within Q103's silence: something he does or looks at that points to Nana's book, not to the ledger alone.
7. **Shade's reason.** Readable as a choice, but "he saw others paying and stopped" doesn't come through as the reason; "protecting Camp" and "a gamble" do.
8. **Sameness, book-wide.** "That's not an answer" / "That wasn't the question" (Ch2, 3, 6–11), one-word retorts ("Cruel." "Fair." "Correct." "Accurate."), "Not X. Y." shapes, scene-ending zingers, the shared deadpan of the minor characters, Elizabeth's long structured speeches.
9. **Wishes.** A quiet scene with Shade and Pathwell alone before Ch14; Stansbury's own want; Ch5 cut down (the school and the guard).
10. **Small unpaid setups.** The changed neighbourhood (Ch2), the mirror (Ch6), Bakhtak (Ch5), "Leo" (Ch10), "He said you would say that" and the bear (Ch4), Thumper, the cost of a prune shown concretely, why the creatures stop after Shade.
