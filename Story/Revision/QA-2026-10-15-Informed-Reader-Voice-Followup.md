## Editorial disposition

**KEEP the author-approved structure.** No reason emerged to reverse the unexpected capture, Shade's desire to live, the competing calls, the nonmagical Milo rescue, Pathwell's culpable omission, the late confession, the hour of browsing, or Elizabeth's independent invitation. Two **high-impact reader-belief tests** remain unresolved: (1) what actionable additional help Pathwell could summon when one Camp man is already attempting to pull Shade free; (2) whether the return to playfulness after the confession reads as forgiveness rather than an autonomous choice made while still troubled. The current manuscript asserts no guarantee that additional help would save Shade, and must not be rewritten to assert one.

### What works and should survive revision

1. **Shade has a life before he loses it.** [Ch13](../Chapters/Chapter_13.txt)'s terrible coffee, green jacket pockets, ongoing Hearts game, invitation to stay and admission that he does not yet know what he likes support [Ch16](../Chapters/Chapter_16.txt)'s practical naming of his death and [Ch17](../Chapters/Chapter_17.txt)'s replacement player at Hearts. His death is an interrupted life, not a lesson performed by a disposable mirror character.
2. **The competing rescue is legible.** [Ch7](../Chapters/Chapter_07.txt) shows the multi-cut, two-arm dagger rescue; [Ch15](../Chapters/Chapter_15.txt#L29-L44) recalls that established limitation and the injured shoulder when Milo calls from the fire. She actually lifts the shelf and brings him out. Neither Shade's danger nor the diary/cookbook loss is presented as his consent or a magical transaction.
3. **Consequences remain material.** The diary and cookbook pass into Camp's custody in Ch4/Ch12; Ch14–15 preserve their positions and burn them with ordinary fire. Ch16 and Ch17 retain missing originals, Stansbury's hand, and work directed by the people harmed. Payment and copying do not reverse loss.
4. **The moral distinction survives.** [Ch18](../Chapters/Chapter_18.txt#L330-L360)'s specific admission and “That's not what I did” distinguish Elizabeth's affirmative rescue choice from Pathwell's strategic silence. The confession is not presented as a bargain for forgiveness.

### Causality / state check

- **Entry state:** Ch13 ends with Shade in the middle of Hearts and his right hand pulled toward the threshold. Ch14 begins at the same unfinished hand; Pathwell builds the prohibited return frame while injured; Shade and Mama Baga explicitly refuse. Pathwell forces payment. The frame collapses toward its practitioner, the loop strikes the lantern, and the already-present ordinary fire meets stored charged records.
- **Competing emergencies:** A girl at the bucket line notices something near the woods. The cleanup creature then squeezes across the Camp boundary for fresh unclaimed possibility. Shade is caught while trying to alert people and join the fire response; one person pulls him. Both calls for help reach Elizabeth. Her dagger is still available but its known extraction requirements conflict with the trapped child and her injury. She goes inside.
- **Exit state:** Milo leaves the archive with one boot lost and a hurt ankle; Shade has been taken offscreen; the cleanup blob consumes fresh spillage and leaves. Stansbury separately salvages originals and receives the burn. The two donated books burn by ordinary flame. Ch16 inventories losses and Shade's named death. Ch17's repair montage leads to Ch18 approximately twenty-five days after the fire. No contrary restoration or invented ability is needed.
- **Uncertainty that must stay uncertainty:** The page does **not** demonstrate that any particular extra shout could save Shade. His death and Pathwell's refusal to help are distinct causal and moral claims. Pathwell's motive is withheld from Elizabeth's limited viewpoint until Ch18.

### Voice and tone notes

- **HOLDS — source voice:** Specific practical absurdity, bad coffee and the shopkeeper's accounting remain consistent with the author's pre-August first two chapters, alongside quick dialogue and human-scale stakes. Ch15 shifts appropriately toward shorter physical action; Ch16 lets ordinary inventory carry grief; Ch18's confession is direct because Elizabeth and the reader genuinely lack the information.
- **HOLDS, with a watch — distinct voices:** The archivist's taxonomy, Mama Baga's practical permission, Stansbury's procedural insistence, Milo's literal rules, Shade's restrained preferences and Pathwell's bureaucratic deflections have separate motives. Ch14 has many quick exchanges before the forced frame; they become less playful as a consent violation comes into focus. A vocal read should still test that the scene does not exhaust urgency before the refusal.
- **WATCH — repeated closures:** The Ch4/Ch15 “That was the choice” pair may be a purposeful thematic echo. Some Ch17/Ch18 aphoristic prose (“redemption”/“evidence”; ownership of the next step) is effective but close to naming the thesis. No broad cutting authorized without comparing what the scene loses.
- **WATCH — tonal reset in Ch18:** The geese jokes are socially rooted, not random joke spam. Their proximity to confession is precisely why cold readers should be asked whether they read reconciliation, deflection, or unresolved companionship.
