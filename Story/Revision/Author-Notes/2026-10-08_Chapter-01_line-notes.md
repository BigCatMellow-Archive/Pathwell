## The reviser's response (R26)

### The two big ideas: do both

- **She opens the door and sees the blob (¶23).** Do it. It gives her an act of her own in the first pages. The panel's prose reader stopped believing at "Do you have shoes?" because she only complies (Panel-2026-10-08, minority stopper). It also answers the readers who couldn't tell what the blob was.
  - Keep it a glimpse: she cracks the door, sees something wet, smells low tide, and he slams it shut over her arm.
  - Then rework the break-in reveal (¶79, "It was some sort of blob").
- **Her panic when he uses the cookbook (¶69).** Grabbing the book is her first real choice, and readers called her want unclear (panel, convergent). If the reason is plainly "that's Nana's book and he's destroying it", it reads as a choice.

### Agreed, with a suggestion

- **¶63, more fantastical magic.** Yes, but through her senses rather than adjectives: two or three short beats (the gold rising off the letters, the room's sound dropping away, the door going quiet for a second). That gives slow motion without explanation.
- **¶65, a better low tide.** Add to it, don't replace it. "Low tide, a long way from any sea" is the line readers quoted. The second mention makes it worse, with one specific thing.
- **¶73, more poetic.** Rather than poetic, name the feeling with one real thing from that kitchen.
- **¶33, the box that falls on its own.** "He shoved a box aside" or "He dropped a box".
- **¶39, more detail.** One physical detail: the chain jumping, paint cracking along the frame, or the smell under the door.
- **¶81, worded poorly.** Try "It rolled over the fallen gold and snuffed it out."
- **¶87, cut the clear bright note.** Yes; "The impact sang." alone is stronger.
- **¶125, blocking after "Huh."** Yes. He steps over the ooze or picks up the banner while she watches from the bathroom.
- **¶167, "His attention".** The paragraph before is hers, so "His" reads as hers. Start with "The man looked down the hall." Note that ¶127 and ¶165 both open the bathroom door.
- **¶185 (Ch2), "Will you slow down!?" has no speaker.** Put it in the same paragraph as "Elizabeth struggled to keep up…".
- **¶59, "Good… that's good."** Yes. It saves "Perfect." for the last line, and two panel readers flagged "Perfect." as a repeated button.
- **¶131 and ¶139, the cuts.** Fine. "I'm not going anywhere until you tell me…" still carries her refusal.

### Pushback

- **¶7, the moved paragraph.** "The party was still out here" now comes before she steps out into the room. Use "out there", or let the party paragraph be what she sees as she steps out.
- **¶9, the rewrite.** It loses the contractions the book's voice uses. Suggest: "But she'd never caught his name, and she'd thought he'd left with the rest of them."
- **Mechanics:**
  - "No, not at all" (¶15) needs the comma.
  - ¶77 is now a comma splice; keep "and".
  - In ¶117, "waived" should be "waved", as its own sentence: `…a mess." He waved it off.`
- **¶5, the cut.** The banner and "since she'd started" already say she's new, but the cut loses the light-switch joke. It's the author's call.
- **¶11, finding him on the floor with the boxes already knocked over.** Yes. It fits R27: the crash is the stack going over, and he keeps reading anyway.

### Not covered by the notes

The panel's top gap is why this man is in her flat at all, and "You really don't know?" never gets answered. Chapter 1 is where one hint could go, such as him glancing at the box he was drawn to.
