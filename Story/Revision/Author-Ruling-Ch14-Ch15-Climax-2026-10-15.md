## Authority reconciled

**The earlier August Q132 voluntary-invitation-to-cleanup lock is superseded by the author's October 15 choices and their implementation.** Shade has not consented to death and is not sacrificing himself to tidy Pathwell's problem. The blob removes him because he is unresolved waste, not because he earned destruction or controlled the mechanism.

**The earlier Q94–Q95 “It was my fault” left unexplained is superseded for the revised Chapter 18 by the author's choice of Version C.** The exact final “Are you ready?” / “No.” / “Perfect.” remains untouched.

The manuscript presently keeps Elizabeth's limited perspective: she cannot know that Pathwell privately decided to withhold aid until he later says so. The Camp response is active, and a nonmagical bystander tries unsuccessfully to pull Shade loose; the enchanted blade remains with Elizabeth, whose injured shoulder and the fire make two full rescues impracticable at once.

## Moral and narrative guardrails

- **Pathwell's omission is independently culpable.** He did not cause the creature's arrival deliberately, but he did create its stronger beacon through an impermissible working and then consciously declined to summon help while Shade struggled.
- **Not acting is not always restraint.** His later cookbook refusal is different from remaining silent as someone needs aid. The novel must not equate them.
- **Elizabeth chooses to rescue the child rather than the blob victim.** This is not consent from Shade or automatic forgiveness of Pathwell. Her eventual invitation belongs to her.
- **Shade's regret is not proof he deserves death.** He had genuinely begun to make humane choices at Camp and still wants to live.
- **Later confession is not a plea for absolution.** Pathwell seeks a witness to his shame and wonders what his inaction means about him, not a declaration that Elizabeth forgives him.
