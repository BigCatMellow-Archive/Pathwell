She found him, staring up at a street light.

“So then, where to?”

“I’m not going anywhere without getting some questions answered.”

“Sounds good Lizzy, What happened to the lo mien?”

“What?”

“Isnt that what your grandmother called you?”

“That's… that’s not an answer. And don't think for a second that I forgot you still have her cookbook.”

“Lizzy, I can answer all your questions, even the ones you’ll have next. We just need to swing by a bookstore first.”

“What kind of bookstore is going to be open this late?”

Pathwell turned, “The right kind.”

He shifted the diary and cookbook under one arm, pulling a folded piece of paper from his pocket with the other. Then pressing it flat against the concrete wall behind her.

Light bled through the creases, spiderwebbing along the wall.

A soft square, no bigger than the paper itself, glowing against the grey. He peeled back a layer and the square doubled. Another layer, and it doubled again. The light stretched past the edges of the paper until a doorway stood where the wall had been, warm and humming faintly.

"What is that?"

"Like I said, a bookstore. We really do need to work on those listening ears, Lizzy. Now, don't be shy. They have the most amazing coffee."

"How do I know this isn't some kind of trap?"

"You don't."

Pathwell stepped through and turned left.

She stood on the sidewalk, lo mein in hand, staring at a door that shouldn't exist. At rows of books stacked floor to ceiling. From somewhere inside, a loose page drifted out, spinning once in the air before settling at her feet. The handwriting was faded, but the ink smelled like lavender.

The smell reached her first. Old paper and dried leather, binding glue gone brittle, and beneath it all, dust that had been settling longer than she'd been alive.

She stepped through.

The street noise cut out like someone had closed a window. No cars, no sirens, no wind. Just the soft creak of shelves bearing more weight than they were built for, and a silence that felt like it had been here practicing for years.

Books. Floor to ceiling, wall to wall, stacked and leaning and wedged into every surface the room could offer. Not books — lives. Journals with cracked spines, letters bundled in ribbon, recipes on index cards spotted with oil. Thousands of them, maybe more, pressed together so tightly the shelves bowed under the effort.

"Lizzy, would you mind closing the door?"

She turned. Pathwell was already halfway down an aisle, moving like he knew the place by feel. She pulled the door shut and the frame dissolved into the wall behind her. Just shelves now. Shelves in every direction.

"Pathwell?"

He was gone.

"Pathwell!"

A shush from somewhere deep between the rows.

She moved forward because standing still felt worse. The first aisle was fine — dusty, cramped, unremarkable. But as she turned a corner, her chest tightened. Not fear exactly. Something heavier, pressing against the inside of her ribs like a fist she couldn't unclench. Her eyes burned. She blinked hard and kept walking.

It passed. Two steps later her skin flushed warm and her pulse dropped and she felt, for no reason at all, like she was about to cry over something she couldn't name. She stopped, reached a hand toward the nearest shelf. The spine of a journal was warm to the touch.

She pulled her hand back.

The next turn hit her like a door slamming. Heart punching against the base of her chest, her mind catching on a single thought and looping it — where am I, where am I, where am I — fingers going numb at the tips, lungs reaching for a breath that wouldn't come. She braced herself against a shelf and the books there seemed to lean toward her, pressing closer, the aisle narrowing with each exhale she couldn't quite finish.

Grey crept into the edges of her vision. The last thing she registered was the carpet against her knees.

When her eyes opened, they were heavy. She was in a chair. Her feet on an ottoman. A thin orange cat sat in front of her, green-eyed and waiting.

A cup of coffee swung into view. "Just in time." A plain white mug on a plain white saucer.

She took it by instinct and sipped. It was awful. Reused filters, dirty pots, dollar store beans. It tasted like late nights spent studying. Like the past.

The room came into focus. A large oaken counter, a man behind it, and dim lights that weren't trying hard enough.

The cat rubbed its head against her shins. She reached down and the skin on the back of her hand split open. Fast, surgical, a single claw dragged through the soft part between her thumb and wrist. She yanked back. Blood welled up in a thin line.

The cat's eyes gleamed as it licked its chops.

"He wanted his tribute," a voice said from behind the counter. “I would let him have it personally.”

Her eyes darted back and forth looking for the punchline.

"Hey, its okay.” Pathwell saw the panic, “You’re fine" He snapped a strip of paper from his pocket and laid it across the cut. Gripping it firmly, he whispered a few words.

The paper glowed warm, and faded into her hand. The wound and the pain was gone leaving behind a lingering feeling that reminded her of a boy she knew in middle school.

"Love note.” he smiled, “Cures most wounds."

"How about this. You finish that coffee there and I’ll answer any questions. Deal?"

She gulped the coffee down..

"I'll take that as a yes.”

The chair huffed as Pathwell flopped into it. “Right, so you asked where we are. The truth is we are in a bookstore. The location and name of which is commonly referred to as, ‘The Space Between’. As for Boots there, he did indeed sample the blood from your cut. He is the full time guardian, and part time blood god.”

"We pay him in mice." The shopkeeper spoke up from behind the counter, rubbing Boots behind his ears.

"A blood god?"

“Neither here nor there, what is important is where we are going."

"Where are we going?"

Pathwell clapped his hands, "Glad you asked."
