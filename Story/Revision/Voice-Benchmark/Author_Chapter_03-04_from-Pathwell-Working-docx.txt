She found him staring into a street light.

“And where exactly do you think you're going?”

“I just need to swing by a bookstore real quick.”

“I'm not going anywhere with you until you start to answer some questions.”

“Lizzy, I could answer all your questions, even the ones you'll have next. But we really do have places to be. ”

“I said I'm not going anywhere with you. Are you even listening to me?”

Her clenched brow softened, “What kind of bookstore is even open this late anyway?”

Pathwell turned, “The right kind.”

Elizabeth huffed, “Well don't think for a second that I forgot you still have my cookbook.”

“This old thing?” Pathwell shuffled the books in his hands and held it out for her. “It’s yours. Take it.”

Her hands moved quickly to take it back from him, running them over the cover checking for any damages.

“Did you remember the lo mien by any chance?” Pathwell asked. Shifting the diary from one hand to another, he pulled a folded piece of paper from his pocket, and pressed it flat against the concrete wall behind them.

“I didnt…” Light bled through the creases, spiderwebbing along the wall. “... I, uh, didn't get… I'm sorry, what's happening?”

A soft square, no bigger than the paper itself, glowing against the grey. He peeled back a layer and the square doubled. Another layer, and it doubled again. The light stretched past the edges of the paper until a doorway stood where the wall had been, steam hissing from its edges.

"What is that?"

"A bookstore, we really do need to work on those listening ears Lizzy.”

“And what if I don’t want to go?”

“Don't be shy, fate awaits. And it has the most amazing coffee."

"I don't believe in fate."

"Well, I say fate because, ‘a series of avoidable mistakes’, doesn't quite have the same ring to it."

Pathwell stepped through the doorway, “Now, let's not keep it waiting.”

She stood there on the sidewalk, staring, staring at a door that shouldn't exist. At rows of books filling the frame.

From inside, a loose page drifted out, flipping once in the air before settling at her feet. A smell followed it, reaching out to her. Old paper, and dried leather. Binding glue gone brittle.

And beneath it all, dust that had been settling longer than she'd been alive.

Cautiously, she stepped through. The street noise cut like someone had shut a window behind her. No cars, no sirens, no wind. Just the soft creak of shelves bearing the weight of centuries.

Floor to ceiling, wall to wall, shelf after packed shelf; journals with cracked spines, letters bundled in ribbon, recipes inked on index cards spotted with oil. All shoved into corners, leaning and wedged into every surface the room could offer.

"Lizzy, would you mind closing the door?" Pathwell’s head poked out from around the corner. She reached, but it swung itself closed and dissolved into the wall. She stepped back as the shelves collapsed in on themselves where the door had been.

She turned back around and he was gone. "Pathwell?"

A shush from somewhere deep between the rows.

Around her it was dusty, cramped, and unremarkable. Standing there though, the air felt heavy in her lungs as shallow breaths caught up with her, each cutting off the last. A bubble inside her chest, pushing out unable to burst. “What's happening? Whats… whats happening to me?” Her head snapped around, her voice cracking, “Pathwell!”

Nothing.

“Am I dying? Oh my god I'm going to die. I’m going to die here. I need to go. I need to get out of here. How do I get out? I'm trapped. I'm trapped and I'm going to die.” Her body fell forwards, her feet doing their best to catch her as she stumbled into the aisle.

Then as suddenly as it came, it passed.

Her shoulders lay flat against the shelf as she found her breath again. Slowly at first, then steady with each draw. A memory lingered, trapped in a place she’d never been. She had to find Pathwell, she had to find an exit.

Two steps later her skin flushed, her pulse dropped.  A flash of a pet she never had, a love she never felt, and a parent she never lost. The tears formed on their own, ready to pour out over something or someone she couldn't name.

She stopped, and reached a hand towards the nearest shelf. Brushing the cracked spine of a journal, it felt… alive. Warm to the touch, and in just a moment, it felt impossible. Her fingers lingered, searching.

The next turn hit her like a door slamming. Heart punching against the base of her chest, her mind catching on a single thought and looping it. Her fingers went numb at the tips, lungs reaching for a breath that wouldn't come. She braced herself against a shelf and the books towered over her, pressing closer, the aisle narrowing with each exhale that she couldn't quite finish.

Grey crept into the edges of her vision. Slowly but completely her mind went blank, her hands and knees catching against the coarse carpet.

[when she wakes up now, the books should be on the counter]

Her eyes strained to open. Slowly as the room came into focus through dim lights, she saw waiting in front of her, a thin orange cat. A cup swung into view, accompanied by Pathwell, "Just in time."

It was a plain white mug on a plain white saucer, sloshing a deep earthy liquid over the edges. The dark, roasted, nutty smell drew her in, and she took the offering quickly.

It was awful.

Dollar store beans forced through reused filters, and left to soak in a dirty pot. The worst part was, it tasted like a memory. It tasted like late nights spent studying.

It tasted like the past. And warmed her like nostalgia only could.

Across from her was a large oaken counter, and a man behind it. A large stack of books to his right.

The cat rubbed its head against her legs asking for attention. To which she obliged, reaching down to scratch behind its ears. A whip crack of pain, and the skin on her palm split open. Surgically, a single claw dragged through the soft part between her thumb and wrist. She yanked back but blood welled up in a thin line.

The cat's eyes gleamed as it licked its chops.

"He wants his tribute," a voice spoke up across from her, a large oaken counter, and a gentle round rimmed glassed man behind it. A large stack of books to his right. “I would let him have it personally.”

[Also need to do a better job of describing the pain, and fear she feels]

Her eyes darted back and forth looking for the punchline.

Pathwell saw the panic, "Hey, it’s okay, you’re fine" He snapped a strip of paper from his pocket and laid it across the cut. Gripping it firmly, he whispered a few words.

The paper glowed warm, and faded into her hand. The wound and the pain was gone leaving behind a lingering feeling that reminded her of a boy she knew in middle school.

He smiled, "Love note. Cures most wounds."

He rubbed his hand over the line left over, "Unfortunately there's not much I can do about scars. How about this, you finish that coffee there and I’ll answer any questions while you do. Deal?"

She gulped the coffee down..

"I'll take that as a yes.”

The chair huffed as Pathwell flopped into it. “Right, so you asked where we are. The truth is, we are in a bookstore. The location and name are one in the same, ‘The Space Between’. As for Boots there, he did indeed sample the blood from your cut. Afterall he is the full time guardian here, and a part time blood god.”

"We pay him in mice." The shopkeeper spoke up from behind the counter, rubbing Boots behind his ears.

"A blood god?"

“Neither here nor there, what is important is where we are going."

"Where are we going?"

Pathwell clapped his hands, "Glad you asked."

The keeper smiled and went back to his ledger, making ticks, and carrying ones.

"So what do we owe?"

Without looking up, the shopkeeper sighed, "You already owe quite a bit."

"Im good for it"

"...All bills come due eventually, Mr. Pathwell."

"What do I owe for today?"

Pulling the stack closer, he looked it over. Journals, a few letters, sheets of poetry, and a couple of sheets of music. "Some of these are very old Mr. Pathwell. They won't come cheap." The register dinged and clacked, numbers jumping. "We could settle today, past bills and all for… a Rai Stone"

Scoffing, "Oh come on be serious. A Rai Stone?"

"Im afraid I am" The keeper removed his glasses, "and quite frankly Mr. Pathwell, you already owe more than I allow most."

"Surely we can barter here."

"Outside of a significant Rai Stone, I could do it for, say thirty Lydian Coins."

"Okay, now youre just messing with me."

The keeper was unmoved.

"Arent you?"

"Unless you have something else to barter with…"

"Ive got potential to sell. Lots."

"You did."

Pathwell chuckled nervously.

“Are you sure?”

“Can we do this before I change my mind?”

The keeper turned to the wall behind him, opening a panel that had blended into the soft brown paneling. Sliding it open revealing a cast iron cube. He pulled a short sheet from his sleeve, and placed it over the door. His hands moved over it, turning it light blue. His fingers danced, a thunk, a clank, and shifting gears.

A door popped open from nothing.

Inside sat a leather bound tome, edges gilded in silver. A branded dot in the center, surrounded by concentric rings and lattice arcs. Thick, heavy, and dense, with thin pages.

He lifted it with both hands and placed it on the counter with reverence.

"Pathwell Shade, are you sure this cost is worth what you must?"

"I am."

"Then say the words"

Pathwell stepped to the book, placing his left hand on the cover, his right across his chest. "I understand the cost of my needs."

The silver pages glowed a beautiful blue, like a distant sky. "I give what may be, to protect what will"

The pages burned bright. Roots of light shot from the book, grasping Pathwells forearm, wrapped tight, intertwined, and wild.

[I need to establish how much he is taking]

"Perdat quod esse poterat."

They pulsed, turning violent blue. New roots split off, snaking up his arm, digging their way in. Splintering thinner, before burrowing, twisting, wriggling. Taking hold.

From the center of the book, fibrous tendrils wove upward. Gnarled and knotted. The tree grew to Pathwell's chest before splitting and branching out. Some growing mangled, withered, dead. The tree bloomed in patches, sick and breathless. Taking its full shape, sitting sickly and worn.

The roots continued to push deeper into his arm. The pain was alive now, front and center in his mind, blocking all other thoughts.

"You don't have much left to give Pathwell Shade."

"Take what you will, and spare me the lesson."

Orange flames took hold of the branches, spreading from around the tree, burning bright against the light blue leaves. The roots burned a frightful orange, searing the flesh they enveloped.

Pathwell screamed in a million voices, voices lost to time, burning out to become memories never made.Each voice fell quiet, leaving a harmony of extinguished cries.

The fire tempered out, taking all but a few living branches at the core, leaving behind only scorched fibers reaching out. Pathwell dropped to his knees, arm held up but the burrowed roots.

"Thank you Mr. Pathwell. Your bill has been paid in full. Will that be paper or plastic?"
