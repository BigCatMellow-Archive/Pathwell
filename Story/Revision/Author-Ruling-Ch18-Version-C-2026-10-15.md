# Author ruling — Chapter 18 ending structure: Version C

**Date:** 2026-10-15

**Author response:** “Lets go C then” — selecting [EXP-0004C](Emergence/experiments/EXP-0004C-Ch18-Confession-Then-Existing-Hour-Prose-Test-2026-10-15.md) after A/B/C comparisons.

## Author-selected Chapter 18 architecture

1. **Preserve** Pathwell's existing, entirely silent decision to put the Merritt family cookbook back. Keep the shopkeeper's reaction rather than forcing a confessional speech at that exact moment.
2. **Later in the same bookshop visit**, Pathwell **specifically confesses** that he witnessed Shade still struggling and calling for help, understood that Shade's removal by the blob would conveniently resolve his problem, and consciously **did not call for aid**. He tells Elizabeth because of shame and uncertainty about who he is, **not** to seek absolution or to inform her as a self-congratulatory ethical gesture. The relevant prior author decisions are [Q60–61](Rung-Q60-Q61-Pathwell-Choice-2026-10-15.md), [Q62](Rung-Q62-Late-Confession-2026-10-15.md), and [Q63–68](Rung-Q64-Elizabeth-Response-2026-10-15.md).
3. Elizabeth says she also heard Shade's call, and then heard Milo inside the burning archive and chose to save the child. Pathwell **gently distinguishes** her rescue of Milo from his own deliberate refusal to help Shade. No sermon or demand for forgiveness.
4. Elizabeth gets **the already-existing hour of browsing AFTER the confession**. Keep ordinary discoveries and the tactile bookshop experience; **do not add a new scene solely to reach a length or process guilt by explanation**.
5. Elizabeth still **freely chooses the geese destination and invites Pathwell**. She does not explicitly announce forgiveness or reassure him that she wants him in her life; the invitation itself says that.
6. **Preserve the exact final exchange**: “Are you ready?” / “No.” / “Perfect.”

**Selection applies to structure and character turns, not to every sentence of the experimental passage.** The proof-stage draft has identified editorial artifacts (an extra joke about bad coffee, “Nothing made her choose”) and must get a normal voice and independent continuity review. The author did not order those sentences to be retained.

## Formal ruling reconciliation

The older August Q94–Q95 lock specified that “It was my fault” remains unexplained with no response. **The author's newer, explicit selection of Version C takes precedence for the *planned rewritten ending***: the confession is now meant to be specific and Elizabeth is meant to respond. That older wording is therefore **superseded in intent for the revised scene**, while remaining an accurate statement of the currently published Chapter 18 **until a coordinated implementation is approved**. Preserve the historical decision record; do not silently erase it.

R9 and prior Q102's exact **“Perfect.”** ending remain in force. Q67–68 affirm Elizabeth's unchanged invitation.

## Dependency / adoption gate

**Do not apply C to Chapter 18 in isolation.** The existing Chapter 15 still depicts Shade voluntarily approaching the creature *after* Milo's rescue, not an unexpected grip around his shin as Milo is trapped, Shade pleading for help, Elizabeth choosing the fire, and Pathwell quietly withholding an available call for aid. Without a coordinated revision of the Chapter 14–15 scene and its Chapter 16–17 aftermath, the proposed Chapter 18 confession would claim events that never happened.

Before production edit:
- Lock physically credible rescue blocking, creature arrival and bystander response; show what Pathwell could do but elected not to do, without mind-reading from Elizabeth's viewpoint.
- Verify Milo is the trapped child, Elizabeth has the enchanted dagger but cannot perform two prolonged extractions, and Shade **continues trying to live**.
- Coordinate Chapters 14–18, especially the old voluntary Shade death, Pathwell's share of responsibility in Ch16 restitution, and the changed meaning of Elizabeth inviting him.
- Run voice / critical reader pass on final Chapter 18. Avoid “elapsed time = instant absolution.”
