# R66 working decisions (reviser's drafts for bt 7274 to correct)

Every round 2 guide said to do the paperwork before the prose: decide the facts the book hasn't decided yet, then rewrite. Under [R66](../Decisions.md#the-authors-rulings-for-this-revision), the reviser drafted the facts below and applied them in the chapters. **Every item is overrulable.** If bt 7274 changes one, the dependent lines are listed so they can be found.

## 1. Causal spine

### Why Pathwell was in Elizabeth's apartment (Ch1)
- **Canon already says it** (world bible, blobs §Chapter 1): he was scavenging charged records for Camp. Shade followed him through the draw. The blob followed the overlapping Pathwell/Shade signature. Elizabeth was never the reason.
- **On the page, new:** he went to her party because her boxes were *loud*. Old handwriting that has been kept a long time carries charge, and some people can hear it across a room. Nana's cookbook was the loudest thing there. Elizabeth asks him in Ch2 why he was at her party (a Ch2 diff, shown before commit). The deeper layer, "I was shopping", comes in his Ch16 apology list.

### The prune (Ch8 → Ch10)
- **The client:** Ada Kell, an old woman, at her kitchen table with a cup of tea she didn't drink. (A and K are both open letters in the Registry.) Her daughter **Tess** left at nineteen and hasn't written in three years.
- **What Ada asked for:** to know Tess was alive. If possible, to have her told that her mother wasn't angry.
- **What Pathwell did:**
  - Finding Tess was easy. Telling her was easy too, and she didn't want to come home.
  - He decided Ada shouldn't have to wait years for a daughter who might never choose to come. He built a working to bring Tess home, *a pull toward home*, and he paid with his own future so that it would be certain and quick.
  - He asked neither of them.
  - This is consistent with the bible: the working accepts future possibility as payment, and the prune itself doesn't edit reality.
- **What happened:**
  - Tess got on a bus the next morning without knowing why.
  - She stayed three days and left again, and this time she didn't write either. Ada got her daughter for three days, and then lost the one thing she had left, which was hoping Tess would choose to come.
  - The payment didn't settle cleanly. The leftover was a pull with nowhere to go.
- **The rhyme this creates:** Shade's draw *is* that working's shape, a compulsion toward someone. Shade is the pull Pathwell built and paid for, given a body, and it pulls toward the man who made it. This answers the council's question about how Shade relates to the prune without explaining how he became a person (that stays deliberately unknown, per the bible).
- **Shade's fragment** (Ch9): "A kitchen table. Somebody's hands around a cup." These are Ada's hands.
- **The release is layered** (guide 28):
  - **Ch8 (ditch):** one fact. He paid for a job with his future, most of it went where he sent it, and the rest he never found. His hand turns toward the road just before Shade arrives.
  - **Ch10 (van):** who, the kitchen, the choice, the cost to Ada and Tess.
  - **Kept for Ch10:** the moral line. "They did not ask me to prune. I did not ask whether they wanted me to."

### The harm at Camp before the frame (Ch13 → Ch14)
- The bucket girl is already on the page. She's the one who is ignored at the fire ("There's something there"). **In Ch13, during the onion scene, she goes down to the stream at the tree line and comes back without her bucket. Her hand is grey to the wrist, it's numb, and she smells of low tide.**
  - It wasn't after her. She was between the cleanup and what it was following.
  - Mama Baga and the healer handle it. The feeling comes back by evening.
  - Shade knows why it came, and so does the reader.
- **Ch14:** Pathwell's case for the frame now has her in it. Mama Baga has to own the risk out loud. One ally (the archivist) visibly half-agrees and still won't help. The chorus of bare "no"s is cut down.
- This stays consistent with R45 and R46. Her restrained choice and Shade's surprise capture are unchanged.

### Elizabeth's own life (the reviser's draft facts)
- **The want she gave up:** a job offer in another city (Chicago), which she got and didn't take, and never told Nana about. It's on a diary page with Thumper's paw print on it.
- **Her mother:** alive, far away, and calls. She once asked whether Elizabeth was "still doing the Nana thing", as if it were a hobby.
- **One override of Nana:** she ground the evening pill into applesauce and said it was cinnamon. Nana said "You're a bad liar, Lizzy" and ate it anyway, and Elizabeth felt relieved that she'd won. This surfaces in **Ch12**, when the healer asks "Can I touch it?" and Elizabeth notices she never asked. The recognition comes in **Ch18**: her own "You could stay here" from Ch13 comes back to her.
- **Nana's first name stays unprinted** (Registry: "spoken in Ch4 but never printed"). Several readers asked for it. It's bt 7274's call, so it's not changed here.

## 2. Voice sheets (Scrubs-shaped)

These use the [Scrubs note](../Author-Notes/2026-10-27_Irreverence-With-Heart-Scrubs.md): need → habitual response → comic complication → emotional consequence. Each character is funny *from their own need*. The shared deadpan ("That is not the same thing", the one-word verdict) goes to its owner and is cut from everyone else.

| Who | Needs | Habit (the joke) | When it hurts | Owns | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pathwell** | to be the one who fixes it | excess: lists, flourishes, answering before he's asked | the fix is the harm | "Lizzy", the grand catalogue, "Cruel." | silence, until he learns it |
| **Elizabeth** | to be useful, and not be explained around | practical worry lists; politeness turned on magic; blunt once she's earned it | she manages people "for their own good" too | "Define ___", "Important distinction." | pronouncements |
| **Shade** | his feelings to be his own | exact nouns, literal corrections, sincere and accidentally funny | precision can't tell him what he wants | "Pockets.", "Accurate." | performing |
| **Stansbury** | things kept accounted for | over-precision, procedure, grievance about property | a scar can't be inventoried | "Noted.", "It's a series." | flourishes; redefining words |
| **Mama Baga** | people to answer for themselves | one-word orders, one question, then waiting | her silence costs her too | "That is not a posture." | comebacks; explaining |
| **The archivist** | things recorded properly | categories, filing as a worldview | some things don't fit a category (LIKED POCKETS) | "Specificity is expensive." | comforting directly |
| **The healer** | consent and plain truth | measurements, conditions ("if the fingers numb") | she can't fix what isn't a body | "Can I touch it?" | joking first |
| **Milo** | to win, and to matter | kid legalism, literalness | a child's pun lands on grief | "You were going to." | irony |
| **The shopkeeper** | the accounts settled | the ledger, distaste for novelty | — | "Accounting." | sentiment |

**House rules for the voice pass:**
1. **Quiet window.** For about a page after a loss or serious injury, a joke may appear only if it *fails* on the page.
2. **Toppers.** Cut the third beat of any three-beat exchange unless it reveals the speaker.
3. **The narrator** gets at most one aside a chapter outside dialogue, and none inside grief.
4. **No more than a third of scene endings** may be quips.
5. **Gaze beats:** at most one "looked" per 300 words. Purposeful blocking is kept.
