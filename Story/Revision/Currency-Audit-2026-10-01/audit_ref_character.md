**Newest by date: Q140 (08-27).** It is a POV lock, not a lock on the ending's content, and it reads like the 08-22 §6 summary carried forward. The reasons to treat it as unresolved rather than as overriding Q96:
- Q96's text says outright that it supersedes the "visibly prove" language.
- Q140 gives the passage as a POV exception, not as a change to the Q96 ruling.
- Q140's own test is that exceptions apply only where "Elizabeth cannot legitimately observe it", while Q103 says Elizabeth may happen to see him.
- The September records (CURRENT_AUTHOR_INTENT §12-13, DISAGREEMENT_REGISTER resolved decision 2) and Decisions.md (the Q96 row: "asks the price, offers 'Future.', shows the branches..., then lifts his hand") side with Q96. They are weaker sources.
- Decisions.md never mentions Q140's coda wording, and cites Q140 only for POV.

Positions in the other documents:
- **08-22 §6:** begin the prune, proof visible, stop.
- **WC contract (matrix lines 779, 797, 880 area):** hedges. "if he begins familiar pruning preparation, he stops cleanly before release because he chooses to stop, not because the power fails." Also "Pathwell still capable of pruning; clean stop before release; no charged receipt requirement."
- **Plan row 18 and the Promise-Ledger:** follow Q96 (silent, no price, no branches, no test), with no note that Q140 says otherwise.
- **Manuscript Ch18 (lines ~440-640):** stages the price ("How much?"), "Future.", the ledger, "Still capable of division. Still his.", then he lifts his hand and nothing is spent. It follows neither Q96 nor Q140 cleanly.

Verdict: a conflict between decisions that needs the author. The question for the author: "On the page, does Pathwell begin the prune and show it works, or does he only consider it and put the book back?" Q96 and Q103 say the second. Q140's wording says the first. Until then, no page should state either as settled.
