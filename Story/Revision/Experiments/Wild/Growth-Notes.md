# Growth notes: the Wild Garden

## Status

**Experiment notes.** These go with [the Wild Garden](README.md). It was written without opening `Story/Chapters/` or `Experiments/Garden/`, and the old texts were compared only by script, after it was written. Nothing here is canon.

## What it grew from

- **Soil:** the goals in the README. The book is Sunday Morning: interesting places, people you like, a small problem with something big behind it. Elizabeth matters without power. Pathwell spends himself and calls it help. Shade is a person.
- **Water:** each chapter grew from the objects and habits earlier chapters left behind. Nothing was planned more than a chapter or two ahead.
- **Pruning:** when a scene wanted a big, loud move, the question was whether a smaller move did more. That question decided most of the second half.

## The roots that took over

These weren't planned. They grew, and then they held the book up.

1. **"Sorry" is Elizabeth's habit.** It's funny in one light and it hurts in another. She says it to banisters, to ducks, to a poster. Her first "No" comes in Ch3. Shade notices she stopped saying sorry (Ch9). By Ch17 she's counting: eleven, then four, then two that she meant. Mrs. Okafor has the same habit, and Elizabeth doesn't mind it in her at all.
2. **Pathwell had never said sorry** in anything Shade remembers. He said it first to her, in Ch1. When he wins at dominoes he says sorry, because "it is the only one he has practiced."
3. **Asking.** This wasn't planned either. It grew from the diary (he read it without asking) and the card in the archive ("ASK BEFORE USING"). After that it became the spine of the book: "Ask me before anyone reads it." "Ask the healer." "Ask him." "Will you?" "Are you asking?" / "I'm asking."
4. **Shade's own things.** Everything in his head was Pathwell's first. His own things are watching the dryer go round, a library card in his own capitals, potato peel in one curl, and losing at dominoes.
5. **Kevin's bear.** It started as a crayon drawing taped to the library door in Ch5. It ended up carrying the book's restitution question.

## New ideas that might feel right by comparison

These are offered as candidates to judge, not recommendations.

- **Asking as the spine of the book.** The archive card ASK BEFORE USING keeps coming back: "Can I?" / "No." / "Right." It does the controlling idea without anyone stating it.
- **The confession is that he picked her because she carries things.** It's a smaller, sharper sin than marking her door, and it's tied directly to her caregiving years.
- **Shade survives by losing what was borrowed.** He is five weeks old, and Mama Baga's "I don't know you yet" pays off.
- **Restitution set by the person wronged.** It's small and public: a bad bear on the door marked BY PATHWELL.
- **Pathwell's growth shown as stopping:** "All right." He puts his empty hand on his knee where everyone can see it.
- **Pathwell can't play anything he might lose,** then loses at dominoes every Thursday.
- **"Sorry" as Elizabeth's habit,** with Mrs. Okafor as a mirror she's gentle with.
- **The crossword clue** "Seven letters. Something you can't take back," answered several ways and never settled.
- **The torn page** comes back gray, and she rewrites it in her own hand rather than Nana's.
- **The healer's "Is there anything you want first?"** and "No one asks that."

## What it cost

- The fire and Stansbury's big burn are gone. His hand is burned instead, by his own baton.
- Shade's death is gone. The second half is gentler, and the stakes are in what's lost rather than who.
- There's no museum, no Marlow, no Ada. The archive and the library carry that weight.
- The Sunday Morning shortness drifted in places: Ch3 is 3.3k words.
