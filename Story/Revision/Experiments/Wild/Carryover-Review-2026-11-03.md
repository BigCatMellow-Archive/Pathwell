# Wild Garden: what to carry over from the older Pathwell

**Editorial comparison — November 3, 2026. Not an author ruling, approved candidate, blind read, or chapter revision.** The canonical book is [Story/Chapters](../../../Chapters/). The 18 chapters in this [Wild Garden folder](README.md) are a separate alternate edition. Compared at repository commit `cfe45e8`; see [Growth Notes](Growth-Notes.md) for its origin and departures.

## Conclusion and scope

**Editorial lean:** Develop Wild Garden as the preferred *candidate*, because Elizabeth's choices, the specificity of Shade's own life, the everyday humor, the recurring objects, and the recipe ending form a more coherent, intimate book. The older manuscript made the cost of Pathwell's decisions more difficult to escape. **Transfer that moral pressure, not the old catastrophes.** No prose should be silently transplanted: the earlier lines are evidence of dramatic function, not automatic canon for this edition.

## C1. Keep Elizabeth separate from his redemption

**Earlier:** [canonical Chapter 16](../../../Chapters/Chapter_16.txt), after Shade's death. Elizabeth says, "I need you not to make me responsible for whether you become a better person." She also refuses to turn his guilt into something useful. The purpose is not simply to rebuke him: it protects her from being assigned another person to carry.

**Now:** [Wild Chapter 18](Chapter_18.txt). He admits reading her diary and deliberately selecting her as someone who would carry his problems. She sends him to the landing, stays angry for about fifteen minutes, then invites him back and says she would have helped if he had asked.

**Why:** The confession is more directly connected to Elizabeth's defining habit than the old version's confession. Without residue, the invitation can look like absolution that Pathwell earned by learning a lesson.

**Target:** Allow Elizabeth to choose friendship and the next adventure *without making her his judge, therapist or proof of reform*. A lived boundary or continuing hesitation fits Wild's voice better than transferring the old speech verbatim. Keep the recipe ending and voluntary invitation. **Test:** the reader can say both "she has not forgotten what he did" and "she genuinely wants him there."

## C2. Respect the 'no' already given

**Earlier:** [canonical Chapter 14](../../../Chapters/Chapter_14.txt). Shade says no to rejoining; Pathwell's attempt to override him becomes the decisive failure.

**Now:** [Wild Chapter 14](Chapter_14.txt). Shade has *already* refused Pathwell's proposal to take him back. Mama Baga says stop. Elizabeth then instructs Pathwell to "Ask him," prompting a second request and a second refusal.

**Why:** If the book's central ethic is that consent matters, needing to repeat a refusal is not an improvement in Pathwell's behavior.

**Target:** Let Elizabeth make him hear the *first* answer. Retain his visible attempt to assemble justifications, his decision not to act, "All right," and the empty hand on his knee. The improvement is restraint, not a better-worded repeat request. **Test:** Shade's first no stands and his personhood is not up for renewed negotiation. **Do not restore forced rejoining.**

## C3. Let Wren's debt remain unresolved

**Earlier:** [canonical Chapter 16](../../../Chapters/Chapter_16.txt) distinguishes feasible restitution from the impossible promise to restore everything lost. Payment cannot substitute for accountability.

**Now:** [Wild Chapter 10](Chapter_10.txt) gives Wren a voice through her letter. She asked for assistance writing to the lighthouse board; Pathwell acted on the official instead. She eventually died. In [Wild Chapter 16](Chapter_16.txt), Shade says Pathwell still "just owe[s] it" even though Wren is gone, but Dot reports this indirectly. The manipulation resurfaces in [Chapter 18](Chapter_18.txt).

**Why:** The new book already offers a hopeful and precise repair with Kevin. Wren's story shows a debt for which no satisfied recipient, replacement object or completed task is available.

**Target:** Preserve Shade's recognition, and test whether the weight remains present after the warmth of the dominoes and the ending. This need not be another scene, confession, death, new magical rule or penance speech; a restrained action may carry it. **Test:** the story distinguishes "I can repair this" from "I cannot make what I did unhappen."

## C4. The duck pond must pass the same moral test

**Earlier:** [canonical Chapter 8](../../../Chapters/Chapter_08.txt) describes Elizabeth steering into a ditch while avoiding more dangerous collision paths. It remains hazardous but is presented as a choice under immediate physical danger.

**Now:** [Wild Chapter 8](Chapter_08.txt) has her deliberately drive Stansbury's library van off the road into the pond to end a conversation in which her passengers withhold information. It is funny and provides an original arrival for Shade, but it involves passengers and public property.

**Why:** If Elizabeth claims her autonomy through a unilateral dangerous choice, she risks repeating the problem the book finds in Pathwell.

**Candidate A — editorial lean:** She pulls over safely, refuses to drive, and demands information. Keep Shade feeding ducks at the pond. This strengthens her right to withdraw cooperation. **Cost:** change the reason for the soaked clothing and laundrette in Ch9, the wet library books, Shade's duck-pond memories and subsequent continuity.

**Candidate B:** Keep the bizarre pond scene but make its safety and property consequences credible. **Cost:** the underlying ethical conflict remains, even when acknowledged.

**Disposition:** This is a structural/taste choice for the author, not a correction to make automatically. Protect the comedy and Shade's introduction whichever way it goes.

## C5. Protect what the newer version already does better

- [Wild Ch11](Chapter_11.txt) and [Ch16](Chapter_16.txt): Kevin's destroyed drawing is not replaceable. Kevin sets his own small, public terms; Pathwell draws a *different* bear and holds the book for Stansbury. This is accountability made legible, not an abstract debate.
- [Wild Ch15](Chapter_15.txt): Shade *chooses* the cut, loses the past inherited from Pathwell, and keeps what he has lived himself. The present-tense passage after the cut makes his independence experiential.
- [Wild Ch17](Chapter_17.txt) and [Ch18](Chapter_18.txt): Elizabeth reconstructs Nana's sugar-cookie recipe and writes her own version onto the gray page. Preserve the crossed-out egg, other people's preferences, and "P. is the horse."

**Do not import** the archive fire, museum catastrophe, lost cookbook/diary, forced rejoining, Shade's death, extra antagonist machinery or an 80,000-word target simply to increase dramatic scale. The new edition's objects and relationships carry the stakes.

## Secondary checks (not settled recommendations)

- **Stansbury and the children's art — Wild [Ch5](Chapter_05.txt) / [Ch16](Chapter_16.txt):** He takes something from the children's drawings. Leaving Kevin's gold star alone is a telling improvement; decide whether that adequately addresses the earlier extraction without consent.
- **The shopkeeper — Wild [Ch3](Chapter_03.txt) / [Ch18](Chapter_18.txt):** Things others actually requested cost nothing, a rule Pathwell discovers after decades of spending himself. Check whether the shopkeeper's silence looks unreasonably cruel. Do not invent a rule/backstory without the author.
- **Timeline:** Verify the work-at-nine remark, Saturday story-hour signs, the Sunday/Monday transition, and bowling-to-laundrette timing before labeling any mismatch a fact.
- **Length:** R42 says the book's natural length wins. No padding merely for industry word count.
