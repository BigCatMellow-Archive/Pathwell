# Reader questionnaire: the whole book

## Status

**Active. A questionnaire for a reader going through all eighteen chapters in order.** It asks what makes sense, which beats land, what needs tuning, and which questions the book leaves open. It is written so the answers can be compared with the author's goals for the story without telling the reader what those goals are. The goals and what each question is listening for are kept in a [separate key](Reader-Questionnaire-Goals.md), which the reader must not open until their answers are written. Created 2026-10-06 at the author's request.

How to use it: give a reader this page and the chapters (`Story/Chapters/Chapter_01.txt` to `Chapter_18.txt`). Nothing else. Afterwards, compare their answers with the key.

---

## For the reader

You're reading a novel from start to finish, the way a person picks up a book. Nobody needs you to like it. What helps is an honest account of what happened in your head while you read.

**Ground rules:**

- Read Chapters 1 to 18 in order, at reading speed. Don't skip ahead and don't go back to check something unless you'd naturally flip back in a paper book (if you do, say so).
- Read only the chapters. Don't open notes, outlines, records or anything else in the repository, including the answer key next to this page. If you've seen any of it, say what.
- Answer in your own words. Point to the moment: chapter number and a short quote.
- "I don't know", "I didn't notice" and "I didn't care" are useful answers. A guess dressed as an answer isn't. Don't try to work out what the author wanted.
- If something bored you, confused you, or pulled you out, say so plainly, even if it's a scene you think is meant to be important.
- Keep the two parts separate: write the running log as you go, and don't revise it after you've finished the book. Answer the end questions only after the last page.

---

## Part 1: the running log (after every chapter)

After each chapter, before you start the next, write a short entry. A few lines is enough; more when something big happened.

1. **What happened**, in a sentence or two, as you'd tell a friend.
2. **The moment that hit hardest**, and what it did to you (laughed, winced, leaned in, got angry, felt nothing when you expected to feel something).
3. **Anything that confused you** or made you stop and reread. What did you have to guess?
4. **What you want to know now.** The questions you're carrying into the next chapter.
5. **What you think will happen**, if you have a guess.
6. **Where your attention dipped**, if it did, and roughly where.
7. **Who you're with**: which character you're most interested in right now, and why.

When a question from an earlier entry gets answered, note it ("Ch4 answered my Ch2 question about…"). When one never does, you'll list it at the end.

## Part 2: after the whole book

Answer every question. Each one is open on purpose; there's no right answer and no yes-or-no. Where a question asks for moments, give chapter numbers and a short quote.

### The book as a whole

1. Tell someone what this book is about in three or four sentences, without listing the plot. What is it *about*?
2. If the book is making an argument about people, what is it? Where in the story did you feel it most?
3. What does the title mean to you now that you've finished?
4. Which part of the book would you tell a friend about first?

### Elizabeth

5. Describe Elizabeth at the start of Chapter 1 and at the end of Chapter 18. What's different about her, and what's the same?
6. List the moments where Elizabeth *decides* something, as opposed to things happening to her. For each one, say what she chose, what it cost her, and whether it felt like her own choice or a door someone else opened.
7. Which of her choices did you believe most? Which felt like the story needed her to make it, rather than her wanting to?
8. Were there stretches where she felt like a passenger in her own story? Where, and did the book seem to know it?
9. Did you ever feel she lost ground, went backwards, or gave in? Where, and did it feel true to her?
10. What does Elizabeth give up over the course of the book? What, if anything, does she get?
11. What do the diary and Nana's cookbook mean to her, and what did you feel when each one was lost?
12. In the last chapter, who is leading, and how could you tell?

### Pathwell

13. What does Pathwell want, and what's wrong with how he goes about getting it? Put it in your own words.
14. Does he change? If so, when did you first believe it, and what made you believe it? If not, what would it have taken?
15. Which of his choices did you find hardest to forgive, and why?
16. In the last chapter he finds another family's cookbook in the shop. What happens in that moment, what was going on inside him, and how did you know?
17. Near the end he says "It was my fault." What is he talking about? Who or what came to mind first?
18. By the end, how do you feel about him? Did the book push you toward that feeling, or let you get there yourself?

### Shade

19. Who is Shade? How would you describe him to someone who hasn't read the book?
20. Is Shade a person? What in the book made you think so, or not?
21. Why does Shade walk toward the creature at the end of Chapter 15? Say it in your own words. Was it bravery, a mistake, guilt, something he was forced into, or something else?
22. What did his death cost, and who paid for it?
23. Who is responsible for what happened to Shade? Does the book agree with you?

### The other people

24. What does Stansbury want, and what changes between him and his brother?
25. Who is Mama Baga, and what is she to the story?
26. What does Milo add? Would you miss him if he were cut?
27. What is Camp Cunnan to Elizabeth by the end? How did it get there?

### Earned, or not

28. Which payoffs felt earned, where something set up early came back and landed? Name the setup and the payoff.
29. Which moments felt unearned or too convenient: a problem solved too easily, a change that happened because the plot needed it, a feeling you were told to have?
30. What was set up and never paid off? Objects, promises, threats, questions.
31. Which consequences stuck, and which got undone or forgotten?
32. Was there anything the book tried to make you feel that you didn't?

### The climax (Chapters 14 and 15)

33. Whose climax is it? Whose choices drive what happens in the fire?
34. What does Elizabeth do during the confrontation and the fire, and does any of it change how things turn out? If she had done nothing, what would have been different?
35. Did the failure of Pathwell's working make sense to you? Say why it failed, as you understood it.
36. Did anything in these chapters confuse you about who was where, who said what, or what happened physically?

### The world

37. Explain how the magic works, as you understood it: what pruning is, what it costs, and what the charged books and records do. What was unclear?
38. Did the story ever break its own rules? Where?
39. What are the Space Between, Camp Cunnan and the cleanup creatures, in your own words? Which of these did you want more of, or less?

### Beats

40. Your five favourite moments in the book, in order, and why each one worked.
41. The three weakest moments, and what was wrong with each.
42. A scene you'd cut, and a scene you wish existed.
43. Where did the book move too fast, and where too slowly?

### Tone

44. How did the book feel to read? Describe the mood, and where it changed.
45. Where was it funny? Did the humour ever undercut something serious, or keep you at a distance?
46. Did the narrator ever tell you what to think or explain a moment you'd already understood? Where?
47. Did anything feel repetitive: a joke, a gesture, a way of ending a scene, a kind of sentence, a way characters talk? Give examples.
48. Do the characters sound different from each other? Who sounds most like themselves, and who blends in?

### What's left open

49. Which questions does the book leave unanswered? For each, did it bother you, or did it feel right to leave it open?
50. Is there anything you still don't understand about what happened?

### The ending

51. What happens in the last scene, and what does the final exchange ("Are you ready?" / "No." / "Perfect.") mean to you? Who says each line?
52. Does the ending feel earned? What would have made it land harder?
53. Where do you think these people go after the last page?

### Last

54. If you could tell the author one thing, what would it be?
