# Decisions

## Status

**Active. Writing reference: a general guideline for Sunday Morning stories in any setting.** This page owns the author's rulings on how Sunday Morning stories are made. Quotations are his words from the working conversations on the first collection (2026-09-27 to 29). They carry into every collection.

- **Standing rulings (D-rows) bind.** Build on them rather than re-deriving them ([the author's watch-list](Craft.md#the-authors-watch-list): "pulling on resolved threads"). Only the author changes one. Record the change here with its date, then update the page it applies to.
- **Working decisions (W-rows)** were made in the work and are reversible until the author reads them.
- **Numbers are kept from the first collection's notes**, so references elsewhere stay stable. Gaps are rulings that only concerned that collection's own stories. D12 and D13 record delegations the author made for the first collection; they aren't standing permissions.
- **Each collection keeps its own decisions list** for rulings about its stories; see [per-collection decisions](#per-collection-decisions).

## Per-collection decisions

Each collection keeps a decisions list in its own folder for rulings about its own stories: plot changes, which relationship stays open, which details link two stories, how far a draft goes, which setting question is still waiting on an answer. Use the same shape:

```text
## The author's decisions          # | Date | Decision (with his words) | Applied in
## Working decisions          # | Decision | Why | Record        (reversible until the author reads them)
## Open for the author             the verdicts, taste calls, lines in the cautious categories and setting questions waiting on him
```

Rulings that turn out to apply to every collection move up to [Standing rulings](#standing-rulings), with their date.

In the first collection, the per-collection list held things like: that two anonymous letters in two stories share one hidden hand, which no character connects; drafting all seven stories to L3; approving one test story before revising the rest; leaving one story's central relationship open; two plot changes made to stop six stories resolving the same way; and a small question about which authority employs a minor official, left vague until a draft needs it.
