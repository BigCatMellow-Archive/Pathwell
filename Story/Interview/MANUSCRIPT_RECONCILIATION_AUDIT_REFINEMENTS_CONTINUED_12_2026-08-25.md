## Audit Question 69 — What innocent action is Shade taking when Pathwell triggers the museum ward?

### Context

Elizabeth has already told Pathwell she does not need rescuing. She chose Camp, and the group is inside the museum because the graffiti wall is the route there. Shade knows how to open that threshold. The ward accident works best if Pathwell's intervention overrides an action Elizabeth has actually chosen rather than stopping an ambiguous movement that might reasonably look dangerous.

### Options

**A. Elizabeth asks Shade to open the Camp threshold, and Shade moves toward the graffiti wall to do it.** Shade gives a small open-handed acknowledgement and starts toward the wall / passes near Elizabeth. Pathwell sees the movement, overrides Elizabeth's stated judgment, and casts the broad protective/restraint working. This makes Shade's action explicitly cooperative with Elizabeth's choice rather than a threat.

**B. Shade steps toward Elizabeth simply to continue the conversation at closer range.** The movement can remain nonthreatening, but it has no practical purpose and gives Pathwell slightly more plausible grounds to misread the approach after the crash/diner history.

**C. Shade moves toward Pathwell because the draw physically pulls him.** This uses an established mechanic, but it weakens the connection between Pathwell's ward and his insistence on protecting Elizabeth; Shade would be moving toward Pathwell, not her.

**D. Shade starts to hand Elizabeth the folded threshold sheet.** This gives the movement a practical reason, but risks reviving the stale choreography where Elizabeth appears to be the person who activates the route. The sheet should remain Shade's tool if he is the practitioner opening the threshold.

### Audit lean: **A**

A makes the overreach causally clean. Elizabeth has said she does not need rescuing and has chosen Camp; she then asks Shade for the specific magical access she cannot perform herself. Shade begins complying. Pathwell overrides that choice anyway because he distrusts Shade and believes his protective judgment should supersede Elizabeth's. The resulting injury is therefore not caused by an ambiguous apparent attack but by Pathwell refusing to accept a boundary he has just been given. It also naturally positions Shade near the graffiti wall for the later threshold opening after the accident. The main cost is that the dialogue must not make Elizabeth seem dependent on Shade for the decision itself: **she chooses Camp; Shade only supplies the magical access.**
