## Existing-story check: Chapter 1 does not currently establish the party-entry mechanism that support canon requires

Current `Chapter_01.txt` begins with the exact structural line `It was the crash that woke her.` Elizabeth then immediately discovers Pathwell already inside her apartment, tearing through boxes. The chapter never explains how he entered the building or apartment.

Current `character_bible.md` locks a broader entry mechanism:

- Pathwell is scavenging for emotionally charged material, not seeking Elizabeth.
- He passes as a guest at a party so he can move around the building without drawing attention.
- He notices Elizabeth's slightly open apartment door and senses the unusually strong diary/cookbook charge inside.
- He walks through the open door because ordinary consent is friction to him.

The character bible explicitly marks the exact party logistics and how much appears on-page as open.

This creates a presentation problem rather than a lore problem. The opening crash line is structurally important and should not be displaced by a Pathwell prelude, but the party-entry setup needs enough on-page evidence that his presence does not feel like unexplained teleportation or burglary by plot convenience.

## Audit Question 112 — How much of the party-entry setup should appear on-page in Chapter 1?

### A. Preserve `It was the crash that woke her.` as the opening and establish the party only through immediate environmental evidence after Elizabeth wakes.

Elizabeth can hear fading party noise/music/voices from elsewhere in the building or hallway, notice cups/people/door traffic, or otherwise register that a nearby apartment gathering has been going on. Pathwell's exact arrival does not need a pre-scene. Later context can make clear he blended in as a guest, wandered the hall, noticed Elizabeth's slightly open door, sensed the charged books, and entered.

This preserves the abrupt opening and keeps the reader aligned with Elizabeth: Pathwell is already inexplicably present when she meets him, but the building circumstances that made his intrusion possible are visible enough to make sense afterward.

### B. Add a short Pathwell prelude before `It was the crash that woke her.` showing him at the party and entering Elizabeth's apartment.

This would make the logistics completely explicit, but it sacrifices the locked opening line's position as the first line and removes some of Elizabeth's disorientation because the reader already knows who the intruder is and why he is there.

### C. Start Chapter 1 with Elizabeth briefly interacting with or hearing the party before she falls asleep, then retain the crash wake-up afterward.

This makes the party clear from Elizabeth's point of view and can explain why her apartment door is slightly open. However, it delays the supernatural intrusion and weakens the clean abruptness of the current opening.

### D. Remove the party-guest mechanism and invent another access route for Pathwell.

This avoids having to seed a party, but discards an already-locked entry behavior that fits Pathwell's casual boundary violation and currently has no contradictory evidence requiring replacement.

### Audit lean: **A**

A preserves the strongest existing opening while supplying enough causal information. The story does not need to watch Pathwell infiltrate the party; it only needs the environment to support how he got there. Keeping the reader in Elizabeth's perspective also protects the opening misread: she wakes into a situation already in motion and initially has no reason to understand that Pathwell was scavenging rather than targeting her. B explains too much too early and displaces the locked first line. C is workable but spends opening momentum on setup. D throws away a compatible existing lock without necessity.

## Q112 LOCK — A: Party setup remains implied after the crash opening

- Preserve `It was the crash that woke her.` as the first line and true opening beat.
- Do not add a Pathwell prelude or an Elizabeth-before-sleep party scene.
- Establish the nearby party only through immediate environmental evidence available after Elizabeth wakes: hallway music/voices, people or cups, door traffic, or similarly brief cues.
- Pathwell's exact route into the building does not need to be shown on-page.
- Later context may make clear that he blended in as a party guest, wandered the hall while scavenging charged material, noticed Elizabeth's slightly open apartment door and strong diary/cookbook charge, and entered.
- The reader should initially share Elizabeth's disorientation rather than know Pathwell's motive in advance.
