## Existing-story check: how Shade knows what Pathwell will try at Camp

Current Chapter 12 has Shade tell Elizabeth before Pathwell arrives:

- `He's going to try to cut me loose.`
- `He thinks that solves the problem.`

Two issues are present:

1. **Mechanics:** newer climax canon says Pathwell intends to collapse the separation and absorb/reintegrate Shade into himself, not simply cut Shade loose.
2. **Information:** current Chapter 11 does not show Pathwell telling Shade this plan. Their museum confrontation establishes grievance, accusation, and Pathwell's control reflex, but no reintegration proposal. The draw cannot transmit plans, thoughts, or current knowledge.

The tree-line conversation still has structural value because it gives Shade room to explain, before the action climax, why loss of independent personhood is an existential threat to him. Removing all advance discussion would force that explanation into the central-fire confrontation itself.

## Audit Question 79 — How should Shade frame Pathwell's likely reintegration attempt before Pathwell arrives?

### Context

Shade knows Pathwell is coming. He knows Pathwell's longstanding pattern of solving consequences by taking control of them, and he carries creation-time fragments from Pathwell. But Pathwell has not yet told him on-page that he intends to absorb/reintegrate him. Shade therefore cannot state the plan as confirmed information unless the manuscript adds a new disclosure.

### Options

**A. Shade predicts reintegration but clearly marks it as prediction.** He says, in substance, that he thinks Pathwell will try to put him back / fold him back into himself because that is the kind of solution Pathwell reaches for. This lets Shade explain the existential stakes to Elizabeth in advance without claiming access to Pathwell's current plans.

**B. Pathwell explicitly announces the reintegration plan during the museum confrontation.** Shade then knows exactly what is coming at Camp. This solves the information problem but adds another major beat to an already crowded museum scene and makes Pathwell's plan fully fixed before the ward injury and Camp interval.

**C. The draw tells Shade what Pathwell intends.** This would violate the locked rule that the draw supplies orientation/compulsion only and no plans or information.

**D. Remove all advance discussion of reintegration.** Shade says only that Pathwell is coming. The actual absorption proposal and Shade's explanation of what it means happen for the first time at the central fire. This is mechanically clean but risks slowing the climax with exposition that the tree-line scene can carry more naturally.

### Audit lean: **A**

A preserves the useful emotional function of the Camp conversation while respecting the information boundary. Shade is perceptive enough to recognize Pathwell's pattern, but the wording must make the distinction visible: **prediction, not knowledge**. The main guardrail is that Shade cannot know the exact working details, price, timing, or outcome; he can only anticipate that Pathwell will try to resolve the anomaly by collapsing the separation and taking Shade back into himself. B adds clutter to the museum scene, C directly violates the draw rule, and D moves necessary stakes explanation into the climax.

## Shade anticipates reintegration through draw pressure plus his own prediction — LOCKED C+A HYBRID

- Shade's current advance statement must no longer read as confirmed knowledge of Pathwell's plan.
- **The draw contributes bodily pressure and the supported certainty that Pathwell is coming/approaching; it does not transmit Pathwell's plan.**
- Shade then uses his own knowledge of Pathwell's habits, creation-time fragments, and observed control reflex to predict the likely solution: Pathwell will probably try to put/fold Shade back into himself.
- Shade should mark the reintegration claim as prediction in substance (`I think`, `he'll probably`, or equivalent), even if his confidence is high.
- The stale `cut me loose` description is superseded; the anticipated action is absorption/reintegration.
- The draw still transmits no thoughts, plans, current memories, exact distance, timing, or working details.
- This preserves the tree-line conversation as the place where Shade can explain why loss of independent self is existentially unacceptable without granting him telepathy.
- No manuscript chapter has been edited.
