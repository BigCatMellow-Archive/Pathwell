## Existing-story check: Chapter 4 treats a successful cookbook healing as leaving blob-attracting waste

Current `Chapter_04.txt` has Elizabeth surrender Nana's handwritten cookbook to help heal an injured Camp woman. The healing succeeds: one page is consumed, the patient stabilizes, and the surviving book remains intact except for the missing page.

Immediately afterward, however, the chapter describes gold residue/afterglow spread across the wagon steps, mud, blanket, and Elizabeth's sleeve. Pathwell says it will `draw`; when Elizabeth asks `Blobs?`, he answers `Among others.` That danger then helps motivate the move to recruit Stansbury.

Locked world rules now say the opposite for successful emotional-text magic:

- normal successful emotional-text casting resolves cleanly;
- it does **not** leave unclaimed casting waste;
- it does **not** summon blobs;
- blob-attracting casting waste belongs to failed, interrupted, or uncontrolled workings.

A successful cookbook healing can still have transient sensory/visual aftermath, but that aftermath cannot function as unclaimed blob food unless the healing itself is reframed as a failed/unstable working.

The story also already has an independent unresolved threat from Chapter 1: the blob incident remains unexplained to Pathwell, and Stansbury's established role is defensive/supportive, including his imbued weapons. Therefore Stansbury does not need to be recruited solely because a successful Camp healing contaminated the area.

## Audit Question 105 — What should happen to the cookbook-healing residue and the reason Pathwell seeks Stansbury?

### A. Keep only a brief harmless afterglow from the successful healing; remove the claim that it attracts blobs, and have Pathwell seek Stansbury because the unresolved Chapter 1 blob/magical threat means he wants additional defensive help.

The cookbook working succeeds cleanly. A fading smell, warmth, gold shimmer, or similar sensory trace can remain for atmosphere, but it is not unclaimed waste and does not draw cleanup creatures. Pathwell's decision to recruit Stansbury instead follows from the larger unresolved problem already in motion: a blob appeared under circumstances Pathwell does not understand, Elizabeth remains involved in whatever he thinks is happening, and Stansbury has useful defensive expertise/imbued weapons.

### B. Reframe the cookbook healing as partially uncontrolled so that it legitimately leaves unclaimed casting waste and can attract blobs.

This preserves the current causal bridge to Stansbury, but weakens the healing as a successful, meaningful use of Elizabeth's sacrifice and adds another magical failure immediately after Chapter 1's interrupted working.

### C. Change the world rule so successful emotional-text workings can routinely leave blob-attracting residue.

This preserves Chapter 4 most directly, but reopens the larger consistency problem the current rule solved: archives, Camp workings, and ordinary successful magic would all risk functioning as blob attractors unless a new distinction were invented.

### D. Remove both the residue and any explicit reason for seeking Stansbury; Pathwell simply decides they need him.

This avoids the rules conflict but leaves the transition to Chapter 5 under-motivated when an existing unresolved threat can supply the reason cleanly.

### Audit lean: **A**

A preserves the successful cookbook sacrifice, keeps the world rule clean, and uses an already-established problem rather than inventing a new contamination mechanism. The Chapter 1 blob is still unexplained from Pathwell's perspective, so seeking his brother's defensive help is a natural escalation. B creates another failed-working beat where the story does not need one. C destabilizes the blob ecology across the book. D is mechanically safe but causally thin.
