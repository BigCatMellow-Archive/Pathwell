## Audit Question 72 — How should Shade's `I've been broken in most of the available directions` line be reconciled?

### Context

Shade has only existed for a relatively short time. He can carry old-seeming knowledge and habits from Pathwell's creation-time fragments, but those are **borrowed residue, not Shade's personal biography**. Immediately before stabilizing Elizabeth's shoulder, the current manuscript makes him sound as though he personally spent years getting injured in every possible way.

### Options

**A. Keep the joke but explicitly mark the experience as borrowed.** Shade says something dry in the spirit of `I've been broken in most of the available directions. Some of those memories aren't technically mine.` This preserves the joke and makes the inheritance visible, but calls attention to the mechanism during an injury scene.

**B. Reframe the line around practical recognition rather than biography.** Shade answers the healer with something like `No. But I know what it looks like when that sets wrong.` His competence remains uncanny and fragmentary without claiming he personally lived the injuries or pausing to explain inheritance.

**C. Cut the line entirely.** Shade simply stops the bad set, identifies the problem, and later performs the already-locked stabilization. This is clean but removes some Shade voice from the exchange.

**D. Keep the line literally and establish that Shade has personally suffered many serious injuries since creation.** This would justify the wording, but requires a substantial off-page post-creation injury history that the current story does not support and would undermine the `recently created` texture.

### Audit lean: **B**

B preserves the useful part of the beat—Shade knows something he should not obviously know—without turning a quick treatment scene into an explanation of inherited memory. Question 71 already gives the underlying mechanical answer in the Bible; the manuscript does not need Shade to explain it every time a fragment surfaces. A is viable if we want the reader explicitly reminded that his knowledge is borrowed, but it risks sounding self-explanatory in a moment that works better with confidence and ambiguity. C is safe but loses character texture; D conflicts with the existing chronology.
