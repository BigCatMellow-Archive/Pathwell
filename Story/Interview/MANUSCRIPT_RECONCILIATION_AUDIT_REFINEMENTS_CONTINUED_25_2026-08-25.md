## Audit Question 86 — How should Shade explain why he is telling Elizabeth what he thinks Pathwell will attempt?

### A. Make Shade explicitly uncertain about Pathwell's disclosure.

Shade says in substance: `I'm telling you because I don't know if he will` or `because I don't trust him to tell you before he acts.` This preserves Shade's distrust and Pathwell's old pattern without claiming certainty about a future choice Pathwell has not made.

### B. Keep `he won't tell you` as Shade's biased opinion.

Shade is allowed to be unfair or wrong. This preserves more of the current line, but the prose would need to make clear that this is Shade's judgment rather than reliable exposition. It also risks muddying Pathwell's Chapter 10 movement.

### C. Remove the disclosure comparison entirely.

Shade says only that he wants Elizabeth to know what he expects is coming. Mechanically clean, but loses the useful tension around Pathwell's habit of controlling information.

### D. Make Pathwell explicitly conceal the plan from Elizabeth before this scene.

This would justify Shade's certainty, but it requires adding a fresh Pathwell regression after Chapter 10 and before the tree-line conversation solely to preserve one line.

### Audit lean: **A**

A preserves the conflict without making Shade omniscient or erasing Pathwell's incremental progress. Pathwell has shown that he **can** tell Elizabeth the truth when she insists; Shade can still reasonably doubt that he will volunteer a new plan before acting. The distinction matters because the story's problem is not that Pathwell is incapable of honesty, but that he repeatedly assumes the right to decide when other people deserve information and choice.
