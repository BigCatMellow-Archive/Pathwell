## Existing-story check: Chapter 3's Camp-supply prune currently has no visible delivery/payoff

Q107/Q108 now establish the intended Chapter 3 transaction:

- Camp Cunnan genuinely needs supplies.
- The need is mentioned or known, but nobody assigns Pathwell the errand.
- Pathwell appoints himself to solve it.
- He acquires the supplies through the Space Between.
- Nobody asks him to prune; he independently chooses pruning as payment.

Current `Chapter_03.txt` shows the Space Between transaction but does not clearly identify the purchased result as Camp supplies. A leather-bound tome appears before the prune; an envelope is handed over afterward. Current `Chapter_04.txt` then arrives at Camp without showing Pathwell deliver a package, hand anything off, or otherwise complete the supply errand.

A repo search did not find an established later function for the Chapter 3 tome or envelope. This leaves the early clean-prune baseline with a missing practical payoff: the reader sees Pathwell spend future possibility but not the ordinary need that his spending successfully satisfies.

The exact contents of Camp's supplies do not need to be systematized unless the story needs them. The important requirement is that the causal chain is legible: **Camp has a need → Pathwell appoints himself → Pathwell prunes to pay → Camp receives the supplies.**

## Audit Question 109 — How should the Chapter 3 Camp-supply purchase become visibly connected to Camp?

### A. Pathwell leaves the Space Between with a modest physical parcel/bundle, and Chapter 4 briefly shows him hand it off at Camp before the cookbook crisis.

The contents can remain only partly specified or generic. The handoff should be routine rather than ceremonious: Pathwell has done this sort of thing before, Camp accepts the supplies, and the story moves on. This makes the prune's successful result visible without turning the supplies into a new plot device.

### B. The Space Between automatically delivers the purchased supplies directly to Camp.

This keeps Chapter 4 uncluttered, but adds a new magical delivery/logistics capability that the story does not otherwise need and hides the useful character image of Pathwell quietly arriving with what he decided everyone needed.

### C. Define the supplies specifically as charged records/material for Camp's archive or healers, with the current envelope/package containing those records.

This gives the purchase clear magical relevance and may fit Camp's practical reserve, but it commits the story to a more specific supply category than current structure requires. It also risks turning a general caretaking habit into a narrowly magical procurement job.

### D. Leave the actual delivery off-page and trust the reader to infer that the transaction was for Camp.

This is economical, but the current manuscript provides no strong inference: the purchase objects disappear and Chapter 4 does not connect them to Camp. The clean pruning baseline remains visually ambiguous.

### Audit lean: **A**

A solves the causal gap with minimal lore. It also sharpens the character pattern without explanation: Pathwell spends part of his future, arrives carrying a package, hands it over, and treats the whole thing as routine. The story does not need to explain exactly what is inside unless a later scene uses it. B invents unnecessary infrastructure; C may over-specify; D leaves the existing disconnect intact.
