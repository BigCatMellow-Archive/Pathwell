## 1. Perception of magic

- **Anyone can perceive magic.** There is no magical veil, chosen class, bloodline requirement, or special perception rule that explains Elizabeth.
- People can rationalize, ignore, misremember, or explain away supernatural events psychologically, but that is not a magical effect.
- Magic is **secret but visible**: rare enough that most people know it through folklore, mythology, rumor, religion, and distorted historical accounts rather than direct experience.
- Mythology has a **variable relationship to real supernatural history**. Some myths are close to truth, some preserve a distorted seed of truth, and some are entirely invented.
- The relationship between a particular myth and reality can vary from story to story. There is no rule that every myth must secretly be true.

## 2. Camp Cunnan

### Nature and movement

- Camp Cunnan is a **moving place**.
- It can be reached through a mixture of guides, signs, known routes, and established thresholds. No single method is universally reliable.
- The Camp moves for reasons that even the Cunning Folk do not fully understand.
- Residents possess practical, generational knowledge of the movement without possessing a complete cosmological explanation.
- Experienced residents can recognize signs that a shift is approaching, but cannot reliably predict the exact time or destination.
- When Camp moves, those clearly inside it move with it. The edges are less reliable: someone entering, leaving, or lingering around the boundary can be separated or left behind.
- Thresholds behave differently after a move. Some durable anchors tend to reconnect; temporary routes may fail, drift, or need to be rediscovered.
- The unknown reason Camp moves is deliberate iceberg lore and may support a later story.

### Camp archive

- The Camp Cunnan archive serves **two equal functions**: cultural memory and practical magical reserve.
- It preserves emotionally significant records because the records matter in themselves, while recognizing that their stored charge may sometimes be needed by the living.
- The archive can reduce ambient overload through spacing, physical separation, grouping by emotional character, containment, cabinets, boxes, wrapping, walls, and other archival practices.
- These methods mitigate ambient charge; sufficiently large or powerful collections still create a noticeable emotional field.
- Ordinary physical barriers dampen ambient charge but do not eliminate it.
- The archive acts as a serious custodian rather than a casual owner, but **donation transfers ownership to the archive**.
- Archivists take donor wishes into consideration, but those wishes are not binding. The archive has the right to use and consume donated material as it sees fit.
- A donation is final in the sense that the donor has no right to demand the object back.
- The archive may nevertheless choose to sell or trade an archived object back. Any such return is entirely at the archive's discretion.
- The archive can accept anything it considers sufficiently valuable in exchange: services, rare objects, charged works, favors, information, labor, access, mundane value, or magical value.
- Camp can technically accept pruning as payment, but strongly discourages it and may refuse it in ordinary circumstances because of the danger.
- **Elizabeth voluntarily gives her diary to the Camp archive.** The act is practical and symbolic: she stops carrying the diary as a private tether and entrusts her record to something larger than herself. She does **not** perform magic by doing this.
- Pathwell's attempt to buy the diary back is part of the intended version currently being recovered/rebuilt. The exact scene mechanics and relationship to the archive fire still need final reconciliation against surviving drafts.

## 3. Pruning

### Core law

- **Pruning is currency, not reality editing.**
- A practitioner spends their own future possibility to pay for something else.
- The act of pruning does not itself alter probability, rewrite events, force an outcome, or create alternate timelines.
- Pruned possibility has no effect by itself. Something capable of accepting it must receive it as payment.
- Possible counterparties include entities, systems, or properly constructed magical workings capable of accepting possibility. Not all magic accepts pruning.
- Pruning cannot be placed into generic storage or turned into a free-floating personal credit reserve. A counterparty must accept the possibility as it is spent.

### Price and cost

- Some workings have relatively fixed costs; entities and systems may negotiate, bargain, or demand context-dependent prices.
- Cost is normally understood only in broad terms: cheap, substantial, dangerous, etc. Some systems may communicate price more clearly, but there is no numerical mana bar.
- A prune usually narrows the practitioner's **overall field of possibility**, rather than removing one clearly identifiable future.
- Occasionally a lost possibility becomes concrete enough to recognize afterward.
- Completed pruning is irreversible.
- Early cancellation, before possibility has truly been released/accepted, can return cleanly.
- A later failure after release can leave possibility stranded as **unclaimed spillage**.
- Shade is an unprecedented edge case produced by a malformed prune under an unusual combination of circumstances. The broad mechanism is understandable; the exact conditions that caused discarded possibility to become a person are unknown and not reliably reproducible.

### Who can prune

- Pruning is a dangerous practitioner technique requiring training and understanding. Ordinary people cannot accidentally prune merely by making a bargain.
- Among experienced practitioners, pruning is broadly known but specialized and controversial.
- A minority are trained to perform it. Traditions differ: some teach it strictly, some discourage it, some forbid it, and some barely preserve the technique.
- Pruning is not inherently evil, but habitual use is broadly regarded as reckless and self-destructive.

### Immediate effects

- Physical cost scales with the transaction.
- Small prunes may be barely noticeable.
- Large prunes can cause shaking, weakness, nausea, disorientation, and other short-lived physical effects.

## 4. Emotionally charged records and art

### 4.1 Core principle

- Pathwell's primary discipline is **handwritten emotional-text magic**.
- Handwriting is required for this branch of the system. Typed and mass-printed text do not hold this kind of charge.
- The broader world contains related forms of emotionally charged art: drawings, paintings, carvings, and other authentic human artistic expression can also retain emotional charge.
- Those other media are distinct branches of practice. They exist in the lore without needing to be explained at the same depth in this story.
- Different charged media can be combined in one working, but cross-medium combinations are significantly harder and riskier than combining works from the same discipline.

### 4.2 What creates charge

- The writer is the primary source of a handwritten text's charge.
- Repeated meaningful handling and use by other people can deepen and layer the charge over time.
- Charge strength can come from several sources at once: emotional intensity at creation, personal significance, repeated use, handling, annotation, inheritance, and accumulated history.
- Age alone does not create charge. Older objects often seem stronger because they have had more time to accumulate meaningful history.
- Charge can continue accumulating after the original creator dies.
- A single intensely meaningful act can establish a strong emotional anchor immediately; more ordinary records can develop one gradually through genuine use and history.
- Anchored charge fades only very slowly through neglect. Meaningful records can retain charge for decades or longer.

### 4.3 Authenticity and the anti-manufacture rule

- **Intent cannot manufacture emotional charge.**
- A person cannot deliberately create 'magic ammunition' simply by deciding to write something emotional for later magical consumption.
- The magic comes from records of lived human expression, not from attempts to produce magical fuel.
- A text created for the purpose of becoming magical is effectively a **dead battery**: it lacks the authentic foundational anchor needed to hold useful charge.
- Emotion may later brush against such an object, but it dissipates quickly rather than taking root.
- This prevents practitioners from making endless supplies of charged notebooks, commissioned grief letters, ritual diaries, or similar manufactured resources.

### 4.4 Emotional character and effect

- A charged work has a natural range of effects determined by **both its emotional content and its specific meaning/context**.
- The practitioner shapes the effect but must work with the grain of the record rather than forcing it to do something wholly unrelated.
- A grandmother's family recipe carrying care, nourishment, memory, and protection may be well suited to a protective or restorative working.
- A single record can naturally contain multiple emotions at once.
- Emotionally complex records are harder to use safely and precisely.
- Contradictory foundational emotions increase difficulty and danger rather than functioning as free versatility.

### 4.5 Skill and specialization

- Most trained practitioners can sense and make basic use of emotional charge.
- Shaping that charge into a specific effect usually requires skill or specialization.
- Some unusually intense, complex, or idiosyncratic records may require particular expertise to use safely.
- Emotional-text magic normally requires the practitioner to understand the type of working they are attempting.
- The charged record supplies power and character; it does not normally replace technical knowledge.
- An exceptionally well-matched record can sometimes bridge part of a practitioner's skill gap and allow something slightly beyond their normal ability.

### 4.6 Reading and sensing charge

- A practitioner does not need to understand the language of a text to sense or access its emotional charge.
- Being able to read and understand the text's meaning, context, and history greatly improves control and interpretation.
- A trained practitioner can roughly sense the amount and emotional character of charge at a distance.
- Physical contact provides a much clearer read.
- Direct physical contact with the original charged work is required to **activate and consume** its charge.
- Handwriting style itself has no magical significance. Penmanship is a vessel, not a source of power.

### 4.7 Consumption is destruction

- **Using emotional charge destroys the original work.** The charge and the original human expression are inseparable.
- A handwritten page, painting, drawing, carving, or other charged artwork is physically consumed, ruined, erased, cracked, burned, dissolved, or otherwise destroyed when its charge is spent.
- Information can sometimes be copied; the original human record cannot.
- A recreated copy is never magically equivalent to the original.
- This cost is universal across charged art. Text is not uniquely sacrificial.

### 4.8 Page-level granularity

- In a book, a practitioner may consume a **single physically distinct page** without consuming the entire book.
- A practitioner cannot selectively consume a paragraph, sentence, or word from a page.
- Once a page is activated, the entire page is committed.
- Multiple pages can be used together in one working.
- Mixing multiple emotional sources increases difficulty. Compatible emotions may reinforce one another; conflicting emotions can create interference, feedback, and unstable results.
- The museum sequence is a story example: Pathwell grasps/mixes multiple emotional sources, loses clean control, and harms himself.

### 4.9 Activation is a point of no return

- Charged text does not activate accidentally. Someone capable of using it must intentionally activate it.
- Strong emotion does not make a diary or other record spontaneously discharge.
- The practitioner needs a general intended purpose before activation, but may refine or redirect the working as charge is released.
- Once a working begins, redirection is limited to the same general purpose. A protection working may change target, shape, strength, or scope, but cannot simply become an attack.
- Some records are more restrictive than others because of their emotional character.
- Once activation begins, the practitioner cannot voluntarily stop and preserve the remaining page.
- Once activated, the page will finish consuming even if the practitioner is forcibly interrupted.
- A practitioner cannot deliberately abandon an activated page and harmlessly vent the released charge.
- Failure to shape the release can cause collapse, distortion, feedback, or uncontrolled discharge.
- If the practitioner is killed or rendered incapable, uncontrolled release can occur.
- If a practitioner dies very late in a working, an effect that has already been substantially shaped may still finish according to the practitioner's final intent.

### 4.10 Echo after consumption

- When emotional-text magic is used, the practitioner temporarily retains an **echo** of the consumed record.
- The echo includes imperfect practical/conceptual information and the emotion impressed into the record.
- The practitioner feels that emotion for roughly as long as the informational echo lasts.
- Both fade together and become increasingly muddled until only ordinary memory remains.
- The practitioner does not receive the creator's complete memories; they experience what was impressed into the record.
- A practitioner may try to rewrite information during the echo, but the new copy is fundamentally a transcription, not a restoration of the original.
- At most, a rewritten copy may retain a faint residual trace; it cannot recreate the original charge.
- **The echo cannot itself be spent as magical fuel.** The original charge has already been used.
- Other charged media produce echoes too, but the medium affects their character: text tends toward verbal/conceptual information; paintings and drawings may produce stronger visual or sensory impressions.

### 4.11 Recipient effects and consent

- The activating practitioner experiences the strongest echo.
- A recipient can receive fragments of the echo when the working is shaped through or onto them.
- Recipient fragments may include emotion, sensory impressions, or brief meaning without reproducing the practitioner's full echo.
- A recipient does **not** have to consent for the echo to affect them.
- Emotional-text magic can make a person experience the emotion carried by a record, but cannot force them to adopt that emotion as their own or compel a particular action.
- A recipient cannot magically block a successfully delivered emotional echo. They still control their judgment, interpretation, and behavior.
- Extremely powerful, traumatic, or personally resonant records can leave rare lingering emotional aftereffects in practitioners or recipients.

### 4.12 Factual impressions and emotional archaeology

- Strong emotional records can sometimes carry fragmentary factual impressions: faces, rooms, sounds, gestures, phrases, or brief actions.
- These fragments are authentic to lived experience but are filtered through the creator's perception, memory, bias, and emotional state.
- They are not objective recordings.
- Most skilled practitioners can read a work's history only impressionistically.
- Specialists can sometimes intentionally reconstruct fragments of an object's history. This is a rare discipline.
- Extremely rare masters can reconstruct portions of a charged object's history with surprising specificity.
- Specialists can sometimes compare emotional signatures across known works to attribute an unsigned text to a likely writer.
- At the extreme edge of mastery, a practitioner may recognize an individual creator directly from emotional imprint alone, but this is exceptional and not perfectly reliable.

### 4.13 Emotional layers and the foundational core

- Emotional layers accumulate and blend over time, but sufficiently skilled practitioners can distinguish strands within them.
- A charged work develops a **foundational emotional core**.
- That core can never be erased by later history while the original record still exists.
- New layers may obscure or complicate the core, but a sufficiently skilled practitioner with physical access can always work down through the layers and reach it.
- The core is permanent but **not frozen**. Later additions or experiences can become so deeply integrated that the foundational core expands.
- The original foundation is never replaced.
- A core can contain genuine contradictions. Love and resentment, protection and harm, devotion and betrayal can all become foundational truths in the same work.
- Contradictory cores are harder and more dangerous to use because all of the emotional mixture matters.
- When a page is consumed, the practitioner cannot selectively spend only one emotional strand. The whole page releases its entire emotional mixture.

### 4.14 Books, pages, separation, and restoration

- Every page in a multi-page charged work shares some of the larger work's foundational core while also carrying page-specific emotional history.
- Consuming or removing a page permanently changes the larger work's emotional history, even though the larger core survives.
- A page physically removed but not consumed retains the core and history it possessed at separation, then stops accumulating new layers alongside the parent work.
- From that point forward, page and book develop independently.
- A separated page can develop a strong secondary identity of its own while retaining its original foundational lineage.
- If the page is later physically restored to the original work, it begins sharing new emotional history with the book again. The separate histories accumulated while apart are not erased.
- A skilled practitioner can detect the break and reunion as part of the work's history.
- Careful restoration can preserve a charged object's identity. Rebinding, mending, and stabilizing do not inherently erase charge.
- Replacing substantial original material can begin changing identity.
- There is no numerical percentage or majority-original rule. Continuity of the record matters, and some components can matter more to the core than others.
- A recreated replacement page cannot recover the lost original's charge.
- Once integrated into the original book, a replacement page can accumulate new shared charge and can eventually become deeply significant in its own right.
- Over enough genuine history, later material can become part of an expanded foundational core without resurrecting what was lost.
- If a contradictory work is physically split, separation may reduce interference where sections have different histories, but each piece retains some trace of the original whole.

### 4.15 Ambient charge

- Non-practitioners can sometimes sense strongly charged works without being able to use them.
- Usually this is only an unexplained emotional impression: grief, warmth, dread, love, longing, unease, etc.
- Extremely powerful works or dense collections can also cause vague sensory flashes or fragments.
- Dense collections can overwhelm non-practitioners. Temporary effects can include emotional exhaustion, nausea, disorientation, and intrusive impressions.
- Extreme or prolonged exposure can cause more serious lingering psychological aftereffects.
- Practitioners can partially filter ambient charge through training and experience but cannot shut it out completely.
- Ambient charge normally weakens with physical distance, though exceptionally powerful works can sometimes be sensed from surprising distances.
- The Space Between library/archive can overwhelm Elizabeth for this reason. Her response is not evidence of hidden magic or special sensitivity.

### 4.16 Locating charged objects

- A sufficiently powerful charged object can sometimes be sensed and roughly located at a distance.
- This is **not scent tracking**. A charged object does not leave a persistent trail behind as it moves.
- Tracking requires repeatedly reacquiring the object's current signal.
- Distance, barriers, and competing charged material make reacquisition unreliable.
- Practical concealment is possible through distance, heavy enclosure, physical barriers, or surrounding the object with other charged material.
- There is no perfect magical invisibility technique established for this system.
- Familiar charged objects have recognizable emotional signatures. A practitioner who knows an object well can often identify it among other charge, though interference can obscure it.
- A charged object's signature evolves as new layers accumulate while retaining a recognizable core.

### 4.17 Ordinary destruction and uncontrolled release

- If a charged work is destroyed normally without being magically activated, it usually simply loses its stored potential.
- Ordinary destruction does **not** give anyone the temporary echo.
- Exceptionally powerful works, or many highly charged works destroyed together, can release charge unpredictably instead of dissipating cleanly.
- Uncontrolled release can affect both mind and environment.
- People nearby may experience waves of grief, fear, love, rage, longing, panic, or other stored emotions.
- Sufficient concentration can also produce physical magical effects loosely shaped by the emotions being released.
- This rule provides a mechanical basis for the Camp archive fire: an archive-scale destruction event can become far more dangerous than an ordinary fire.
- Uncontrolled fallout usually burns itself out quickly once the source is gone.
- Exceptionally concentrated events can leave lingering emotional residue for hours, days, or longer.
- Lingering emotional residue **cannot be harvested or reused as magical fuel**.
- Residue can muddy the emotional environment and make later charged-text workings harder to read or control until it dissipates.
- Practitioners cannot deliberately clear this residue; it must fade naturally.
- Emotional residue is distinct from pruning **spillage**. Do not conflate the two systems.

### 4.18 Backlash and failed workings

- Mixing incompatible charge or losing control can harm the practitioner and destabilize the resulting magic.
- Backlash may involve emotional overload, physical pain, burns, shaking, weakness, disorientation, collapse, intrusive emotion, or lasting injury.
- A sufficiently severe backlash can permanently injure or kill a practitioner.
- A practitioner who realizes a working is failing can attempt to collapse it inward and take the backlash onto themselves, but cannot guarantee that all of it will be contained.
- Backlash can affect both mind and body; the balance depends on the working and the emotional material involved.
- Whether scars or bodily marks from magical backlash can themselves hold charge belongs to another branch of magic and remains deliberately undefined for this story.

### 4.19 Persistent and shared workings

- Some effects are instantaneous; others can be structured to persist independently after the practitioner stops maintaining them.
- Persistent effects last only as long as their charge and structure support them.
- Only workings deliberately designed to accept additional charge can be reinforced later.
- Multiple practitioners can contribute to one working only if the working is deliberately structured for shared participation.
- The practitioner who physically activates the charged text experiences the strongest echo; other participating practitioners may receive weaker fragments through the shared working.
- A non-practitioner cannot shape the magic, but can participate physically in a working by carrying, positioning, opening, holding, providing access, or completing mundane steps.
- This preserves Elizabeth's ability to matter during magical scenes without giving her magical ability.

### 4.20 Ownership and magical access

- Ownership and permission do not create a magical barrier.
- A practitioner with physical access and the necessary skill can use someone else's charged work without permission.
- The consequences are moral, cultural, legal, and personal rather than mechanically prevented.
- This is why custodianship and consent practices matter: the magic itself does not protect a person's record from misuse.

## 5. Elizabeth — magic boundary

- **Elizabeth does not perform magic.**
- She does not prune, imbue, cast emotional-text magic, or secretly possess a special supernatural sensitivity.
- She can own charged objects, give them away, surrender them, carry them, position them, and make choices that affect magical events.
- Giving her diary to the Camp archive is an act of ownership and agency, not a spell.
- She may experience ambient emotional charge or receive an echo directed through a working because ordinary non-practitioners can be affected by those phenomena.
- Any draft in which Elizabeth uses her diary as a magical weapon is a continuity error against this locked rule.
