## Audit Question 121 — When does Pathwell decide Stansbury should be the next stop?

### A. Pathwell already decides soon after the Chapter 1 blob encounter that he wants Stansbury's defensive help; the Space Between/Camp book errand is an existing obligation he completes first, and Chapter 4 simply resumes that plan.

This makes Stansbury part of Pathwell's response to the unresolved threat from the beginning. The Camp visit does not need to generate a new reason. After the cookbook scene, `Stansbury` can function as `back to the next thing I already intended to do` rather than a sudden realization.

The downside is that Pathwell knowingly completes the Camp errand before recruiting defensive help despite believing Elizabeth may still be connected to danger. That is compatible with his overconfidence, but the manuscript should not make him look as though he thinks the threat is immediately lethal while casually running errands.

### B. Pathwell decides at Camp after briefly telling Mama Baga or another Camp practitioner about the Chapter 1 blob; they point him toward Stansbury.

This supplies a direct trigger and lets Pathwell seek a second opinion before escalating. However, it makes the decision partly assigned by Camp rather than emerging from Pathwell's own assessment, and introduces an extra consultation beat.

### C. Pathwell decides on his own at Camp after the book delivery/cookbook healing gives him his first quiet moment to reassess the unresolved Chapter 1 incident.

This avoids an external recommendation and keeps the plan spontaneous. The weakness is that nothing materially new happens at Camp to make Stansbury suddenly become the answer; the decision risks feeling like authorial timing unless Pathwell's delayed reassessment is made legible.

### D. Leave the timing unexplained and simply transition to Stansbury after Camp.

This preserves economy but leaves the Chapter 4-to-5 causal bridge thin once the old blob-attracting residue is removed.

### Audit lean: **A**

A uses the already-locked Chapter 1 threat rather than inventing another trigger. It also fits Pathwell's habit of stacking obligations and assuming he can keep control of several moving problems at once. The key constraint is tonal: he should regard the blob problem as unresolved and concerning, not as an immediate emergency he inexplicably postpones. B adds unnecessary assignment; C works but needs extra internal justification; D leaves the transition unsupported.
