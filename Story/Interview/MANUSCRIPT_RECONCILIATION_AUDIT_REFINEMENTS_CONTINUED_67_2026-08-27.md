## Q148 — Future possibility is generative but not restorative; absolute depletion means death — USER-REFINED / LOCKED A

- A person's future possibility is **not a fixed lifetime tank** that only decreases from birth onward.
- Future possibility is better understood as a living branching structure: as a person continues to exist, makes choices, encounters other people, learns things, changes circumstances, and experiences unexpected events, **new possible futures can grow from the futures that still remain available to them**.
- Pruning permanently removes branches from that structure. The exact possibilities that were pruned do **not** regenerate or return.
- Because downstream possibilities depend on upstream branches, removing one meaningful branch can also permanently remove all futures that could only have grown from that branch.
- Life can nevertheless create **new branches from what remains**. This is generation, not restoration.
- Therefore a person who has pruned heavily can gradually regain some breadth of future possibility simply by continuing to live and encounter new choices, but they do not recover the specific futures they sold.
- This preserves the established rule that completed pruning is irreversible while avoiding a simple finite-mana model.
- A heavily pruned person's future can become **thin without necessarily becoming short**. They may still have decades or centuries ahead of them while possessing far fewer genuinely divergent paths through those years.
- Near depletion should not turn a person into a mind-controlled automaton or remove their ability to choose. They retain agency; there are simply fewer futures available for those choices to open into.
- This also means pruning should not be reduced to a direct lifespan exchange such as `this transaction costs five years`. A prune can be extremely expensive because it cuts deeply into future breadth even if the person's remaining life is still long.
- Pathwell's centuries of pruning are therefore compatible with his continued longevity: he has repeatedly cut away future breadth while continued life has also generated new branches from whatever remained.
- His long history can be imagined as an old tree that has been aggressively pruned again and again: still alive and still growing, but much narrower than it could have become.
- **Absolute zero is different.** If a person truly spends every remaining future that contains their continued existence, there is no future state left into which they can continue. Absolute depletion therefore results in death.
- This death is not a punishment, magical attack, or arbitrary lifespan tax. The person has literally exhausted every remaining future in which they continue to exist.
- Exact practitioner methods for judging how close someone is to depletion remain non-numerical. Experienced practitioners/entities may recognize `broad`, `thin`, `dangerously thin`, etc., but there is no percentage meter.
- **Still open:** whether a practitioner/counterparty can knowingly complete a transaction that spends the literal final remaining future, or whether some acceptance/settlement rule prevents a knowingly terminal prune. Do not assume an answer until separately locked.

## Q149 — The Pathwell–Shade draw is bidirectional but asymmetric — USER-APPROVED / LOCKED

- The unresolved malformed prune that created Shade left a continuing connection between Pathwell and Shade.
- That connection is **bidirectional in sensation but asymmetric in strength and function**.
- **Shade experiences the dominant side of the draw:** a deterministic directional compulsion toward Pathwell. Its effective content remains `Pathwell is that way. Go.` It can orient his body, open/direct his right hand toward Pathwell, and become harder to resist as Pathwell draws nearer.
- Shade can resist and delay the compulsion, but resistance requires effort. The draw does not transmit thoughts, current memories, plans, exact distance, ETA, or other information.
- **Pathwell experiences the weaker side of the same connection:** intermittent tension, tugging, pressure, or the sense that something attached to his pruning history has gone taut.
- Pathwell has been feeling this weak draw **from time to time since the major malformed prune that produced Shade**.
- Pathwell's side is not the same compulsion Shade experiences. It can catch his attention, make him turn or look, produce restlessness, or create a vague sense that something unresolved is pulling at him, but it does not force his movement or decisions.
- At unusually strong moments the sensation may have a vague directional quality, but it is not reliable enough to function as tracking, radar, or GPS.
- Pathwell receives no telepathy, no current Shade memories, no knowledge of Shade's plans, and no clean explanation of what the sensation means.
- Before recognizing Shade, Pathwell can plausibly misread the intermittent draw as an aftereffect of a large prune, accumulated pruning damage, exhaustion, or an unfamiliar but non-urgent settlement phenomenon.
- The bar-mirror realization can therefore connect several previously separate anomalies for Pathwell: the long-standing unexplained tug, Shade's resemblance/presence, and blob behavior all begin pointing back toward Pathwell himself without giving him a complete creation history.
- This remains compatible with Q141: Pathwell can recognize that Shade is tied to his pruning history and to his own choices without identifying the exact historical transaction that created him.
- The connection also helps explain why blobs have difficulty cleanly distinguishing Pathwell and Shade: the cleanup system encounters an unresolved magical structure linking the source practitioner and the stranded anomaly/person, while their signatures remain strongly overlapping.
- Shade remains a fully independent person. The continuing connection does **not** make him Pathwell's property, incomplete self, remote body, or current memory extension.
- The draw must never excuse Pathwell's choices. Pathwell's pursuit, museum overreach, forced reintegration attempt, and other consequential decisions remain his own. The weak tug can attract attention; it does not compel his moral actions.
- Conceptual distinction: **Shade feels compulsion; Pathwell feels tension.**
