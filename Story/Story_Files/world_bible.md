## WHO CAN SEE MAGIC

**Anyone can perceive magic.** There is no magical veil, chosen class, bloodline requirement, or special perception rule that explains Elizabeth.

People may rationalize, ignore, misremember, or explain away supernatural events psychologically, but that is not a magical effect.

Magic is **secret but visible**: rare enough that most people understand it through folklore, mythology, rumor, religion, and distorted historical accounts rather than direct experience.

Mythology has a variable relationship to supernatural history. Some myths are close to truth, some preserve only a distorted seed, and some are entirely invented. Do not force every myth to be secretly true.

## PRUNING

### Core law

**Pruning is currency, not reality editing.** A practitioner spends their own future possibility to pay for something else. The prune itself does not rewrite probability, force outcomes, create alternate timelines, or alter reality by selecting a future.

Pruned possibility does nothing by itself. Something capable of accepting it must receive it as payment: an entity, a system, or a properly constructed magical working. Not all magic accepts pruning.

There is no generic pruning battery or stored personal credit. A counterparty must accept the possibility as it is spent.

### Cost

- Some costs are relatively fixed; others are negotiated or context-dependent.
- Practitioners normally understand cost only broadly: cheap, substantial, dangerous, and so on. There is no numerical meter.
- A prune usually narrows the practitioner's **overall field of possibility**, not one clearly labeled future.
- Occasionally the lost possibility becomes concrete enough to recognize afterward.
- Completed pruning is irreversible.
- Early cancellation can return cleanly if possibility has not truly been released/accepted.
- Later failure after release can leave **unclaimed spillage**.
- Immediate physical effects scale with the transaction: small prunes may be barely noticeable; large ones can cause shaking, weakness, nausea, disorientation, or collapse.

### Practice and culture

Pruning requires training. Ordinary people do not accidentally prune by making bargains.

Most experienced practitioners know of pruning, but only a minority are trained to perform it. Traditions differ: some teach it under strict limits, some discourage it, some forbid it, and some barely preserve the technique.

Pruning is not inherently evil, but habitual use is broadly regarded as reckless and self-destructive.

### Shade

Shade is an unprecedented edge case: a malformed prune under an unusual combination of circumstances left possibility stranded and somehow produced a person. The general failure mechanism is understandable; the exact crossing into personhood is unknown and not reproducible on demand.

## THE BLOBS

**What they are:** Magical cleanup crews — effectively janitors for **unclaimed magical waste**. Practitioners know of them, and ordinary blob incidents are regarded as unpleasant but mostly harmless rather than as monster attacks.

**What they clean:**
- **Pruning spillage:** future possibility released by a transaction but never successfully accepted or settled.
- **Unclaimed casting waste:** short-lived waste that can remain when a magical working fails, is interrupted, or releases charge uncontrollably instead of resolving cleanly.

These are different substances that can attract the same cleanup creatures. Ordinary lingering emotional residue from catastrophic destruction of charged art is also distinct and is not automatically blob food.

**What this explains:**
- The Space Between and the Camp archive are not buffets because stored/claimed charge is not unclaimed waste.
- Successful emotional-text casting is ordinarily clean and does not summon blobs.
- Shade is the largest known unresolved pruning-spillage anomaly: a failed prune that took on flesh.
- Pathwell and Shade are not magically cleanly distinguishable. Their signatures overlap closely enough that a blob can follow the ambiguous Pathwell/Shade cleanup signal without resolving which of the two is the actual waste source.

**Arrival:** Response time depends on both signal strength and accessibility. Larger spills are easier to detect and generally produce faster or more reliable responses. Difficult locations slow arrival. Small spills can dissipate before any blob reaches them. There is no fixed numerical trigger or universal delay.

**Targeting:** Blobs go for the unclaimed waste, not for people as prey. They can still injure or absorb someone who is in or coated with the waste, blocks the path to it, or is caught in an unusually large or unstable cleanup event.

**After cleanup:** Once the waste that drew it has been consumed, a blob briefly searches the immediate area for more unclaimed waste. If it finds none, it leaves on its own. It normally does not need to be dismissed or redirected.

**Chapter 1:** The blob reaches Elizabeth's apartment by following the effectively overlapping Pathwell/Shade signature. Shade has followed Pathwell because of the draw; Pathwell has wandered into Elizabeth's apartment while scavenging charged material. Elizabeth is not the reason the blob is there. Pathwell sees the blob breaking down her door, does not know Shade exists, and incorrectly concludes that the blob must be after Elizabeth. Once inside, the blob redirects toward fresh unclaimed casting waste created when Elizabeth physically interrupts Pathwell's already-active cookbook working.

**Climactic cleanup:** Shade is the actual unresolved pruning-waste anomaly; Pathwell only resembles the cleanup target because their signatures overlap. When the climactic blob absorbs Shade, it removes the real waste source and collapses the ambiguous Pathwell/Shade signal at the same time. The blob can then re-check Pathwell and the immediate area, finds no remaining Shade-scale cleanup target, and leaves according to its ordinary post-cleanup behavior. Absorption removes the anomaly; it does not retroactively turn Shade's creation into a successfully settled pruning transaction.

**Sensory signature:** stench of wet-rot / low tide. Amorphous, at least six feet. Dark ooze. The eye wants to slide off them.

## EMOTIONALLY CHARGED RECORDS & ART

### The broad law

Authentic human expression can retain emotional charge. Pathwell's primary discipline is **handwritten emotional-text magic**, but drawings, paintings, carvings, and other personal art can hold related forms of charge.

The branches are related but distinct. Cross-medium workings are possible and significantly harder and riskier.

### What creates charge

For handwritten text, the writer is the primary source of charge. Repeated meaningful handling, use, annotation, inheritance, and emotional connection by later people can deepen and layer it.

Charge can arise from:
- emotional intensity when the work is made;
- personal significance;
- repeated use and handling;
- annotation and inheritance;
- accumulated human history.

Age itself adds nothing. Old works are often strong because they have had more time to accumulate meaning.

Charge can continue accumulating after the original creator dies.

A single intensely meaningful act can establish a strong anchor immediately. More ordinary records can build one gradually through genuine life and use.

Anchored charge fades through neglect only very slowly.

### Authenticity: no manufactured fuel

**Intent cannot manufacture charge.** A person cannot create useful magical ammunition simply by sitting down to write something emotional for future magical use.

A work made for the purpose of becoming a magical resource is effectively a **dead battery**: it never formed the authentic foundational anchor needed to retain useful charge. Later emotion may brush against it, but that charge dissipates rather than taking root.

The magic comes from records of lived human expression, not from attempts to produce fuel.

### Handwriting and other media

For Pathwell's text branch, handwriting is required. Typed and mass-printed text do not carry this form of charge.

Handwriting style itself has no magical significance. Beautiful calligraphy is not stronger than ugly handwriting.

Other art forms can hold charge under the same broad authenticity principle. They are not fully systematized for this story.

### Charge, meaning, and effect

A charged work's usable range is shaped by **both its emotion and its specific meaning/context**. The practitioner works with the grain of the record.

A family recipe infused with care, nourishment, memory, and protection may support protection or restoration. A practitioner cannot simply force it to become an unrelated effect.

A single work can contain several emotions at once. Emotional complexity and contradiction make a working harder to shape safely.

### Skill

Most trained practitioners can sense and make basic use of emotional charge. Shaping it into a precise effect usually requires skill or specialization.

The work supplies charge and character; it does not normally replace technical knowledge. An exceptionally well-matched record can sometimes bridge part of a skill gap.

A practitioner does not need to understand the language to sense or access the emotion. Reading and understanding the content greatly improves control and interpretation.

### Sensing and contact

A trained practitioner can roughly sense the strength and emotional character of a charged work at a distance. Physical contact gives a much clearer read.

**Direct physical contact with the original work is required to activate and consume it.**

Non-practitioners can sometimes sense strongly charged works as unexplained emotion. Extremely powerful works or dense collections can also cause vague sensory flashes.

Elizabeth's overwhelm in the Space Between collection is an ordinary-person reaction to extraordinary ambient charge, not evidence that she is secretly magical.

### Consumption is destruction

**Using a charged work destroys the original work.** Charge and original human expression are inseparable.

A handwritten page, painting, drawing, carving, or other charged artwork is physically consumed, ruined, erased, cracked, burned, dissolved, or otherwise lost when its charge is spent.

Information can sometimes be copied. The original human record cannot.

### Page rule

A practitioner can consume one physically distinct page from a book without consuming the entire book.

They cannot selectively consume a paragraph, sentence, word, or emotional strand from that page. Once activated, the whole page and its entire emotional mixture are committed.

Multiple pages can be used together. Compatible material may reinforce; conflicting emotional material increases interference, feedback, and instability.

Pathwell's museum injury is a direct example: he mixes multiple emotional sources and hurts himself while trying to force a working through the interference.

### Point of no return

Charged text does not activate accidentally. Someone capable of using it must intentionally activate it.

The practitioner needs a general purpose before activation and can refine the effect while the charge is releasing, but only within that general purpose. Some texts are more restrictive than others.

Once activation begins:
- the entire page is committed;
- the practitioner cannot preserve the remainder;
- the practitioner cannot deliberately dump the charge harmlessly;
- forced interruption does not stop the page from consuming.

Loss of control can cause collapse, distortion, backlash, or uncontrolled release.

If a practitioner dies early in the working, the release is likely to become uncontrolled. If the effect was already substantially shaped, it may continue to completion according to the practitioner's final intent.

### The echo

When charged art is intentionally consumed, the activating practitioner temporarily carries an **echo** of the original record.

For handwritten text, this includes imperfect practical/conceptual information and the emotion impressed into the text. Both fade together and become increasingly muddled.

The practitioner does not receive the creator's complete memories — only what was impressed into the record.

A practitioner may rewrite information while the echo remains, but the copy is a transcription rather than a restoration. At most it may carry a faint residual trace; it cannot reproduce the original charge.

**The echo cannot be reused as fuel.**

Other media produce medium-shaped echoes: paintings and drawings tend toward stronger visual/sensory impressions, while text tends toward clearer conceptual/verbal information.

### Recipients and consent

The activator experiences the strongest echo. A recipient can receive weaker emotional, sensory, or meaning fragments if the working is shaped through or onto them.

Consent is not a mechanical requirement. A recipient can be made to experience the emotion carried by a record without agreeing to it.

However, the magic cannot force the recipient to adopt that emotion as their own or compel a specific action. The magic can affect what someone feels; it cannot decide what they do.

A recipient cannot magically block an emotional echo once it has been successfully delivered.

Rarely, exceptionally powerful, traumatic, or personally resonant records can leave lingering emotional aftereffects in practitioner or recipient.

### Emotional archaeology

Strong records can carry fragmentary factual impressions: a face, room, sound, gesture, phrase, or brief action.

These impressions are authentic to lived experience but filtered through the creator's perception, memory, bias, and emotional state. They are not objective recordings.

Most skilled practitioners read history impressionistically. Investigative reconstruction is a rare specialty. Extremely rare masters can reconstruct portions of a charged object's history with surprising specificity.

Specialists can sometimes compare emotional signatures across known works to attribute an unsigned text. At the extreme edge of mastery, a practitioner may directly recognize an individual creator's imprint.

### Foundational core

A charged work develops a **foundational emotional core**.

Later experiences can obscure, complicate, or expand that core, but the original foundation is never erased while the record exists.

A sufficiently skilled practitioner with physical access can always work down through accumulated layers and reach the foundational core.

The core is permanent but not frozen. Later additions can become so deeply integrated that they join what the object fundamentally is.

A core can contain genuine contradictions. Contradiction makes the object harder and more dangerous to use because all of the emotional mixture is present when charge is released.

### Books, separation, and restoration

Every page in a multi-page charged work shares some of the larger work's core while also carrying page-specific history.

Removing or consuming a page permanently changes the larger work's emotional history.

A removed but unconsumed page retains the history it had at separation, then develops independently. It can form a strong secondary identity without losing its original lineage.

If restored to the original book, it begins sharing new history with the book again. The years spent apart remain visible in the record.

Careful restoration can preserve identity. Rebinding, mending, and stabilizing do not inherently erase charge.

There is no fixed percentage at which repaired material stops being the same object. Continuity matters, and some components matter more to the core than others.

A recreated replacement page cannot regain the destroyed original's charge. Once integrated, however, it can accumulate genuine new history and may eventually become part of an expanded foundational core.

Physically splitting a contradictory work may reduce interference if different sections carry different histories, but the pieces retain traces of the original whole.

### Ambient charge

Dense collections of charged works can create a strong ambient emotional field.

Ordinary people may experience emotion, nausea, disorientation, exhaustion, or intrusive impressions. Extreme or prolonged exposure can leave more serious psychological aftereffects.

Practitioners can partially filter ambient charge through training, but not block it completely.

Distance generally weakens ambient charge, though exceptionally powerful objects can be sensed from surprising distances.

Archives can reduce ambient overload through spacing, separation, grouping, containers, cabinets, walls, wrapping, and other physical storage practices. These measures mitigate; they do not eliminate.

### Locating charged objects

A sufficiently powerful charged object can sometimes be sensed and roughly located at a distance.

This is **not a scent trail**. The object does not leave a persistent track behind as it moves. Tracking requires repeatedly reacquiring the object's current signal.

Distance, barriers, and competing charged material interfere.

Practical concealment is possible through heavy enclosure, physical barriers, distance, or surrounding the object with other charged material. No perfect magical invisibility technique is established.

Familiar objects have recognizable emotional signatures. Those signatures evolve as history accumulates but retain their underlying core.

### Ordinary destruction and emotional fallout

If a charged work is physically destroyed without being magically activated, it normally just loses its stored potential. No one receives the echo.

Exceptionally powerful works, or many highly charged works destroyed together, can release charge unpredictably.

Uncontrolled release can affect both mind and environment: emotional waves can hit nearby people, and sufficiently concentrated charge can produce physical magical effects loosely shaped by the emotions involved.

This is the mechanical basis for an archive-scale fire becoming more than an ordinary fire.

Uncontrolled fallout normally burns itself out quickly. Extreme events can leave lingering **emotional residue** for hours, days, or longer.

This residue:
- cannot be harvested or reused as magical fuel;
- can muddy later emotional-text workings;
- cannot be magically cleared and must dissipate naturally.

**Emotional residue is not pruning spillage or unclaimed casting waste.** Keep the systems distinct.

### Backlash

Failed or incompatible workings can harm the practitioner and destabilize the effect.

Backlash can include emotional overload and real physical injury: pain, burns, shaking, weakness, disorientation, collapse, intrusive emotion, permanent injury, or death.

A practitioner may try to collapse a failing working inward and take the backlash onto themselves, but cannot guarantee complete containment.

Whether bodily scars from backlash can themselves hold charge belongs to another branch of magic and remains undefined here.

### Persistent and shared workings

Some workings are instantaneous. Others can be shaped to persist independently until their charge/structure runs out.

Only persistent workings deliberately designed to accept more charge can be reinforced later.

Multiple practitioners can contribute to one working only if it was deliberately structured for shared participation.

The physical activator experiences the strongest echo; other participating practitioners may receive weaker fragments.

A non-practitioner cannot shape emotional-text magic, but can participate physically by carrying, positioning, opening, holding, supplying access, or completing mundane steps.

### Ownership

Ownership and permission do not form a magical barrier. A practitioner with physical access and the necessary skill can use another person's charged work without permission.

The restraint is ethical, cultural, institutional, and personal — not automatic magic.

## FAILED / INTERRUPTED CASTING WASTE

Normal successful emotional-text casting resolves cleanly and does **not** automatically create blob-attracting residue.

A failed, interrupted, or uncontrolled working can leave short-lived **unclaimed casting waste** when released charge does not resolve cleanly into the intended effect. Blobs can respond to this material as part of their cleanup role.

Unclaimed casting waste is distinct from pruning spillage, even though blobs can consume both. It is also distinct from lingering emotional residue left by catastrophic ordinary destruction of charged art.

**Chapter 1 example:** The blob is already at Elizabeth's door before Pathwell activates the cookbook, so the cookbook working did not cause its arrival. After Pathwell activates the recipe, Elizabeth pulls the cookbook away while the page is already committed. That physical interruption destabilizes the release enough to create fresh unclaimed casting waste around the active page/working in Pathwell's hand. When the blob gets through the door, it redirects toward that fresh local mess and lunges for the glowing page/light. The interruption does not make Elizabeth magical and does not retroactively explain why the blob came to the apartment.

## THE SPACE BETWEEN

The Space Between is fundamentally an **archive**: a place devoted first to collecting and preserving strange, significant, and emotionally valuable things.

Its marketplace/shop function is secondary and grew around that archival purpose. Preserved objects, services, information, access, mundane value, and magical value can be exchanged there, but commerce is not the place's deepest function.

A **crossroads function** is layered onto the archive. The Space Between connects places that normally cannot connect, so it can be reached through thresholds and can potentially provide routes toward multiple otherwise separate locations.

The shopkeeper/custodian is a **distinct supernatural entity** tied to the Space Between and its archive. It is not the Space Between itself and is not merely an avatar or face generated by the archive.

A transaction is formally settled when the custodian accepts the bargain. That acceptance automatically causes the Space Between/archive to register the exchange; the custodian negotiates and accepts, while the deeper archival system performs the underlying ledgering. The visible form of that registration, if any, is undefined unless needed.

**Threshold access depends on the threshold, not the person.** There is no universal practitioner-only restriction. Different thresholds may be open to anyone or may require particular knowledge, objects, permission, or conditions. A non-practitioner can use a valid threshold when its own requirements are satisfied.

**Threshold direction is also threshold-specific.** Some connections work both ways, some only into the Space Between, and some only out of it. A threshold may change direction under conditions intrinsic to that threshold; the custodian does not automatically control all exits.

**The Space Between can connect to ordinary places as well as magical ones.** Established anchors are substantially more reliable than temporary or improvised thresholds. Temporary routes can work, but they are more prone to failure, drift, altered conditions, or disappearing altogether.

The archive contains an extraordinary concentration of emotionally charged material, sufficient to overwhelm an ordinary person such as Elizabeth.

The exact origin of the Space Between, the custodian's deeper nature/origin, the full internal geography of the place, and any hard upper limit on its network of thresholds remain deliberately undefined unless the story requires them.

## CAMP CUNNAN

Home of the Cunning Folk — charmers, blessers, oracles, readers, diviners, and related practitioners. A community of wagons, cook fires, an archive of charged works, and practical traditions older than any one resident's explanation of them.

### Movement

Camp Cunnan is a **moving place**.

It can be reached through a mixture of guides, signs, known routes, and established thresholds. No single method is universally reliable.

The Camp moves for reasons even the Cunning Folk do not fully understand. Residents possess practical generational knowledge without a complete cosmological explanation.

Experienced residents can recognize signs that a shift is approaching, but cannot reliably predict the exact timing or destination.

When Camp moves, people and things clearly within it move together. Its boundaries are less reliable; someone entering, leaving, or lingering near the edge can be separated or left behind.

Some durable thresholds tend to reconnect after a move. Temporary routes may fail, drift, or need rediscovery.

### Archive

The archive is both **cultural memory** and **practical magical reserve**.

It preserves emotionally significant records because the records matter, while acknowledging that some may eventually be consumed to meet a living need.

Donation transfers ownership to the archive. Archivists consider donor wishes but are not bound by them. They have the authority to preserve, use, or consume donated works.

A donor has no right to demand a donated work back. The archive may nevertheless choose to sell or trade a work back at its own discretion.

The archive can accept whatever it considers sufficiently valuable in exchange: services, rare objects, charged works, information, favors, labor, access, mundane value, or magical value.

Pruning can technically be accepted, but Camp strongly discourages it and may refuse it in ordinary circumstances.

**Elizabeth gives her diary to the archive voluntarily. She does not perform magic.** The act is a surrender of ownership and a choice to stop carrying the diary as a private tether.

## ELIZABETH — MAGIC BOUNDARY

**Elizabeth does not perform magic.**

She does not prune, imbue, cast emotional-text magic, or possess a hidden special perception ability.

She can own, carry, surrender, donate, position, or provide access to charged objects. She can participate physically in circumstances around a magical working. Those choices are agency, not magic.

She can be affected by ambient charge or by an echo directed onto her because ordinary non-practitioners can experience those effects.

Any draft in which Elizabeth uses the diary as a magical weapon is a continuity error.
