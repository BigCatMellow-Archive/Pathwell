## Q134 — Elizabeth and Pathwell rebuild enough trust through shared restitution work for her final invitation to be a genuine choice — LOCKED B

- During the three-week restitution interval, Elizabeth and Pathwell spend some time working alongside each other on Camp-directed tasks rather than remaining completely separate until the coda.
- This is **not** a reconciliation montage, romanticized forgiveness, or a return to their earlier dynamic.
- Elizabeth observes Pathwell beginning to behave differently in small, practical ways while the work is happening: asking instead of assuming, accepting `no`, allowing other people to define their own needs, following someone else's plan, leaving capable people to handle their own part, and accepting when an irreplaceable loss cannot legitimately be fixed.
- Pathwell's changed behavior matters more than speeches. He does not persuade Elizabeth to trust him again, demand forgiveness, explain her feelings back to her, or use restitution as leverage for renewed closeness.
- Elizabeth can remain irritated, guarded, or uncertain about him during this period. Their relationship becomes **possible again**, not magically repaired.
- The coda's offhand admission of fault remains understated and should land after Elizabeth has already seen behavioral evidence of change; do not replace it with a full apology/reconciliation scene.
- Elizabeth's final `Are you ready?` invitation remains entirely her choice. She is not following because Pathwell possesses something of hers, claims she is important, needs rescuing, or has convinced her to come.
- The intended meaning is that Elizabeth now knows Pathwell much more clearly — including the harm he caused — has seen enough evidence that he is beginning to change how he acts, and independently decides that she still wants the next adventure.
- This prevents the three-week jump from reading as `time passed, therefore everything is fine` and prevents the ending from resetting Elizabeth into the same compliance pattern she began with.
- Exact shared tasks, dialogue, and scene/montage placement remain deferred to manuscript reconciliation.
- No manuscript chapter has been edited.

## Q135 — Elizabeth herself initiates the next adventure; the replacement-cookbook quest is cut — LOCKED A

- Once Pathwell recognizes that another family's old handwritten cookbook is **not** Nana's and cannot replace Elizabeth's lost family history, he sets that substitute cookbook down and **does not** immediately turn it into another object he can earn through a quest or transaction.
- The current coda beat in which the Shopkeeper gives Pathwell a card/quest as payment for that replacement cookbook is therefore superseded.
- Elizabeth arrives at the Space Between with the **next direction/adventure already chosen by her**. The exact destination, problem, lead, or reason can remain for manuscript reconciliation or future-story setup; the important point is that it originates from Elizabeth's own decision rather than being handed to Pathwell first.
- Pathwell does not accept a quest and then have Elizabeth merely accompany or rhetorically lead him. The role reversal should be literal in the story's causal chain.
- The opening relationship pattern is `Pathwell decides there is an adventure and tells Elizabeth to come`; the ending pattern becomes `Elizabeth decides there is an adventure and asks Pathwell to come`.
- The exact final dialogue remains locked and unchanged: `Are you ready?` / `No.` / `Perfect.` No dialogue follows it.
- This completes Elizabeth's `Pulled → Pushing → Choosing` arc through action rather than wording alone.
- It also preserves the coda's replacement-cookbook insight: another family's authentic history can be valuable without being interchangeable with Nana's, and Pathwell demonstrates growth by leaving it alone rather than finding a new way to obtain it.
- Exact setup for Elizabeth's chosen next adventure remains deferred to manuscript reconciliation/future-story planning unless a later causal contradiction requires a specific answer.

## Q136 — Elizabeth's ordinary-life logistics remain intentionally unresolved on-page — LOCKED D

- The manuscript does **not** need to explicitly resolve Elizabeth's apartment, packed boxes, missed meeting, job status, lease, or other ordinary-life logistics before the coda.
- Those details may still exist in her life, and the story should not imply they magically disappeared simply because she entered the supernatural world.
- The ending's required character fact is narrower: Elizabeth is no longer being carried forward by Pathwell's urgency, property leverage, or circumstance. She independently chooses the next adventure.
- Do not add a mandatory `I quit my old life`, apartment-cleanout, job-resolution, or `return to normal` scene merely to close logistical loops.
- Likewise, do not define adventure as automatically superior to ordinary life. Leaving the mundane logistics unstated preserves the possibility that Elizabeth's broader life continues in whatever form she later chooses.
- Q134 and Q135 provide the necessary agency bridge: Elizabeth has observed Pathwell's changed behavior during the three-week restitution period, then arrives with the next adventure/direction already chosen by her.
- Exact ordinary-life consequences can remain off-page/iceberg material unless a later manuscript scene creates a concrete continuity contradiction that requires one of them to be specified.
