## Exact resume point

Ask exactly one question:

> **What ordinary physical condition makes the child need Elizabeth to physically rescue them once she reaches them?**
>
> **A. The recoil knocks the child down and a fallen archive shelf/crate pins one leg.** The child is conscious and frightened but cannot get free alone. Elizabeth has to wrench/lift the obstruction enough to pull them loose, then guide/drag them back out with her injured arm. This gives her a concrete hands-on rescue without making the child gravely injured or adding a magical hazard.
>
> **B. The child is stunned/disoriented by the recoil and smoke but not pinned.** Elizabeth reaches them, gets them upright, and physically guides them out. This is simpler but slightly weaker because the reader may wonder why the child could not follow her voice or crawl out alone.
>
> **C. The child is briefly unconscious from the recoil.** Elizabeth must drag/carry them out. This makes the rescue undeniably necessary, but raises the medical severity and may distract from the object-choice beat.
>
> **D. A fallen shelf blocks the child's route out without pinning them.** Elizabeth reaches the child and helps force a path back through. This is plausible, but risks making the geography feel more complicated than the emotional beat needs.
>
> Audit lean: **A**. It uses only ordinary consequences of the already-established violent recoil and archive contents, makes Elizabeth's physical intervention necessary, and keeps the focus on her running past both books rather than on a new magical threat.
