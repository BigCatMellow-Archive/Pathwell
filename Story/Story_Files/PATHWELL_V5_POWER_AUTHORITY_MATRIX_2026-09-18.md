# Pathwell — V5 Power / Authority Matrix — 2026-09-18

Status: **experimental diagnostic; not manuscript canon**

Purpose: sharpen Pathwell's central flaw by distinguishing kinds of power that the story previously risked collapsing into one word: `authority`.

## Dimensions

- **EXPERTISE** — who knows how the relevant system works?
- **INFORMATION ADVANTAGE** — who knows facts others do not?
- **RESOURCE CONTROL** — who controls objects, routes, tools, access?
- **PRESTIGE / STATUS** — who is trusted/respected because of history?
- **DEPENDENCE** — who needs whom in order to act/escape/understand?
- **DELEGATED AUTHORITY** — who has actually been asked/authorized to decide?
- **ABILITY TO IMPOSE** — who can make a decision effective despite disagreement?
- **CAUSAL RESPONSIBILITY** — who helped create the current problem?
- **ACTUAL DECISION OWNERSHIP** — whose choice is materially being made?

The diagnostic question:

> **Which kinds of power does Pathwell genuinely possess here, and which additional kind is he assuming follows from them?**

Do not use this vocabulary as thematic dialogue.

# 1. Opening apartment

| Dimension | Pathwell | Elizabeth |
|---|---|---|
| Expertise | High relative to Elizabeth | Low |
| Information advantage | High, but incomplete | Very low |
| Resource control | Holds diary/cookbook; controls magical method/escape knowledge | Owns apartment/property; limited practical control once danger appears |
| Prestige/status | None yet with Elizabeth | Homeowner in her own space, but supernatural situation strips practical leverage |
| Dependence | Low-to-moderate | High: needs his knowledge to survive immediate danger |
| Delegated authority | None | None transferred to Pathwell |
| Ability to impose | High because he acts quickly and controls information | Limited |
| Causal responsibility | High but unknown to him | Low |
| Actual decision ownership | Elizabeth owns whether she leaves/follows and what happens to her property; Pathwell owns his own magic/actions | Same |

## Pathwell's mistaken promotion

> **Expertise + urgency + resource control -> assumed right to direct Elizabeth.**

The scene works because he is not faking expertise. The error is the promotion.

# 2. Space Between / Camp supplies

| Dimension | Pathwell | Elizabeth / Camp |
|---|---|---|
| Expertise | High | Elizabeth low; Camp collectively varied |
| Information advantage | High | Elizabeth learning |
| Resource control | Can access Shopkeeper/pruning; controls his own future cost | Camp has the need but did not assign him the task |
| Prestige/status | High in magical world | Elizabeth none; Camp has history with him |
| Dependence | Camp may benefit from him, not necessarily depend entirely on him | Elizabeth no longer dependent once her books/exit return |
| Delegated authority | Supply task is self-appointed | No explicit mandate |
| Ability to impose | High | Others can accept/refuse resulting help depending on context |
| Causal responsibility | None for the supply need necessarily | Shared/institutional |
| Actual decision ownership | Pathwell owns whether to prune his own future and buy supplies | Camp owns how/if supplies are incorporated |

## Why this scene is important

This is an example where self-appointment can be **useful and non-catastrophic**.

The story should not imply:

> unasked help is always wrong.

Instead:

> Pathwell's habit receives enough successful reinforcement that he learns the wrong general rule from many successful cases.

| Ability to impose | Very high | Moderate | Moderate |

| Actual decision ownership | Pathwell owns whether he participates and how he uses his own powers, but not Elizabeth's choice to go | Elizabeth owns whether she attempts the route | Shade owns whether he helps and what he does |

## Important ambiguity

Pathwell may also have a legitimate interest in **bystander/public safety** if the route attempt can endanger others.

That does not automatically make him sole decision-maker.

This is where the story should be hardest:

> **real expertise + real danger + real responsibility still may not equal unilateral authority.**

## Pathwell's mistaken promotion

> **Risk assessment + causal responsibility + ability to intervene -> assumed sole veto.**

# 5. Reintegration crisis

| Dimension | Pathwell | Shade | Camp / Mama Baga | Elizabeth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expertise | Highest on pruning / origin mechanics | Highest on his own lived continuity and what he is willing to risk | Highest on Camp's own safety/operations | Strong witness to prior failures; lower magical expertise |
| Information advantage | Historical/magical | Subjective continuity/identity | Camp conditions/resident risks | Relationship/history with both |
| Resource control | Can construct reintegration working | Controls consent/cooperation only if respected | Controls Camp space/resources | Limited |
| Prestige/status | Very high historically | Growing local social standing | Institutional/local authority | Relational standing |
| Dependence | Camp may depend on his expertise; Pathwell emotionally depends on being useful | Depends on others not treating him as waste/problem | Residents depend on collective safety | Depends on magical actors for some logistics |
| Delegated authority | May be asked to help solve danger, but **not automatically authorized to erase/reintegrate Shade** | Has direct claim over risks to his continued separate existence | Has authority over what risks Camp accepts on its premises | No sole authority, but legitimate participant/affected witness |
| Ability to impose | Extremely high | Low-to-moderate | Can order stop/evacuation, but may not physically prevent Pathwell | Limited |
| Causal responsibility | Very high | None for origin | Responsibility for residents | None for origin |
| Actual decision ownership | Pathwell owns his own contribution/cost; Camp owns Camp-risk decisions; Shade owns whether to accept existential intervention | Shade | Camp | Elizabeth owns her participation/intervention choices |

## Pathwell's central failure

He possesses:

- expertise;
- causal responsibility;
- prestige;
- ability to impose;
- possibly partial delegated responsibility to help.

He promotes these into:

> **authority to decide the existential tradeoff for Shade and the collective risk for Camp.**

That is the cleanest late-story form of his flaw.

---

# Restitution diagnostic

Pathwell's growth should not be:

> he stops using power.

It should be:

> he distinguishes the kind of power he has from the kind he does not.

Therefore restitution needs contrasting cases.

## Case A — legitimate delegated authority

Someone explicitly asks Pathwell to take charge.

He should still be fast, decisive, irritating, and excellent.

This proves the story is not curing competence.

## Case B — expertise without decision authority

Someone asks for advice/help, hears his recommendation, and chooses differently.

Pathwell lets the different choice stand.

## Case C — ability to fix without permission

Stansbury's scar.

Pathwell may be capable of doing more.

Stansbury says:

> **Let it be.**

Pathwell does.

## Case D — Elizabeth

Elizabeth needs his expertise to reach or accomplish something.

She chooses the destination/goal.

He provides capability without converting necessity into control.

This prepares the ending.

---

# Failure condition

If the manuscript reduces all of these to the repeated lesson:

> ask permission first,

the model has become too simple.

The deeper distinction is:

> **Competence, responsibility, prestige, dependence, and capability can all be real without collapsing into one person's ownership of the decision.**
