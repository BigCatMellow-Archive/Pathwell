## Shoulder-stabilization letter carries love expressed through letting go — LOCKED D

- The handwritten Camp letter Shade uses to stabilize Elizabeth's shoulder has a clear emotional/meaningful grain of **loving someone while accepting that they must remain separate / be allowed to go**.
- Its exact human relationship and biography remain unspecified on-page. Do not turn it into a miniature explanatory backstory unless later evidence requires one.
- The useful emotional structure is: **support without possession; hold without trapping; care without converting love into ownership.**
- That grain legitimately supports Shade's narrow set-and-hold working: keep Elizabeth's shoulder where it needs to be, prevent worsening, then release control to Elizabeth and the healer rather than magically erasing the injury.

- Avoid a deathbed/permission-to-die letter because that would foreshadow Shade's eventual death too directly.

## Existing-story check: the consumed letter necessarily leaves an echo in Shade

The established emotional-text rules say the activating practitioner temporarily carries the strongest **echo** of the consumed record: its emotion plus imperfect meaning/information. Shade therefore cannot use this symbolically matched letter and experience nothing from it.

That creates a handling question rather than a mechanics question. The echo can deepen the Camp sequence, but it must not magically make Shade forgive Pathwell, accept reintegration, surrender his refusal, or supply the correct moral answer. Emotional-text magic can affect what someone experiences; it cannot decide what they do.

## Audit Question 84 — How visible should the letter's `letting go` echo be in Shade after he stabilizes Elizabeth?

### Options

**A. Barely visible.** The echo exists mechanically but stays almost entirely below the prose surface; perhaps only a momentary stillness or altered expression suggests it.

**B. Briefly perceptible but not explained.** Shade gets a short, recognizable wash of love-without-possession / letting-go emotion after the working. He does not explain the letter or draw a lesson from it. The echo becomes subtext for the tree-line conversation that follows, while his choices remain his own.

**C. Make the echo explicit and informational.** Shade receives a concrete fragment from the original writer's situation and recognizes the parallel to himself. This makes the symbolism clear but risks turning the letter into an author-delivered lesson.

**D. Suppress the echo entirely for this working.** This would conflict with the existing rule that the activator carries the echo of consumed emotional text.

### Audit lean: **B**

B lets the symbolic choice register without making the magic instruct Shade what to believe. The letter can put him briefly inside the emotional truth of caring without keeping, then leave him to face Pathwell with the same unresolved fear, refusal, and need for independent personhood he already had. A is mechanically valid but makes the symbolic letter largely invisible to the reader; C overexplains and risks making Shade's later stance feel magically coached; D violates the established echo rule.
