## Audit Question 124 — Does using one of Stansbury's pre-imbued weapons consume its magical effect?

### A. The imbuement is persistent/reusable rather than one-shot; ordinary use can trigger the effect repeatedly, though a tool may eventually need maintenance or re-imbuing without the story tracking a numerical charge count.

This lets the Chapter 5 demonstration remain a real demonstration rather than spending a weapon just to prove it works. It also lets Stansbury sell durable crafted tools rather than magical consumables. His income can come from making new tools, replacing worn/damaged ones, repairs, re-imbuing, and different specialized effects.

The story need not define an exact number of uses or a visible charge meter.

### B. Each weapon is magically single-use; the first successful trigger spends the imbuement and leaves an ordinary foam object behind.

This creates very clear limits and a natural repeat-sales model, but the Chapter 5 tree demonstration then consumes one finished weapon before the actual danger. Stansbury would need to use fresh weapons afterward, and Elizabeth's dagger would become ordinary immediately after its rescue use.

### C. Each weapon carries a small finite number of activations.

This supports repeated use while preserving depletion, but introduces charge bookkeeping the story currently does not otherwise need. Even without exact numbers, readers may reasonably start asking how many uses remain in each weapon during action scenes.

### D. Stansbury can make both one-shot and reusable tools depending on how he imbues them.

This is flexible and plausible for a craft discipline, but expands the imbuing system substantially for a distinction the current plot does not require.

### Audit lean: **A**

A is the least bookkeeping-heavy rule and best matches the current staging: Stansbury can demonstrate a sword and put it back into usable inventory, while Elizabeth later simply uses the dagger when needed. It also keeps the tools feeling like crafted equipment rather than ammunition. B is clean but adds replacement choreography; C creates an implicit charge meter; D adds flexibility without a present story need.

## Q124 follow-through — imbued weapons are reusable but not established as infinitely permanent — LOCKED A, REFINED

- A finished Stansbury weapon can trigger its prepared effect repeatedly through ordinary physical use.
- One successful activation does not consume the imbuement or reduce the object to an ordinary foam weapon.
- The story does **not** establish that an imbuement lasts forever or provides unlimited power without upkeep.
- Wear, physical damage, time, or accumulated magical strain can eventually weaken a tool enough to require repair and/or re-imbuing.
- There is no fixed activation count, visible charge meter, or action-scene bookkeeping requirement.
- Stansbury's income can therefore include new tools, specialized effects, replacements, repairs, and re-imbuing existing equipment.
- This remains compatible with the locked charged-record rules because imbuing is a distinct magical discipline; no rule says these weapons manufacture or indefinitely recycle authentic emotional-text charge.
