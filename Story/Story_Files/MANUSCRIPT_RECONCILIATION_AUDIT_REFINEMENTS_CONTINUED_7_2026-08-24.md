## Audit Question 58 — How much of the old two-part locating mechanic survives?

**A. Restore the full old mechanism.** A handwritten letter carrying longing establishes the personal connection, requiring Pathwell to focus on a shared memory/experience that Elizabeth might also be touching mentally; once the connection catches, grocery lists provide the directional/location-tracking function. Both types of text are consumed through use.

**B. Simplify to grocery lists alone.** The lists embody searching for/finding specific things and can be shaped directly into a locating spell. Cleaner, but it removes the old shared-memory/longing requirement and makes locating people substantially easier.

**C. Keep the shared-memory requirement but drop the love letter.** Grocery lists can locate only after Pathwell supplies a sufficiently specific shared-memory anchor. This preserves difficulty while simplifying the number of magical ingredients.

**D. Make grocery lists directional rather than precise.** They can tell Pathwell/Stansbury which way Elizabeth is or which route to take, but not generate an exact dot/location. This weakens the older version's precision and leaves an ordinary search component at the end.

Audit lean: **A**. It is the most existing-story-first solution. The old manuscript already supplies both the magical ingredients and the limitation, and the shared-memory requirement keeps locating a person from becoming trivial utility magic.
