## 4. Q127 — active direction, with a required terminology correction

User chose Q127 **A**, replacing `protects people by keeping them small` as Stansbury's primary flaw.

The intended new direction remains:

- Stansbury is competent, observant, cautious, and often correct about Pathwell.
- He has spent a long time occupying the safer role of observer, critic, adviser, and supporting player while Pathwell remains the person who compulsively intervenes and appoints himself fixer.
- Stansbury can let being correct about Pathwell substitute for putting himself in the position where he must act, choose, fail, and bear consequence.

### Correction from the user's subsequent challenge

Do **not** describe Pathwell as habitually `taking responsibility`.

Established Pathwell canon is the opposite distinction:

- Pathwell habitually **acts, intervenes, pays, fixes, and appoints himself the solution**.
- He often does **not** take moral/accountability responsibility for what he caused or for the agency he overrides.
- His willingness to bear a cost can become his justification for control rather than evidence of accountability.

Therefore the earlier Q127 shorthand `Pathwell turns responsibility into control` is withdrawn as inaccurate.

A safer working contrast is:

- **Pathwell turns fixing/helping into control.**
- **Stansbury can turn justified criticism/prudence into disengagement.**

This is a clarification of Q127, not a new claim that Pathwell has historically been accountable.
