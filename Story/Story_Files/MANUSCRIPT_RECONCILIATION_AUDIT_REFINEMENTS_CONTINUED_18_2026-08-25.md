## Audit Question 77 — How should Nana's surviving cookbook be positioned when Elizabeth enters the burning archive?

### Context

The diary has just been physically spilled into the entrance aisle by the recoil. The child is farther inside. Elizabeth must also encounter Nana's handwritten cookbook while it is still saveable, recognize it instantly, and continue past it toward the child. The cookbook has been Camp property since Elizabeth surrendered it for the earlier healing; only one charged page was consumed, so the surviving book has remained in the archive.

### Options

**A. The cookbook is already stored on an accessible front reserve shelf.** The archive keeps it near the front because surviving charged pages still have practical restorative/protective value as well as cultural value. The recoil/fire exposes the shelf enough for Elizabeth to see and reach the book, but does not need to fling it into her path. She passes the diary in the aisle first, then the cookbook on the shelf.

**B. The recoil also knocks the cookbook into the entrance aisle.** Both objects become physically obvious through the same blast. This is easy to stage, but having the recoil conveniently produce both exact family objects risks making random destruction look like magic deliberately arranging Elizabeth's thematic test.

**C. The cookbook is stored deeper in the archive than the child.** This would be plausible archival storage but breaks the locked rescue choreography: Elizabeth must have a genuine chance to save both books before she reaches the child.

**D. The archive child or archivist happens to be carrying the cookbook when the recoil hits.** This places it naturally in motion but requires a coincidental reason for someone to be handling Elizabeth's specific family cookbook at that exact moment.

### Audit lean: **A**

A uses an already-established fact rather than another accident: the archive is also a practical magical reserve, and the surviving cookbook still contains charged handwritten pages. Keeping it on a front reserve shelf gives it a credible reason to be accessible. It also makes the two object encounters physically distinct—**the diary has been thrown into disorder; the cookbook is still where Camp deliberately kept it**—which reduces the sense that the blast has staged Elizabeth's choice for her. The only staging requirement is that the shelf be close enough to the entrance route that Elizabeth can plainly reach the cookbook before continuing to the child.
