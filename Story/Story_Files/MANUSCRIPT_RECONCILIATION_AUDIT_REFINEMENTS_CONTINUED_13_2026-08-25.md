## Audit Question 70 — How much should Shade's Camp treatment actually repair?

### Context

Pathwell's museum ward throws Elizabeth into a display case and seriously injures her shoulder/upper arm. She reaches Camp with Shade. The current Chapter 12 lets Shade use a charged letter to treat the injury, but Elizabeth must still be hurt enough that the sling, pain, and limited arm matter during the archive rescue later that morning.

### Options

**A. Shade stabilizes/sets the injury but does not heal the damage away.** Preserve the current basic scene: Shade recognizes that the shoulder will set wrong, waits for Elizabeth's consent, and uses the charged letter to put the joint/structure into a stable position. The Camp healer then checks/supports it, and Elizabeth remains in a sling with substantial pain, bruising, weakness, and restricted use through the climax.

**B. Preserve the current scene as near-complete magical healing.** The sling is mostly precautionary and Elizabeth is only mildly impaired afterward. This conflicts with the locked climax, where the museum injury must materially complicate freeing and supporting the pinned child.

**C. Remove Shade from the treatment and let the Camp healer handle everything.** This avoids adding healing competence to Shade, but discards an existing scene where Shade demonstrates practical inherited knowledge and waits for Elizabeth's consent before touching the injury.

**D. Shade only diagnoses/corrects the healer verbally; the Camp healer performs the actual magical treatment.** This preserves Shade's practical knowledge without making him the caster. It is mechanically clean, but reduces the contrast between Shade waiting for Elizabeth's permission and Pathwell's earlier refusal to respect her judgment.

### Audit lean: **A**

A reconciles the current scene with the already-locked climax rather than replacing either. `That'll hold` naturally supports stabilization rather than cure, and the sling already signals unresolved damage. It also preserves the consent contrast without requiring a new rule: Shade waits for Elizabeth's nod before treating her, while Pathwell's museum mistake came from overriding what she had just told him. The cost is that the manuscript must make the limits of the treatment unmistakable; `settled back into itself` can describe setting/stabilizing the shoulder, but subsequent pain, weakness, bruising, and restricted motion must remain visible so readers do not read the magic as full restoration.
