## Existing-story check: the next errand is still tied to acquiring a cookbook the coda has just established is not Elizabeth's

Existing coda/canon now establishes several things at once:

- Nana's cookbook is a unique handwritten family record; a different family's old handwritten cookbook may be charged, but it cannot replace Nana's history.
- Preserve/adapt the coda exchange `That isn't mine.` / `No.` / `Good.` specifically as a rejection of treating one family's history as interchangeable with another's.
- Q96 keeps the other family's cookbook as the object that tempts Pathwell toward the old pruning shortcut, but Pathwell chooses not to test whether the contemplated prune would settle.
- Q97 now makes Elizabeth the person who chooses the next errand and asks Pathwell `Are you ready?`.

The current manuscript still has the shopkeeper say, in effect, `Do this for me, and you can have the book.` If preserved, Elizabeth's newly locked decision to take the errand would mean that immediately after establishing `That isn't mine`, she chooses the quest whose reward is acquiring that same substitute cookbook for Pathwell.

That muddies the coda's object logic. If the book is not a replacement and Pathwell has just declined the old shortcut, the next adventure does not need to remain a delayed method of obtaining it.

## Audit Question 98 — What is the relationship between the next errand and the other family's cookbook?

### A. None. Pathwell leaves the cookbook behind; the shopkeeper then presents a separate possible errand, which Elizabeth chooses.

The cookbook completes its function before the ending: it tempts Pathwell toward the familiar shortcut, he chooses not to force the unanswered pruning question, and Elizabeth's `That isn't mine.` confirms that another family's history is not a replacement for Nana's. The book stays in the archive/shop. Only after that beat is complete does the shopkeeper present an unrelated address card/opportunity. Elizabeth chooses that next direction and asks Pathwell `Are you ready?`.

### B. The errand still earns the cookbook, but Pathwell wants it only as a useful charged record, not as a replacement for Nana's.

This can technically preserve `That isn't mine`, because the book would be a magical resource rather than a sentimental substitute. But it shifts the final movement back toward acquiring consumable magical material and weakens the coda's rejection of Pathwell's old transactional habits.

### C. The errand earns the cookbook for the Camp archive rather than for Pathwell or Elizabeth.

This would connect the job to Pathwell's restitution and make the book preservation-oriented rather than personally substitutive. But Q93 already establishes three weeks of concrete Camp restitution, and adding another restitution objective at the exact ending makes Elizabeth's final chosen direction partly an extension of Pathwell's debt rather than her own open-ended choice.

### D. Remove the other-family cookbook from the coda entirely and keep only the unrelated errand.

This would simplify the ending, but it removes the live object that currently stages Pathwell's temptation to prune, his choice not to test the uncertainty, and the `That isn't mine.` echo. Those functions would then need another setup.

### Audit lean: **A**

A lets each beat do one job. The other family's cookbook tests whether Pathwell still treats loss as something to replace or buy around; he leaves it behind. The address card then becomes a genuinely new possibility rather than another transaction for an old object. Elizabeth can choose that possibility without accidentally choosing to obtain a substitute she has just identified as not hers. B is mechanically possible but turns the ending back toward harvesting charged material. C over-burdens the final adventure with restitution. D throws away a useful coda test that already has support.
