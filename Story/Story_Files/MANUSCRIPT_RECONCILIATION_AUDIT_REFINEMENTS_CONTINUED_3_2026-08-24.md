## Elizabeth names Camp; Shade opens the route — LOCKED A

- **Elizabeth says `Camp` first at the museum.** The destination is her own stated choice, not Shade's suggestion.
- Shade then uses his own magical knowledge/tooling — including the already-established folded sheet/anchor knowledge — to open the museum graffiti-wall route to Camp.

- Elizabeth remains entirely nonmagical. Her agency is expressed through choosing the destination and choosing to step through the route once Shade has opened it.
- This preserves the distinction between **agency** and **magical capability**: Elizabeth does not need supernatural power for the choice to belong to her.
- The stale Chapter 11 sequence where Shade says `Camp Cunnan` first and Elizabeth presses the folded sheet to the wall as the apparent activator must be revised later.
- Intended sequence: **Elizabeth chooses Camp → Elizabeth says `Camp` → Shade opens the established anchor → Elizabeth chooses to cross.**

## Shade does not answer for Elizabeth after `I don't need to be rescued` — LOCKED A

- After Elizabeth tells Pathwell **`I don't need to be rescued,` Shade says nothing.**
- Cut the stale follow-up **`She means it. She came in here on her own.`** The second sentence conflicts with the locked museum blocking, and neither sentence is necessary for Elizabeth's statement to function.
- Elizabeth's boundary stands on its own. Pathwell must respond to what Elizabeth herself said rather than receiving a second character's interpretation or certification of it.
- **This is not a male/female thematic beat.** Do not frame the moment as Elizabeth needing independence from `men`, male authority, or a gendered rescue dynamic.
- The governing principle is character agency: no person's stated choice requires another character — of any gender — to validate, translate, or certify that it is real.
- Pathwell and Shade happen to be the other characters in this scene. Their gender is not the reason Shade stays silent.
- This should remain consistent with the broader story structure: Shade owns his own refusal in the climax; Elizabeth owns her own choices; Pathwell must learn to hear choices he does not control.
