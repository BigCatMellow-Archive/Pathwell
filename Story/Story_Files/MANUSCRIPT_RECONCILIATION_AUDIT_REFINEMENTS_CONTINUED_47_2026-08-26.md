## Audit Question 110 — When should Camp actually receive the large Chapter 3 book shipment?

### A. The books have already been delivered/released to Camp on Pathwell's Space Between credit before the Chapter 3 prune; the Chapter 3 transaction settles the current large invoice plus older debt.

Pathwell hears that Camp needs books, arranges the order himself, and uses his established credit to get the material moving without waiting for payment. He later returns to the Space Between and settles the accumulated account by pruning. Chapter 4 can briefly show the newly arrived crates/books already being unpacked or integrated at Camp.

This preserves `Your bill has been paid in full`, uses the later `extensive credit` line as continuity rather than incidental banter, and avoids inventing a new bulk-delivery mechanism inside the Chapter 3 scene. It also fits Pathwell's habit: solve the problem immediately, put the cost on himself, deal with the price afterward.

### B. The books are released only after the Chapter 3 payment and transferred to Camp through an established threshold/trade route.

Camp canon already allows established thresholds, so this is mechanically plausible. Chapter 4 would show crates arriving or being unloaded. However, it requires establishing a specific Space Between-to-Camp bulk trade route that the current story does not otherwise need.

### C. Pathwell physically escorts or carries the shipment through thresholds himself.

This makes his involvement highly visible, but a large quantity of books makes the logistics cumbersome and risks turning the transition into transport choreography rather than character/story movement.

### D. Leave shipment timing and delivery entirely off-page.

The purchase can remain understandable in retrospect, but Chapter 3's large cost would still lack a visible practical payoff and the reader may not connect the pruning transaction to Camp's new books.

### Audit lean: **A**

A uses two existing manuscript facts instead of adding a new system: Pathwell has an ongoing ledger relationship with the Space Between, and the shopkeeper later describes his credit as `extensive`. It also sharpens the flaw without making Camp complicit: nobody asks Pathwell to take on the job or the cost; he hears the need, gets the books moving on his own account, then spends future possibility to clear the bill. B is viable but adds a dedicated logistics route; C over-stages the shipment; D leaves the causal payoff weak.
