## Existing-story check: Stansbury currently destroys a museum artifact to heal Pathwell without the story acknowledging that cost

At the end of current `Chapter_11.txt`, after Elizabeth and Shade leave through the graffiti-wall threshold, Pathwell remains injured on the museum floor. Stansbury then finds a handwritten Civil War soldier's poem in the exhibit, presses it to Pathwell's chest, activates it, and uses the working to heal/stabilize him.

Locked emotional-text canon says **using a charged work destroys the original work**. A handwritten page consumed for magic cannot survive as the same historical artifact. The current scene therefore means Stansbury destroys an authentic museum-held soldier's document without permission in order to repair Pathwell.

A repo search did not locate a support lock establishing this as an intentional Stansbury moral compromise, giving him permission to consume the artifact, or assigning later restitution/consequence for the destruction.

This matters because the surrounding chapter is already about Pathwell treating other people's property, choices, and consequences as material he can decide around. Leaving Stansbury's museum consumption unexamined accidentally repeats that exact ethic immediately after Pathwell's failure.

## Audit Question 104 — What should happen with Stansbury's post-museum healing of Pathwell?

### A. Remove the magical healing entirely; Stansbury gives ordinary aid and Pathwell carries the museum injury forward into Camp/the climax.

Pathwell's recoil can leave him battered, sore, bruised, cut, or otherwise impaired without making him unable to travel. Stansbury helps him up and handles immediate practical first aid rather than consuming another person's historical record. This preserves consequence and requires no new magical object or permission structure.

### B. Preserve magical healing, but Stansbury uses a personal charged record of his own that is established/seeded elsewhere.

This avoids destroying museum property and gives the healing a real personal cost to Stansbury. However, it requires planting a suitable authentic record and may introduce a new object primarily to erase Pathwell's immediate physical consequence.

### C. Keep the museum poem consumption, but make it an intentional ethical compromise with later acknowledgment/restitution.

Stansbury chooses his brother over preservation and destroys the artifact knowingly. This could expose his own compromised relationship to Pathwell, but it creates another moral debt and museum-consequence thread very late in the book.

### D. Keep the scene as written and treat the museum poem as expendable.

This conflicts with the world rule that authentic charged records are destroyed when used and with the museum's obvious role as custodian of historical originals.

### Audit lean: **A**

A preserves the consequence of the museum catastrophe instead of spending another irreplaceable human record to patch Pathwell immediately afterward. Pathwell does not need to be fully healed to reach Camp; he only needs to remain functional enough to travel and participate. B is mechanically sound but adds setup mainly to remove a consequence. C could work if Stansbury's ethical compromise were a desired subplot, but nothing in current support asks for one and it would create cleanup obligations at the end of the book. D leaves a direct rules/ethics contradiction.
