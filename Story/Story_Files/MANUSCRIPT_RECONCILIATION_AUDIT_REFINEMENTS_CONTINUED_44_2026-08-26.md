## Existing-story check: Chapter 3's clean pruning transaction does not clearly identify what Pathwell actually buys

Current `Chapter_03.txt` is the story's important early example of a **successful, ordinary pruning transaction**. Pathwell enters the Space Between because he wants to stop at a magical bookstore/archive, bargains with the custodian, offers `potential to sell`, completes a prune, physically shakes from the cost, and receives the result.

The object staging is unclear, however:

1. Before the prune, the shopkeeper produces a **leather-bound tome** and places it on the counter.
2. Pathwell completes the pruning transaction.
3. The shopkeeper says his bill is paid in full, asks `paper or plastic?`, and slides Pathwell an **envelope**.
4. Neither the tome nor the envelope has a clear later function in the current manuscript.

Later support intentionally relies on Chapter 3 as the clean baseline proving that pruning works normally before the climactic malformed failure. The exact purchase therefore should be legible enough that the reader understands this is an ordinary accepted bargain rather than mysterious ritual machinery.

Existing support fixes the purpose of the beat — **a casual clean prune showing Pathwell treats future possibility as routine currency** — but does not assign a required later plot function to either the tome or envelope.

## Audit Question 107 — What should Pathwell's early clean prune actually purchase?

### A. A useful charged record/material he wants but does not urgently need; keep the transaction deliberately casual.

Pathwell goes to the Space Between for magical material, finds something useful enough to want, and readily spends future possibility because pruning is his normal shortcut. The purchased object does not need to become a major plot device; its function is to establish his baseline relationship to pruning. The tome can serve this role, while the unexplained envelope can be removed or treated only as ordinary packaging during manuscript reconciliation.

### B. Something essential to the immediate plot — for example access, defense, or a necessary tool for the blob problem.

This gives the purchase a stronger causal purpose, but makes Pathwell's pruning easier to justify. The early beat would demonstrate `he prunes when necessary` rather than the more important flaw that he reaches for pruning casually because it is convenient.

### C. Information specifically about the unexplained blob/Elizabeth situation.

This would connect the transaction directly to the mystery and could explain why Pathwell stays involved, but it risks prematurely giving the Space Between transaction investigative importance and weakens the later mirror/Ask progression where Pathwell gradually realizes the problem points back to him.

### D. Preserve the current tome-plus-envelope ambiguity.

The mechanics can technically remain mysterious, but because this is the clean comparison case for later pruning failures, unclear purchase staging makes it harder for the reader to understand what a normal pruning bargain looks like.

### Audit lean: **A**

A best supports Pathwell's starting flaw. He is not pruning under extraordinary necessity; he is spending pieces of his future to acquire something useful because he can. That makes the later crisis about his habit rather than merely about one disastrous emergency choice. It also requires no new plot object: the leather-bound tome can simply be the purchased charged material, while the envelope becomes removable staging rather than unexplained transaction machinery.
