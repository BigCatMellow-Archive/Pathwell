# Pathwell — V5 Reader-State / Disclosure Ledger — 2026-09-18

Status: **experimental diagnostic; not manuscript canon**

Purpose: distinguish story truth, character knowledge, reader belief, and reader expectation across the central mystery/reveal chain.

This exists because a reveal can fail even when the facts are technically planted if the reader is carrying the wrong model.

---

## Governing distinctions

Track separately:

- **STORY TRUTH** — what is actually true.
- **ELIZABETH STATE** — what Elizabeth reasonably knows/believes.
- **PATHWELL STATE** — what Pathwell knows/suspects.
- **SHADE STATE** — what Shade knows/suspects.
- **READER MODEL** — what the intended reader should currently believe or keep open.
- **OPEN QUESTION** — what uncertainty should pull the reader forward.
- **RETRIEVAL / REVISION CUE** — what later beat should reactivate or change the earlier model.

Do not over-engineer uncertainty. The reader should usually be uncertain for a human reason, not because narration hides something Elizabeth would obviously notice.

---

# 1. Opening apartment / first crash

## Story truth

- Pathwell lingered after Elizabeth's welcome party looking opportunistically for a charged handwritten object.
- He did not come looking for Elizabeth specifically.
- The blob pressure is fundamentally Pathwell/Shade-related.
- The interrupted sugar-cookie working creates fresh local spill that redirects the already-present cleanup manifestation.
- Pathwell's claim that Elizabeth/apartment is "marked" contains real concern but exaggerated certainty.

## Elizabeth state

She knows:

- a strange man is still in her apartment;
- he has taken her diary/cookbook;
- something impossible and dangerous appears;
- Pathwell acts as though he understands it;
- his explanation is the only available explanation.

She should not have evidence that she is cosmically special.

## Pathwell state

He knows:

- the danger is real;
- the charged objects are unusually potent;
- he does not actually have enough evidence to know why the blob is there.

He suspects Elizabeth or the objects may be involved and overstates that suspicion because he wants immediate compliance/movement.

## Shade state

Shade is not yet a social presence in the scene.

He is part of the unresolved causal background.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Pathwell is probably more knowledgeable than Elizabeth, but he may be improvising and overselling his certainty.

The reader should **not** confidently conclude:

> Elizabeth is chosen / magically marked / the center of the supernatural problem.

The reader may provisionally accept Pathwell's explanation because he appears competent.

## Open question

What exactly brought the impossible danger here?

## Retrieval / revision cue

Later:

- bar/mirror turns suspicion toward Pathwell;
- diner reveal reclassifies the opening;
- Ask reclassifies Pathwell's certainty and information management.

---

# 2. Food / aftermath

## Story truth

Pathwell still lacks the full causal answer.

Elizabeth is following partly because he has her property and partly because he appears to be the only person who understands what just happened.

## Elizabeth state

She is angry, destabilized, and dependent on his knowledge.

She may begin enjoying him despite herself.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Pathwell is funny, capable, and difficult to resist; dependence on him is understandable.

Do not make him look transparently manipulative this early.

## Open question

Is Pathwell actually protecting Elizabeth, using her, or simply moving too fast to distinguish the two?

---

# 3. Space Between

## Story truth

Pathwell's pruning is real, costly, technically useful, and not inherently corrupt.

Camp genuinely needs materials.

Nobody specifically assigned Pathwell to solve the supply problem.

## Elizabeth state

She sees direct evidence that Pathwell can do astonishing, useful things.

She gets her property back and can leave.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Pathwell's self-appointed behavior often works and benefits people.

This is critical.

The reader should not yet interpret every self-appointment as abuse.

## Open question

What does Pathwell's habit of solving cost him, and why is he so comfortable assuming responsibility?

---

# 4. First Camp visit

## Story truth

Camp has real history with Pathwell.

Some people have legitimately asked him for help and benefited.

The cookbook can heal cleanly without causing blob trouble.

## Elizabeth state

She sees evidence that Pathwell's reputation is not invented.

She voluntarily gives the cookbook for a concrete need.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Pathwell's usefulness is socially real. The eventual critique cannot be reduced to "he should stop helping."

## Open question

Where is the actual boundary between help, expertise, responsibility, and decision ownership?

Do not answer this in prose.

---

# 5. Stansbury / investigation

## Story truth

The abnormal cleanup pattern is becoming increasingly Pathwell-adjacent.

Stansbury is better at procedure and uncertainty management than Pathwell.

## Elizabeth state

She notices the brothers share an information tier that excludes her despite relying on her practically.

## Pathwell state

He is increasingly uneasy that the problem may point back toward him.

He is not ready to say that plainly.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Something about Pathwell is increasingly suspicious, but the exact mechanism remains unknown.

The reader should begin revising the opening explanation without yet having the full answer.

## Open question

What is Pathwell not saying?

---

# 6. Smaller adventure / investigation

## Story truth

Pathwell's decisiveness is legitimately useful here.

Elizabeth's contribution materially matters.

The investigation should produce the clue/need that leads to the bar.

## Reader model

Desired:

> These two are good together.

This is important reader-state work.

The coming rupture should feel like damage to something worth keeping, not liberation from an obviously bad pairing.

---

# 7. Bar / mirror

## Story truth

Pathwell sees Shade clearly enough to understand that the problem points toward himself.

Elizabeth does not receive enough information to identify Shade.

## Elizabeth state

She sees Pathwell react strongly to something and then bolt.

She later saves him from the blob manifestation.

## Pathwell state

This is the first high-confidence internal recognition:

> the mystery is about me.

He may still not understand exactly how.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Pathwell has just seen evidence that terrifies him and is withholding it.

The reader should **not** yet receive a clean visual/identity explanation of Shade if doing so would make the diner recognition redundant.

## Open question

What did Pathwell see, and why does it make him run?

---

# 8. Bar aftermath / drive / second crash

## Story truth

Pathwell and Stansbury know enough that withheld information materially affects the decision Elizabeth is being asked to execute.

## Elizabeth state

She has just saved Pathwell and reasonably believes she has earned full standing in the situation.

Instead, she remains operationally useful but informationally subordinate.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Her frustration is justified; Pathwell's fear is also real.

Avoid:

> obvious oppressor versus obviously correct Elizabeth.

The reader should understand why Pathwell wants to keep moving even while recognizing why Elizabeth refuses.

## Open question

Will Pathwell voluntarily stop controlling the frame?

The answer here is no.

---

# 9. Wrong Pathwell -> diner

## Story truth

Shade is not impersonating Pathwell.

Resemblance, Elizabeth's shock, and her adaptive habits create the temporary misidentification.

Shade knows he originated from Pathwell but has incomplete historical knowledge.

## Elizabeth state

At first:

> something is wrong.

Then:

> this man is not Pathwell.

Then:

> the original danger was not centered on me.

## Shade state

He genuinely wants Elizabeth to know the truth.

He also wants her reaction to pressure Pathwell.

## Reader model

Desired sequence:

1. wrongness;
2. active curiosity;
3. accumulating mismatch;
4. explicit model revision;
5. recontextualization of the opening.

This should function more as **recognition and reclassification** than a gotcha twist.

## Open question

What exactly did Pathwell do that created Shade?

---

# 10. Tracking rewind / Ask

## Story truth

Pathwell and Stansbury use a passive working to locate Elizabeth.

Pathwell chose pruning when help—not pruning—was requested.

## Elizabeth state

She now has enough information to ask the human question underneath magical jargon.

## Reader model

Desired:

> The technical explanation is no longer the important explanation.

The Ask should simplify, not expand lore.

## Retrieval / revision cue

The phrase sequence should reactivate:

- Pathwell's self-appointed Camp supply behavior;
- opening certainty;
- bar withholding;
- the reader's growing sense that expertise and decision authority are not identical.

---

# 11. Museum

## Story truth

Shade's presence / current magical instability creates a credible risk.

Elizabeth still chooses Camp and asks Shade to help.

Pathwell's danger assessment contains legitimate evidence.

His error is converting that assessment into unilateral decision authority.

## Elizabeth state

She knows Pathwell has reasons to be afraid.

She still rejects his right to override her choice.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Pathwell may be right about the danger and still wrong about who gets to decide.

This is one of the most important reader states in the book.

If the reader simply thinks Pathwell is obviously irrational, the scene fails.

---

# 12. Camp recovery / Shade ordinary life

## Story truth

Shade has already diverged through post-creation experience.

He has relationships, habits, jokes, work, preferences, and future expectations Pathwell never lived.

## Elizabeth state

She no longer needs a philosophical argument for Shade's personhood.

She experiences it.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Shade is now simply someone in the story.

Do not keep asking the reader to vote on whether he is a person.

## Open question

Can the danger be resolved without making Shade's continued existence the only cost-bearing variable?

---

# 13. Alternatives / waiting

## Story truth

No clean option preserves every legitimate good.

Pathwell genuinely attempts restraint at least once.

At least one alternative produces partial relief and a new cost.

## Reader model

Desired:

> There may not be a clean answer.

The reader should feel conflict among:

- Shade's autonomy;
- Camp safety;
- responsibility;
- the cost imposed on other residents;
- Pathwell's causal responsibility;
- the danger of continued delay.

This is where the story should stop feeling like a simple lesson.

---

# 14. Forced reintegration

## Story truth

Pathwell is not ignorant of the boundary.

He reaches a genuine cancellation point and continues.

## Reader model

Desired:

> I understand why he thinks action is necessary; I also understand that he has once again promoted his judgment into authority.

The reader should not need the story to explain this sentence.

---

# 15. Fire / climax resolution

## Story truth

The selected Shade outcome remains unresolved until mechanics support it.

## Reader model

Desired:

Mixed rather than singular:

- anger;
- fear;
- grief;
- absurd practical energy;
- admiration for competence;
- recognition of cost;
- uncertainty about whether any option could have been clean.

Do not make the climax read as the moment the novel proves its thesis.

---

# 16. Restitution

## Story truth

Pathwell's changed behavior must become legible across contexts.

## Elizabeth state

She needs evidence that he can behave differently **with her**, not merely around her.

## Reader model

Desired:

> Improvement is real but does not erase damage.

The reader should gradually recover the possibility that Elizabeth could want his company again.

Reciprocal humor/play can be a useful retrieval cue for the early companionship state.

---

# 17. Ending

## Story truth

Elizabeth has chosen the next direction.

Pathwell freely accepts the invitation.

His pruning capability need not be tested here.

## Reader model

Desired:

> uncertainty remains, but the direction is chosen rather than imposed.

The final exchange should reactivate the opening exchange automatically:

> "Ready?"
> "No."
> "Good."

Recognition should be enough.

No thematic explanation after it.

---

# Failure conditions to check during drafting

- the reader believes Elizabeth is chosen/magically special;
- the reader identifies Shade too early;
- the diner reveal feels like a retcon rather than reclassification;
- Pathwell is obviously wrong before the museum decision;
- Pathwell becomes morally simple during alternatives/waiting;
- Shade keeps needing personhood proof after Camp life has established it;
- callbacks require narration to explain that they are callbacks;
- final invitation arrives before the reader has recovered the possibility of companionship.
