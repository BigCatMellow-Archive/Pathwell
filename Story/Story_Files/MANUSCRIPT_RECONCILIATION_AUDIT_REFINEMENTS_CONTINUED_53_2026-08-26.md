## Existing-story check: Stansbury's current climax leadership contradicts his locked arc turn

Current `character_bible.md` defines Stansbury's flaw as a controlling form of protection: he keeps people small because he believes limiting their risk is how he protects them. His intended climax turn is explicit: after entering the archive fire and being burned, he `takes over leadership not by controlling but by trusting people to act`.

Current `Chapter_12b.txt` resolves the beat differently. After Stansbury comes out burned, he immediately points and directs specific actions — `that wagon, water there, get the mother and the boy back from the smoke` — and the narration emphasizes that Camp people begin `doing what he told them`.

That staging makes the payoff **successful command/control**, which is close to the behavior his arc is supposed to move away from. The contradiction is not whether Stansbury can lead; it is what kind of leadership represents his change.

A repo search found no later support deliberately redefining his turn as command authority.

## Audit Question 116 — What should Stansbury's leadership turn look like during the archive fire?

### A. Keep Stansbury active as a leader, but make the turn coordination-through-trust rather than command/control.

Stansbury can identify immediate dangers, connect people/resources, take responsibility for his own part, and then trust Camp members to execute within their own competence without micromanaging them. The exact prose can be deferred, but the scene should not culminate in `everyone finally does what Stansbury tells them`.

This preserves both parts of his arc: he stops being passive/dependent on Pathwell, but he also does not replace that passivity with controlling everyone else. He becomes useful by participating, coordinating, and trusting.

### B. Keep the current command structure and redefine Stansbury's turn as accepting authority/leadership.

This makes the manuscript climax easier to preserve, but changes the character-bible endpoint. His growth would become `I finally take charge` rather than `I trust other people to act`, which is a materially different resolution to his over-protective/control flaw.

### C. Have Stansbury refuse to direct anyone and focus only on his own rescue task.

This avoids control but risks turning `trust` into passivity. It also loses the intended leadership payoff after he enters the fire.

### D. Give emergency leadership entirely to Mama Baga/Camp and remove Stansbury's leadership turn from the climax.

This is plausible for Camp structure, but leaves Stansbury's established arc without its intended endpoint; his fire rescue and scar would remain, but the control/trust problem would not resolve on-page.

### Audit lean: **A**

A preserves the distinction the character bible is already trying to make: **leadership does not have to mean control**. Stansbury should still act decisively and accept responsibility, but his change is visible when he stops treating other capable people as people he must constrain or direct every move. B resolves his passivity but reinforces the other half of his flaw. C risks repeating passivity. D abandons the arc payoff rather than reconciling it.
