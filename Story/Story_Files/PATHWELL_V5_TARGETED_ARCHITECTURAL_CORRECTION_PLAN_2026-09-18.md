# Priority order

## P0 — Solve the severance / cleanup contradiction

This is the only current issue capable of invalidating the entire Shade-survival climax.

Current problem:

- pruning Pathwell's future claim to Shade can plausibly affect Pathwell;
- it does not yet explain present signature separation;
- it does not explain why Shade ceases to be unresolved cleanup material;
- under the current rules, cleaner separation may make Shade easier for blobs to target.

Required outcome:

Choose one of three honest directions.

### Direction A — Derive survival from already-established rules

Only keep Shade survival if a non-bespoke rule can be established **earlier in the novel** that makes the climax inevitable rather than convenient.

The rule must explain:

1. what unresolved condition makes Shade cleanup material;
2. what Pathwell's action changes in that condition;
3. why the change affects Shade without Pathwell deciding Shade's future for him;
4. why the same mechanism could in principle have existed before the climax.

### Direction B — Change the survival mechanism

If pruning cannot honestly perform the required operation, keep the philosophical goal but use another already-established system.

Any replacement must still preserve:

- Shade's choice;
- Pathwell acting only on something genuinely his to surrender;
- permanent consequence;
- no magical moral referee.

### Direction C — Abandon Shade survival

If A/B require a climax-only exception, restore a version where cleanup removes Shade.

Do not keep survival merely because it is thematically attractive.

This decision should be made **before** fixing lower-priority pacing details.

## P1 — Architect the alternatives / waiting chain

This is the most important earnedness correction.

The future architecture must name at least:

1. the first alternative;
2. why it is chosen;
3. what it genuinely improves;
4. what new cost/limitation it creates;
5. a moment where Pathwell wants to override but stops;
6. the next alternative;
7. a concrete worsening event produced despite restraint;
8. why a reasonable Pathwell now believes continued waiting is irresponsible;
9. why Shade still reasonably refuses reintegration;
10. why Pathwell proceeds anyway.

The chain should be written in explicit causal form:

> therefore -> but -> therefore -> but.

No generic montage language such as `they try other things` is sufficient.

---

## P2 — Recalibrate Pathwell's danger judgments

The museum and reintegration must remain morally difficult.

### Museum

Before Pathwell overrides Elizabeth, the reader needs a **credible threat signal** associated with Shade / the threshold / the current magical instability.

Pathwell should have enough evidence that intervention is understandable.

The mistake is not:

> Pathwell invents danger from nothing.

The mistake is:

> real danger exists, but Pathwell converts his risk assessment into unilateral authority.

### Reintegration

The same rule applies at a larger scale.

By the time Pathwell forces reintegration:

- Camp has experienced concrete harm or near-harm during the waiting period;
- at least one alternative has genuinely failed;
- Shade's refusal is still real and understandable;
- doing nothing is no longer morally neutral.

This preserves ambiguity.

---

## P3 — Turn dwell functions into stories

Do not protect every V5 chapter-function.

For each proposed dwell interval, require:

- somebody wants something now;
- somebody/something resists;
- the scene changes a practical relationship/state;
- the exit creates or alters the next problem.

Likely combinations:

### Camp first-visit + Camp exists

Combine unless a distinct active Camp conflict emerges.

The reader can learn routines while Elizabeth is participating in a real Camp problem.

### Shade established-person + diary/archive life

Likely overlap.

Elizabeth's diary choice, Shade's ordinary Camp obligations, cookbook/archive afterlife, and the known archive object can coexist inside an active community scene.

### Restitution tests

Do not make one scene per lesson.

Choose a smaller number of scenes where multiple restraint tests collide naturally.

---

## P4 — Define the smaller adventure

The pre-bar smaller adventure must do four jobs naturally:

1. show Elizabeth and Pathwell functioning well together;
2. demonstrate a case where Pathwell's decisiveness is legitimately useful;
3. give Elizabeth a materially important contribution;
4. produce the clue/need/destination that causally leads to the bar/mirror sequence.

Do not add a disconnected side quest merely to create friendship time.

---

## P5 — Define the post-bar exclusion that causes the crash

Before the crash, make the practical chain explicit:

- where they are going;
- why that destination matters;
- why Elizabeth is driving;
- what decision she is being asked to execute;
- what information Pathwell/Stansbury are withholding;
- why that withheld information materially affects whether she would choose the route.

The crash should result from a real decision conflict, not an argument staged in a convenient moving car.

---

## P6 — Make Camp emotionally specific

Select now:

### One recurring routine

Example categories:

- breakfast ritual;
- card table;
- archive check-in procedure;
- children's chore;
- communal repair work;
- moving-day ritual.

The exact choice should recur naturally.

### One specific non-Elizabeth archive record/object

It must:

- appear before danger;
- have an ordinary Camp relationship around it;
- not exist solely so its destruction can be sad.

### Cookbook afterlife

Show at least one ordinary post-healing use/presence of Nana's cookbook before the fire.

Do not merely state that Camp used it.

---

## P7 — Give Shade a concrete expected future

Before the unresolved crisis takes over, Shade needs a plan that extends beyond the current plot.

It should be small enough to feel ordinary.

Possible functions:

- a place he wants to visit;
- work he agreed to do next week;
- a standing card rematch;
- something he wants to learn;
- a person he intends to see;
- an object/project that is becoming his.

The purpose is not symbolic foreshadowing.

The purpose is for Shade to begin behaving as though **tomorrow belongs to him**.

---

## P8 — Earn Elizabeth choosing Pathwell again

Restitution must include relationship-specific evidence.

At least one post-catastrophe scene should involve Elizabeth and Pathwell directly in a situation where:

- old Pathwell would have taken over;
- Elizabeth's preference genuinely matters;
- Pathwell behaves differently without turning it into a performance;
- they are also capable of enjoying each other again.

The final invitation should then feel like:

> Elizabeth wants another adventure with this changed-but-still-Pathwell person.

Not:

> Elizabeth has judged that Pathwell is now ethically acceptable.

---

## P9 — Reassign the Milo choice's thematic weight

Keep the fire choice.

Do not use it as Elizabeth's ultimate self-direction proof.

Primary functions:

- person over objects;
- acceptance of irreversible loss;
- ordinary courage without magic;
- consequences remain consequences.

Elizabeth's central self-direction proof remains distributed across:

- choosing to continue after recovering her books;
- turning the wheel;
- choosing how to respond to Shade;
- choosing Camp;
- rejecting Pathwell's museum override;
- choosing the next adventure.

---

## P10 — Hide the design

The following are author-side concepts and should not become explicit manuscript conclusions:

- `whose decision is this?`;
- jurisdiction/domain language;
- `origin explains Shade; origin does not own Shade`;
- Camp as preservation of possibility;
- Pathwell as centuries of narrowed selfhood;
- Elizabeth as self-direction thesis;
- severance as philosophical answer.

The climax especially must not become:

> Pathwell states the correct principle -> casts the correct metaphorical spell.

The reader should infer the principle from behavior.
