## Audit Question 120 — What should happen to the Chapter 3 leather-bound tome and envelope now that the purchase is a physical stack of Camp books?

### A. Remove both as obsolete transaction props; stage the purchased Camp books themselves at/behind the counter and release them after the prune settles the account.

The shopkeeper has the Camp order gathered in carryable stacks/bundles. Pathwell's prune settles the current order plus old debt. The books then become physically available for Pathwell and Elizabeth to carry onward. Elizabeth later places her recovered diary/cookbook atop her assigned stack and follows Pathwell.

This makes the object staging say exactly what the transaction is doing and removes two unexplained props.

### B. Remove the tome but keep the envelope as an ordinary receipt/itemized statement showing the old balance and current order are paid.

This can give `bill paid in full` a tangible account artifact, but the story does not currently need Pathwell to retain a receipt and the envelope risks becoming another prop with no later function.

### C. Keep the leather-bound tome as one especially valuable book within the Camp purchase and keep the envelope as a receipt, while also adding the larger stacks around it.

This preserves more current imagery, but it gives one book disproportionate staging importance even though no later plot function has been established for it.

### D. Make the tome/envelope magical claim tokens that represent or release the larger book order.

This could preserve both props, but adds abstract logistics to a transaction that now works more clearly if the actual books are simply present and carried away.

### Audit lean: **A**

A aligns the physical staging with the newly locked story function. The chapter no longer needs symbolic stand-ins for the purchase: the reader should see that Pathwell paid heavily, see the Camp books that payment secures, and then see Elizabeth choose to help carry them onward. B and C preserve props without a downstream need; D invents a logistics system to protect obsolete staging.
