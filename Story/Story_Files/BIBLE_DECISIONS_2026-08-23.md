## 1. Climax — Elizabeth's non-magical rescue choice

### Child over diary — LOCKED

- **During the archive fire, Elizabeth gets a genuine opportunity to save either her diary or the child already present in the current `Chapter_12b` staging, and she chooses the child.**
- Elizabeth does **not** activate, spend, burn, or otherwise use the diary as magic. She does not use the diary to shield, repel, redirect, or destroy magical waste.
- The diary is destroyed by the archive fire because Elizabeth leaves it behind in order to physically rescue the child.
- This is not a ceremonial rejection of the diary or a theatrical `burn the past` gesture. Elizabeth already surrendered the diary earlier; the climax tests that surrender through action rather than symbolism.
- The child remains a relative stranger. Elizabeth chooses the child because a living person in immediate danger matters more than recovering the irreplaceable object.
- This deliberately echoes the earlier cookbook sacrifice: Elizabeth again gives up something irreplaceable for someone who is not important because of status, destiny, or a special relationship to her.
- **Stansbury's separate climactic turn remains intact.** He still chooses to enter the burning archive, is permanently burned, and receives a scar Pathwell cannot undo. Elizabeth's rescue of the child must not replace or absorb Stansbury's own choice.
- The current `Chapter_12b` draft therefore contains useful staging material in the child rescue, but its diary-as-magical-tool sequence is stale and must be removed during manuscript reconciliation.
