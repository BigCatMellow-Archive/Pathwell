# Agency Ladder

**[[Elizabeth]]'s visible climb from passive to active. Each rung costs more than the last. No skipping.**

---

## The Rungs

### 1. Lo Mein (Chapter 2)
Chooses to stay with Pathwell. Costs nothing material. First choice she's made.

### 2. Books on the Stack, Through the Door (Chapter 3)
She came to get her cookbook back. Instead, she puts her books on top of Pathwell's stack and carries them into his world. Chooses uncomfortable over comfortable. Costs her the meeting, her job, her routine. She could cobble together borrowed clothes, chug coffee, and be at that meeting. She walks through the door instead. The dry cleaning ticket is already gone (blew away in Ch2 — respect that moment).

### 3. Cookbook to Heal Mama Baga (Camp Cunnan)
An elder she can look in the eye needs healing. The cookbook is full of love — the strongest healing magic. Elizabeth gives it up knowing it will be consumed. Costs her grandmother's love, the sugar cookies, the golden light. Permanent.

### 4. Uses Her Own Diary as a Weapon
The pivotal moment. Instead of handing the diary to Pathwell (giving power to someone else, watching), she uses it herself. She feels every moment she ever felt invisible — every swallowed anger, every fear. Her OWN emotions become power. The only character who doesn't borrow someone else's feelings.

⚠️ **Tension with thesis:** The novel's thesis requires Elizabeth to matter *without* power. The diary burns in the archive fire and she runs past it. See [[Thesis and Controlling Ideas]].

### 5. Runs Past the Burning Cookbook (Archive Fire)
Pathwell's forced prune ignites the archive. Whatever remained is burning. Elizabeth runs past her past to save children. Not a realization — an action. **No magic, no weapon, no leverage.**

### 6. "Are You Ready?" (Ending)
She initiates the next adventure. She's ready before Pathwell is. The roles have flipped. The agency ladder is complete.

---

## Rules for the Ladder

- **Every rung must be a choice, not a reaction.** Responding well to pressure is growth, but initiating action is real agency.
- **Each step must cost slightly more than the last.**
- **There's a ceiling on sacrifice.** At some point, giving things away stops being a higher rung. The next level is INITIATING rather than RESPONDING.
- **Feelings are fuel, not engines.** Excitement, curiosity, and comfort are not choices. Agency requires action.
- **If the protagonist is being dragged, the option is wrong.** Test every scene: who is choosing and who is being dragged?
