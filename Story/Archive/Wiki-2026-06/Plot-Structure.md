# Plot Structure

The plot follows a therefore/but chain. Every scene must cause the next one. If the connection between two chapters is "and then," one of them is in the wrong place.

---

## Act I — Hook & Rules

### Chapter 1
**Pathwell breaks into Elizabeth's apartment BECAUSE he needs her cookbook.**

THEREFORE he uses it to fight the blob (sugar cookie protective spell — the benchmark scene). BUT the apartment is now marked and destroyed. Elizabeth can't go home. She's stuck with this stranger because she has nowhere else to go.

Ends on: "Ready?" / "No." / "Perfect." — comedy after crisis, earned by emotional breathing room.

### Chapter 2
**Elizabeth is at the dumpling stand — not because she chose to follow, but because she was dragged.**

She has no agency. The laundry ticket blows away (last piece of normality, gone — RESPECT THIS MOMENT, don't undo it). Quiet transition at the plastic table. Pathwell's sigh and "Oh fine, you can have a dumpling." Deceleration.

THEREFORE Elizabeth makes her first choice: the lo mein. She orders it. She stays. Ends on: "He still has it" — referring to the cookbook. Her first act of agency is decided: she stands and goes not to follow Pathwell but to reclaim what is hers.

### Chapter 3
**Elizabeth follows Pathwell to the bookstore BECAUSE he still has her grandmother's cookbook and her diary.** She wants them back. That's a character reason, not a logistics reason.

She confronts him about knowing her nickname and having the cookbook. He takes her to [[Locations#the-space-between|The Space Between]]. She experiences the threshold crossing (sensory, physical, overwhelming). Fainting. Waking to Boots. Love note healing. Awful coffee. The Shopkeeper. Pathwell prunes to pay — the Keeper notes he doesn't have much left.

Elizabeth sees her books on the counter next to Pathwell's stack. She could take them and go home. She could borrow clothes, chug coffee, make her meeting. Instead, she puts her books on top of Pathwell's stack and carries them through the door into the forest. She chooses uncomfortable over comfortable. Her job, her meeting, her routine — gone.

## Act II — Camp, Recruitment, Early Pressure

### Camp Cunnan
Pathwell goes to camp BECAUSE he needs to deliver books as contingency — if the blobs get him, the camp needs supplies. (This is his flaw in action: managing contingencies, controlling what happens after he's gone.) Elizabeth goes BECAUSE she's climbing the ladder — choosing the unknown over the comfortable.

Elizabeth meets [[Supporting Characters#mama-baga|Mama Baga]]. Sees the community, the archive. It starts feeling comfortable — **dangerous for her.**

### Cookbook Sacrifice
Mama Baga needs healing. Elizabeth holds the cookbook — love, protection, sugar cookies. She knows using it will consume it. She gives it up to heal Mama Baga. She can look Mama Baga in the eye. This is the rung: someone else's life over her last connection to her grandmother. Permanent cost.

### Stansbury Recruitment
Pathwell needs more help. Goes to recruit [[Stansbury]]. Tense history between them. "Chance to kill me" joke. Stansbury comes but doesn't trust Pathwell. Tries to keep Elizabeth safe by limiting her involvement (his flaw). Elizabeth pushes back — she's tired of being managed.

### Elizabeth's Agency Proof
Bar scene. Blob appears. Elizabeth gets absorbed. She cuts herself out using a Nerf dagger (imbued by Stansbury). Proves she can affect outcomes. She's not furniture. BUT — this draws the blobs' attention to her specifically. Agency has consequences.

---

## Midpoint — Crash + Shade Separation

### Escalating Pressure
Blobs chasing all of them. Pathwell and Stansbury cope badly — drinking, arguing. Not generically drunk — drunk BECAUSE of something specific (establish the cause). Elizabeth is sober and furious. They're making decisions about her life while wasted. She's furniture again.

### The Crash
Elizabeth grabs the wheel not to drive the car but to prove she exists in it. She wants control, not collision. The crash is unintentional — agency with unintended consequence. Echoes "it was the crash that woke her." She reached for agency and got consequence.

### Shade Intervenes
Shade, tracking them, casts protection at impact and takes Elizabeth. He's interested in her BECAUSE she chose. The most deterministic character in the story is drawn to genuine free will.

---

## Act III — Shade's Framing + Museum + Convergence

### Diner Scene
[[Shade]]'s core accusation: "He saved you from a mess he brought to your door." One sharp point, not a list. Elizabeth genuinely doubts Pathwell. She stays with Shade — not because she believes him entirely, but because at least Shade is honest about what she is to him.

⚠️ Risk: the diner accusation scene carries a risk of characters articulating theme too cleanly.

### Elizabeth's Choice While with Shade
She must make at least one meaningful choice. She cannot just be transported. **(NEEDS DEVELOPMENT)**

### Museum Scene
The dramatic question: Elizabeth chooses who she goes with, and why. Pathwell shows up. She chooses to go back to him — NOT because she forgives him, NOT because Shade is wrong. Because she recognizes that Shade's honesty is still manipulation. He uses truth as a weapon the same way Pathwell uses charm as a shield. **She rejects BOTH their framings of her.**

The fight follows. Pathwell overreaches trying to protect Elizabeth (his flaw). Internal overload — NOT explosion. Elizabeth gets hurt as collateral of his control.

Shade, his plan disrupted, portals to Camp Cunnan. The draw compels him toward confrontation regardless.

---

## Act IV — Camp Pressure Cooker + Climax

### Convergence
Everyone at Camp Cunnan. Elizabeth needs healing. Shade heals her from the archive — competent and unsettling. Camp doesn't recognize Shade at first. Camp begins to trust him. BUT Shade is exposed by his draw tell.

### Confrontation
[[Blobs]] arrive, sensing Pathwell, Shade, and Elizabeth. They go for immobile Elizabeth.

### Pathwell's Break
Tries to [[Pruning|prune]] to lure blobs away. **Pruning CANCELS** — he doubts his own control for the first time. "I can't control the cost anymore." He **forces it** by injecting anger from an intense text. The forced pruning **TEARS**. The archive ignites.

### Elizabeth's Moment
The archive is burning. Her grandmother's cookbook is in there. The camp's histories. Letters, journals, lives. **Elizabeth RUNS PAST her past to save children.** Not a realization. An action. The theme demonstrated through what she does under pressure.

### Stansbury's Moment
Sees the fire first. Snaps Pathwell out of his spiral. Takes over leadership — not by controlling, but by trusting people to act. His flaw, overcome in crisis.

### Shade's Death
Still demanding admission. Eye contact with Pathwell. The draw stops. Relief. Happiness. He **NEVER** gets the confession. Presence is enough.

---

## Final Stretch

**Pathwell denies fault.** Won't admit what he did.

**Private retry at The Space Between.** Goes alone. Tries to prune to buy back the cookbook. Fails quietly. And instead of rage — relief. The crack of humility.

**Offhand admission to Elizabeth.** Not a speech. Not a breakdown. Just an offhand comment while doing something else. "It was my fault." Thrown away, almost.

**Shopkeeper's exchange.** "Do this for me and you can have the book." An adventure. A quest.

**Ending:**

> Elizabeth: "Are you ready?"
> Pathwell: "No."
> Elizabeth: "Perfect."

She leads. He follows. The roles have flipped. The [[Agency Ladder]] is complete.
