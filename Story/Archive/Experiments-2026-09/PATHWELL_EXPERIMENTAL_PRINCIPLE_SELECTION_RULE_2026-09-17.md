# Pathwell — Experimental Principle Selection Rule — 2026-09-17

Status: experimental editorial guidance, not manuscript canon.

## Decision

The existing Pathwell writing principles are **not individually sacred merely because they already exist**.

For the experimental rewrite, preserve only principles that still improve the story under the new architecture.

The governing rule is:

> **Preserve the underlying craft principle when it still works; revise or discard historical wording, assumptions, plot assignments, and character functions that belonged to an older version of the novel.**

Examples:

- `Write the Sequel` still strongly serves the novel and remains a hard constraint.
- `Therefore, But — Never And Then`, scene value turns, character wants, the agency ladder, incomplete answers, earned big moments, theme through action, rules becoming sacred once chosen, breathing room, subtext, and the chapter necessity/deletion tests still serve the experimental version.
- The older `Antagonist Is a Mirror` wording does **not** require Shade to remain the villain or `Pathwell if Pathwell doesn't stop`. Preserve the useful craft function—major opposition should expose or counter a character's governing lie—while allowing Shade's new role as consequence/emergent person.
- Old magic-specific rules may be redesigned during the experimental architecture phase. Once the chosen experimental system is promoted into canon, those new rules become binding.
- Old character or plot requirements that weaken the revised story may be removed rather than preserved out of loyalty to prior planning.

## Selection test

For every inherited principle or non-negotiable, ask:

1. Does this still make the current story better?
2. Does it reinforce the intended reader experience, character arcs, tone, causality, or thematic pressure?
3. Is the valuable part a general craft principle, or merely an old implementation of that principle?
4. If removed, is the experimental novel recognizably worse?
5. Does preserving it force contradictions with stronger discoveries made during the current revision?

If the answer shows that only the old implementation is obsolete, keep the principle and redesign its expression.

If the principle itself no longer improves the story, drop it.

## Short version

**Do not preserve history for history's sake. Preserve what still earns its place.**
