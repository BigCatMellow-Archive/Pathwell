# Pathwell — Experimental Writing Contract — 2026-09-17

Status: experimental architecture guidance, not manuscript canon.

Purpose: keep the experimental/foundational rewrite subordinate to the story's established writing principles. The experimental version may change plot, character roles, magic mechanics, or philosophy, but it should not become a lore-delivery novel or discard the craft laws that made `Pathwell` feel like `Pathwell`.

## Authority relationship

The top-level `Writing-Principles.md` remains the upstream writing contract.

This file is an **overlay for the experimental version**, explaining how those principles apply when older story-specific assumptions have changed.

The planning documents may know exactly how pruning, Shade, Camp, blobs, and jurisdiction work.

The manuscript should reveal only what characters would naturally perceive, need, misunderstand, argue about, or suffer consequences from.

---

# Primary law: Write the Sequel

`Writing-Principles.md` defines this correctly:

> The sequel assumes you were there. Characters don't explain gravity and they shouldn't explain magic. The reader catches up by watching, not by being taught.

Treat this as a hard constraint on the experimental rewrite.

The foundational work is **author knowledge**, not dialogue.

Do not create scenes whose real purpose is to teach the reader:

- how pruning mathematically works;
- why handwritten objects contain charge;
- how blobs classify magical residue;
- what Camp's philosophy is;
- why Shade's identity is philosophically distinct from his origin;
- what `jurisdiction` means as the book's moral framework.

Instead, let the reader infer those things from use, failure, argument, habit, and consequence.

Examples of the intended approach:

- Pathwell prunes before Elizabeth understands what pruning is.
- Stansbury can object to the cost without explaining the entire mechanism.
- Camp residents treat bizarre archived objects as shelving problems, not lore opportunities.
- A blob's behavior teaches its rules more effectively than a taxonomy speech.
- Shade demonstrates separateness by wanting, choosing, remembering, and changing in ways Pathwell does not.
- Nobody needs to say `competence does not create jurisdiction`. The plot should prove it.

Elizabeth is an outsider, but being an outsider is not permission for tutorial dialogue. Her questions should often receive partial answers, impatient answers, contradictory answers, answers aimed at the immediate problem, or no answer at all.

The reader is allowed to be behind.

# Experimental application of the established principles

## Therefore, But — Never And Then

This becomes even more important in the experimental plot.

The new causal spiral should make the middle feel inevitable:

Pathwell solves an immediate problem **therefore** the overlap narrows; **but** narrowing strengthens the Pathwell/Shade draw; **therefore** cleanup pressure rises; **therefore** Pathwell is tempted to narrow again.

If a chapter exists because the outline says it is time for Camp, the museum, a diner, or a magic demonstration, redesign it around a character want and causal consequence.

## Every Scene Turns a Value

Every scene must change something meaningful: safety, trust, authority, possibility, belonging, knowledge, independence, or relationship.

A scene that only adds lore fails even if the lore is excellent.

## Show the Iceberg's Tip / One Unexplained Thing Per Chapter

The experimental rewrite is building a deeper magic system. That is a reason to explain **less**, not more.

Use casual references to old events, tools, scars, places, rituals, debts, failed systems, and people who never enter the plot.

Do not resolve every reference.

Pathwell should feel ancient because the world keeps implying a life larger than this book, not because he summarizes that life.

## The Body Knows First

Especially important for pruning and narrowing.

Do not explain `Pathwell has fewer possible futures` before the reader feels the effect.

Let narrowing appear physically and behaviorally first: a route that no longer feels available, an option he cannot quite imagine taking, a moment of stillness where alternatives should exist, bodily resistance before intellectual recognition.

Likewise Elizabeth should feel the cost of choosing before she formulates what choice means.

## Characters Want Things

Elizabeth may be confused, but `understand what is happening` is not enough as her scene engine.

Give her concrete wants: retrieve the cookbook, leave, stay, get an answer, stop the car, protect someone, force Pathwell to answer one specific question, keep Shade at Camp, save Milo, choose the next route.

Pathwell, Stansbury, Shade, and Mama Baga should also enter scenes wanting things that can conflict.

## Agency Is a Ladder

Preserve the law; revise the old label.

Experimental Elizabeth is not simply `passive -> active`. She begins adaptive and overly open-ended.

Her ladder becomes approximately:

1. choose a small preference when compliance is easier;
2. choose to continue into uncertainty;
3. surrender something meaningful by her own decision;
4. oppose how others are directing her;
5. turn the wheel and accept unintended consequence;
6. seek Shade on purpose;
7. choose where she stands when no option is safe;
8. initiate action during the archive catastrophe without magical leverage;
9. choose the next adventure and invite Pathwell.

Every rung must still cost more, alter the plot, and be a choice rather than a reaction.

Do not make every Elizabeth choice correct. Agency includes responsibility for bad choices.

## The Lie and the Truth

Preserve as pressure, not speeches.

- Pathwell: `If I can solve it and pay the cost, the decision is mine.`
- Elizabeth: `If I keep adapting and avoid closing doors, I can avoid the cost of choosing.`
- Stansbury: `If I do not intervene, I am not responsible for the outcome.`
- Shade: initially fears that origin determines identity.

The climax should break these beliefs through behavior.

## Don't Explain the Metaphor Before Performing It

This is essential to the new pruning philosophy.

Do not have anyone explain that pruning represents closing doors, that Shade represents emergence, or that Camp preserves possibility before the story has made those ideas tangible.

The image/action comes first. Interpretation, if it comes at all, comes later and incompletely.

## Incomplete Answers Build Worlds

Keep this aggressively.

Not every question about Shade should be answered.

Nobody has seen his exact case before, so certainty would be dishonest anyway.

Different experienced characters can disagree about what they think is happening without one immediately being identified as the author's correct lecturer.

## Breathe Between Punches

The experimental version carries heavier philosophy and larger consequences. It therefore needs ordinary life more, not less.

Use food, chores, travel, cards, strange jobs, minor annoyances, brotherly bickering, Camp routines, and useless arguments as emotional breathing room.

This is also where Shade becomes a person and where the reader learns to enjoy Pathwell rather than merely analyze him.

## Locations Are Characters

The Space Between, Camp, Stansbury's spaces, the diner, museum, and archive must each create behavior that could not happen identically elsewhere.

Camp especially should demonstrate preservation through what it physically does, not through a mission statement.

## Subtext Over Text

The new philosophy creates a temptation to let characters debate it cleanly. Resist that.

Pathwell rarely says the true reason he is acting.
Stansbury can call caution `procedure` when fear is underneath it.
Shade can demand practical independence instead of making a speech about personhood.
Elizabeth can ask one concrete question that exposes the moral problem without naming the theory.

`The Ask` works because Elizabeth keeps asking what was actually requested, not because she delivers a thesis about authority.

## Promise, Progress, Payoff

The opening promises:

- strange funny danger;
- Pathwell as irresistible problem-solver;
- Elizabeth being pulled into something much older than she understands;
- a Pathwell-shaped mystery;
- costly tactile magic;
- an irreverent adventure with real consequences.

The middle must keep paying those promises while deepening them. Do not let the book become a philosophical chamber drama after the midpoint.

## Antagonist Is a Mirror — experimental reinterpretation

Do **not** preserve the old story-specific claim that Shade must be the villain or `Pathwell if Pathwell doesn't stop` merely because it appears in the old wording.

Preserve the deeper craft function: **major opposition should expose a character's lie by reflecting or countering it.**

In the experimental version:

- Shade is primarily consequence/emergence, not villain;
- Pathwell's repeated attempts to resolve Shade expose Pathwell's own flaw;
- Stansbury mirrors the opposite error of Pathwell;
- Elizabeth sits between compulsive closure and compulsive openness.

The mirror principle survives even though the old villain assignment does not.

## Theme Through Action, Not Speeches

Hard rule.

The terms used in planning — `jurisdiction`, `unrealized selfhood`, `origin does not determine identity`, `preserving possibility` — are primarily for us.

The manuscript earns those ideas through:

- Pathwell acting when not asked;
- Elizabeth turning the wheel;
- Shade accumulating ordinary experiences;
- Stansbury eventually choosing to intervene;
- Camp preserving things it cannot yet classify;
- Pathwell relinquishing only what is actually his to relinquish;
- the final choice not to prune;
- `Ready? / No. / Good.`

If a paragraph explains what one of those actions means immediately afterward, first try deleting the paragraph.

## Rules Are Sacred

The experimental rewrite is currently allowed to redesign the magic rules.

Once the experimental rules are selected and promoted into canon, they become binding.

Do not use the rewrite as permanent permission to change a rule whenever a scene needs help.

Before manuscript drafting begins, reconcile the experimental pruning/handwritten-object/blob rules into the authority-bearing bible documents and freeze the chosen version.

## Earn the Big Moments

This particularly protects the two required echoes.

`It was the crash that woke her.` only works at midpoint if earlier Elizabeth choices visibly climb toward turning the wheel.

The final `Ready? / No. / Good.` only works if Elizabeth has increasingly initiated direction and Pathwell has learned to accompany without automatically taking over.

Do not rely on the callback itself to create meaning that the intervening story failed to earn.

## Read It Aloud

Especially important because the book's irreverence depends on rhythm and wrong-register timing.

A line can be clever on the page and still sound like an author writing a clever line. Read Pathwell's dialogue aloud. If it sounds performed rather than reflexive, simplify it.

# Chapter gate for the experimental version

Before preserving or drafting any chapter, run both the existing `Quick-Diagnostic.md` and the `Writing-Principles.md` Universal Chapter Necessity Test.

At minimum confirm:

- Elizabeth wants something concrete;
- the chapter enters and exits through `therefore` or `but`, not `and then`;
- a meaningful value changes;
- the chapter does not explain something the story already demonstrated;
- at least one piece of the world remains unexplained;
- a later moment is measurably weaker if this chapter is removed;
- if the chapter prepares a loss, the reader is being allowed to possess the person/place/object/possibility before losing it;
- the chapter remains fun, strange, human, or emotionally necessary rather than existing to deliver architecture.

---

# Critical warning for the experimental rewrite

The more elegant the underlying architecture becomes, the greater the danger that the book starts showing its homework.

Do not confuse a complete story bible with a story that should explain itself completely.

**Know the whole machine. Show the reader the moving parts they happen to be standing near.**

The experimental version succeeds only if a reader can experience it first as a funny, strange, increasingly consequential adventure and discover the philosophy through the choices people make.

Short version:

> **Build the first book like a sequel. Let the characters know more than the reader. Let the world extend beyond the explanation. Let the theme happen before anyone can name it.**
