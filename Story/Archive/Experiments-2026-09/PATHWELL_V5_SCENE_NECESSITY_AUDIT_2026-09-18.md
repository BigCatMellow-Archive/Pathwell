Author's hard editorial test:

> **If I remove a scene, does it impact the story at all? If not, remove it.**

This audit applies that rule to every current V5 chapter-function.

## What counts as impact

A scene survives only if removing it materially weakens at least one later effect:

1. **Causality** — a later event no longer follows naturally.
2. **Character** — a later choice becomes less believable.
3. **Relationship** — trust, affection, resentment, rupture, or reconciliation loses evidence.
4. **World attachment** — later danger/loss becomes emotionally abstract.
5. **Setup/payoff** — a later object, rule, behavior, or reversal loses preparation.
6. **Escalation** — pressure no longer visibly accumulates.
7. **Necessary contrast/rhythm** — a major later beat loses weight because no prior normal state existed.

A vague answer such as `we need more time here` is **not sufficient**.

If two scenes create the same later effect, combine them.

If a scene exists mainly to prove the outline has earned something, it fails unless it has its own immediate want/conflict/turn.

---

# Verdict key

- **HARD KEEP** — removing the scene/function breaks the spine.
- **KEEP, BUT ACTIVE** — the function is necessary, but it must become a real scene with an immediate dramatic engine.
- **COMBINE** — the material matters, but not enough to justify its own chapter/scene container.
- **CUT AS STANDALONE** — no unique later effect justifies a separate scene.
- **BLOCKED** — cannot be approved until a rules/causality problem is solved.

# 2. Food and aftermath

**Verdict: KEEP, BUT ACTIVE**

Unique later effects:

- establishes Elizabeth's grief/disorientation in ordinary space;
- gives the first evidence that Pathwell is genuinely enjoyable;
- gives their relationship an ordinary register before magic consumes the book;
- keeps Elizabeth's immediate motive anchored in getting her property back.

If removed entirely, Elizabeth's voluntary continuation at the Space Between risks being driven mostly by fascination/plot necessity.

However, this scene does **not** deserve survival merely because `the book needs breathing room`.

It must contain a present-tense engine: Elizabeth wants answers/books/a route home; Pathwell wants food/movement/avoidance; their interaction changes how she feels about continuing with him.

**Future cut test:** if a rewritten Space Between chapter can carry grief, companionship, ordinary contrast, and property motive without crowding itself, this scene may later be absorbed.

# 7. Smaller adventure / investigation

**Verdict: KEEP, BUT ACTIVE AND CAUSALLY BOUND TO THE BAR**

This function has a unique job that no other current scene performs well enough:

- prove Elizabeth and Pathwell work well together;
- prove following him can be fun and productive;
- show a case where decisive Pathwell is legitimately useful;
- give Elizabeth a material contribution before the later exclusion;
- make the midpoint rupture damage something genuinely valuable.

If removed, the book can still function mechanically, but the Elizabeth/Pathwell rupture becomes materially less earned and Pathwell becomes easier to condemn.

However, a disconnected side quest fails.

**Pass condition:** the smaller adventure must produce the clue, need, object, witness, or destination that sends them to the bar/mirror.

If it cannot, cut it rather than keeping a friendship proof-exercise.

# 26–27. Restitution over time

**Verdict: KEEP, BUT REDUCE TO THE MINIMUM NUMBER OF ACTIVE SCENES THAT PROVE DISTINCT THINGS**

Restitution has two genuinely distinct later jobs:

### Job A — behavioral evidence

Show Pathwell behaving differently under different authority conditions.

Without this, his growth exists mostly in the climax.

### Job B — relationship evidence

Show why Elizabeth specifically wants his company again.

Without this, the final invitation reads like ethical certification rather than renewed companionship.

These jobs may require **two scenes/chapters**, but not one chapter per lesson.

Recommended split:

1. **Camp/Stansbury restitution:** Pathwell works under other people's terms; `Let it be`; some authority is legitimately granted and he uses it well.
2. **Museum/Elizabeth restitution:** incomplete museum repair and a direct Elizabeth/Pathwell interaction where old Pathwell would take over, new Pathwell does not, and they are still capable of enjoying each other.

If one excellent chapter can honestly accomplish both without compression, combine them.

# Result — what actually survives

The V5 candidate exposes roughly 27 chapter-functions.

After applying the hard removal test:

## Standalone containers removed

- `Camp gets to exist`;
- `The exclusion becomes intolerable`;
- separate `Pathwell-looking man` chapter;
- separate tracking chapter;
- `Shade is simply a person now`;
- `Diary and archive life`;
- pure `No good answer` discussion chapter;
- separate Shade-survival philosophy/resolution chapter.

Their **useful functions survive inside active scenes**.

## Surviving lean structure

A plausible lean structure is approximately **22 chapters**, not as a target but as the current maximum justified by unique story effects:

1. First crash
2. Food / aftermath
3. Space Between
4. First Camp visit + ordinary Camp texture
5. Stansbury
6. Smaller adventure/investigation -> causes bar destination
7. Bar / mirror / Elizabeth rescues Pathwell
8. Bar aftermath + withheld route information + second crash
9. Wrong Pathwell -> diner recognition/revelation
10. Tracking rewind + Ask -> Elizabeth chooses Camp
11. Museum threshold catastrophe
12. Camp recovery + diary/archive integration
13. Shade ordinary life + concrete expected future
14. No-good-answer conflict + first alternative
15. Alternative test / Pathwell restrains himself / partial success or new cost
16. Waiting under temporary arrangement -> concrete worsening event
17. Forced reintegration
18. Archive fire + Shade resolution
19. Immediate aftermath
20. Camp/Stansbury restitution
21. Museum/Elizabeth restitution + renewed companionship
22. Space Between / cookbook / final invitation

Nothing about `22` is protected.

If Chapters 14–16 can be reduced without weakening Pathwell's attempted restraint, reduce them.

If Chapters 20–21 can be combined without weakening either behavioral proof or Elizabeth's renewed desire for his company, combine them.

If Chapter 2's effects can later be absorbed naturally into Chapter 3, cut Chapter 2.

---

# Strongest editorial consequence

The story should now stop asking:

> **What extra scene would help earn this?**

and instead ask:

> **Can an already-necessary scene carry the missing earnedness function?**

Only add a new scene when the answer is no.

This reverses the expansion bias that entered during the dwell-time pass.

---

# Hard rule going forward

Before any scene enters the next architecture, write one sentence:

> **If this scene is removed, ______ becomes materially weaker or stops making sense.**

If that blank cannot be filled with a specific later consequence, cut the scene.

If two scenes fill the blank with the same answer, combine them.

If the answer is only `pacing`, `more time`, `worldbuilding`, `character development`, or `earnedness` without naming a later affected beat, the scene has not passed.
