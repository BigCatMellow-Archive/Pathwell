# Pathwell — V5 Conflicting-Goods / Alternatives Matrix — 2026-09-18

Status: **experimental late-architecture diagnostic; not manuscript canon**

Purpose: make the pre-reintegration crisis morally and dramatically difficult by ensuring every plausible option protects something legitimate and imposes a real cost.

This file does **not** resolve Shade survival/death.

It should help architect Chapters 14–16 without inventing a clean moral answer.

---

# Legitimate goods in conflict

The late story needs to preserve all of these as real:

1. **Shade's continued separate existence**
2. **Shade's freedom / ability to build a future**
3. **Camp residents' safety**
4. **Camp's right to decide what risks the community accepts**
5. **Pathwell's responsibility for a danger he helped create**
6. **Elizabeth's insistence that people not be reduced to problems**
7. **Preservation of irreplaceable records/history**
8. **The practical need to act before cleanup pressure becomes catastrophic**
9. **Avoiding the export of danger to uninvolved outsiders**

No option should preserve all nine.

If one does, the climax problem is too easy.

---

# Option 1 — Pathwell leaves Camp; Shade remains

## What it protects

- Shade keeps Camp/home/routines.
- Shade is not displaced simply because Pathwell caused the problem.
- Camp gets to choose whether it will continue sheltering him.
- Pathwell acts on something he unquestionably owns: his own location.

## Who pays

- Pathwell loses access to Camp/community/help.
- Camp bears continuing risk.
- Shade may still experience the draw or cleanup pressure.
- Elizabeth may lose Pathwell's immediate expertise during a dangerous period.

## What it tests

- whether physical distance weakens the draw;
- whether cleanup is confused by overlapping signatures or independently targets Shade.

## Experimental likely result

**Partial success, not solution.**

Distance reduces some Pathwell/Shade pull or reduces event frequency.

But cleanup pressure does not disappear.

This is useful because it establishes:

> the problem is not solved merely by Pathwell getting out of the way.

It may also reveal more clearly that Shade himself remains part of the unresolved magical condition.

## Story value

This is a strong first alternative because Pathwell can volunteer the cost himself.

It is the least hypocritical intervention available to him.

---

# Option 2 — Shade voluntarily leaves / relocates temporarily

## What it protects

- Camp residents and archive gain distance from the immediate danger.
- Camp can remain functional.
- The test may produce useful information about whether cleanup follows Shade.

## Who pays

- Shade loses home/routines/future plans.
- The person who did not create the problem bears the largest practical cost.
- Danger may simply be exported elsewhere.
- Any destination/host must consent to the risk.

## Ethical problem

This option can easily become:

> the inconvenient person is removed for everyone else's comfort.

That is too simple unless Shade himself meaningfully chooses it and understands the cost.

## Story use

This option is valuable less as "the solution they try" and more as a real proposal people could reasonably support.

Shade may reject it because:

- he has just begun to have a life;
- exile does not solve the mechanism;
- he does not want his existence defined by where he is least dangerous to everyone else.

That refusal should not make him selfish by default.

# Option 3 — Camp chooses temporary risk-management around Shade

Use only already-supported tools/procedures.

Do not invent a new magical shield simply because the outline needs one.

Possible components using established material:

- Stansbury's defensive/disruptive weapons;
- watch/response procedures;
- limited access around vulnerable archive areas;
- evacuation readiness;
- Pathwell kept physically separate where possible;
- observation/logging of draw/cleanup events.

## What it protects

- Shade remains at Camp.
- Camp retains collective choice.
- No one is subjected to reintegration.
- The group gathers real evidence.

## Who pays

- Camp residents accept elevated risk and disruption.
- Stansbury/Mama/others carry operational burden.
- Shade lives under procedures partly caused by his presence.
- Pathwell must tolerate people choosing a slower, less certain method.

## Pathwell restraint beat

This is the best place for the required moment where Pathwell **could override and does not**.

For example:

- a warning sign appears;
- Pathwell wants an immediate stronger intervention;
- Mama/Elizabeth/Shade choose to continue the agreed procedure;
- Pathwell stops himself.

The result should be **partial success**.

That matters.

If the procedure fails instantly, Pathwell never truly learns that restraint can work.

# Option 4 — Continued observation / waiting under the temporary arrangement

This is not "do nothing."

It is a chosen policy with safeguards and real costs.

## What it protects

- Shade's separate existence;
- Camp's chosen process;
- possibility of learning more before irreversible action.

## Who pays

- everyone lives with uncertainty;
- risk accumulates;
- records/residents remain exposed;
- Pathwell experiences responsibility without control.

## Critical story requirement

Waiting must eventually produce a **concrete worsening event**, not simply Pathwell becoming impatient.

Candidate class of event:

> cleanup breaches the temporary arrangement and causes a near-catastrophe or real loss despite everyone following the agreed plan.

The event should be mechanically consistent with whatever final cleanup rules are selected.

Do not lock the exact event until P0 Shade/cleanup mechanics are resolved.

## Why this matters

Pathwell must have real evidence for:

> continuing to wait also has a cost.

Otherwise his final intervention is too easy to condemn.

# Option 5 — Reintegration

## What it protects / promises to protect

- potentially ends the Pathwell/Shade anomaly;
- potentially lowers cleanup danger;
- potentially protects Camp and outsiders;
- appeals to Pathwell because it is decisive and legible.

## Who pays

- Shade may cease to exist as a separate person;
- the key risk is borne by the person least responsible for creating the problem;
- Camp/Elizabeth are asked to accept Pathwell's confidence in a high-risk irreversible solution.

## Authority problem

Even if Pathwell's technical estimate is correct, technical superiority does not answer:

> who gets to accept Shade's existential risk?

## Why Pathwell chooses it

By this point he should have:

- tried leaving;
- seen collective procedures partially work;
- actually restrained himself once;
- watched the temporary arrangement suffer a serious breach;
- concluded that delay now exposes people to unacceptable danger.

His conclusion should be understandable.

His error is not:

> he thinks danger exists when it does not.

His error is:

> he decides that because the danger is real and he caused it, the final tradeoff is his to impose.

# Recommended causal chain for Chapters 14–16

This is a **provisional experimental chain**. It should be pressure-tested against final magic mechanics before promotion.

## Chapter 14 — Pathwell pays first

Because the anomaly appears Pathwell-linked, **Pathwell leaves Camp** while Shade stays.

**Therefore:** they can test whether removing Pathwell reduces the problem without displacing Shade.

**But:** distance provides only partial relief; cleanup pressure persists.

This demonstrates that Pathwell is willing to bear cost and that the problem is not merely proximity.

## Chapter 15 — Camp chooses a slower method

Because distance is insufficient, Camp adopts a **temporary procedural risk-management plan** using established tools and observation.

Pathwell proposes a stronger intervention at a tense moment.

**But:** Camp/Shade choose to continue the agreed plan.

Pathwell **actually stops himself**.

The plan then works well enough to produce real relief.

This is the critical restraint proof.

**But:** relief comes with ongoing burden and does not eliminate the underlying condition.

## Chapter 16 — The cost of waiting becomes real

Because the partial method is working, they continue it.

Shade resumes enough ordinary life that his future remains visible.

**But:** a mechanically consistent cleanup event breaches the arrangement and causes serious harm / near-loss / forced evacuation / equivalent consequence.

Now continued waiting has evidence-based cost.

Pathwell returns with the reintegration argument.

Shade still refuses.

Camp/Mama do not grant Pathwell blanket authority.

Pathwell decides that the risk has crossed the line anyway.

**Therefore:** forced reintegration.

---

# Why this chain is stronger than "alternatives fail"

The alternatives do not merely fail.

They teach different things:

1. **Pathwell leaving** proves he will bear cost himself and that distance alone is insufficient.
2. **Collective procedure** proves restraint can genuinely work for a time.
3. **The breach** proves restraint is not cost-free.
4. **Reintegration** becomes attractive for real reasons without becoming authorized by those reasons.

That is the conflict the story needs.

# Important interaction with Shade survival mechanics

This chain may make the existing survival proposal harder, not easier.

If distancing Pathwell reduces overlap but cleanup still persists around Shade, that supports the rule:

> Shade himself remains unresolved cleanup material.

Therefore:

> simply severing Pathwell's claim/overlap may not be enough to save Shade.

That is not a defect in this matrix.

It is useful evidence.

Do not distort Chapters 14–16 to protect a preferred climax outcome.

Let the mechanism tell us whether survival remains possible.
