# Pathwell — Experimental Architecture V3 — 2026-09-17

Status: **current preferred experimental architecture**, not yet manuscript canon.

This file supersedes `PATHWELL_EXPERIMENTAL_ARCHITECTURE_V2_2026-09-17.md` as the working experimental reference. It incorporates the recovered later decisions audit rather than allowing older manuscript/planning states to silently re-enter the story.

Earlier files remain useful as history and rationale.

Governing rule:

> **Preserve what still earns its place. Do not preserve old implementation merely because it is documented.**

Writing rule:

> **Build the whole machine. Show only the parts the characters happen to be standing near.**

---

# 1. Protected core

These remain fixed unless explicitly changed by the author:

1. Opening / midpoint echo: **`It was the crash that woke her.`**
2. Opening / ending echo: **`Ready? / No. / Good.`**, with speakers reversed at the end.
3. Pathwell retains his fast, eccentric, ancient-but-boyish irreverence: velocity, curiosity, physical oddness, sideways humor, appetite, emotional obliqueness, and rare abrupt seriousness. Matt Smith's Doctor is a tonal reference point, not a template.
4. Elizabeth remains ordinary and nonmagical. She is never secretly chosen, destined, or cosmically important.
5. No alternate timelines and no alternate-universe Shade.
6. Magic stays tactile, costly, personal, strange, and incompletely explained.
7. Consequences remain real; replacement does not make loss equivalent.
8. Humor remains present even as the story darkens.
9. Pathwell's growth is ethical rather than temperamental. **Do not cure Pathwell of being Pathwell.**
10. The Space Between is experienced, not toured or explained aisle by aisle.

# 2. Writing principles that govern V3

## Hard keep

- **Write the Sequel.** The world existed before Elizabeth arrived. Characters do not lecture each other about ordinary magical facts.
- **Therefore, But — Never And Then.** Scene causality comes from wants, choices, and consequences.
- **Characters Want Things.** Lore is never sufficient reason for a scene.
- **Agency Is a Ladder.** Elizabeth's authorship must increase through increasingly consequential choices.
- **Theme Through Action, Not Speeches.** The book proves its argument behaviorally.
- **Rules Are Sacred once selected.** Experimental rules may still be designed now; after promotion into canon, they stop moving for convenience.
- **Earn the Big Moments.** The two crash lines and final callback must be earned by prior action.
- **Subtext Over Text.** Planning language such as `jurisdiction`, `preserving possibility`, and `origin does not determine identity` is primarily author-side language.
- **Breathe Between Punches.** Ordinary life is necessary for attachment and irreverence.
- **Promise, Progress, Payoff.** The middle must still feel like the strange, funny adventure promised by the opening.
- **Read It Aloud.** Pathwell's tone depends on rhythm rather than authored cleverness.

## Useful but not mechanical

- Every major scene should turn something meaningful, but transitional beats need not manufacture reversals.
- The body often knows before the intellect, especially at emotionally significant moments, but this is not a universal formula.
- The world should imply more than it explains, but do not insert mystery tokens to satisfy a quota.
- Incomplete answers are often stronger, but clarity is allowed when drama requires it.
- Major locations should shape action; not every conversation needs a spectacular backdrop.
- Character lies are useful pressure systems, not screenplay boxes.

## Explicitly dropped / reinterpreted

- Shade is **not required to be the antagonist** or Pathwell's literal dark future.
- `One unexplained thing per chapter` is not a quota.
- Elizabeth is not simply `passive`.
- Camp is not a uniformly correct consent culture.
- Growth does not require Pathwell to become cautious or solemn.

# 3. Philosophical center

The book should not reduce to `agency is good` or `consent matters`.

The stronger proposition is:

> **A life is partly made from the doors a person closes. Power does not automatically give you the right to close someone else's.**

Short form:

> **Living means closing doors. The difficult part is learning which doors are yours to close.**

The recurring practical question is:

> **Whose decision is this?**

This is an author-side diagnostic, not a slogan characters repeat.

Sometimes decisive intervention is necessary.
Sometimes authority has already been delegated.
Sometimes waiting harms people.
Sometimes another person's choice exposes others to danger.
Sometimes there is no clean answer.

The novel should resist simple moral sorting.

# 4. Magic foundations

## Pruning

Pruning does not select a timeline.

A prune closes some of the practitioner's own reachable future possibilities in order to force, strongly bias, or bind a reachable outcome.

The payment is unrealized future selfhood: possible relationships, places, skills, habits, choices, changes of mind, and versions of the practitioner that can no longer happen.

The practitioner does not receive a clean receipt explaining exactly what was lost.

Magic is morally indifferent.

A nonconsensual prune may work perfectly.
A consensual prune may fail.
A selfish prune can be technically clean.
A noble prune can be catastrophic.

Pathwell's central rationalization is:

> *I am paying the cost, therefore the decision is mine.*

Magic allows him to make that mistake.

## What centuries of pruning have done to Pathwell

Pathwell was always quick, curious, intrusive, funny, brave, hungry, and certain that problems wanted solving.

Repeated pruning has concentrated him by spending futures in which he might have:

- waited;
- reconsidered;
- stayed;
- lived quietly;
- let another person solve the problem;
- tolerated uncertainty;
- admitted that no available solution belonged to him.

His arc is not `reckless -> cautious`.

It is:

> **a man who has spent centuries closing doors learns to deliberately leave one open.**

## Handwritten objects

Authentic handwritten records carry charge because they physically accumulate human decisions: what was written, omitted, corrected, crossed out, stained, amended, inherited, or refused.

A copy preserves information.

It cannot preserve the physical history of choosing that produced the original.

Magical use consumes or irreversibly alters that accumulated history.

## Blobs

Blobs are cleanup organisms / mechanisms for unresolved magical spill, unstable signatures, contradictory residue, and magical material that has failed to settle coherently.

They are not predators in a moral sense and do not understand personhood.

A blob classifying Shade as unresolved residue says nothing about Shade's moral status.

## Shade

Shade originates from a malformed large prune in which Pathwell's foreclosed possibility fails to collapse cleanly and coheres around residual identity patterns.

At creation, Shade genuinely contains Pathwell-derived material: memory fragments, habits, tendencies, abandoned impulses, and magical signature.

But every experience Shade has that Pathwell did not have adds continuity that belongs only to Shade.

Author-side rule:

> **Origin explains Shade. Origin does not own Shade.**

Shade is not omniscient. He has creation-time fragments, his own experience, observation, and inference—not a current feed of Pathwell's mind.

# 5. Character foundations

## Elizabeth — openness becoming authorship

Elizabeth is adaptive, observant, and skilled at accommodating stronger personalities.

Her starting error is not cowardice. It is closer to:

> **If I keep every door open and adapt around everyone else, I can avoid making the wrong choice.**

The cost is that unmade choices become choices made by other people.

Her arc:

> **adaptation -> preference -> voluntary continuation -> refusal -> deliberate choice -> ownership of consequence -> authorship**

She is allowed to make bad choices.

Agency does not make a choice morally correct.

## Pathwell — competence mistaken for jurisdiction

Starting assumption:

> **If I can see the best solution and I am willing to pay for it, I should act.**

End-state behavior:

> **I can see what I would do without assuming the decision belongs to me.**

He remains recognizably Pathwell throughout.

## Shade — origin becoming selfhood

Shade's ordinary life matters more than a speech about personhood.

He wants things, gets bored, changes his mind, makes plans, likes some people, dislikes others, and accumulates experiences Pathwell never had.

The reader should already experience him as a person before the climax asks what should be done with him.

Shade currently **survives** the experimental version; this remains an intentional experimental choice, not yet canon.

## Stansbury — still an open scale choice

V3 does not yet force a large parallel philosophical arc.

Two viable versions remain:

1. **smaller brother-specific turn:** `Let it be` — Stansbury chooses to keep a permanent scar / consequence and Pathwell finally respects the boundary;
2. **larger containment arc:** Stansbury must eventually act before certainty and own what his intervention destroys.

Do not assume the larger arc is automatically better. Choose based on what the final architecture needs.

## Mama Baga — stewardship

Mama Baga is wise but not an oracle.

Her strength is distinguishing care from ownership, expertise from authority, and preservation from control.

She may misjudge something and revise herself.

# 6. Camp

Camp is a messy institution for things the world does not yet know what to do with.

It preserves dangerous artifacts, displaced people, unfinished research, unclassified magical phenomena, stories, records, mistakes, and things once thought useless.

Residents are not morally uniform.

Some trust Pathwell too much.
Some dislike Shade unfairly.
Some are petty about storage.
Some are generous.
Some make presumptions and then correct them.

Camp's values should emerge from behavior, not a mission statement.

Pathwell loves what Camp preserves and is temperamentally infuriated by what it refuses to finish.

# 7. Recovered opening decisions — now part of V3

## Welcome-party setup

Pathwell did **not** conventionally break into Elizabeth's apartment.

Preferred setup:

- Elizabeth recently held a welcome party.
- Pathwell crashed it and blended in as though he belonged.
- He lingered after everyone else left.
- He was opportunistically looking for an old handwritten book / record with usable charge that he believed nobody would seriously miss.
- While snooping, he finds Elizabeth's diary and Nana's cookbook, whose charge is far stronger and more personal than the expendable thing he expected to find.

The party itself is not a prologue. The reader infers it from aftermath: cups, coats, trash, rearranged furniture, maybe music or hallway noise fading out.

This is stronger than a burglary because Pathwell's violation is specifically Pathwell-shaped: social audacity, assumed practical permission, and opportunistic certainty rather than conventional criminal intent.

## Pathwell retrieves the cookbook himself

Elizabeth may tell him where it is under pressure.

Pathwell gets it.

Do not make the opening a voluntary handoff. Elizabeth's later cookbook sacrifice must remain meaningfully voluntary.

## Opening blob logic

The abnormal blob is following the effectively overlapping Pathwell / Shade signature.

Pathwell initially misreads the encounter as centered on Elizabeth or her charged objects.

The interrupted sugar-cookie working creates fresh unresolved magical waste, redirecting the blob toward that spill.

This opening should remain the novel in miniature:

> Pathwell sees incomplete evidence, decides what the problem means, acts before fully understanding it, and pulls Elizabeth into consequences that were actually following him.

# 8. Plot engine

The central external engine remains:

> Pathwell sees danger.
>
> He solves the immediate problem by narrowing possibility.
>
> The immediate problem improves.
>
> The unresolved Pathwell / Shade overlap becomes more concentrated.
>
> Cleanup pressure increases.
>
> The greater danger makes another intervention feel more justified.

The escalating plot is what Pathwell's flaw physically does to the world.

Healing or ordinary magical use should **not** automatically summon blobs. The cleanup pressure must remain specifically tied to unresolved spill / instability, not every act of magic.

# 9. Revised 18-chapter working architecture

Exact chapter boundaries remain adjustable. The causal and emotional functions matter more than the number 18.

## PART I — SOMEONE ELSE'S ADVENTURE

### Chapter 1 — The Crash

Opening:

> **It was the crash that woke her.**

Elizabeth wakes in the aftermath of her welcome party and discovers that the strange guest who should have left is still there, going through things.

Pathwell had lingered looking for an expendable charged handwritten object. Instead he has found the diary and the much stronger cookbook.

He is funny, intrusive, useful, and obviously behaving according to a private logic he assumes will eventually justify itself.

A blob arrives.

Pathwell incorrectly assumes the danger is attached to Elizabeth / her objects.

Elizabeth tells him where the cookbook is; Pathwell gets it himself.

He begins the sugar-cookie working. Elizabeth interrupts it. The torn working spills charge, and the blob redirects toward the fresh magical waste.

Pathwell gets them out.

He leaves carrying her diary and cookbook.

At the threshold:

> "Ready?"
>
> "No."
>
> "Good."

Elizabeth follows because the danger is real and **he still has her books**.

Optional final sting: an impossible glimpse of another Pathwell-shaped figure.

**What this earns:** opening misread; property motive; later cookbook choice; final echo; Pathwell's flaw in miniature.

### Chapter 2 — Aftermath / Food

This is breathing room, not an agency thesis.

Food may remain because it is funny, human, and lets Elizabeth/Pathwell occupy ordinary space after catastrophe.

Do **not** make ordering lo mein a load-bearing agency rung.

Elizabeth's world feels wrong because of panic, grief, disrupted routine, newly awakened attention, and the fact that ordinary life has continued without her—not because the city geography is magically distorting.

Her concrete goal is simple:

> **Get her diary and cookbook back.**

Pathwell keeps moving with them.

Therefore Elizabeth follows.

The chapter should make Pathwell enjoyable before the book asks the reader to understand how dangerous his certainty can be.

### Chapter 3 — The Space Between

No walking exposition tour.

Elizabeth experiences the place directly: charged objects, sensations she cannot classify, impossible architecture, the Shopkeeper, Pathwell's history implied through transaction and banter.

She sees pruning before she understands it.

Pathwell makes a useful, costly transaction for Camp supplies.

He gets no moral lecture. The Shopkeeper knows the rules; Elizabeth is behind.

Elizabeth finally gets the diary and cookbook back.

This matters structurally because the reason she followed has now been resolved.

A genuine ordinary-life exit is available.

Pathwell is continuing to Camp.

Elizabeth chooses to continue anyway.

That is the first major voluntary agency rung.

### Chapter 4 — Camp

Camp appears as functioning chaos rather than ethical utopia.

Elizabeth sees that Pathwell has genuinely helped people before, including cases where they explicitly trusted him to take charge.

This protects the story from arguing that decisive intervention is always wrong.

A younger resident needs help that can be achieved using the cookbook's accumulated charge.

Elizabeth **voluntarily** gives the cookbook.

Nobody asks her to prove anything.

Nobody explains the symbolism afterward.

The healing should resolve cleanly enough that it does not itself create the next blob problem.

The continuing abnormal blob behavior remains unresolved, so Mama Baga pushes Pathwell toward Stansbury rather than letting him keep improvising alone.

Shade appears / is glimpsed again.

Pathwell sees enough to stop joking.

### Chapter 5 — Stansbury

Pathwell goes to Stansbury because Stansbury reliably resists his momentum.

Keep the foam weapons, procedural seriousness, labels, backups, and brotherly irritation.

Stansbury identifies the important causal fact: cleanup pressure is Pathwell-adjacent rather than centered on Elizabeth.

Pathwell wants action.
Stansbury wants observation.

Elizabeth notices both brothers discussing a situation that affects her while repeatedly treating her information needs as secondary.

### Chapter 6 — The Bar / Mirror

Preserve the later sequence:

1. Pathwell sees Shade clearly in the mirror.
2. He realizes the problem points toward him.
3. Fear makes him accelerate rather than explain.
4. He bolts.
5. A blob engulfs **Pathwell**.
6. Elizabeth chooses to cut him free using Stansbury's ridiculous imbued dagger / toy weapon.

This is important:

- Pathwell becomes unmistakably the person who needs saving;
- Elizabeth's agency is action, not magical specialness;
- the blob logic keeps pointing toward Pathwell / Shade;
- Stansbury's absurd equipment earns a real payoff.

## PART II — PUSHING

### Chapter 7 — Solutions

Pathwell experiments on the overlap.

The chapter should be lively and funny on the surface: impossible measurements, improvised tools, Pathwell delighted by readings that should scare him, Stansbury documenting everything like a future accident report.

The experiments work locally.

That is the problem.

Each intervention reduces immediate instability by narrowing how the signal can express itself.

The remaining Pathwell / Shade overlap becomes more concentrated.

Cleanup pressure increases.

Elizabeth keeps asking what happened before she arrived.

Pathwell keeps treating the answer as his information to manage because he still thinks the old event is his problem to solve.

### Chapter 8 — The Second Crash

Opening:

> **It was the crash that woke her.**

This is the true midpoint and the protected agency hinge.

Elizabeth is driving while Pathwell and Stansbury argue around her.

She says they are stopping until somebody tells her what she is actually participating in.

They keep talking as though her decision has not changed the plan.

Elizabeth **turns the wheel**.

Current preferred mechanics: she deliberately leaves the route / turns toward a shoulder, turnout, or side road because she refuses to continue under their terms; the actual crash is an unintended consequence of that deliberate choice.

She owns the consequence.

She does not apologize for refusing to continue.

Before this point her dominant question has been:

> What is happening to me?

Afterward:

> What am I going to do?

She leaves Pathwell / Stansbury rather than simply resume their route.

## PART III — ELIZABETH CHOOSES

### Chapter 9 — Shade / Diner approach

This remains an explicit fork in V3:

**Option A — knowing choice:** Elizabeth knows the man is Shade and chooses to go with him because he may answer what Pathwell will not.

**Option B — wrongness first:** Elizabeth senses something is wrong but initially mistakes him for Pathwell; she still chooses to continue, and the diner accumulates evidence until Shade referring to Pathwell as another person confirms the reveal.

Do not decide by inertia.

Criteria for final choice:

- preserve Elizabeth's post-crash authorship;
- preserve enough mystery to make the diner dramatically useful;
- do not make Elizabeth foolish merely to hide information from the reader.

### Chapter 10 — The Diner / The Ask

The diner revelation establishes:

- Elizabeth was never the point;
- Shade is drawn to Pathwell, not her;
- the opening blob was following overlapping magical residue;
- Pathwell's misread pulled Elizabeth into his history;
- Shade arose from a malformed prune performed while Pathwell was helping someone who asked for help but **did not ask him to prune**;
- the connection is worsening as Pathwell keeps narrowing around it.

Shade only knows what he can plausibly know through origin-fragments, observation, inference, and his own experience.

Elizabeth later confronts Pathwell with the simplest possible sequence:

> What did they ask you to do?
>
> Help.
>
> Did they ask you to prune?
>
> No.
>
> Why did you?
>
> Because it was the best solution I could see.
>
> But you still chose it.
>
> Yes.

No thematic paragraph afterward.

Elizabeth chooses the next move rather than waiting for Pathwell to assign it.

## PART IV — CONSEQUENCES BECOME PEOPLE

### Chapter 11 — Camp, Again

Elizabeth returns by choice.

Shade is provisionally allowed to remain.

This chapter must make Shade's personhood ordinary before the climax threatens it.

Keep / use material such as:

- terrible coffee;
- green jacket;
- pockets;
- Hearts with Milo;
- an annoying job;
- somebody who dislikes him;
- somebody who stops treating him as supernatural;
- a private joke Pathwell never had;
- one future-facing desire unrelated to Pathwell.

Elizabeth's diary may be donated to the archive here under clear terms.

Pathwell is trying to behave differently, but the real test has not yet come: whether he can accept an answer he believes is dangerous.

### Chapter 12 — Museum

The museum is Pathwell's first major relapse after The Ask.

A charged historical object offers a plausible way to disperse / quarantine the overlap.

The plan is intelligent and might work.

It requires using Elizabeth and/or Shade in a way they do not accept.

Pathwell decides the risk of waiting is too high and acts anyway.

The working technically succeeds at first.

Then the narrowed signal rebounds.

A protected object is destroyed.

Elizabeth is injured or endangered.

The cleanup pattern worsens.

Use ordinary first aid when ordinary first aid is enough. Do not pull out convenient healing magic simply because the world contains magic.

### Chapter 13 — No Good Answer

The museum failure makes the actual dilemma unavoidable.

Camp may become unsafe.
Distance may no longer solve the draw.
Cleanup events are increasing.
Shade's Pathwell-derived and independently developed signatures are conflicting.

Possible options include:

- separation;
- exile / distance;
- long-term containment and research;
- Pathwell leaving while Camp shelters Shade;
- reintegration, which may erase Shade as a separate person.

No choice is clean.

Shade understands the danger and refuses reintegration.

The reader should understand why Pathwell cannot tolerate leaving the problem unresolved **without** concluding that Pathwell therefore owns the decision.

## PART V — THE LAST SOLUTION

### Chapter 14 — Reintegration

Pathwell prepares the reintegration working anyway.

Preserve irreverence at the edges: bad chalk, labels, tools, petty procedural annoyances. The situation remains frightening precisely because the normal register persists around it.

The working contains a mechanical contradiction: Pathwell wants Shade both to remain the person he has become and cease to exist as a separate person.

Shade says no.

Pathwell reaches the clean cancellation point.

He cannot tolerate the unresolved danger.

He releases it.

The working malforms.

The archive tears open with spilled charge.

Fire starts.

Cleanup organisms converge.

### Chapter 15 — Archive Fire

No single hero owns the solution.

Independent choices matter.

**Elizabeth:** sees meaningful objects / archive material and a trapped person (Milo remains the preferred emotional target). She chooses the person. No magical reward follows.

**Stansbury:** exact arc depends on the final scale choice. If using the larger arc, he must break containment / procedure before certainty to save people and own what is lost. If using the smaller arc, keep his role practical and reserve `Let it be` for the brother boundary payoff.

**Mama Baga:** coordinates without becoming moral dictator.

**Shade:** refuses to run toward reintegration merely because danger makes it expedient.

**Pathwell:** finally stops asking how to solve Shade and recognizes that the part he unquestionably controls is his own claim / connection.

He asks Shade whether Shade wants that connection severed despite uncertain consequences.

Shade says yes.

Pathwell performs a prune on his own side of the relationship:

> **Close every future in which what became Shade can ever return to me.**

Pathwell permanently relinquishes the possibility of reclaiming what cohered into Shade.

The overlapping signature breaks.

The blob recalculates mechanically, not morally.

Shade survives in the current experimental version.

## PART VI — AFTER THE SOLUTION

### Chapter 16 — What Survived

Losses remain real.

Original archive material is gone.
The diary / cookbook may be gone or irreparably damaged depending on the final object architecture.
The museum loss remains a loss.
The wrecked Cadillac stays wrecked; use another ordinary vehicle later.

Shade survives but may lose / blur some Pathwell-derived memory while retaining the experiences that are indisputably his.

He does not need to forgive Pathwell.

Pathwell cannot magically know where Shade goes next.

For the first time, another person's future is truly opaque to him.

### Chapter 17 — Restitution

Do not redeem Pathwell through one heroic sacrifice.

Show boring, incomplete behavioral evidence:

- repairs what can actually be repaired;
- accepts that copies are not originals;
- follows other people's directions;
- asks before moving / ordering / changing things;
- hears `no` without converting it into a technical obstacle;
- documents losses honestly;
- keeps showing up when no clever solution is available.

Compress repetition. A few strong examples are better than a morality montage.

If the diary-buyback function survives, it must be mechanically credible within Camp's revised archive rules. Preserve the thematic function, not necessarily the old literal transaction:

> a valid price / legal transaction / solvable reversal is not automatically equivalent to respecting the meaning of someone else's choice.

### Chapter 18 — The Door Left Open

Return to the Space Between.

Pathwell encounters a charged family cookbook that strongly resembles Nana's.

He can prune for it.

Nobody prevents him.

The old solution arrives instantly.

He begins to form the working.

Then stops.

Not because pruning is evil.
Not because he lost the ability.

Because resemblance is not identity, Camp did not ask him to replace the lost object, and willingness to pay does not make the decision his.

He leaves the possibility open.

Elizabeth arrives with another impossible route / destination / problem.

She has chosen to go.

She asks whether Pathwell wants to come.

He chooses yes.

At the threshold:

> "Ready?" Elizabeth asked.
>
> "No," Pathwell said.
>
> "Good."

End immediately.

No explanation of the callback.

# 10. Elizabeth's current agency ladder

Do not use lo mein as a load-bearing rung.

Current working ladder:

1. **Follow to recover what is hers.** Pathwell still has the diary / cookbook.
2. **Continue after recovery.** At the Space Between she has the books back and a route home, then chooses the adventure anyway.
3. **Give the cookbook.** Meaningful voluntary surrender to help someone.
4. **Save Pathwell at the bar.** She acts with Stansbury's ridiculous weapon while Pathwell is helpless.
5. **Refuse the route.** She says they are stopping.
6. **Turn the wheel.** Deliberate redirection with unintended crash; she owns the consequence.
7. **Choose how to pursue truth.** Shade / diner choice, exact recognition timing still open.
8. **Choose where she stands when no option is safe.** Camp / Shade / museum aftermath.
9. **Choose a person during the archive catastrophe without magical leverage.**
10. **Choose the next adventure and invite Pathwell.**

This is an authorship arc, not an assertiveness checklist.

# 11. Tone guardrails

The deeper architecture must not make the novel solemn.

Pathwell's humor comes from wrong-register priorities, logistics, appetite, curiosity, physical behavior, and treating impossible things as practical inconveniences—not from quip density.

Useful tonal pattern:

> serious situation -> slightly wrong human response -> gap -> reader holds both

Elizabeth should become drier and funnier as she becomes more comfortable occupying conversational space.

Stansbury's seriousness should itself become comic when applied to absurd equipment.

Shade's ordinary discoveries can be funny without trivializing his identity problem.

The most serious Pathwell moments should become powerful because his normal register drops away, not because the whole final act adopts a grave tone.

---

# 12. Remaining conscious forks before canon promotion

These are **not accidental omissions**. They require deliberate choice:

1. **Shade recognition timing after the crash:** knowingly chooses Shade vs initially mistakes him for Pathwell.
2. **Exact crash mechanics:** current preference is deliberate turn / unintended crash, but final staging should be tested in scene.
3. **Stansbury arc size:** small `Let it be` brother turn vs larger containment/responsibility arc.
4. **Shade survival:** V3 prefers survival because it best fits the new philosophy, but this remains experimental until canon promotion.
5. **Diary-buyback function:** preserve the thematic problem while finding a transaction/setting that remains credible under revised Camp rules.
6. **Shade's exact creation event:** who asked Pathwell for help, what the actual danger was, why pruning looked like the best solution, and what malformed.
7. **Pruning limits:** what can and cannot be forced; how scale and contradiction behave.
8. **Museum object:** what it is, why it is irreplaceable, and why the proposed use is plausible.
9. **Post-severance Shade:** exactly which Pathwell-derived memories/signature elements fade and what remains.
10. **Memory-before-loss:** which ordinary Camp/archive moments must be lived before the archive burns.

# 13. Current short-form story identity

Surface story:

> A strange ancient fixer accidentally drags an ordinary woman into a magical cleanup problem involving a person made from possibilities he discarded.

Underlying architecture:

> Pathwell keeps solving uncertainty by closing possibilities until the story forces him to recognize that not every future is his to spend.

Emotional spine:

> Elizabeth begins by being carried by other people's momentum and ends by choosing a direction, while Pathwell begins by choosing for everyone and ends capable of accompanying someone else's choice.

Tone shorthand:

> **A funny fantasy adventure about choosing.**

Final structural shorthand:

- First crash: Elizabeth wakes into somebody else's adventure.
- Second crash: Elizabeth wakes into authorship.
- Final `Ready? / No. / Good.`: neither certainty nor readiness is required for a choice freely made.
