## PROSE RULES (from forbidden_patterns.md — hardest ones)

1. **No filtering**: Cut "she saw / she felt / she heard / she noticed." Render the thing directly.
2. **No magic lectures**: If a character explains how magic works, cut or convert to action.
3. **No telling after showing**: After an image or action does emotional work, delete the sentence that names the feeling.
4. **Narration needs a voice**: Not just stage direction. The narration should observe and judge. Dry, slightly absurdist — Pratchett-adjacent.
5. **Body-part cataloging**: If a sentence names three body parts, cut to one and make it specific.
6. **Elizabeth's passivity**: She must be climbing the ladder. If she's drifting, fix it.

## VOICE / TONE RULES (from pathwell_prose_voice.md and pathwell_tone_register.md)

1. **Body before mind**: Elizabeth's body should react before she narrates understanding.
2. **Magic through sensory experience**: do not explain magic; make smell/taste/sound/memory carry it.
3. **Wrong-detail response**: in serious moments, find the response that is wrong for the situation but true to the character.
4. **The gap is the tone**: real danger + slightly wrong response. If the response matches the seriousness, the register is gone.
5. **Pathwell notices appetite, logistics, trajectory, and administrivia before emotion.**
6. **Stansbury notices precision and procedure.**
7. **Shade's wrongness is eerie precision, not comedy.**
8. **Do not explain the joke, the image, or the theme after it lands.**

## ORAL-STORYTELLING / NARRATOR PRESENCE RULES

Use `natural-oral-storytelling-prose-ai-guide.md` as an overlay, not a replacement for the Ch1-2 benchmark. The desired effect is a narrator who feels present in the telling: dry, selective, slightly irreverent, and aware of what details matter.

Use the oral-storytelling mode when:

1. **A scene needs remembered logic**: start with the detail that stuck, then let meaning arrive after it.
2. **Narration is only stage direction**: add a small opinion, comparison, or qualifying turn that sounds like Pathwell's world being told by someone with taste.
3. **A detail needs emotional texture**: let the narrator say what kind of strange, quiet, bad, or funny it was, without naming the theme.
4. **The register needs the gap**: real danger can be sharpened by a slightly wrong, dry narrator observation.
5. **A paragraph lands weakly**: end on the strongest remembered image, action, or turn.

Use clean/direct prose when:

1. **Action is urgent**: do not add storyteller commentary that releases pressure early.
2. **Magic is being experienced**: keep body-first sensory sequence; do not explain mechanics or step outside Elizabeth's immediate experience.
3. **A fragment is doing percussion work**: do not soften or chat it up.
4. **A joke or wrong-detail beat has landed**: stop there.
5. **The reader can infer the meaning**: narrator presence should not become telling-after-showing.

Do not turn the book into first-person chat. This is third-person limited through Elizabeth. Spoken-memory texture belongs in sentence shape, selective detail, dry qualification, and occasional narrator judgment, not in literal "you know" filler or direct address.
