**The pattern across the whole draft:**

The voice degrades from Ch4 through Ch11 and recovers in Ch12-13. The consistent fault is the **catalog instinct under uncertainty** — when a scene is structurally unclear, the prose adds more words instead of finding the one right sensory detail. The second consistent fault is **characters speaking subtext** instead of the subtext living in the gap between what's said and what's done. Ch9 and Ch13 are the exceptions that prove the rule — both are tight because both have clear dramatic purposes and don't need to fill space.

The chapters that need the most prose work, in order: 11, 8, 4, 12.

The actual problem:
The back half has started to believe in itself. Ch1-2 works because Pratchett's core trick is operating: the situation is genuinely dangerous and the tone refuses to acknowledge it. When that gap closes — when characters respond to serious situations with appropriate seriousness — you lose the register entirely and you get competent genre fiction instead of what this is supposed to be.
The fix isn't to add jokes. It's to ask, in every scene, what Pathwell would notice that nobody else would, and whether the response in the scene matches the gravity of what's happening. If it matches, it's wrong. That gap is the whole voice.
