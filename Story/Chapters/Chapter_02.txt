Chapter Two

“Will you slow down!?”


Elizabeth struggled to keep up with Pathwell as he charged down the stairs, “What about all my stuff?”
“Replaceable.”
They ran through the lobby, bursting through the front doors.
The street air was cold, shocking her breath.


It took her a moment to find it again, the breaths coming shallow and rapid.


Looking up she could see her apartment from the street, the light still on. She'd been there for just three weeks. Three weeks. Twenty-one days of unpacked boxes and takeout trying to figure out what came next. Her feet almost turned back. The only thing she bothered to unpack, her grandmother's mixing spoon, was still sitting on the kitchen counter.


And now this. Whatever this was.


"You okay?"
"No," she snapped.
"That's fair."


She stopped walking. "Where are you taking me?"
"There's a lovely Chinese spot just around the corner here."
"I have a meeting at nine tomorrow."
"I swear it's really just around the corner."


"My landlord is going to kill me. How am I going to explain the door? I have to get my dry cleaning before the meeting. Where am I going to get a new door? How am I going to even get to the meeting? Do you think someone's going to steal my stuff? Should I call the cops?"


Pathwell nodded politely, waiting for her to finish, his eyes on the streetlight above them.


"This is all a dream. Tell me it's all a dream."
He pinched her arm.
She slapped his hand away. "Why would you do that?"
"It's not a dream. Just a really weird Tuesday for you."
"It's Thursday."
"Which makes it all the weirder." He kept walking, leaving her standing there rubbing her arm. She looked around one more time, looking back at her apartment window. A shadow filled the space. She hurried to catch him at the corner.


She turned left, toward the coffee shop she passed every morning. Except there was no coffee shop.
Just a shuttered laundromat she'd never seen before.
Beyond that, a park that shouldn't have been there.
Three weeks in this neighborhood and she didn't recognize a single thing.
.
"So then what will it be? You more of a lo mein girl? I'm all about the dumplings, the dumplings are perfect here."


"You just destroyed my home, and now you're hungry?"
"I wouldn't say hungry, I'm more peckish. And for the record, I saved you. Most people are thankful when someone does that.”
“Thankful!?”


The shop was open to the street, a neon red dragon flickering above it, a beacon to all late night cravings.
"Two orders of fried dumplings please."
"I'm not hungry."
"Oh these aren't for you… and I don't share."


Elizabeth moved to a plastic table sat on the street while Pathwell waited at the counter. She let her weight hit the chair and slouched forward on the table. The cold forced her hands into her pockets, her fingers toying with the coins in them. A piece of paper brushing up against her hand.
The ticket.


Her laundry ticket was in her pocket. She could still have a clean outfit tomorrow.


She could still make it.


She dumped the contents of her pockets on the table, the bright yellow ticket landing on top. As she reached for it, the wind kicked up as a truck zipped past. The ticket skipped across the sidewalk, caught the gutter, and was gone.
"Okay, I can just… I can…"
Her breath caught. Her stomach turned, and the smell hit her again. Wet. Rot. Clinging to the inside of her coat. She pressed her hands flat on the table and her mouth opened one more time but nothing came out.
She wasn't sure when the tears started, only that she couldn't stop them. Three weeks of bare walls and boxes she couldn't bring herself to open. A funeral she still hadn't cried at. A life she'd packed into garbage bags and moved across the city because staying felt worse than leaving but leaving didn't feel like anything at all.
She put her face in her hands and just let it happen.
Pathwell sat down brimming with anticipation before noticing Elizabeth. He took one look at her and sighed, "Oh fine, you can have a dumpling."


Elizabeth stared at the table as Pathwell placed a dumpling in front of her.


The neon dragon flickered against the night, as Elizabeth sat staring down at a single dumpling, wiping traces of tears. The sounds of the night took over, a distant siren, a taxi splashing through a puddle, a group, talking and laughing as they passed. The city was in motion even now.


Pathwell inhaled his second one, and nudged her slightly, “You gonna eat that?”


“No.”


"I'm tellin’ ya Lizzy, you don't know what you're missing here.” he said, snatching up the rogue dumpling with his fork.
“I’ve had dumplings before”
“Ah, but not these dumplings.” He said through half chewed food. He drummed his fingers on the edge of the table, looking around aimlessly.


"So, where to next?"
"Excuse me?"
"The night is young, Lizzy! Where do you want to go?"
"Home."
Pathwell chuckled softly.


Her eyes snapped to him. "You wrecked my house. There's pieces of some… thing, all over the place. I have a meeting that I cannot miss in the morning. Nothing to wear to it. And all you can think to do is get some crappy food and ask me ‘whats next’?"


"First off, I wouldn’t go back there tonight. But that’s just me.” Popping another dumpling into his mouth, "Secondly, these things are perfect. You have no idea how hard it is to find this kind of quality."


He stood up, brushing his hands off. "That being said, It's up to you what you want to do next. The choice is yours, Lizzy."
She watched him walk off, leaving her in the red neon glow.


She sat there for a moment, staring at the empty night in front of her where he had been when the diary caught the corner of her eye.


She thumbed through the pages, their charred edges catching on her skin. A gap in the spine, a page missing, burned down to a blackened ridge.


She turned to the next page. Thumper. His six toed paw print pressed into the center of the paper, the ink still holding after all these years. She ran her finger across it and felt the faintest warmth of a faded memory, an orange tabby thwacking the ground when he wanted to play.


Then another warmth, the taste of butter and sugar, the heat of an oven she hadn't stood in front of since she was twelve.


Her eyes swept the table, slowly at first. Then darting. She jumped to her feet.


He still has it.
