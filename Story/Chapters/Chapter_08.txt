Chapter Eight

Elizabeth chose the ditch.

Not the telephone pole farther up the road. Not the trees beyond it. The ditch: broad, shallow, cut along the edge of an empty field with enough grass in it to suggest that other bad decisions had been there before.

She had time to choose.

That mattered.

The Cadillac hummed beneath her hands.

Pathwell and Stansbury had been quiet for almost a mile.

Not finished.

Quiet.

There was a difference.

Elizabeth watched the road unwind under the headlights.

"Which prune?" she asked.

Neither brother answered.

"Stansbury said 'the prune that went wrong.' Which one?"

Pathwell shifted in the back seat.

"We don't know that it matters yet."

"That wasn't my question."

Stansbury looked out the passenger window.

Elizabeth waited.

The Cadillac kept moving.

"Pathwell."

"Elizabeth."

"Which prune?"

"Not while you're driving."

Her hands tightened on the wheel.

"Then we stop."

"There isn't anywhere to—"

Elizabeth took her foot off the accelerator.

Pathwell leaned forward between the seats.

"Lizzy, keep driving."

She looked at him in the rearview mirror.

"Elizabeth."

"Elizabeth. Keep driving."

The correction did not improve the sentence.

Ahead, the road ran straight and empty for half a mile.

To the right, the ditch opened alongside a gravel farm entrance. No traffic. No house. No headlights coming the other way.

Elizabeth put both hands higher on the wheel.

Stansbury looked at her.

He understood first.

"Elizabeth."

"Seatbelt."

His eyes dropped to hers already across her chest.

Then to Pathwell.

"Put yours on."

Pathwell stared at both of them.

"Why?"

Elizabeth turned the wheel.

The Cadillac left the road.

Gravel hit the underside like thrown coins.

Pathwell swore.

Stansbury braced one hand against the dash.

Elizabeth hit the brakes after all four tires were off the pavement.

The rear end swung.

For one long second the car moved sideways through its own dust.

Then the right front wheel dropped into the drainage cut beside the farm entrance.

Metal struck concrete.

The sound was enormous.

The front end stopped.

The rest of the Cadillac objected.

Elizabeth's shoulder hit the belt. Stansbury's hand came off the dash. Something in the back seat struck the roof with a noise Pathwell would later insist had been the car and not him.

The engine coughed once.

Twice.

Stopped.

Dust moved through the headlights.

Nobody spoke.

A hubcap rolled past the windshield, crossed the gravel entrance, and fell over in the grass.

Elizabeth kept both hands on the wheel.

Her heart was beating hard enough to make the steering column feel alive beneath her palms.

Stansbury looked at her.

Then at the ditch.

Then at the front of his Cadillac, which was sitting approximately six inches lower than the rest of it.

"You broke my car," he said.

Elizabeth swallowed.

"Yes."

Pathwell pushed himself upright in the back seat.

His hair had finally achieved something he could not defend.

"You crashed the car."

Elizabeth turned around.

"Yes."

"On purpose."

"Yes."

Pathwell stared at her.

She had expected anger.

What she got was surprise.

That was worse in a more satisfying way.

Elizabeth unfastened her seatbelt.

"Now we're stopped."

Stansbury leaned forward and turned the key.

The engine gave him nothing.

He tried once more.

A dry click.

"Very stopped," he said.

Elizabeth looked at him.

"I'm sorry about the car."

Stansbury looked through the windshield at the grass where his hubcap had died.

"I believe you."

"I can pay for—"

"You cannot."

"That's fair."

Pathwell opened the rear door.

It stuck halfway against the slope of the ditch.

He pushed harder.

"This is an extraordinary overreaction."

Elizabeth got out on the driver's side.

"So was pulling me out of my apartment because you decided a monster was after me."

"That seemed reasonable at the time."

"So did the ditch."

Stansbury made a sound that might have been a cough.

Pathwell looked at him.

"Do not encourage her."

"My axle is in a drainage culvert. I am evaluating several positions."

Elizabeth stepped onto the road.

Her knees felt weak.

Not injured.

Just late to the decision.

The night was bigger outside the car.

Fields on both sides. A line of trees far back from the road. The stars beginning to show through breaks in the cloud.

Behind her, Stansbury climbed carefully out of the passenger side and put one hand against his ribs.

Elizabeth noticed.

"Are you hurt?"

"Bruised."

"I really am sorry."

"I know."

Pathwell finally got the rear door open far enough to crawl out.

He stood on the slope beside the Cadillac and looked at Elizabeth.

For a moment nobody talked around her.

Good.

Elizabeth pointed at him.

"The prune."

Pathwell looked toward Stansbury.

Elizabeth laughed once.

"You really cannot help yourself."

"I'm thinking."

"You always seem to do that in someone else's direction."

Pathwell looked back at her.

Then he went still so abruptly that Elizabeth stopped speaking.

Not looking at her.

Past her.

Down the road.

Stansbury saw it too.

"Again?"

Pathwell nodded once.

Elizabeth turned.

Headlights appeared around the bend behind them.

One car.

Moving slowly.

The road was otherwise empty.

"Perfect," Elizabeth said.

Pathwell did not answer.

The approaching car slowed further when its headlights found the Cadillac in the ditch.

An old dark sedan rolled onto the shoulder fifty yards away and stopped.

For several seconds no one got out.

Then the driver's door opened.

A man stepped onto the road.

Elizabeth's first thought was that Pathwell had somehow crossed behind her without her seeing him.

Her second was that the idea made no sense.

Her third arrived in her body before either of the first two had finished.

Wrong.

Not dangerous.

Not yet.

Wrong in the way a familiar room is wrong when someone moves one piece of furniture in the dark.

Same height.

Near enough to the same face that distance made the differences disappear.

But the man wore a plain dark coat, buttoned correctly. His hair lay flatter. His posture carried less of Pathwell's forward momentum, as though he had learned to stop before entering rooms.

He shut the sedan door.

His right arm lifted away from his side.

Not a wave.

The hand opened toward Pathwell.

The man's jaw tightened.

He caught his own wrist with the other hand and pushed it down.

Pathwell stepped forward, staring at him.

Then stopped.

Elizabeth looked from one to the other.

"No."

Nobody answered.

"Absolutely not."

Stansbury moved closer to her, not in front of her.

The man from the sedan looked at Pathwell.

Not Elizabeth.

Not Stansbury.

Pathwell.

His expression held recognition without welcome.

"You," he said.

Pathwell's face had gone very still.

"Apparently."

The man looked down at his right hand.

He closed it into a fist and put it in his coat pocket.

Pathwell looked at the same hand as if it had answered a question he had not wanted phrased aloud.

Stansbury broke the silence.

"You know him?"

"No," Pathwell said.

The other man said, at almost the same time, "Not exactly."

Elizabeth closed her eyes for one second.

"That is a terrible start."

The stranger looked at her for the first time.

His eyes paused on the foam dagger still clipped at the back of her waistband.

Then on the ruined Cadillac.

Then on Elizabeth herself.

No recognition.

That helped.

A little.

"Are you hurt?" he asked.

"No."

Stansbury said, "Bruised."

Pathwell said nothing.

The stranger nodded as though collecting only the information he had actually been given.

Elizabeth appreciated that against her will.

Pathwell stepped onto the road.

The tension in his posture changed with the distance between them.

Not compulsion.

Awareness.

"How did you find me?" he asked.

The man's mouth tightened.

"I didn't."

Pathwell waited.

"Something keeps telling me which way you are."

Elizabeth looked at Pathwell.

He did not look back.

The stranger continued.

"Not who. Not where. Which way. I can ignore it for a while. Then it gets worse."

His right hand moved inside his coat pocket as if proving the point.

"So I drove."

Stansbury's attention sharpened.

"How long?"

The stranger looked at him.

"Long enough to be tired of it."

"Days? Weeks?"

"Long enough."

Pathwell almost smiled.

The stranger saw it.

"Don't."

The smile vanished.

Elizabeth noticed that too.

"Name?" Stansbury asked.

A pause.

"Shade."

"First name?"

"Enough name."

Pathwell made a small sound through his nose.

Shade looked at him.

"You have opinions about naming?"

"Several."

"Keep them."

Elizabeth looked between the two men.

The resemblance improved the longer she stopped looking directly at it.

Same economy in the hands.

Same slight tilt of the head when listening.

Same habit of looking at an object as if it had personally chosen to become relevant.

But everything landed differently.

Pathwell filled silence because silence looked undefended.

Shade seemed willing to let it suffer.

Pathwell's coat moved like punctuation.

Shade's barely moved at all.

Same ingredients.

Different recipe.

The thought made her unexpectedly angry.

"The prune," she said.

Pathwell looked at her.

Shade looked at Pathwell.

Stansbury looked at the road.

Elizabeth spread both hands.

"Really? Again?"

Shade's attention returned to her.

"What prune?"

Elizabeth laughed.

"Excellent. The one person here who looks like he should know does not."

"I know pieces," Shade said.

Pathwell's eyes snapped to him.

Shade noticed.

He looked almost pleased.

Not kindly.

"Pieces of what?" Stansbury asked.

Shade took his right hand out of his pocket.

It immediately opened a few inches toward Pathwell.

He shoved it back in.

"Him."

The word sat on the road between them.

Pathwell took another step closer.

Shade stepped back.

"Don't."

Pathwell stopped.

For once, immediately.

Shade watched him long enough to make sure the stop was real.

"I remember things I did not live," Shade said. "Places I have never been. People I have never met. Habits that arrived before I had anything to be habitual about."

Pathwell's face had lost whatever remained of its performance.

"Current memories?"

"No."

"Since when?"

Shade looked irritated.

"I don't know your current life. I don't know what you did yesterday. I don't know her."

He nodded once toward Elizabeth.

"I know things that were already there when I started."

Stansbury looked at Pathwell.

"Started."

Shade's jaw set.

"Existing. Pick a word."

Nobody did.

Elizabeth looked at Pathwell.

The man who always had a nearby answer had none.

That should have been satisfying.

It wasn't.

"Do you know what he did?" she asked Shade.

Pathwell turned toward her.

Shade did not answer immediately.

"Some of it."

"Enough to explain?"

"Enough to know he won't do it cleanly."

Pathwell's expression hardened.

"You don't know me."

Shade looked at him.

"I know old versions of your excuses."

Stansbury stepped between the sentence and whatever might have followed it.

"Nobody knows enough yet."

Shade nodded.

"Correct."

That seemed to annoy Pathwell more than the insult.

Elizabeth rubbed the place where the seatbelt had caught her shoulder.

The adrenaline was beginning to thin.

Cold moved in underneath it.

Her hands had started shaking.

Shade noticed.

"There's coffee somewhere along this road."

Elizabeth looked at him.

"Is that an invitation?"

"No. I'm getting coffee."

"Good."

He turned toward his sedan.

Pathwell moved.

Not toward Shade.

Toward Elizabeth.

"You're not going with him."

The sentence arrived with such familiar certainty that for one second she nearly obeyed it out of reflex.

Then she heard it.

Elizabeth looked at him.

"Was that a question?"

Pathwell stopped.

"You don't know who he is."

"Neither do you."

"That is exactly the problem."

"No. It is one of them."

Shade had reached his car.

He opened the driver's door and waited for no one.

Elizabeth looked at Stansbury.

He was standing beside the wreck of his Cadillac with one hand against his ribs.

He did not tell her what to do.

She appreciated him for it.

She resented that she noticed.

Elizabeth looked back at Pathwell.

"You had all night to tell me what you knew."

"I don't know what he knows."

"Then I guess one of us should ask him."

Pathwell's face tightened.

"Elizabeth."

She turned toward Shade's car.

The wrongness was still there.

That was important.

Every part of her that recognized Pathwell recognized the man by the sedan and objected to the substitution.

She did not trust Shade.

She did not even particularly like him.

But the body was tired, the road was cold, the Cadillac was dead, and somebody had finally said there were pieces Pathwell had not given her.

Shade started the sedan.

Elizabeth reached the passenger side.

Her hand stopped on the handle.

Front seat.

Too close.

She opened the rear door instead.

Shade glanced at her in the mirror.

"Front is empty."

"Back is fine."

He shrugged.

No argument.

That almost made the choice feel safer than it was.

Elizabeth got in.

Pathwell stood in the road behind them.

For a moment their eyes met through the rear window.

He looked less angry than afraid.

She almost got back out.

Then she remembered his voice in the Cadillac.

Drive.

Elizabeth shut the door.

Shade pulled onto the road.

The wreck receded behind them.

She watched it until the darkness took it.

Only then did she realize she was following another man who sounded certain.

Her stomach tightened.

Too late to call it an accident.
