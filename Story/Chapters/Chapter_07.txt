Chapter Seven

The smell reached Elizabeth before the shape did.

Low tide.

Wet rot.

The same wrongness that had come through her apartment door.

Her hand went to the foam dagger at the back of her waistband.

Pathwell stopped beside the Cadillac.

Not his usual theatrical stop. The other kind.

Still.

Stansbury saw it too.

"Where?" he asked.

Pathwell looked toward the far side of the car.

"I don't know."

Something black moved beneath the Cadillac.

Not under it exactly. Around it. A dark wet fold gathering itself from the shadow under the chassis, too large to have been hiding there and too shapeless for the word hiding to help.

The blob rose beside the rear bumper.

Elizabeth took one step back.

The thing did not turn toward her.

It turned toward Pathwell.

There was no hesitation.

Pathwell's face changed.

"Oh," he said.

The blob crossed the parking lot.

Stansbury moved first.

He had one of the foam swords out of the box before Elizabeth saw him reach for it. He struck across the blob's leading edge.

The sword sank halfway into blackness.

The thing shuddered.

It did not stop.

Pathwell went for his coat.

Too late.

The blob hit him at the waist and folded upward.

One second he was there.

The next there was only black shape and one hand still visible at the edge of it, fingers closing on nothing.

"Pathwell!"

Elizabeth moved.

Stansbury caught her shoulder.

"Don't get close."

She looked at the mass swallowing his brother.

"That's useful advice late."

The blob climbed over Pathwell's chest.

His visible hand disappeared.

Stansbury struck again.

The foam sword drove into the mass and came back slick with nothing Elizabeth could see. The blob pulled away from the impact, then closed again around the gap.

"It's not holding shape," Stansbury said.

Elizabeth already had the dagger in her hand.

The tool was absurdly light.

Cuts separation into things that don't separate easily.

Stansbury had said that at the school as though there were ordinary situations in which such a sentence belonged.

Elizabeth ran toward the blob.

"Elizabeth—"

She ignored him.

The smell hit hard enough to make her eyes water.

Up close the thing had no surface she could focus on. Every edge slipped sideways when she looked directly at it. She could see Pathwell only in fragments: the sole of one shoe, the corner of his coat, a pale patch of wrist that vanished before she could reach it.

Elizabeth drove the foam dagger into the line where blackness met the edge of his coat.

The dagger should have bent.

It did not.

Something opened.

Not flesh.

A seam.

The blob recoiled from it in one violent ripple.

Elizabeth cut downward.

The seam followed the dagger.

For one second she saw Pathwell's shoulder inside the black mass.

Then his face.

Eyes shut. Mouth open. Skin gray beneath the parking-lot light.

She cut again.

"Come on," she said.

Pathwell did not answer.

Elizabeth hooked one hand into the front of his coat and pulled.

Nothing.

She planted a foot against the Cadillac tire and pulled harder.

The blob tightened around him.

Cold ran through the sleeve of her coat where it touched the black edge.

Her fingers went numb.

She cut once more, this time across the band holding his ribs.

The thing released him all at once.

Elizabeth fell backward.

Pathwell came with her.

They hit the dirt together.

His weight drove the breath out of her.

For half a second neither moved.

Then Pathwell coughed.

Once.

Twice.

A wet, ugly sound that was still considerably better than silence.

Elizabeth rolled him off her.

"Breathe."

Pathwell opened one eye.

"That was the plan."

"Do it quieter."

"Criticism received."

His voice barely worked.

That ended the joke.

Elizabeth stayed on one knee beside him.

Across the lot, Stansbury had changed tools.

The sword lay in the dirt.

In his hand was one of the short foam batons from the box, orange from end to end and ridiculous enough that under different circumstances Elizabeth might have laughed.

The blob rolled toward Pathwell again.

Stansbury stepped between them.

He struck the pavement once with the baton.

Heat hit the parking lot.

Dry, immediate, furnace-door heat.

A line of orange-white light ran through the cracks in the asphalt beneath the blob.

The black mass convulsed.

Stansbury struck it directly.

For one instant flame moved through the thing without catching on anything else.

The blob collapsed inward.

Not burned away.

Unmade, at least here.

Its shape broke into heavy wet sections that flattened, hissed, and thinned until only black streaks remained on the dirt and pavement.

The smell stayed.

The parking lot went quiet.

Inside the bar, someone sank a pool ball.

The screen door never opened.

Elizabeth looked at the place the blob had been.

"Is it dead?"

Stansbury set the baton down carefully.

"That one is gone."

"That was not the question."

"It's the answer I have."

Elizabeth accepted that because lately she had developed an appreciation for people who knew where their answers ended.

Pathwell pushed himself upright.

His hands shook.

Not the small tremor from the Space Between.

Both of them now.

Elizabeth reached for his arm.

He leaned on her before either of them could pretend he did not need to.

Stansbury came over and crouched in front of him.

"Look at me."

"I'm familiar with the face."

"Pathwell."

Pathwell looked.

Stansbury watched his pupils for a second, then pressed two fingers against the side of his neck.

"Anything missing?"

"Several dignities."

"Anything important?"

Pathwell swallowed.

He looked toward the black streaks on the pavement.

"No."

Stansbury followed his eyes.

"It went for you."

Pathwell said nothing.

Elizabeth was still holding his arm.

"Not me," she said.

Pathwell looked at her.

"No."

The word was quiet.

Not reassurance.

Correction.

The first blob had come through her door.

Pathwell had told her the apartment was marked.

He had pulled her out of it because he was certain the danger belonged to her.

This one had crossed an empty parking lot and swallowed him.

Elizabeth looked at the remains again.

"So what does that mean?"

Stansbury looked at Pathwell.

Pathwell looked away.

There it was.

Small enough that a day ago Elizabeth might have missed it.

A conversation happening before anyone spoke.

"The mirror," Stansbury said.

Elizabeth turned back.

"What mirror?"

Pathwell's jaw tightened.

Stansbury stood.

"Not here."

"You said that inside."

Pathwell got slowly to his feet.

Elizabeth let go only when she was sure he could stand without her.

"I know," he said.

"Then where?"

Pathwell glanced toward the road.

"In the car."

"Good. Then tell me in the car."

Neither brother answered.

Elizabeth looked at Stansbury.

He bent to pick up the foam sword.

"Stansbury."

He paused.

"I just cut him out of that thing."

"I know."

"Then stop talking around me."

Stansbury's expression changed.

Not enough.

"You're right," he said.

Pathwell looked at him.

Elizabeth waited.

Stansbury looked back at Pathwell.

Years passed between them in about half a second.

Then Stansbury said, "Let's get off the road first."

It was reasonable.

That made it worse.

Elizabeth looked down at the foam dagger in her hand.

Black moisture clung to the blade and vanished before reaching the handle.

She wiped it on the grass anyway.

Pathwell reached for the Cadillac passenger door.

Stansbury stopped him.

"Back."

Pathwell frowned.

"I dislike the back."

"Survive it."

Stansbury opened the passenger door for himself.

Elizabeth looked at him.

"You gave me the keys because you wanted that seat."

"I still do."

Of course he did.

Elizabeth got behind the wheel.

She started the Cadillac.

The engine caught on the second turn.

Pathwell sat behind her.

For several seconds nobody said anything.

Elizabeth waited.

She pulled onto the road.

The bar disappeared behind them.

Fields opened on either side, black beyond the headlights.

"Mirror," Stansbury said.

Pathwell shifted in the back seat.

"The pull got stronger in the hall."

Elizabeth looked at him in the rearview mirror.

"What pull?"

Pathwell's eyes met hers in the glass.

Then moved to Stansbury.

"I've felt it before."

"How long?" Stansbury asked.

"A while. Off and on. Long enough that I stopped treating it as new."

Elizabeth tightened her hands on the wheel.

"What pull?"

Stansbury turned slightly in his seat toward Pathwell.

"Since the prune that went wrong?"

"Maybe."

"Which prune?"

Pathwell was quiet.

Elizabeth looked at the road.

"Hello?"

"Drive," Pathwell said.

Her hands went still on the wheel.

The word had come out automatic.

Not cruel.

Worse.

Useful.

She drove.

Stansbury continued.

"And the mirror?"

"Nothing clean. My face felt wrong. The tension had direction if I tested it. Barely."

"Toward what?"

"I don't know."

"But then this one came for you."

"Yes."

"And the first one found you in her apartment before there was fresh waste."

Pathwell did not answer.

Elizabeth did.

"My apartment."

Neither brother responded immediately.

The Cadillac moved through the dark.

The road hummed under the tires.

She had become very aware of how much machine she was currently responsible for.

"If whatever it keeps finding is close enough to you that it can't tell the difference—" Stansbury began.

"Not me," Pathwell said.

"Then tell me what is different."

"I can't yet."

Elizabeth laughed once.

Both men stopped.

Not because anything was funny.

"There she is," Elizabeth said.

Pathwell looked at her in the mirror.

"What?"

"The person driving the car."

"Lizzy—"

"Elizabeth."

"Elizabeth."

"I was apparently important enough to get dragged out of my apartment. Important enough to carry your books. Important enough to give up mine. Important enough to bring along while you figure this out. Important enough to cut you out of that thing."

Pathwell's mouth opened.

She kept going.

"But somehow every time the part comes where somebody explains what the hell is happening, I become the driver."

Silence.

Stansbury looked forward again.

That was answer enough from him.

Pathwell leaned between the seats.

"We're trying to understand it."

"So am I."

"There are pieces you don't have."

"Whose decision was that?"

Pathwell stopped.

There.

Elizabeth felt it land.

Not resolution.

Not victory.

Just the first time the shape of the problem had been said inside the car.

Stansbury rubbed one thumb along the edge of his seatbelt.

"She's right," he said.

Pathwell sat back.

"Yes."

Elizabeth waited.

Nothing followed.

The brothers did not resume the conversation.

That should have helped.

It didn't.

Because now they were quiet together.

Still holding whatever came next between them.

Elizabeth drove.

The dagger pressed against the back of her waistband.

Her hands were steady on the wheel.

The headlights found a reflector.

Then a mile marker.

Then a long empty stretch of road with nothing coming the other way.

For the first time since the apartment, nobody was telling Elizabeth where to go.
