Chapter One

It was the crash that woke her.

Stepping out into the living room she found a man going through her boxes. One stack had gone over, and her winter sweaters were all over the rug.

The party was still out here. Plastic cups on every flat surface, a tray of cheese cubes going dry, and half a sheet cake that now only read ELIZ, because her coworkers had started at the wrong end. A WELCOME ELIZABETH banner hung by one corner over the kitchen doorway. It was a good party, better than she'd expected. The guests had left hours ago, and she'd gone straight to bed.

She knew him, a little, he'd been at the party. She assumed someone from work had brought him. She had seen him peering into an open box of old notebooks once, before somebody asked for more cake. He'd laughed at her joke about the landlord, which was more than anyone from work had managed. But she'd never caught his name and she thought he had left with the rest.

But there he was, sat on the floor, with a box open beside him, reading her diary like a magazine in a waiting room.

She watched for a moment before interrupting. "What are you still doing here?"

"Reading," he said, turning a page.

A thud hit the front door. Wet. Heavy.

He looked up at the door. Then at her.

"Friend of yours?"

"What?"

She went to the door and turned the lock and opened it against the chain. Something wet and dark sagged into the gap, bringing with it a stench of low tide. It shifted against the jamb, leaving a smear that ran toward the floor. The man threw himself across the room, and the door slammed hard enough to rattle the chain. The bolt shot home.

The door bowed in its frame and settled back. His face changed.

He went straight back to the boxes. "Where's your cookbook?" he said without looking up. He shoved a box aside. "The old one."

"Excuse me?"

"The old one, the handwritten one with notes. I know there's one around here somewhere."

Another thud, harder.

"Now."

"They're… they're in a box in the kitchen?"

He blew past her, and she heard tape rip in the kitchen before he came back with the book in hand.

Nana's cookbook, three rubber bands around the spine and the title in her blue marker.

"Don't think. What's your favorite recipe?"

"I—"

"Don't. Think."

The door creaked and strained.

"My Nana always made the best sugar cookies?"

"Good… that's good."

Flipping through worn and stained pages, he found a tagged recipe. 'Nana Jones Sugar Cookies'.

He placed his hand flat on the page. "You should probably get behind something."

Gold ran along the ink in little starts and stops, tracing the page, even through the crooked places where the pen had hesitated.

The door began to crack and splinter. Ooze searched through the gaps, reaching out for her.

The glow consumed the page, a pool of light forming under his hand.

"What are you doing!?" Elizabeth grabbed the cookbook by the spine and pulled it from his hands. The sugar-cookie page tore free, sticking to his palm.

Everything went white, and a scent hit her: buttery and sweet. Followed by a taste… and something else, someone else. A voice that couldn't be there.

She was twelve again, baking cookies with her grandmother. Sun shining through an open window above the sink, the fresh smell of rain filled her lungs. Her grandmother smiled at her, and Elizabeth felt something she hadn't felt in years.

Her body moved back before she did, sending her stumbling into the wall with the cookbook falling to the floor.

The light under his palm had gone wrong. It spat and flickered, loose threads of gold dripped onto the carpet.

Another wet thud and the door crumbled beneath the behemoth's weight. Now she could see the whole thing: some sort of blob, pushing its way through the door frame, taking pieces with it.

The blob rolled over the fallen threads of gold. Taking their light with it.

It darted for the light in the man's hand. He raised the glowing page to meet it.

The impact sang.

The blob rippled and recoiled, the stench of wet-rot splattered across the room. Elizabeth ran for the bathroom, her stomach fighting to keep its contents to itself.

Lunging again, the blob knocked the man from his feet and back across the room. Elizabeth could only see it looming over him as she slammed the door shut.

Sounds of struggles, pounding, and kicking. A loud wet smack, and a thud against the wall.

Then nothing.

She pressed her ear to the door.

Stillness. There was only her racing heart and stillness. A stillness interrupted by a dull rumbling, rumbling that grew into shaking, a shaking that rocked the walls around her.

Light streaked in from under the door, and then a pop.

"You can come out now. It's gone… mostly."

She cracked the door. The living room had exploded, covered in bits of rotting gelatinous ooze. The banner had come down into it.

The cake, somehow, was fine.

And there in the middle of it all stood her last guest, holding Nana's cookbook and her diary.

"What. What just happened?" she asked from behind the door.

He glanced around. "That? That was nothing. Just cleaning up a mess."

Then he looked at her more carefully.

"You really don't know?"

"Know what?"

"Huh." He looked from her to the wrecked doorway and back again.

She opened the door slowly, peeking out.

"Right, so…" He shifted the books under his arm. "We should probably get going."

He was already moving toward the empty doorway, stepping over and around debris. Sticking his head out, he checked up and down the hall. Then he went still.

When he turned back, he was sure of himself again.

"Your house has now been marked. Magically speaking," he said, nodding his head to the side. "So we should probably be anywhere but here."

"I'm not going anywhere until you tell me what is going on."

"You really have no idea?" He looked her over, searching for a reaction. "Well that confusion is quite normal. Do you have shoes?"

"Yes, but—"

"Good. Now here's the thing. We really do need to get going. So please. Grab your things."

She looked at the cookbook in his hand, bent open at the ragged edge where the page had been.

"Why could I hear her voice?"

"What?"

"Why could I hear my Nana?"

"Because it was hers, now really, we should be going."

"No, look at what you did, my apartment is wrecked, how am I going to even clean… whatever this even is?"

"Understandable reaction. Reasonable, even. Unfortunately. We really need to be going."

"I already told you I'm not—"

"Yes, I did mention the whole marked thing, didn't I?"

She stepped out of the bathroom. Her stomach dropped as the wave of rot hit her again.

His attention shifted down the hall. "Shoes. Now. I did say please."

A wet thud hit from down the hall.

Her heart skipped and without thinking, she jumped into her shoes, and she threw on a coat.

With a smile he grabbed her by the hand.

"Ready?"

"No."

"Perfect."
