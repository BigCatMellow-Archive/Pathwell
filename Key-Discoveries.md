# Key Discoveries

**Insights earned through revision work. These aren't rules imposed from outside — they're things the story taught us.**

---

## About Craft

- Scene connections come from character wants, not plot logistics. When a connection feels weak, don't add mechanics — ask what the character wants.
- Test every option by asking **who's choosing and who's being dragged**. If the protagonist is being dragged, the option is wrong.
- Important objects leave characters' hands through character-driven moments under pressure, not through transactions or convenience.
- [[Agency Ladder|Agency is a ladder]] — you can't skip rungs. Each step must cost slightly more than the last.
- There's a **ceiling on sacrifice** as growth. At some point, giving things away stops being a higher rung. The next level is INITIATING rather than RESPONDING.
- Feelings (excitement, curiosity, comfort) are fuel, not engines. Agency requires action, not emotion.
- "Because" and "therefore" are the same causal link seen from different directions. The test isn't the word — it's whether you can answer "why does this happen?" with character motivation.
- Comedy requires emotional breathing room. Trauma must be processed before punchlines can land.
- Don't build plot devices to do the character's emotional work. No portals that reveal true desires. No fortune tellers. The character earns their own revelations.
- Respect what's already on the page. Don't undo a character moment to reuse an object or beat.

---

## About [[Elizabeth]]

- She uses her OWN emotions as magic — the only character who does. Everyone else borrows. This is the thematic answer: real agency comes from within.
- The diary's magical cost (reliving her anger, fear, hurt) IS the character moment. The [[Magic System]]'s rules create the crucible automatically.
- She can't go back to comfort at any point without losing her arc. Every safe landing must be followed by a choice to leave it.
- Her choices must escalate from reactions (responding to pressure) to initiations (starting something herself).

---

## About [[Pathwell (Character)|Pathwell]]

- His arc is about the **loss of leverage**, not the gain of wisdom. He doesn't learn to be good. He loses the ability to cheat.
- The private retry at The Space Between is the most important quiet moment in his arc. He tries the thing he's always done — prune to buy something — and it doesn't work. And he's *relieved*. That's the crack.
- His offhand admission must be OFFHAND. Not a speech. Not a breakdown. Thrown away.

---

## About the [[Magic System]]

- The strongest moments are sensory, not explanatory. Sugar cookies > classification charts.
- The consent rule for [[Pruning]] creates automatic drama — if it works, he meant it; if it cancels, he's lying to himself.
- Elizabeth using her own diary breaks the system's usual pattern (borrow from others) and that violation IS the thematic statement.

---

## About Tone

A dry, precise, slightly absurdist voice — treating the extraordinary as unremarkable — was identified as the tone the novel wants. This is consistent with what already works in the manuscript. It's about recognizing what the story already is, not changing it.
